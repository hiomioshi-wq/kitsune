Incarnate: Prophecy
A spinoff of the Corruption Saga. After Sorry.

Trigger warning: This story contains addiction at its worst, revenge, grief inflicted on purpose, the deaths of gods, the end of everything, self-hurt themes, adult language, and a narrator who reads her own prophecy and decides to make it true.
Read at your own risk.



Chapter One: The Page

When the end begins, corruption will fall, and there shall be one with shining blonde hair, the beloved sister, fueled by grief to harbor this corruption and end everything in a wasted attempt at redemption.

I've known those words since I was seven. People would say them at me in the grocery store. Whisper them. Kids at school would write them on my locker. My brother Tim used to cross them out with a Sharpie and write BULLSHIT underneath, and we'd laugh.

She will end everything. All of it.

I'm walking barefoot through the Gray Abyss of Shattered Ruins with every god Calypso ever ate inside me, and all the corruption I ever relapsed on, and both of them singing, and I'm thinking about that page.

I always thought prophecies were like weather. They just happen to you. I didn't know until today that somebody writes them down on purpose.

Hi. I'm Ena. Day one.

That's a joke. There aren't days anymore.



Chapter Two: What It's Like

You want to know what it's like? Having it in you? The song and the corruption at once?

You know the hum. The corruption hum. The one that says you're right. Whatever you do next, you're right. You're not a girl whose brother stabbed her. You're a weapon. I've told you about that. I've told everybody about that, at meetings, on folding chairs, with chicory in a styrofoam cup.

Now add a voice to it.

Not my voice. A voice made out of every god she ate. The god of fire's wanting. The god of rivers' wanting. Conquest taking off her crown. Thousands of them, all the old bored cruel immortal things, and every last wish they had, inside me, singing.

The corruption tells me I'm right.

The song tells the whole universe.

I can feel every world. Like Calypso said. Like a sky full of radios. And every world can hear me. And I can hear every world. All of them, everyone, everything, every person in every story, wanting. Wanting so loud.

It's the most powerful I've ever been.

And it's the worst I've ever felt.

That's the trick of it, baby, says Harumi, inside, so warm. It's always both.



Chapter Three: The Highway

They're on the guardrail. Where Eri said.

Himo, in her white coat, with the stitches in her chest where I stabbed her once, for five seconds, in another story. Sitting very straight. And next to her, leaning on her shoulder, a big old woman made of scars. Chimera. I met her once in the Nursery. She called me a bitch and then apologized for it. She called Himo a lot worse.

Chimera's almost gone. I can see it. She's going soft at the edges, like a drawing somebody's erasing. Her scars are letting go one by one. She's smiling. Her eyes are closed.

And Himo's holding her hand.

Turned around. All the way. Looking at her. With her face wet.

I stand in the road thirty feet away with red coming off me like heat and I watch them.

I watched this woman through a kitchen window, once. Except it was the other way around. She watched me. For a whole year. Pancakes on Sundays. My dad burning them. My mom singing about a river.

She chose us because we were happy.



Chapter Four: Himo Looks Up

She knows I'm there. She doesn't stop holding Chimera's hand. She just lifts her eyes.

"Ena," she says.

"You remember my name."

"I remember everything I make."

"You didn't make me."

"I made what you are," Himo says. Not cruel. Just true. Like reading a number off a chart. "The prophecy. The grief. The corruption. The girl in the kitchen. I made that."

"You killed my parents."

"Yes."

"In front of us. On a Sunday."

"Yes."

"So my brother would believe the prophecy. So he'd put a knife in me. So I'd come back and end the world for you."

"Yes," Himo says. "And you did. Most of it. And then you stabbed me. And then I needed a plan C." She looks at me. At the red. At the gold and blue leaking out of my mouth when I breathe. "And now you've taken plan C out of the tank and put it in yourself. Which I didn't plan."

"Surprise."

"No," she says. "Not really. You were always going to end up here. It was written down."

Chimera stirs. Opens one eye. Looks at me.

"Oh, fuck," Chimera says, weakly. "Oh, kid. You too?"



Chapter Five: What Eri Wanted

Eri wanted me to make Himo sorry.

That's what she said, lying on the floor of the Nursery in the green water with her legs broken, after she saw everything in my head. After she saw Dov and the tins and the forty-one days that were nine. She didn't scream at me. She didn't tell me not to.

She told me to punch it.

I know why. I'm not stupid. She's been waiting ten thousand years for two words. Sorry, Nine. And she saw me, the girl who lied to her every Tuesday, glowing, full of corruption, about to be the strongest thing in any universe, and she thought: there. There's how I get it.

She used me.

And I let her. Because it gave me permission. I asked her to tell me not to, and she told me to, and now it's her fault too. It's not just mine.

That's what a sponsor's for, baby, says Harumi. To share the weight.

I'm going to get Eri her sorry. That's the plan. That's what I tell myself, standing on the old highway. I'm doing it for Eri.

It's a lie. I know it's a lie. I've been lying since day thirty-two.

I'm doing it because it was written down.



Chapter Six: The Song

I sing at her.

It comes out of me so easy. Like breathing. Like it's been waiting at the back of my throat my whole life. The ruins shake. The guardrail rattles. Somewhere a dog with a cat's head wakes up from smiling and starts barking.

And I go looking for what Himo wants.

That's how the song works. Calypso taught me, in a way, without meaning to. I can feel what's in her, laid out like a map. Her want. Her one big want. The one thing she's wanted since before the first war. End the gods. End the gods. End the gods. A plan. Neat and cold and humming like a refrigerator.

But it's not the only thing anymore.

Right next to it, new, raw, so bright it hurts, is something else. A want she's never had in her whole existence. Not like this.

She wants Chimera to stay.

She wants it so much it's shaking her. She wants the scarred old woman leaning on her shoulder to not go soft at the edges. She wants one more hundred thousand years. She wants the ugly bird. She wants to be attached.

She finally turned around.

And I know exactly what to do with that.



Chapter Seven: Grief Is Fuel

"You told me something once," I say to Himo. "Or you told your notebook, and I read it later, because Eri reads everything. Grief is the most powerful fuel in any universe. A grieving mind will burn down every star to get back one face."

Himo's hand tightens on Chimera's.

"Ena," she says. "Don't."

"You chose us because we were happy. You needed us happy. So it would hurt more when you took it."

"Ena."

"Pancakes on Sundays," I say. "My mom singing about a river. My dad burning them every time. You climbed through the kitchen window."

I sing.

Not at Himo. At Chimera.

You want to end, I sing. Calypso's words. In my voice. With the corruption in it. Now. Right now. You don't have to wait.

And Chimera, who's been holding on, I don't know how, I don't know why, maybe for an hour, maybe for her, just to let Himo look at her a little longer, just lets go.

It's fast. Like a candle. Her scars unstitch all at once. She goes soft and gray and then she's dust, she's dust on the guardrail, in Himo's hand, blowing off into the ruins.

The last thing she says is, "Worth it."

Himo screams.



Chapter Eight: Soi Kijo

I've never heard a sound like it. And I've heard my brother. In the second after the knife.

Himo is on her knees on the old highway with her hands full of gray dust and her white coat spread around her and she's screaming, and the stitches in her chest have torn open, and she's not trying to hold them shut. She's trying to hold the dust. It keeps running through her fingers.

And I stand over her, glowing, and I feel it come off her. The grief. Oh my god. The grief. A hundred thousand years of not getting attached, all at once, coming due.

It's the best fuel in any universe. She was right.

The corruption drinks it. The song drinks it. I get bigger. I get so much bigger.

She looks up at me with her face wrecked. Like a child. Like the girl in the kitchen.

"Why?" she says.

And I think about telling her. About the window and the Sunday and the pancakes and my brother with the knife and my grave on the hill that's actually his grave and the cat named Tim and the laundromat and Dov and the tins and Eri on the floor of the Nursery with her legs broken, waiting ten thousand years for two words.

I could explain all of it. I think she'd understand. She's smart.

"Soi Kijo," I say. "Joi Kiieanian Kio."

Sorry. But the war is over.

I watch her hear it. I watch her understand where I learned it.



Chapter Nine: Sorry

"Say it," I tell her.

She's still on her knees. Still holding dust. Her chest's bleeding through her coat.

"Say it. For Eri. For Nine. She's on the floor of the Nursery. She can't walk. You broke her legs. You've been breaking her for ten thousand years. Say you're sorry."

Himo looks at me. And then she looks down at the dust in her hands.

"I'm sorry," she says.

And for one second I think I did it. I think I got it. I'll go back to the Nursery and carry Eri out and tell her, she said it, she said it, she's sorry, and Eri will cry, and it'll be worth it.

Then I hear who she's saying it to.

"I'm sorry," Himo says again, to the dust, to the ruins, to nothing. "I'm sorry. I'm sorry. I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I should have turned around. I'm so sorry."

Not to Eri. Not to Nine. Not to me. Not to my parents, or Tim, or the girl in the kitchen, or a single one of the gods she ended, or the trillions of mortals in the first war, or the jar on the shelf.

To Chimera.

That's the only one she's sorry for.

That's the only one she'll ever be sorry for.

I stand there with everything in every world inside me and I realize there's no sorry coming. Not for Eri. Not for anybody. There was never going to be. She doesn't have enough sorry in her for that. Nobody does.

You see, baby? says Harumi. You see now?



Chapter Ten: The Beloved Sister

I sit down on the guardrail. Where Chimera was. Himo's still on her knees in the road, whispering to the dust. I let her. She's not going anywhere. She's got nowhere left.

I can feel every world. All of them. Humming. Waiting. Calypso left them all tuned to the song, and now the song is me, and they're all listening.

The cat's on the roof. Tim. Orange, half an ear, a jerk. I can feel him from here. He's sitting on my mattress under the tarp that leaks on the left side, waiting for me to come home and say a number.

Eri's on the floor of the Nursery. Waiting for two words.

Marguerite's in her apartment with her map, wondering why the hounds have all stopped moving.

Noor's making tea in Pittsburgh. Hattie's tucked in, in Halcyon. Juniper's chalking on the walls of a grain elevator. Somewhere, a girl in a crown is sitting in the corner of a vault with her knees up, saying that's mine, that's mine.

And my brother's grave is on a hill. With a stone I carved with rebar. TIM. BROTHER. I'M SORRY. ME TOO.

I could stop. I want to say that. I could close my mouth. I could let it all go quiet. I could walk back to my roof and lie down on my mattress and tell the cat it's day one and, for once in my life, mean it.

I've been running from the page my whole life.

I'm tired, baby, says Harumi. Aren't you tired?

I am.

And the thing about a prophecy is, it isn't weather. Somebody writes it. And once it's written, somebody has to decide whether to make it true.

Himo wrote mine.

I'm deciding.



Chapter Eleven: Revelation Soon After

When the end begins, corruption will fall, and there shall be one with shining blonde hair, the beloved sister, fueled by grief to harbor this corruption and end everything in a wasted attempt at redemption.

I always thought the "wasted" was the cruelest word in it.

It isn't. It's the most honest. I'm not doing this for redemption. Nobody's going to forgive me. There's nobody left who could. I'm not doing it for Eri, or for my parents, or for Tim. I'm not doing it to stop Calypso, or to beat Himo, or to save anyone from anything.

I'm doing it because I'm a girl who was told her whole life she would end everything, by everyone, in grocery stores and on lockers and in the dark of her own head, and I spent every day of my life saying no, saying it's bullshit, saying day one, day two, day forty-one, and I am so tired of carrying a sentence somebody else wrote.

If I can't put it down, I'll finish it.

That's all. That's the whole reason. It's not a good reason. It's mine.

I stand up on the guardrail. The ruins go on forever, gray, under a gray sky. In every world, everything goes quiet. Listening.

Himo looks up from the dust. She sees my face. She knows what I'm going to do. She wrote it.

"Ena," she says. "Please."

First time anybody's ever said please to me.

I open my mouth.

Fueled by grief.

She will end everything.

All of it.

Day one.
