Incarnate: Sorry
A spinoff of the Corruption Saga. During Main Character, Uncut.

Trigger warning: This story contains broken bodies, mind reading, the discovery of a friend's lies, addiction, enabling, revenge, adult language, and a narrator who knows exactly what she's handing her best friend and hands it over anyway.
Read at your own risk.



Chapter One: Elbows

I crawled for six hours on my elbows.

Legs don't work. The Nursery babies held me by them too hard when they carried me out of the vault, and they don't know how hard is too hard, because they don't know anything, and when they put me down in a drainage pipe on the way to wherever they put things Himo's done with, both my shins were in more pieces than shins should be.

I'm a demon of the mind. I can go into anybody's head. I can pull out their worst day like a splinter and sell it. I can make a man forget his own name.

Can't fix a shin, though. Nobody can fix a shin.

So I crawled.

And the whole time, the song. Twenty-two's song. Coming up out of the bottom of everything. You want to end. You want to end. It's not for me, not exactly, it's for gods, but I was built out of god parts like all of Himo's kids, and some of it splashes. I'd be crawling through the rubble and I'd think, this is a nice puddle, this would be a nice place to stop. And I'd bite the inside of my cheek until it bled, and keep going.

I wasn't going to stop until somebody said sorry.

Ten thousand years. That's all I've wanted. Since the edge of the pit. Her hand on my back. Don't get attached. I want her to turn around and look at me and say it.

Sorry, Nine.

That's all. It's so stupid. It's two words.



Chapter Two: Chimera

Chimera walked right past me.

You know that part. I screamed at her about the tank, and she stepped over me like I was a crack in the sidewalk, and kept going, toward the old highway, toward Himo.

I was in her head when she did it. I saw why. I saw a little ugly bird made out of a fish and a moth and a scrap of a dead star, singing off-key. I saw a hundred thousand years of loving a woman who never once said it back.

And I understood. That's the worst part. I understood completely. I've been wanting the same woman to turn around for ten thousand years. Chimera just wanted it a different way.

"Oh, you idiot," I said.

She didn't turn around either. Nobody in this family ever turns around.

So I kept crawling. Toward the tank. Because somebody had to.



Chapter Three: The Throat

The Nursery's quiet now. That's what gets me. It used to hum with machines. Now it just hums with her.

The tanks are all still there, rows and rows, the babies floating in them. But their eyes are closed now. All of them. Every one. Smiling. The song got into them too, finally. Twenty-two always said you can't sing to something that doesn't want. Turns out if you sing long enough, loud enough, even the empty ones start to want something.

They want to end, too.

The ushers are lying on the floor of the vault. All six. Gray. Still. Smiling.

And the big tank.

It goes up into the dark like a skyscraper. Green fluid. And inside it, the throat. The thing Himo built out of every experiment that failed before me. And inside the throat, if you look hard, if you go into it with your mind, there's a girl. Barely. Mostly gone. A crown floating at the top, bumping against the lid.

I put my hand on the glass. It was warm.

I went in. Just a little.

And she was so happy, babe. That's what she'd say. Babe. She was so happy. She was everywhere. She was in every world. She was looking at everything and everything was looking at her, and she was eating, and eating, and it was the best thing she'd ever tasted.

And underneath that, way down, where she keeps the things she doesn't say. A jar on a shelf. Humming. Waiting for someone to turn around.

Same as me. Same as Chimera. Same as all of us.

I took my hand off the glass.

I couldn't break it. I tried. I pounded on it with my fists until my knuckles split, and the glass didn't even shake. I don't have that kind of strength. I'm not that kind of thing. Mind demons crack heads, not glass.

I lay down on the floor next to the six smiling ushers and I said, out loud, to nobody, "Somebody come. Somebody, please, come."

Somebody came.



Chapter Four: Ena

She came through the vault door glowing red.

Not at the edges. Through. All the way. Red coming off her skin like heat off asphalt. Her eyes were red. Her hair, her shining blonde hair, she'd cut it short and it was matted and dirty, but it was still shining, it was shining red now. Her left hand was black to the elbow. Smoking.

She was humming. Not the song. Something else. Something older. The prophecy laugh, low, like a drain.

"Eri," she said.

"Babe," I said. "Babe, oh my god. Ena."

She knelt next to me. She put her black hand on my face. It was hot. It felt like a stove.

"Your legs," she said.

"Doesn't matter. Ena, listen. The tank. You can break it. You're the anti-god of destruction, you can break anything, you can—"

"I know," she said. "That's why I'm here."

And I went into her head.

I didn't mean to. It's a reflex. When I'm scared I go into people, the way you grab a railing. And I went into Ena's head, my best friend, the one I climb seven flights for every Tuesday, the one I bump shoulders with on the roof, and I saw.



Chapter Five: What I Saw

A red-and-white mint tin behind a water tank. Fourteen shards.

Day thirty-two.

Day nine, when she told me forty-one.

A laundromat. Glass in the windows. Six hounds coming apart like sugar. A kid named Dov behind the dryers saying, "You're using." Her hand moving. Him coming apart like sugar too.

"They got him. I was too late."

The funeral. Her crying. Me holding her hand. Me saying, you did everything you could.

A blue button tin. Twenty-two shards. On her hands and knees on the laundromat tile at three in the morning picking up pieces of a boy and pieces of dogs and not knowing which.

"Day one, Tim."

Every Tuesday. Day two, day three, day five. Every one a lie. Every single one. To me. On the roof. While I bumped her shoulder and said I was proud of her.

And Marguerite. In the back of it all. Leaving tins on rubble. Pointing her at hound packs. Saying I'm proud of you.

And the last three weeks, since the song started. Ena on the roof, feeling it come up through the building, you want to end, and Harumi in her head, laughing. Laughing so hard.

That's not your song, baby. That's ours. That was supposed to be you. The beloved sister. Fueled by grief. She will end everything. That's your prophecy. Some cheerleader's stealing it.

And Ena going behind the water tank. And taking out the blue tin. And not picking one shard.

Tipping all of them into her hand.

I pulled out of her head so fast I hit mine on the floor.



Chapter Six: The Truth

"You lied to me," I said.

Ena looked at me. Red eyes. Something sad in there, way down, under the red.

"Yeah," she said.

"For months. Every Tuesday."

"Yeah."

"You killed Dov."

She closed her eyes. "Yeah."

"And you're not here to break the tank to save everybody, are you." My voice was shaking. "You're here because Harumi wants it. The song. You're going to break the tank and then you're going to take it. All of it. Everything Twenty-two is singing through that throat. You're going to put it in you with the corruption, and you're going to be what they said. The one who ends everything."

Ena didn't say anything for a long time.

Then she said, very quietly, in her own voice, Ena's voice, "Somebody's going to do it, Eri. It might as well be the one it was written for."

And she looked at me, my best friend, glowing red, kneeling on the floor of the Nursery next to my broken legs.

"Tell me not to," she said. "Please. You're the only one I'd listen to. Tell me not to and I'll walk out. I'll let the cheerleader do it. Just tell me."



Chapter Seven: What I Wanted

Here's what I should have said.

Don't. Ena, don't. Put the shards down. Come here. Let me hold you. We'll figure it out. We'll find another way. You don't have to be the prophecy. You were never the prophecy. Himo wrote it. Himo made it up. She corrupted a prophet for one second and wrote you down on a page and then she climbed in your kitchen window and killed your parents to make it true. You don't owe her the ending. You don't owe anybody the ending.

That's what a friend would say.

That's what I'd say on the roof, on a Tuesday, bumping her shoulder.

But I was lying on the floor of the place Himo made me. Six smiling ushers next to me who'd broken my legs because she told them to. And somewhere out on the old highway, Himo was sitting on a guardrail holding Chimera's hand. I'd seen it in Chimera's head. She'd turned around for Chimera. She'd looked at her.

She never turned around for me.

Ten thousand years. Two words.

And here was Ena. The anti-god of destruction, full of corruption, about to have the voice of everything. The only thing in any universe strong enough to walk out to that highway and make Himo turn around.

And make her sorry.



Chapter Eight: Punch It

"Ena," I said.

She waited.

"Break it."

Her face did something. Relief and horror at once. Like when you've been holding your breath underwater and somebody finally lets you up and you realize you're in the middle of the ocean.

"Eri—"

"Break it," I said. "And take it. All of it. The song. Twenty-two. Every god she's eaten. Put it in you with the corruption. Be the prophecy. Be the one it was written for. And then go out to the old highway."

My voice wasn't shaking anymore.

"She's out there. Himo. With Chimera. Sitting on a guardrail like nothing happened. Go out there with the song in you and make her want to end. Make her feel what she did to you. To your parents. To me. To all of us."

"And then what?" Ena asked.

I didn't answer. I didn't know. I didn't care. I'd stopped caring about then what a long time ago, somewhere in hell, on a stool by a river, taking pearls out of people.

"Make her sorry," I said. "That's all I want. Make her say it."

Ena looked at me for a long time. My best friend. Who lied to me every Tuesday.

I'm lying to her now. I want you to know that. I'm a mind demon. I could reach into her right now, at the soft part, and turn her around. I could make her put the shards down. I could make her walk out. I've done it to a thousand damned souls by a river that isn't water. It would take one push.

I didn't push.

That's the lie. Not what I said. What I didn't do.



Chapter Nine: Glass

She stood up.

She put her black hand flat on the tank. Where mine had been. The glass started to smoke.

"Thank you, Eri," she said. Not Harumi. Ena. "For being there for me."

And she pulled her arm back and punched it.

I've seen a lot of things break. Worlds. Gods. People. Me. I've never seen anything break like that tank. It didn't crack. It came apart all at once, like a soap bubble, like it had been waiting to. The fluid came out in a wave and knocked me across the floor. Green and warm and smelling like the sea.

And the song stopped.

Everywhere. All at once. In every world, in every dimension, in Cleveland and Pittsburgh and a town in Illinois and a laundromat on Fifth. For one second, there was no song.

The silence was the loudest thing I've ever heard.

And then it started again.

Lower. Rougher. Red at the edges.

Ena was standing in the middle of the flood with her arms out and her head back and her mouth open, and the throat, the thing Himo built, was pouring into her. Blue light. Gold light. The light of every god Twenty-two had eaten. Every want. Every story. Going into her like water down a drain. And mixing with the red.

And out of her mouth came the song.

Not Twenty-two's song.

Hers.

On the floor of the Nursery, in the puddle where the tank had been, there was a girl. Small. Wet. Shaking. A crown next to her, bent. Mostly human-shaped. She lifted her head and looked at Ena with blue eyes and opened her mouth to sing and nothing came out.

"Hi," she whispered. Little voice. Jar voice. "Hi. That's mine."

Ena didn't look at her. Ena was looking at the vault door. Toward the highway. Toward Himo.

She walked out. Glowing. Singing. Barefoot in the green water.

She didn't look back at me either.

Nobody in this family ever does.



Chapter Ten: Waiting

I'm still on the floor of the Nursery. My legs still don't work.

Twenty-two crawled into the corner after Ena left. She's sitting there with her knees up, holding her bent crown, humming to herself. Barely. Like a radio with the batteries going. She keeps saying "that's mine, that's mine," and then "whatever, next," and then "that's mine" again.

I could go into her head. I could see what's left. I don't.

I can hear Ena's song from here. It's coming down from the old highway, through the ruins, through the walls, into the tanks. It's not saying you want to end anymore.

It's saying something else.

It's saying: This is what you get.

And I'm lying here in the green water, and I'm waiting. For Ena to come back. For Himo to come walking back through that vault door with red in her eyes, turned all the way around, finally, looking for me.

Sorry, Nine.

That's all I want. I'll know it when I hear it. Two words.

I handed my best friend the end of the world so I could hear them.

I'd do it again.

I'm still waiting.
