Incarnate: Leftovers
A spinoff of the Corruption Saga, told by Calypso. After Sorry, during Prophecy.

Trigger warning: This story contains the eating of a want that took ten thousand years to grow, a friend's mind emptied for snacks, body horror, the end of everything, adult language, and a narrator who has just lost almost all her power and feels zero guilt about anything, still, obviously, have you met her.
Read at your own risk. She's smaller now. She's not nicer.



Chapter One: Okay So, Rude

Okay so first of all, hi, it's me, and I need everyone to know that I am not okay, and also that it's not my fault.

Recap, fast, because I literally don't have the energy for a long one. I was in a tank. I was a voice. I was the main character of every story that ever got told. I was singing to ten thousand gods and they were going out like birthday candles and it was the best meal of my entire existence.

And then a blonde girl with a black arm punched my tank.

Punched it. Like a vending machine. Like it owed her a Twix.

And everything I was came pouring out of the glass and went straight into her mouth. Every god. Every want. My whole song. Gone. Down her throat like a Capri Sun.

And I was on the floor.

On the floor, babe. In a puddle. Like a goldfish somebody dropped.

I'm Calypso. I'm a cosmic hunger older than your planet's salt. I was Westfield High's homecoming queen. And right now I'm sitting in the corner of my mom's vault with my knees up, soaking wet, holding a crown that's bent in the middle like somebody sat on it.

She didn't even look at me. Ena. She walked right past. Barefoot. Singing my song in her voice. Wrong. She's doing it wrong. She's doing it angry.

You don't sing angry. You sing cute. Everybody knows that.



Chapter Two: Inventory

Let's see what's left.

I close my eyes and reach out for the first time since the glass broke, the way you pat your pockets after you get mugged.

Phone? Gone. Wallet? Gone. Every world, every radio, every sky full of stars and the gods sitting on top of them?

Gone. Gone. Gone.

I can hear the room. That's it. One room. Like going from Spotify Premium to a car radio that only gets the farm report.

I can hear the six ushers on the floor. Gray. Still. They don't want anything anymore. I did that. Nice.

I can hear the babies in the tanks. All of them smiling. All of them ended. Also me. Also nice. Honestly, I did great work in here. Somebody should give me a plaque.

And I can hear one more thing.

It's on the floor across the vault, in the green water, by the place where my tank used to be. A girl with broken legs, lying very still, with her eyes open.

And it's loud.

Oh my god. It's so loud.

It's the loudest want in the room. It might be the loudest want I've heard since I got out of the tank. It's been growing for ten thousand years like a tree in a basement, all twisted and pale and huge, pressing on every wall of her.

Two words.

Sorry, Nine.

"Hi, Eri," I say. Little voice. Jar voice. I can't help it. That's all I've got left.



Chapter Three: Eri

She turns her head and looks at me.

We have history. Okay, a lot of history. She's my sister, technically. Nine. One of Mom's earlier attempts. Before me. Before the good one. She's the demon of the mind, she runs a little shop by a river in hell where she takes pearls out of people, and she has never once liked me, and I have never once cared.

"Twenty-two," she says. "You look like shit."

"You look like shit," I say. "Your legs are going the wrong direction."

"Yeah."

"Does it hurt?"

"Yeah."

"Cute."

She almost laughs. It turns into a cough.

"She's out there," Eri says. "Ena. On the highway. With Mom. I told her to make Mom sorry."

"I know, babe. I can hear it on you. It's like, all you are."

"And you know what's funny?" She stares up at the dark where the tank used to go. "I'm still waiting. Lying here. I keep thinking any second the vault door is going to open and Mom's going to walk in. Turned around. Looking for me. And she's going to say it."

I don't say anything.

I'm starving.



Chapter Four: Here's the Thing About Mom

Here's the thing about my mom. I've tasted her.

Not a lot. A sip. Back when I was in the tank and I was everywhere, I got a little bit of everyone, and that includes her. I know exactly what she wants.

I'll tell you. It's not a secret anymore. It's not even a want, really. It's more like a plan that learned to breathe. End the gods. End the gods. End the gods. Clean and cold and humming like a refrigerator.

And one new thing. Next to it. Small and bright and so sad it's honestly kind of embarrassing.

A scarred old woman on a guardrail.

That's it. That's the whole list. Two things.

There's no Nine on it. There's no Eri. There's no me. She does not want to say sorry to anyone, babe, and she's not going to, and I know that the way you know what's in your own fridge.

"She's never going to say it," I tell Eri.

"Shut up."

"I've literally tasted her."

"Shut up, Twenty-two."

"I'm just saying. You've been carrying that around for ten thousand years and it's never going to get delivered. It's like the world's saddest Amazon package."

She turns her face away from me.

And the want gets louder. Because that's what happens when you tell somebody they're never going to get what they want. It doesn't go away. It gets bigger. It gets hungrier.

It gets riper.



Chapter Five: The Shard

I'm not crawling for any reason. I want that clear. I'm just shifting position. In the direction of the tank.

There's glass everywhere where it broke. Thick green glass, curved, in pieces as big as surfboards and as small as teeth. The green fluid is draining out through the grates in the floor, slow, glugging.

And on one of the big pieces, on the inside, where it was touching me, there's a stain.

Blue. Gold. Shiny. Like the inside of an oyster.

That's me. That's what's left of me. A little film of the throat, stuck to the glass, like the last bit of frosting on the cardboard under the cake.

I lick it.

Don't make that face. You'd lick it too if it was the last of your whole entire self.

It tastes like gods. It tastes like ten thousand candles. It tastes like the best day of my life, a little stale, a little gross, like eating birthday cake out of the trash at three in the morning.

And I get some back.

Not a lot. Not the sky. But more than one room. I can feel the Nursery now. All of it. And past that, the ruins, and past that, faint, the old highway, where something red is singing.

And I can feel my teeth again.

I lick the whole piece clean. Then the next one. Then the next.



Chapter Six: Math

When I'm done, I sit on the wet floor and do math. I'm good at math. People don't expect that.

What I have: a little. Enough for maybe one good meal. Enough to hold one person's eyes at blue.

What Ena has: everything. My song. My gods. All the corruption. She's out there on the highway making Mom watch something. I can feel Mom from here and she is not okay. Something just happened. Something bad. Mom's want for the old woman on the guardrail just turned into a hole, and the hole is screaming.

Eri can feel it too. She's a mind demon. She's lying there with her eyes wide.

"What happened?" she whispers. "What did she do?"

"Babe, I think Ena just took Mom's girlfriend."

"Is Mom—"

"Mom's crying."

Eri shuts her eyes. She's smiling a little. Ugly. Wet. Ten thousand years of waiting. Finally. Mom on her knees.

And now.

Now. Here it comes. I can feel it go through her like a current. The want. Sorry, Nine. Bigger than it's ever been, because now there's a chance. Because Mom's finally soft. Because any second now.

It's so ripe it's glowing.

I've never wanted anything as much as I want that.



Chapter Seven: Hi

I crawl over to her. I'm not shifting position anymore. I'm crawling. Whatever. My knees are in green water. My bent crown is in my hand.

"Hi," I say.

She opens her eyes. She sees my face. She's a mind demon. She knows faces.

"No," she says.

"Babe."

"Twenty-two. No. Don't. Please. It's all I have."

"I know," I say. "That's why it smells so good."

I put my hand on her face. She can't move away. Her legs are going the wrong direction. She tries to get into my head, I feel it, a little hook, the thing she does to the damned by the river. But I'm not damned, babe. I'm not even really a mind. I'm a mouth.

And I look at her with my eyes going blue at the edges.

"You were literally just thinking," I say, "that you're so tired of waiting."

"I was literally just thinking that," Eri says.

Her pupils go big and soft.

And I eat it.



Chapter Eight: Sorry, Nine

Oh my god.

Okay. I need to describe this, because nobody else is ever going to taste it, because there was only ever one, and now it's in me.

Ten thousand years.

It tastes like the edge of a pit. Like a cold hand on your back and the floor going out from under you. Like waking up in the dark and calling for someone and not hearing her come. Every single day of ten thousand years, the same two words, pressed down, folded over, kneaded like bread dough until they're dense and heavy and sweet and sour all the way through.

It tastes like being somebody's daughter.

I don't know how else to put it.

It is honestly the best thing I've had since the gods. It might be better than the gods. The gods wanted to stop. That's boring. That's just being tired. This wanted something specific. This wanted one person to turn around. There's nothing in any universe more delicious than a want that specific. I've said that before. I'll say it on my deathbed. If I get one.

I eat the whole thing. Down to the stone in the middle.

And Eri smiles.

That's the thing. That's my rule. Always leave them happy.

She's lying on the floor of the vault with her legs broken and her eyes half closed and the green water around her and she's smiling, just a little, peaceful, the way the babies are smiling in their tanks.

"Huh," she says, in a light little voice. "I don't care anymore."

"Nope."

"I don't care if she says it."

"Nope."

"That's weird," Eri says. "I used to care so much. What was it about?"

"Honestly, babe? Nothing. Don't worry about it."

"Okay," she says. And she closes her eyes.

She's still breathing. She's still Eri. She's still a demon of the mind with a little shop by a river. She'll wake up later and she'll have her pearls and her pliers and her damned and her rules.

She just won't be waiting anymore.

You're welcome, Eri.



Chapter Nine: Some of You Are Mad

Okay. I can feel some of you being mad at me through the page, and I want to address it.

First of all, it was mine. She's my sister. Family shares. That's like the whole point of family.

Second of all, she was miserable. Ten thousand years of miserable. You saw. She handed her best friend the end of the world just to maybe hear two words. That's not a healthy want, babe, that's a tumor, and I'm basically a surgeon. You should be thanking me. Somebody should be.

Third of all, nobody was ever going to deliver that package. I did the math. I tasted Mom. You think Eri was going to get a better ending somewhere else? There's no somewhere else. There's an old highway and a blonde girl with my song in her mouth about to finish the page.

And fourth of all, and I need you to really hear this one.

I was hungry.

That's it. That's all it ever is. You keep waiting for me to have a reason. I keep telling you. You keep not listening.

No character growth. Don't even ask.



Chapter Ten: The Highway

I walk now. I can walk. Eri's want is in me like a hot meal and I've got legs again and a little bit of glow at my edges, enough to see by.

I put my bent crown on. It sits crooked. It's giving tragic. It's giving fallen queen. Honestly, I'm into it.

I go out of the vault door, past the tanks of smiling babies, out into the ruins, toward the singing.

The Gray Abyss of Shattered Ruins goes on forever. Broken temples. Melted cities. Bones of things too big to have bones. Everything gray under a gray sky. And down the middle, the old highway, cracked, with a guardrail running along it.

And on the guardrail, there's a girl.

Standing on it. Barefoot. Arms out. Red coming off her like heat. Gold and blue leaking out of her mouth every time she breathes. My gold. My blue.

And in the road in front of her, on her knees, in a white coat with her chest bleeding through it and gray dust running out of her hands, is my mom.

I've never seen her on her knees. Not once in forty thousand years. I want to take a picture. I don't have a phone. Unfortunate.

Ena hasn't seen me yet. She's looking at the sky. She's about to say something. I can feel it gathering in her, the way you feel a sneeze coming in somebody else. Everything in every world has gone quiet. Every radio. Every sea. All listening.

She will end everything. All of it.

Oh, babe, I think.

I can taste it from here.



Chapter Eleven: The Last Want

Here's the thing. Here's the math I did on the floor of the vault, and nobody else did, because nobody else is as smart as me, and also nobody else is a mouth.

When everything ends, everything stops wanting.

Every god. Every planet. Every person. Every story. All that wanting, just switching off, like a city at a blackout.

Except one.

Because somebody has to want it to end. Somebody has to keep wanting it right up until the last second, right through the last word, or it doesn't finish. That's how wanting works. That's how everything works.

The last want in the universe is going to be hers.

Ena's. The beloved sister. Standing on a guardrail, wanting to put down a sentence somebody else wrote. The biggest, oldest, tiredest, most specific want there is.

And it'll be alone. In the dark. With nothing around it. Nothing to protect it.

And I'm going to be right there.

I'm not trying to stop her. Let's be so clear. Gods, worlds, Pittsburgh, Ohio, whatever. Let it all go. I've already eaten the good parts.

I just want to be the last girl standing at the end of the party when the lights come on. With the last slice. With my crooked crown on.

I step out onto the highway. My bare feet on the cracked gray road. My mom on her knees. The blonde girl on the rail.

Ena looks down. She sees me. Her red eyes go wide.

Mom turns her head. She sees me too.

"Twenty-two," she says.

First time she ever turned around for me. Took the end of the world. Whatever. Noted.

I smile at both of them. All my teeth. I hold it at cute.

"Hi," I say. "Don't mind me, babe. Go ahead. Finish your sentence."

I sit down on the guardrail. Cross my legs. Fix my crown.

"I'll wait," I say. "I'm just here for leftovers."

Ena opens her mouth.

I open mine.
