BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY

SPECIAL: “MADISON, NOMAD”

TEASER

INT. GAS STATION – NOWHERE, NEVADA-ISH – 3:12 A.M.

Fluorescent lights. One working. Two flickering. One that has given up and just hums with resentment.

Behind the counter sits DOT, sixties, nametag reading DOT, reading a romance paperback with a shirtless centaur on the cover. She has the energy of a woman who has seen every possible thing walk into this gas station and charged all of them full price.

On the counter in front of her sits a single cup of instant ramen. Lid peeled back. Steaming.

Nobody bought it.

Nobody added water.

Dot turns a page.

The ramen bubbles.

Dot turns another page.

The ramen bubbles harder. A single sprinkle of pink glitter rises out of the broth like a tiny firework.

Dot lowers the book.

DOT
No.

The broth rises. It climbs up out of the cup in a wobbling column, noodles coiling, glitter swirling, and shapes itself, slowly, horribly, into a girl.

Auburn hair. Pastel pink sweater vest, slightly damp. A ribbon. A smile that is too wide and has been too wide for a very long time.

MADISON stands on the gas station counter, dripping broth, and spreads her arms.

MADISON
Bestie!

DOT
Get off my counter.

MADISON
Oh my god. Oh my god, I'm back. I'm literally back. I was soup. I was soup for like a year. Do you know what it's like being soup? It's so boring. Everybody just stirs you.

DOT
Off the counter.

Madison hops down. Her shoes squelch. She leans across the counter, eyes wide and glittering, and reaches into her damp vest.

She pulls out a cupcake.

It is perfect. Pink frosting. A little sugar heart on top. Somehow, impossibly, completely dry.

MADISON
You look like you've had such a long night. Have a little treat. On me. Then we can be besties forever and you can give me your car.

Dot looks at the cupcake.

Dot looks at Madison.

Dot picks up a can of cooking spray from the shelf behind her and sprays the cupcake directly in its face.

The cupcake makes a small, sad hiss.

DOT
I been working nights at this station for thirty-one years. I've had a man made of bees try to buy cigarettes. I've had a vampire ask for a receipt. I've had a guy who was mostly a door. You think I'm eating a cupcake that came out of a soup?

MADISON
It's a friendship cupcake.

DOT
It's a felony with frosting.

Madison's smile wobbles.

Somewhere outside, very faintly, a car alarm goes off for no reason.

MADISON
You don't want to be my friend?

DOT
Lady, I don't want to be anybody's friend. It's three in the morning. You owe me a dollar ninety-nine for the ramen.

MADISON
I was the ramen.

DOT
Then you owe me for the cup.

Madison stands in the middle of the gas station, dripping, holding a cupcake that has been sprayed with cooking oil, and for the first time in her life, nobody is in love with her.

She looks down at herself.

MADISON
Oh no.

CUT TO TITLE.

MADISON, NOMAD.

ACT ONE

INT. MORTALITY SERVICES – BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY – MORNING

The printer is screaming.

Not printing. Screaming. A long, high, continuous wail, like a kettle that has seen something it cannot unsee.

YUKI TANAKA stands in front of it holding a mug that says WORLD'S MOST TIRED.

YUKI
What.

PRINTER
ANOMALY.

YUKI
Be specific.

PRINTER
DECEASED STUDENT HAS RECONSTITUTED. REMOTE LOCATION. FORM INCOMPLETE.

Yuki puts down her mug.

YUKI
Which student.

The printer spits out a single sheet. Yuki picks it up.

A photograph. Auburn hair. A ribbon. A smile that is too wide.

MADISON. FRIENDSHIP FESTIVAL. CAUSE OF DEATH: DISSOLVED INTO RAMEN BROTH AND GLITTER.

STATUS: UPDATED.

Yuki stares at it.

YUKI
No.

GRIM REAPER (O.S.)
Yes.

The GRIM REAPER sits at her desk across the office, holding a cup of coffee and twelve clipboards. She does not look up.

GRIM REAPER
I felt it at three twelve. She's in a gas station in the desert. She's trying to recruit the night clerk.

YUKI
How's that going?

GRIM REAPER
The night clerk sprayed her cupcake with canola oil.

YUKI
I like the night clerk.

GRIM REAPER
Everyone likes the night clerk. Her name's Dot. She has been on our Do Not Collect list for thirty years because every time I go out there she makes me pay for my coffee.

Yuki looks at the photograph again.

YUKI
Why is this ours? Bring her in, put her back in a bowl. Done.

GRIM REAPER
Because she's not dead.

YUKI
She turned into soup.

GRIM REAPER
She turned into soup and then stopped being soup. That's not dead. That's a recipe. And she's not a student anymore. She was expelled posthumously. The Board voted.

YUKI
So she's not ours.

GRIM REAPER
She's not anyone's. That's the problem.

The Reaper finally looks up.

GRIM REAPER
An unaffiliated reconstituted friendship-cult leader with memory powers, wandering around the desert with no university, no faculty oversight, no paperwork. Do you know what that is?

YUKI
A Tuesday.

GRIM REAPER
A liability. Go get her.

YUKI
And do what?

The Reaper slides a form across the desk.

STUDENT RE-ENROLLMENT ASSESSMENT.

GRIM REAPER
Find out if she's still dangerous. If she is, bring her back for containment. If she isn't—

YUKI
If she isn't?

GRIM REAPER
Then find out what she is. Because she's never once in her life been anything else.

Yuki looks at the form.

Then at her mug.

WORLD'S MOST TIRED.

YUKI
I'm going to need a car.

KEVIN, a ghost in reading glasses, raises his hand from the filing cabinet.

KEVIN
Can I come?

YUKI
No.

KEVIN
I've never seen a desert.

YUKI
You've never seen anything, Kevin. You're dead.

KEVIN
That's why I want to see it.

Beat.

YUKI
Get in the car.

EXT. DESERT HIGHWAY – DAY

Heat shimmer. Cacti. A single vulture on a single post, looking unimpressed by everything.

Madison walks down the shoulder of the highway in her damp pink sweater vest, holding her thumb out.

A pickup truck approaches.

Madison spins around, beaming, and holds up a cupcake.

MADISON
Hi! Friendship cupcake for a ride?

The truck does not slow down.

It sprays her with gravel.

Madison spits out a pebble.

MADISON
Rude.

A second car. A minivan. A FAMILY inside: two parents, three kids, one dog.

Madison steps right into the road and holds up three cupcakes like a magician fanning cards.

MADISON
Besties! Besties for the road!

The minivan brakes.

The father rolls down the window.

FATHER
Are you okay? Do you need help?

Madison's eyes glitter. She leans in. Her voice drops, sweet, warm, like weaponized syrup.

MADISON
Oh, you're such a good person. You remember, don't you? Last summer? At the lake? When I pulled your son out of the water? Remember? You said you'd always owe me.

The father blinks.

Behind his eyes, something soft and pink turns over.

Then stops.

FATHER
No.

Madison blinks.

MADISON
What?

FATHER
No, I don't remember that. My son can't swim. We don't go to lakes. We're from Arizona.

The ten-year-old SON leans forward from the back seat.

SON
Dad, she's trying to do a memory thing.

MOTHER
Roll up the window.

FATHER
Sorry, ma'am. Good luck.

The window rolls up.

The minivan drives away.

The family dog watches Madison through the back window the entire time with an expression of deep, uncomplicated pity.

Madison stands in the middle of the desert highway holding three cupcakes.

She looks at her own hands.

MADISON
That should have worked.

She tries again on the vulture.

MADISON
You remember me, don't you? I'm the girl who brought you that whole dead rabbit—

The vulture turns its back on her.

MADISON
Oh, come on.

ACT TWO

INT. DOT'S GAS STATION – NIGHT

Madison has come back. There is nowhere else to go. The desert is very big and very empty and none of it wants to be her friend.

She sits on the floor by the ice machine, knees pulled up, eating one of her own cupcakes. It does nothing to her. It never has.

Dot is behind the counter, reading the centaur book.

DOT
You can't sleep there.

MADISON
I'm not sleeping. I'm processing.

DOT
Process somewhere else.

MADISON
There's nowhere else. I tried. I walked eleven miles. Nobody would give me a ride. A whole family said no. A vulture said no.

DOT
Vultures always say no. That's their whole personality.

Madison puts her chin on her knees.

MADISON
It doesn't work anymore. The thing. My thing. I could make anybody remember anything. I could make the Dean of Blackwood think I was the best student who ever lived. I made a whole campus love me. I made a sales lady give me a dress worth more than her car. And now I can't even get a minivan to stop.

Dot turns a page.

DOT
Maybe you used it up.

MADISON
You can't use up a power.

DOT
Honey, you can use up anything. I used up three husbands and a perfectly good Buick.

Madison almost laughs. Doesn't.

MADISON
Lux took the core out of me. The Ena thing. The cosmic thing. At the festival. She reached right in and took it. Maybe that was the strong part. Maybe without it I'm just—

She stops.

DOT
Just what?

MADISON
Just a girl with a tray of cupcakes.

The ice machine clunks. Somewhere outside, the car alarm goes off again for no reason, and then stops.

Dot puts down the book.

She looks at the girl on the floor by the ice machine. Damp. Glittery. Holding half a cupcake. Smiling the too-wide smile even now, even like this, because she has forgotten how to stop.

DOT
You hungry?

MADISON
I'm a reconstituted soup. I don't think I get hungry.

DOT
That's not what I asked.

Madison looks up.

Beat.

MADISON
Yeah.

Dot gets up. She goes to the hot dog roller, which has been rolling the same four hot dogs since approximately 1997. She takes one off, puts it in a bun, puts mustard on it with the precision of a surgeon, and walks around the counter.

She holds it out.

Madison stares at it.

MADISON
What do I owe you?

DOT
Nothing.

MADISON
Nothing?

DOT
It's a hot dog, not a mortgage.

Madison takes it. Slowly. As if it might explode.

She looks at it for a very long time.

MADISON
Nobody's ever given me anything for nothing.

DOT
Yeah, well. Nobody gave you anything at all, if you think about it. They just thought they owed you.

Madison's smile goes away.

Not slowly. All at once. Like a light going out. And underneath it, for the first time, there is just a face. A tired, damp, ordinary girl's face.

She takes a bite of the hot dog.

MADISON
This is terrible.

DOT
It's been on there since the Clinton administration.

MADISON
It's so terrible.

She keeps eating it.

“NOBODY OWES ME”

A low country twang from the gas station radio. Steel guitar. Madison, sitting on the floor by the ice machine, half a hot dog in one hand, sings quietly, not to Dot, not to anyone.

MADISON, SINGING
I baked my way through every town
With sugar on my hands
I made them think I pulled them out
Of rivers, fires, and sands

I made the Dean think I was good
I made the city kneel
And every single one of them
Owed me for something not real

Dot leans on the counter. Doesn't sing. Doesn't stop her.

MADISON
Now nobody owes me
Nobody at all
Nobody remembers
When I wasn't there to fall

Nobody owes me
And it's cold out on the road
But this terrible hot dog
Is the first thing I've been owed

That I didn't make up
That I didn't bake in
Somebody just handed it
To the girl I've really been

The steel guitar wavers. The car alarm outside goes off once, harmonizing, and then stops.

DOT
That was pretty good.

MADISON
Really?

DOT
The rhyme on "bake in" was garbage.

MADISON
I'm a nomad now. I don't have time for good rhymes.

ACT THREE

EXT. DESERT HIGHWAY – NEXT MORNING

A black sedan with a MORTALITY SERVICES parking permit in the windshield is pulled over on the shoulder. Steam pours from under the hood.

Yuki stands beside it, staring into the engine with the expression of a woman staring into the abyss.

Kevin floats over the hood, enchanted.

KEVIN
This is incredible. Look at all the sand. Look at the sky. Look at that bird.

YUKI
That bird is a vulture, Kevin. It's waiting for me to die.

KEVIN
That's so nice of it.

The vulture lands on the sedan's roof. Looks at Yuki. Looks at Kevin. Looks back at Yuki, as if to say: one of you is already done, so I'll just wait on the other one.

YUKI
Fuck off.

The vulture does not fuck off.

A distant sound: someone singing. Badly. Off-key. A song about a hot dog.

Yuki turns.

Down the highway, walking along the shoulder, comes Madison. She is wearing a gas station T-shirt that says NOWHERE, NEVADA-ISH: I GOT GAS over her damp sweater vest. She is carrying a plastic bag of convenience store snacks. She is not holding a single cupcake.

She sees the black sedan. She sees the parking permit. She sees Yuki.

She stops.

MADISON
Oh no.

YUKI
Hi.

MADISON
Oh no. Oh no, no, no. You're the girl from the library. From before. With the thesis. I gave you a cupcake and we went shopping.

YUKI
You made me think we'd been best friends for three years and I carried your bags through six stores.

MADISON
That's fair.

YUKI
My credit card still hasn't recovered.

MADISON
That's also fair.

They look at each other across the hot shimmering asphalt.

Kevin, floating above the car, waves.

KEVIN
Hi! I'm Kevin!

MADISON
Hi, Kevin.

KEVIN
I've never met a soup before!

MADISON
I'm not soup anymore.

KEVIN
I've never met a former soup before!

Yuki pulls a form out of her blazer. STUDENT RE-ENROLLMENT ASSESSMENT.

YUKI
Mortality Services sent me to find out if you're still dangerous.

Madison looks at the form.

Then she looks at the plastic bag of snacks in her hand.

Then she looks back at Yuki.

MADISON
I don't know.

YUKI
That's not on the form.

MADISON
Well, it should be.

Yuki considers this.

Then she takes a pen out of her pocket and writes, in small letters, at the bottom of the form: SUBJECT DOES NOT KNOW.

YUKI
Okay. Get in.

MADISON
Your car's broken.

YUKI
Then get in and be broken with me. It's a hundred and four degrees. I'm not doing this on the shoulder.

INT. MORTALITY SERVICES SEDAN – CONTINUOUS

Madison sits in the passenger seat. Yuki sits behind the wheel. Kevin floats in the back, pressed against the window, staring at a cactus with naked wonder.

The air conditioning does not work.

For a long time, nobody says anything.

YUKI
Your powers stopped working.

MADISON
How did you know?

YUKI
Because if they worked, I'd already be remembering how you saved my life at a car accident and offering you my kidney.

MADISON
That's actually really specific.

YUKI
I've been thinking about it.

Madison digs in the plastic bag. Pulls out a packet of cheap powdered donuts. Opens it. Holds it out.

Yuki looks at it.

YUKI
Is that going to make me love you?

MADISON
No. It's just donuts. Dot gave me five dollars and said to buy something that wasn't pink.

Yuki takes a donut. Eats it. Powdered sugar gets all over her Mortality Services blazer.

YUKI
These are terrible.

MADISON
I know. Everything out here is terrible. It's kind of great.

Kevin leans forward between the seats.

KEVIN
Can I have one?

YUKI
You're a ghost.

KEVIN
I can hold it.

Madison hands him a donut. He holds it with great reverence, like a sacred relic.

KEVIN
Thank you.

MADISON
You're welcome.

Beat.

MADISON
That's the first time anybody's said thank you to me without it being the cupcake.

Yuki looks at her.

YUKI
How many times did you do it? The memory thing. Total.

Madison thinks.

MADISON
Four thousand? Five? I stopped counting after the festival. It was the whole campus by then. It was the spores. It was everybody.

YUKI
And none of it was real.

MADISON
None of it was real.

YUKI
Did any of it make you happy?

Madison opens her mouth to say yes.

She stops.

She looks out the window at the desert. The empty, enormous, terrible, honest desert, where nothing pretends to be anything else.

MADISON
No. It made me feel full. Like eating a whole cake. And then an hour later you're starving again, and you're also kind of sick.

YUKI
That's what being lied to feels like from the other side too.

MADISON
Yeah. I'm getting that.

ACT FOUR

EXT. DESERT DINER – SUNSET

A tow truck has towed the sedan to the only other building for forty miles: a diner called THE LAST STOP, which is also, according to its sign, a motel, a post office, a laundromat, and a "notary (crow)."

A CROW sits on the sign holding a rubber stamp.

INT. THE LAST STOP DINER – CONTINUOUS

Booths. Vinyl. A jukebox that only plays songs about trucks. A waitress named BIRDIE, twenties, tattoos up both arms, chewing gum with the jaw strength of someone who has chewed through worse.

Madison, Yuki, and Kevin sit in a booth. The Reaper's re-enrollment form lies on the table between them, half filled out.

Birdie comes over.

BIRDIE
What do you want?

MADISON
Oh, hi! Do you want a cupcake?

Yuki kicks her under the table.

MADISON
Sorry. Reflex. I'll have the pancakes.

BIRDIE
We're out of pancakes.

MADISON
Waffles?

BIRDIE
Out of waffles.

MADISON
What do you have?

BIRDIE
Pie. And a guy in the back booth who's been trying to get my number for three hours.

She jerks her thumb.

In the back booth sits a MAN in blindingly bright spandex, staring at his gold-plated phone, posing for a selfie with a slice of cherry pie.

It is CAPTAIN HERO.

Madison freezes.

So does Captain Hero, when he looks up and sees her.

CAPTAIN HERO
YOU.

MADISON
Oh, fuck.

Captain Hero stands up so fast his pie hits the ceiling.

CAPTAIN HERO
YOU'RE ALIVE. YOU'RE ALIVE AND YOU'RE IN A DINER. DO YOU KNOW WHAT YOU DID TO MY ENGAGEMENT? THE MINI-MADISONS BIT MY PECS ON LIVESTREAM. I LOST TWO MILLION FOLLOWERS. TWO. MILLION.

MADISON
I'm sorry—

CAPTAIN HERO
"I'M SORRY"? THAT'S IT? THE BITE MARKS ARE STILL THERE. I HAVE TO WEAR A CAPE OVER MY CHEST ON THUMBNAILS.

He flexes. Somewhere, a light bulb pops.

CAPTAIN HERO
I'VE BEEN WAITING FOR THIS. I'VE BEEN WAITING A YEAR. "HERO DEFEATS RAMEN VILLAIN: THE RETURN." THIS IS GOING TO BE MY BIGGEST VIDEO EVER.

He holds up his phone. Hits record.

Madison stands up slowly from the booth.

Yuki puts down her fork.

YUKI
Sit down, Madison.

MADISON
He's going to fight me.

YUKI
He's going to fight you for a video. He doesn't care about you. He cares about the thumbnail.

CAPTAIN HERO
I CARE ABOUT JUSTICE.

YUKI
You care about engagement.

CAPTAIN HERO
JUSTICE IS ENGAGEMENT.

Captain Hero lunges across the diner.

Madison, on instinct, reaches into her sweater vest.

She pulls out a cupcake.

Everyone stops.

The cupcake glows. Faintly. Pink. Just at the edges.

Madison stares at it. Her hand is shaking.

MADISON
It's back.

YUKI
Madison.

MADISON
I can feel it. It's back. A little. If he eats it—

She looks at Captain Hero, frozen mid-lunge, phone held high.

MADISON
—if he eats this, he'll remember that I saved his career. He'll remember I made him famous. He'll love me. He'll leave. He'll never post the video. And I'll be safe.

Kevin floats up beside her.

KEVIN
Will you?

Madison looks at the ghost.

KEVIN
Be safe. If he loves you because of the cupcake. Will you be safe?

Madison looks at the glowing pink cupcake in her hand.

She looks at Yuki, across the booth, with powdered sugar still on her blazer from a donut that was just a donut.

She looks at Birdie the waitress, chewing her gum, watching all of this with total indifference.

She thinks about Dot, and the terrible hot dog.

MADISON
No. I won't.

She puts the cupcake down on the table.

Then she picks up a fork, and squashes it.

The pink glow goes out with a tiny sad fizz.

Captain Hero blinks.

CAPTAIN HERO
What are you doing?

MADISON
I'm not doing the thing.

CAPTAIN HERO
Why not?

MADISON
Because I'd rather you hated me for real than loved me for fake.

Silence in the diner.

Even the jukebox stops.

Captain Hero lowers his phone.

CAPTAIN HERO
That's not how villains talk.

MADISON
I'm not sure I'm a villain anymore. I'm not sure I'm anything. I'm a nomad.

She sits back down in the booth.

She picks up the menu.

MADISON
Birdie? Can I get the pie?

BIRDIE
Which kind?

MADISON
I don't know. I've never picked before. Somebody always gave me what they thought I wanted.

Birdie looks at her for a second. Chews.

BIRDIE
Get the peach. It's the only one that's good.

MADISON
Okay. The peach.

Captain Hero stands in the middle of the diner, holding his phone, with nobody to fight.

CAPTAIN HERO
This is going to get terrible engagement.

YUKI
Sit down and eat your pie, Hero.

He sits down.

He eats his pie.

He does not post the video.

ACT FIVE

EXT. THE LAST STOP – NIGHT

The desert sky is enormous. A billion stars. No pink spores. No friendship festival. Just sky.

Madison sits on the hood of the repaired Mortality Services sedan, eating the last of the peach pie out of a takeout box with a plastic fork.

Yuki sits beside her with the re-enrollment form on her knee.

Kevin lies on his back on the roof of the car, staring up at the stars, holding his donut.

KEVIN
I didn't know there were this many.

YUKI
There are always this many. You just can't see them from campus.

KEVIN
Why not?

YUKI
Light pollution. And the east stairwell eats them.

KEVIN
That's so sad.

Yuki clicks her pen.

YUKI
So. Last question on the form. Is the subject a danger to herself or others?

Madison scrapes the last of the pie off the box.

MADISON
What do you think?

YUKI
I'm not supposed to think. I'm supposed to check a box.

MADISON
Then check whatever. I don't care. I'm not going back to Blackwood anyway.

YUKI
You're not?

MADISON
No. I don't know where I'm going. I've never not known before. I always had a plan. A tray. A target. A bestie.

She looks out at the dark desert.

MADISON
I think I want to find out what I'm like when nobody owes me anything.

Yuki looks at her for a long time.

Then she looks down at the form.

She does not check either box.

Instead, at the bottom, under SUBJECT DOES NOT KNOW, she writes in small letters:

SUBJECT IS TRYING.

She folds the form and puts it in her blazer.

YUKI
That's good enough for me.

“LAST STOP”

The jukebox inside the diner, which only plays songs about trucks, plays a song about trucks. Madison hums along. Then sings, very quietly, with the stars overhead.

MADISON, SINGING
I was the sugar in the batter
I was the frosting on the lie
I was everybody's best friend
And I never knew why

I was soup for a year
In a cup on a shelf
And the only thing I learned there
Was I didn't know myself

Yuki doesn't sing. But she taps her pen on her knee. In time.

MADISON
So here's to the last stop
Here's to the end of the line
Here's to a peach pie I picked
And a hot dog that was fine

Here's to the girl from the library
Who should've left me in the sand
Here's to nobody loving me
And still holding out a hand

KEVIN, FROM THE ROOF
That rhyme was better!

MADISON
Thank you, Kevin!

The song ends. The jukebox switches to another song about trucks.

Madison hops off the hood of the car.

YUKI
Where are you going?

MADISON
North, I think. Or west. Birdie says there's a town four hundred miles west where everybody's a cryptid and nobody remembers anybody's name. Seems like a good place to start over.

YUKI
How are you getting there?

Madison holds out her thumb.

MADISON
Like a normal person. I'll wait. Somebody will stop. Or they won't. Either way it'll be real.

Yuki looks at her.

Then she reaches into her blazer and pulls out a business card. MORTALITY SERVICES. YUKI TANAKA. A phone number.

She holds it out.

YUKI
If you ever need anything. Paperwork. A ride. Proof you existed. Call.

Madison takes the card. Looks at it. Her smile comes back, a little. Not the too-wide one. A smaller one. Crooked. Real.

MADISON
Are we friends?

YUKI
No.

MADISON
Okay.

YUKI
Not yet.

MADISON
Okay.

YUKI
Ask me again in a year. Without a cupcake.

Madison puts the card in her pocket.

She walks to the edge of the highway. She holds out her thumb. The desert stretches away forever under the stars.

A car approaches. Headlights.

It slows down.

It's Dot. In a Buick. The perfectly good Buick, apparently not quite used up after all.

Dot rolls down the window.

DOT
You need a ride or what?

MADISON
I thought you didn't want to be anybody's friend.

DOT
I don't. I'm going west to see my sister. Get in or don't. I'm not asking twice.

Madison looks back at Yuki. At Kevin, on the roof. At the diner, and the crow on the sign.

Then she gets in.

The Buick pulls away into the dark, west, toward a town full of cryptids where nobody remembers anybody's name.
