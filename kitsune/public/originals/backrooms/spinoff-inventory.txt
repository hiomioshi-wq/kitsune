The Backrooms: Inventory
A spinoff of No Wind Resistance. The ledger and confessions of Duke, B.N.T.G. trader, Level 1.

Trigger warning: This story contains human trafficking, the sale of body parts, drug dealing, cults, betrayal, adult language, and a narrator who has a price for everything and is proud of it.
Read at your own risk.



Opening Stock

Welcome to my stall, friend. Pull up a crate. Mind the leak, the ceiling on Level 1 drips whenever the lights cycle, nobody knows why, nobody's ever found where it comes from. Habitable Zone, they call this level. Habitable. Ha. Concrete and fluorescent lights and puddles forever. But it's got walls that stay put and doors that mostly stay doors, and that makes it the best place in the Backrooms to do business.

Name's Duke. Backrooms Non-Aligned Trade Group, Level 1 Market, Stall 44. Hawaiian shirt, gold tooth, you can't miss me. I've been here eleven years. I came in through a Kroger in Tulsa. Leaned on a freezer door. Fell through the back of it. Landed face-down in the Lobby with a frozen pizza in my hands.

I traded that pizza for my first flask of Almond Water. Best deal I ever made. Been trading ever since.

You want to know what the B.N.T.G. is? We're the only people in the Backrooms who don't want anything from you except a fair price. M.E.G. wants your loyalty. ARGOS wants your data. The Followers of Jerry want your face. Us, we just want what's in your pockets. That's honest. That's the most honest thing left down here.

Here. I'll show you my book. I keep a ledger. Every trade. Eleven years of it. Some of the pages are stuck together with frosting, that's from the Fun deals, don't touch those.



Page 1: Staples

ALMOND WATER, standard flask. Price: 2 batteries or 1 Royal Ration.
Note: Keeps you sane. Keeps you from going Wretch. Tastes like vanilla and wet dog. Everybody needs it. Never run out of this. Never.

CASHEW WATER. Price: 1 battery.
Note: Like Almond Water's dumber cousin. Gets rid of headaches. Some people swear it makes them feel calmer. Some people swear a lot of things.

ROYAL RATIONS, canned. Price: 1 flask Almond Water each.
Note: Tastes like whatever you want it to taste like. That's the trick of it. I eat mine with my fingers so I can pretend it's my mother's pot roast.

BATTERIES, AA. Price: varies.
Note: You can't make them down here. Everything that runs on them came from the Frontrooms in somebody's pocket. When they run out, they run out forever. This is the real currency. Almond Water keeps you alive. Batteries keep you seeing.

LEVEL KEYS. Price: everything.
Note: A key that opens a door to a specific level. No noclipping, no wandering, no guessing. You put it in a door and you go where you want. Rarest thing in the Backrooms. Most people never even see one. Partygoers have dozens. Nobody knows where they get them. I've stopped asking.



Page 9: Specialty Items

FIRESALT, Level 2 pipes. Price: 4 flasks per pinch.
Note: Explodes when it touches air too long. M.E.G. runs generators on it. Morons try to cook with it. I sold a pinch to a guy who wanted to "light a campfire" in Level 4 and they're still finding pieces of the office cubicles.

DEATHMOTH SILK, Level 8. Price: 3 batteries per spool.
Note: Strongest thread in the Backrooms. You harvest it from the nests in the Cave System, real careful, while the moths are sleeping. Mae brings me good silk. Mae brings me good everything.

HOUND HIDE, tanned. Price: 6 flasks.
Note: Waterproof. Bites don't go through it. Makes a damn good jacket. Mae wears one. Looks like hell. Works great.

SMILER TEETH. Price: 2 flasks per tooth.
Note: Glow in the dark. Forever. People use them as nightlights in Level 6, where the lights don't come on. Don't ask how they're harvested. The answer is: from the dark, very quickly, and not everybody comes back.

LIQUID PAIN. Price: not listed.
Note: I don't sell it. I want that on record. Black stuff, comes in little vials, turns your veins dark when you inject it and makes you feel every bad thing you ever felt all at once. Nobody takes it on purpose. Somebody slips it in your Almond Water, you scream for six hours. Followers of Jerry use it for "conversions." I don't sell it.

(I know a guy who sells it. I take ten percent for the introduction. That's different.)



Page 33: Faces

SKIN-STEALER CONTRACTS. Price: negotiable.
Note: Here's a thing nobody tells you about Skin-Stealers. They're not just monsters. They're customers.

A Skin-Stealer wears a face. That's its whole thing. It peels you off and climbs into you and walks around being you, and it's real good at it for about a week, and then the face starts to go. Sag. Slip. Gray at the edges. And it needs a new one.

So it can hunt, sure. Risky. M.E.G. shoots them on sight. Or it can come to Stall 44 and buy one.

I don't sell faces. I sell introductions. A lonely wanderer, three days off the Lobby, nobody's registered him, nobody's looking. I tell him there's a guy in Level 9 who'll trade him a Level Key for his shoes. Level 9's the Suburbs. Houses that look just like home. He goes. The guy in Level 9 is wearing somebody's neighbor's face. The wanderer doesn't come back. The Skin-Stealer comes back next week wearing the wanderer, a little taller now, a little younger, and pays me in batteries.

I keep a separate ledger for those. Red pen.



Page 41: Fun

PARTYGOER EXCHANGE. Level Fun =).
Price: 1 Level Key per guest. 5 if young.

Now. This one, I need you to understand.

The Partygoers want guests. That's all they want. They want people at their party. You go to Level Fun, there's balloons and cake and streamers forever, and the Partygoers are yellow and smooth and smiling, and they're so happy to see you. They hug you. And then you're one of them. Smiling. Yellow. Throwing the party.

It's not death. That's what I tell myself, and it's what I tell my suppliers. It's not death. It's just a party that never ends. Maybe that's better than the Lobby. Maybe that's better than walking through yellow hallways forever drinking vanilla dog water until your brain goes soft and you end up a Wretch gnawing on chairs in the dark.

At least at the party, you're happy.

They pay in Level Keys. That's how I have so many. That's how I've got more Level Keys than anybody in the Backrooms outside of the Partygoers themselves. Every key in my strongbox is a person.

I don't think about that. I keep it in the frosting pages.

Recent entries, Fun:

Girl from Level 10 wheat fields, harvest smock. Supplied by unnamed. 1 key.
Twins, 16, came through a Chuck E. Cheese in Akron. Supplied by Mae. 6 flasks paid out. Sold for 10 keys. Best trade this year.



Page 58: Jerry

FOLLOWERS OF JERRY. Price: depends what they want.

They're the worst customers I have, and I have Skin-Stealers.

Blue robes. White porcelain masks. They worship Jerry, a little blue bird, a parakeet, I think, who apparently lives in the Frontrooms in some lady's apartment and doesn't know any of this is happening. Down here, the Jerry they pray to is something else. An entity. Something that took the shape of a bird and the name of a bird and the love of a bird, and turned it into a hook.

They want recruits. Young ones best. They put a mask on you, they give you Liquid Pain until you'll do anything for it to stop, and then they give you the Bird, and the Bird stops it. And after that you'll do anything for the Bird. You forget your name. You forget your family. You forget everything except Jerry loves you.

Mae's brother is with them. Leo. I knew it before she did. I saw him at a Level 11 trade six months before she found him, in his mask with his skateboard on a rope, and I didn't tell her.

Why didn't I tell her?

Because Mae with a reason to stop looking is Mae who stops bringing me fresh stock. A woman searching for her brother goes to every corner of every level. She finds things. Noclippers. Doors. Silk. Hides. She was the best supplier I ever had because she couldn't stop moving.

So I didn't tell her. I let her keep searching. That's just good business.



Page 59: Mae

I'm going to write this one down careful, because it's the one that's going to get me killed, and I want it in the book.

Mae stopped bringing me people.

Six months ago. After she found Leo in the city. Something in her broke, or maybe something in her fixed, I couldn't tell. She still brought hides. She still brought silk. But no more fresh ones. She'd walk past the new noclippers in the Lobby now. Point them toward M.E.G. Even walked one all the way to Base Beta once. Hays told me. Hays doesn't like me. He likes Mae less, now, since his little vault business. He thinks she judges him. She does.

Without Mae's supply, my Fun page went dry. No guests. No keys. The Partygoers started getting impatient. They sent a balloon to my stall one night. Yellow. Tied to my strongbox. =) in Sharpie.

That's a warning. The Partygoers don't stay unhappy for long. If they're unhappy, they come over, and they make you happy.

So I radioed her. Told her I had a buyer. Five keys for a teenager. Five. That's a door home. That's the Frontrooms. I told her so.

She didn't answer.

So I did the other thing.

I went to the Followers of Jerry.

FOLLOWERS OF JERRY. Price paid: 3 Level Keys.
Item sold: Mae's route. Level 0 to Level 11 Hub, current week. Last known heading: Endless City metro. Traveling with a fresh noclipper, 19, graphic tee, looks just like Leo.

They paid me in keys. Where did they get keys? Same place everybody gets keys, I figure. Somebody's party.

And they went to the metro station in the Endless City, and they waited, with Leo in front, in his mask, with his skateboard.



Page 60: Closing Out

You know what happened at the metro. Everybody does. It's a story now. The Backrooms loves a story. Some kid with a datapad blasted a Sadness Bomb so loud it broke the Bird's hold on half the Followers in the station. Leo took his mask off. Mae grabbed him. They jumped on a train that went somewhere nobody's ever gone.

Out.

They got out. The kid, Mae, Leo. All three. The first people anybody ever heard of to just... get out. I hear they're in a Denny's somewhere. I hear Mae says she hates it.

Good for her. Honestly. I mean that.

The Followers didn't get their recruit. They want their keys back. They've stopped by twice now in their robes, real polite. "Jerry loves you, Duke. Jerry would love the keys back, Duke."

The Partygoers didn't get their guest. They've sent three more balloons. One of them was tied to my wrist while I slept.

And Mae's gone. The best supplier I ever had.

I've got two choices. I can run. Noclip somewhere. Go to the Snackrooms, Level 6.1, lay low among the vending machines and the neon, maybe sell stale chips to tourists. Or I can pay my debts.

I've got the keys, see. Three from the Jerry freaks. And the twenty-seven in my strongbox. Thirty keys. A key opens a door to anywhere. One of them opens a door to the Frontrooms, I'm pretty sure. A Kroger in Tulsa, maybe. A frozen pizza aisle. Home.

I could go home.

I'm not going to.

Because here's what I worked out, sitting at Stall 44 with a balloon on my wrist, eating Royal Rations that taste like my mother's pot roast. Eleven years. Eleven years of every person who came through my stall, every key in my strongbox, every red-pen entry, every frosting page. If I go home, I'm just a guy who used to sell people. A guy in a Tulsa apartment with the TV on, who can't sleep.

Down here, I'm the Duke. I'm the best trader on Level 1. I'm somebody.

So I'm paying my debts. Tomorrow, a new batch comes through the Lobby. They always do. Hays has his orientation class. Nine fresh ones. One of them keeps a log, I hear. I've got a guy who can get into Base Beta's registry. I've got a balloon to tie to a door.

Business is business.

Here. Before you go. Let me write you in.

NEW STOCK. Price: TBD.
Note: Pulled up a crate at Stall 44. Read the whole book. Didn't run. Fresh. All teeth.

Don't look at me like that, friend. You leaned on a wall to get here, same as everybody.
