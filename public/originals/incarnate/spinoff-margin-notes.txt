Incarnate: Margin Notes
A spinoff of the Corruption Saga, told by Himo. The old highway, during Prophecy.

Trigger warning: This story contains grief, the death of a loved one, a mother who never turns around, murder remembered without regret, cruelty written down as data, the end of everything, and a narrator who has the power to stop it and chooses not to.
Read at your own risk.



Entry One

Observation, Gray Abyss of Shattered Ruins, the old highway, mile marker none.

Subject Chimera is dying.

Cause: the song. Corruption-022's broadcast, You want to end, delivered through the throat apparatus at full volume to every god in every told world. Chimera is a god. Chimera is old. Chimera has wanted to stop for a long time. She has been sitting on this guardrail for three hours, going soft at the edges.

She has asked me to hold her hand.

I have done so.

Note: this was not part of any plan.



Entry Two

I should explain the notebook.

I have kept one since before the first war. Not this notebook. This is the four hundred and twelfth. The paper is made from the bark of a tree that grew in a world I ended, and the ink is made from the tree's sap, and that is not a metaphor, that is just where I got the paper.

I write down everything. That is how you make things. You observe, and you record, and you adjust.

Experiment Nine: the mind. Too attached. Discarded at the edge of a pit. Note: do not get attached.

Experiment Twenty-two: the hunger. Promising. Put in a jar. Note: do not get attached.

Subject Ena, age seven: shining blonde hair, beloved sister, a family that eats pancakes on Sundays. Selected for maximum grief yield. Note: do not get attached.

I have written that sentence, by my count, eleven thousand times.

I have never once had any trouble following it.

Until a few hours ago, when an old woman made of scars sat down on a guardrail next to me and said, "Well, you fucking did it, didn't you," and laid her head on my shoulder, and I found that I could not stand up.



Entry Three

Chimera and I met before the first war.

She was already ugly. She was always ugly. She was the god of things put together that should not go together, and she wore a body made of every scar she had ever earned, and she said every word she could think of that would make an angel cover its ears.

I was making something then. I do not remember what. I was making something in a quiet place and she walked in without knocking and looked at my work and said, "That's a fucking mess, Himo," and then she sat down and helped me.

She did that for a hundred thousand years.

She made me a bird once. Out of a fish and a moth and a scrap of a dead star. It sang off-key. It was the worst thing I have ever seen. I told her so. I did not throw it away.

I have it here. In my coat pocket. It does not sing anymore. The song got it too.

Note: I should have thrown it away.

Note: I am glad I did not.

Note: these two notes are in contradiction. Investigate later.



Entry Four

I turned around.

I need to record that precisely. It is significant. It has not happened before in the entire history of my existence, and I have been existing for a long time.

When Chimera put her head on my shoulder, I turned. All the way. I looked at her. Her stitches. Her bad teeth. Her stupid ugly face. I looked at her for three hours and I did not look away once and I did not write anything down.

She noticed.

"Took you long enough," she said.

"I was busy."

"You were always busy, you stuck-up bitch." She closed her eyes. "Still. Nice view."

I have no data for what happened in my chest after she said that. I have some theories. None of them are good.



Entry Five

The girl arrived at approximately hour three.

Ena. Subject Ena. Shining blonde hair. She was standing in the road thirty feet away, glowing red, with gold and blue coming out of her mouth when she breathed.

Twenty-two's gold. Twenty-two's blue.

So she broke the tank. And she took it. All of it. The song and the corruption in one vessel.

I did not plan for this. I want that recorded too. I plan for most things. I planned for her parents. I planned for her brother. I planned for the knife and the grave on the hill. I did not plan for her to walk into my Nursery and drink my best work like water from a hose.

And then she started talking about pancakes.

I knew where it was going. I am not stupid. I wrote the sentence she was quoting. Grief is the most powerful fuel in any universe. A grieving mind will burn down every star to get back one face.

I wrote that about her. Not about me.

I said, "Ena. Don't."

She did it anyway.



Entry Six

I am going to try to record what happened next accurately, because accuracy is the only thing I have ever been good at.

The girl sang to Chimera. Not to me. To her. You want to end. Now. Right now. You don't have to wait.

And Chimera, who had been holding on, I realize now, who had been holding on for three hours on purpose, out of spite or stubbornness or something worse, just so I could keep looking at her, let go.

She said, "Worth it."

And then there was dust in my hand.

I screamed. I am told. I have no record of it. The notebook was on the road. My hand was busy.

The dust kept running through my fingers. I kept trying to hold it. I am a scientist. I know that dust cannot be held. I kept trying.

There is a theory I have been developing for a hundred thousand years about grief, and its yield, and how much a mind will burn to get back one face.

My data was correct.

Entry Seven

She asked me to say I was sorry.

For Eri. For Nine. The girl was standing over me, full of the end of the world, and she said, "Say it. She's on the floor of the Nursery. You broke her legs. Say you're sorry."

I want to record that I heard her.

I want to record that I thought about Nine. At the edge of the pit. A small thing with too many feelings. I remember my hand on her back. I remember that she reached for me as she fell. I remember writing the note about it afterward with a perfectly steady pen.

And I want to record, accurately, that I said, "I'm sorry," and that it was not to her.

It was to the dust.

I kept saying it to the dust.

I find that I do not have it in me to be sorry for Nine. I have looked. The girl was right to look in my face the way she did. There is nothing there for my daughters. There is one shape, and it is on the guardrail, and it is gone.

I made eleven thousand things and I was only ever sorry about the one I did not make.

Note: that is the most accurate thing I have ever written.



Entry Eight

I have the page.

That is what the girl does not know. She thinks a prophecy is something that was written once and is done.

I am the one who wrote it. I corrupted a prophet for one second, years ago, a frightened little seer in a stone room, and I put my words in her mouth, and she said them, and then I wrote them down myself, on paper from my own notebook, in my own ink. The original. The copy that all the other copies came from. The one they whispered in grocery stores.

It is folded in the back of this notebook. It has been there the whole time.

When the end begins, corruption will fall, and there shall be one with shining blonde hair, the beloved sister, fueled by grief to harbor this corruption and end everything in a wasted attempt at redemption.

I can edit it.

That is how prophecy works, when you are the one who wrote it. It is not weather. It is a document. And a document is only true until somebody with the pen decides otherwise.

One word. That is all it would take. I could cross out everything and write something smaller. A city. A girl. Me. I could end her prophecy here, on the road, and every world in every story would go on wanting things, and Noor would keep making tea in Pittsburgh, and the girl's cat would keep sitting on her mattress waiting for her to come home.

I have the pen in my hand right now.

I am writing these notes with it.



Entry Nine

Arguments for editing the page:

1. The preservation of every told world.

2. The plan was to end the gods, not everything. Ending everything exceeds the scope of the experiment. It is, technically, a mistake.

3. Nine is on the floor of the Nursery and will also end. So will Twenty-two. So will the ushers and the babies and the tanks, which represent forty thousand years of work.

4. Chimera would have told me to. She would have said, "Fix your fucking mess, Himo."

Arguments against:

1. She is dust.



Entry Ten

The girl is standing on the guardrail.

She is about to say it. I can see it gathering in her. Every world has gone quiet. I have never heard it this quiet. Not even before the first war. Not even in the quiet place where I was making something when an ugly god walked in without knocking.

Something else has come out of the ruins and is walking down the highway toward us. Small. Wet. Crooked crown. It is smiling. It sits down on the guardrail beside the girl and says something about leftovers.

Twenty-two.

I look at her. I turn my head and I look at her. It occurs to me that this is the first time.

She sees me do it. She has the strangest expression.

Note: interesting.

Note: too late.



Entry Eleven

I said, "Ena. Please."

The girl thinks it was a plea. That I was begging. I could see it in her face. First time anybody's ever said please to me.

It was not that.

I want to record, accurately, for the four hundred and twelfth notebook, for whoever finds it, though no one will, that I had the pen and the page, and I unfolded the page, and I held the pen over the word everything, and I thought about crossing it out.

And then I thought about a world where the wind blows and the tea gets made and the cat waits on the mattress and there is no ugly old woman anywhere in it saying "That's a fucking mess, Himo."

And I folded the page back up.

Please did not mean stop.

Please meant hurry.



Entry Twelve

She is opening her mouth.

Twenty-two is opening hers.

I have closed the notebook. I am writing this last entry on the inside of the cover, very small, with the ink running out.

Observations. Subject Ena: fueled by grief. Subject Himo: also. Yield: everything. Result: as predicted. As written. As I wrote it, in my own hand, years ago, without once thinking it would be about me.

Final note.

I got attached.
