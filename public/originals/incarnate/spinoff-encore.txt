Incarnate: Main Character, Encore
A spinoff of the Corruption Saga. The sequel to Main Character.

Trigger warning: This story contains mind control, the eating of minds, a mass hollowing at a school dance, betrayal, body horror, and a narrator who feels absolutely nothing about any of it. Still don't take notes from her.
Read at your own risk.



Chapter One: Sorry, Who Invited You?

Okay so you remember how I said there's always a girl with chalk?

Plot twist. Sometimes there's a boy with my song.

I'm on the front steps of Westfield High in a homecoming queen sash (first week, babe, no big deal, I'm just like that), and I'm doing my little laugh with my little crowd, and I feel it. Across the street. A hum. A blue hum. In my key.

Somebody's covering my song. Again.

I turn my head and there he is, in his dad's beat-up Corolla, gripping the wheel like it owes him money. Mark. Pudding Mark. Little glowing Mark. His eyes are blue all the way across, no white left, and the air around his car is doing the heat-shimmer thing, and the grass on the median is leaning toward him like he's the sun.

He came all the way here. Three states. For me.

Aw.

And also: ew? Because I literally left. That's what leaving means. You don't get to follow a girl across the country just because she accidentally turned you into a cosmic hunger. Boundaries, Mark.

But I'm not dumb. I can feel how much of the song he's got in him now. It's grown. It's like when you give someone a little cutting from your plant and three months later they show up with a whole tree. He's been eating. I can taste it on the wind. A dad. A bully. A best friend. Mm. Brad. Kind of a lot of Brad, actually.

So I smile. All my teeth. And I wave.

"Hi Mark!" I mouth.

And he gets out of the car like I pulled him on a string.



Chapter Two: Lunch Date

We sit in the Westfield cafeteria at a table that was full until I looked at it.

He's so much more interesting now. Before, he was a dessert. Now he's like a dessert that can talk back. He's got bags under his eyes. He hasn't showered. He keeps rubbing his hands like they're cold, which they would be, because the song runs cold once you've used it enough. I could tell him that. I don't.

"How do you make it stop?" he says.

"Make what stop, bestie?"

"Being hungry."

I laugh. Not the cute laugh. The drain one. A girl at the next table drops her fork and doesn't pick it up.

"Babe," I say. "That's like asking how you make water stop being wet."

"There has to be a way."

"There is. You die." I eat one of his fries. "But like. Have you tried not wanting it to stop? Because that's way easier. That's the actual glow-up."

He stares at me. I can see him thinking about Brad. I can actually see it; it comes off him in a little gray cloud, a memory of a boy on a bed saying the same sentence. He wants to be sad about it. He's trying so hard to be sad about it. But the song in him is already eating the sadness, because sadness is wanting too, and the song doesn't care what flavor wanting is. It's all food.

"See?" I say, softly. "It's already better. Isn't it?"

He doesn't answer. But his shoulders come down an inch.

That's my boy.



Chapter Three: Rules For Two

So I decide to keep him. Temporarily. Like a rental.

Here's my logic. A girl with chalk is coming. She always is. And this time I can feel her from three states away, which is new, which is rude, because usually they're just girls. This one feels like a girl with a whole barn behind her. Like something huge and old is walking under the highway toward me, pushing the dirt along like a wave.

I don't love that.

Mark, though, he's a decoy. He smells like me. He sings like me. If something big and hungry comes looking for the song, it's going to find two of us, and it's going to have to pick.

So I teach him things. I'm a great teacher. I've done this for forty high schools.

Rule one: Never eat anyone all the way at school.

"You did that to Brad," I tell him. "Messy. Very freshman of you."

"I didn't mean to."

"Nobody means to the first time. That's literally the only time it counts as an accident."

Rule two: Always leave them happy.

"Your dad's a great example," I say. "Pancakes, aprons, love you, proud of you. That's a happy shell. That's chef's kiss. Devon, less so. Devon cries. Crying is noise."

Rule three: Never let anyone hear the whole song unless you're done with the whole town.

"And are we done with this town?" Mark asks.

I look out the cafeteria window. Across the parking lot. Past the football field. To the horizon, where the dirt looks a little darker than it should, a little wetter, a little closer than it was this morning.

"Homecoming dance is Saturday," I say. "Wear something cute."



Chapter Four: The Dance

Okay so I've done a lot of school dances. I've done sock hops. I've done cotillions. I did a Victorian ball in 1891 where everyone's corset strings came undone at once and I ate the orchestra.

This was the best one.

The Westfield gym, strung with gold streamers and those paper stars that are always a little crooked. A DJ named DJ Kyle who was actually a junior named Kyle. Four hundred and forty kids. Teachers. Parent chaperones. A photo booth with feather boas.

And me and Mark, on the little stage, in the crowns.

I'm queen, obviously. I made Mark king. He didn't even go here. Nobody noticed. I told the vice principal Mark had always gone here, and she agreed so hard she cried a little.

Mark was nervous. You could tell. He kept looking at the doors.

"She's coming," he said. "Raven. I can feel it."

"Who's Raven?" I said, sweetly, because it's fun to watch him remember that he made her forget him. "Oh, wait. That's the girl you wiped, right? That's so funny. She's coming all this way to fight a boy she doesn't even remember."

"Calypso."

"Mark." I took his hand. Cold. Like mine. "Look at them."

He looked. Four hundred and forty kids, under the crooked stars, dancing badly to DJ Kyle, all of them wanting something. A slow dance with somebody specific. A good photo. To get into a college. For their parents to stop fighting. For this night to never end. The whole gym was humming with it like a power line.

"We're going to need our strength," I said. "Right? For when she gets here?"

His eyes went brighter.

"Right," he said.

And I counted us in. One, two, three.

And we sang.



Chapter Five: The Whole Song

Two voices. Did you know it was possible? I didn't. Forty thousand years and I'd never once had backup.

His part came in under mine like a bass line. Rougher. Younger. A little off-key. It made mine sound so much better. The song came off the stage and rolled through the gym like a tide, and the crooked paper stars spun on their strings, and DJ Kyle's speakers blew out one after another, pop, pop, pop, and nobody cared, because they could hear it now. The real music. The symphony.

Four hundred and forty pairs of eyes went blue.

And then we ate.

Not a sip. Not a skim. Rule three, broken, gone, done. The whole thing. I ate the slow dance. I ate the good photo. I ate the college applications and the parents fighting and the never-ending night. Mark ate beside me, and he was messy about it, and he was crying while he did it, which was so sweet, honestly, but he didn't stop. Not once. He ate as much as I did.

The gym went quiet. Four hundred and forty kids standing in rows under the streamers, smiling, empty, like dolls on a shelf. The teachers. The parents. DJ Kyle, still wearing his headphones.

"Yes," they said, all together, to nothing.

I have never been so full in my life. I was glowing so bright the windows of the gym turned blue from the outside. Somebody across town probably thought it was aliens. Kind of was.

Mark sat down on the edge of the stage. He looked at his hands. They were glowing too.

"I didn't feel anything," he said.

"I know! Isn't it amazing?"

"No," he said. "I mean I didn't feel anything about them. At all. Four hundred people. I didn't feel bad. I kept waiting to."

I sat down next to him and put my head on his shoulder. Bestie mode.

"Welcome to the main cast," I said.

And then the floor of the gym cracked down the middle.



Chapter Six: The Tenant

So the chalk girl showed up and she brought the whole barn.

The gym floor split like a lip. Hardwood buckling up in big splintered ridges. And under it wasn't concrete or dirt. Under it was dark. Wet. Moving. Breathing. A smell came up like old hay and pennies and something underneath both.

And standing on the far side of the crack, in the doorway, with her black backpack and her chalk hand and her eyes black all the way across, was Raven.

"Hi, Calypso," she said.

She didn't sound like a girl anymore. She sounded like a girl with a lot of other people standing behind her voice. A boy saying sorry. A girl saying no. Others. Older.

"Okay, so," I said, standing up, smoothing my dress, "we've got to talk about this whole vibe. Because this is not giving goth. This is giving landfill."

She didn't laugh.

She looked at Mark. Right at him. And for a second her black eyes flickered. Like she was trying to remember something and the static was in the way.

"Do I know you?" she said.

Mark opened his mouth. I saw it. He was going to tell her. He was going to say his own name. He was going to say I'm sorry. He was going to try to un-wipe her, right there, in the middle of a gym full of empty kids, and turn himself in.

I squeezed his hand so hard something cracked.

"Nope," I said for him. "Never met him. He's new."

And the thing under the floor rose.



Chapter Seven: The Choice

You know how sometimes you meet someone and you just immediately know they're bigger than you?

That was the Tenant.

It came up out of the crack in the floor in pieces. A shoulder made of dirt and roots. A long arm with too many elbows. A head, sort of, if a head can be a hole. It didn't have a mouth. It was a mouth. The whole thing. And it was pointed at the stage. At the glow. At the two of us, full to bursting with four hundred and forty kids' worth of wanting.

It wasn't here for Raven's revenge. Raven was just the door. It was here for the buffet.

And here's the thing about me. Here's the thing I'm good at. I'm good at knowing what's on the menu.

The Tenant was hungry for the song. It could smell the song. And there were two songs on that stage. One was old and smart and had been running for forty thousand years. And one was new and messy and sixteen and had never once in its life run away from anything.

I looked at Mark.

He was looking at the Tenant. Shaking. Glowing. Holding out his hand, the same way he did in the barn, a blue shield starting to shimmer up around us both.

He was trying to protect me.

That's so cute. Like, genuinely, I want that on record. That was cute.

"Mark," I said. "You trust me, right?"

He looked at me. His eyes were blue all the way across. Full of four hundred kids and his dad and Devon and Brad and me.

"Yes," he said.

He didn't even need me to sing. He just said it. He just meant it.

And I pushed him off the stage.



Chapter Eight: Lowkey A Strategic Decision

Okay so before anyone gets weird about this.

He was going to die anyway. You saw the thing. That was not a thing you fight. That was a thing you leave. And you can't leave a thing like that if it's chasing two people who smell exactly the same. Somebody has to be the distraction. That's just math. Himo taught me math, actually, when I was little, in the jar, before I got out. Spoiler: I'm better at it than her.

He fell into the crack.

He didn't scream. He turned around in the air, the way you turn when you trip, and he looked up at me, and his face did a thing I've never seen a face do. He wasn't scared. He wasn't even surprised. He looked like he finally got the answer to his question. How do you make it stop being hungry.

You trust someone who's hungrier.

The Tenant closed around him. All of it. The roots and the dirt and the elbows. And the glow went out of him all at once, a big blue flash that lit up every hollow face in the gym, and then it was just dark down there, and chewing.

Raven screamed. Not a fighting scream. A remembering scream. I think, when he went, her static cleared. I think his song took her forgetting with it, and she saw his face, his whole face, for the first time since the barn, just as it went down.

"MARK!"

She knew his name again. That's sad, I guess. I wouldn't know.

I was already out the back door.



Chapter Nine: New Season

I ran so fast. In heels. In a crown. Through the parking lot, past the football field, into the dark. Behind me the gym was coming apart, the Tenant digging down to finish the meal, Raven on her knees at the edge of the crack doing whatever chalk girls do when they lose. I didn't look back. Main characters don't look back. It's a whole rule.

And I was so full. Four hundred and forty kids. I was the biggest I'd been since the boats. I could have walked to the ocean. I could have walked across it.

I stopped on the highway overpass to catch my breath and fix my hair, because priorities.

My phone buzzed.

I don't have a phone, technically. I have a phone-shaped thing I made out of a kid's phone in Ohio. Nobody has the number. Nobody could have the number.

It buzzed again. Unknown caller.

I answered. Obviously. I'm not scared of anything.

"Hey bestie," I said. "Who's this?"

Nothing. Just breathing. Slow. Neat. Humming a little. Like a refrigerator.

And then a voice I have not heard since before your planet had salt in its oceans. Cold. Patient. Like a hand on your back at the edge of a pit.

"Corruption-022," Himo said. "There you are. You've gotten so big."

I didn't say anything. That's the first time in forty thousand years I haven't had anything to say.

"I'm starting plan C," she said. "I need a song. Come home."

And I want to tell you I hung up. I want to tell you I said something iconic. I had like six iconic things ready. "New phone who dis." "Not you calling me by my government name." "Babe, I'm literally booked."

I said, "Okay."

That's what came out. Okay. In a little voice. A jar voice.

Then I hung up. And I stood on the overpass in my crown, glowing so bright the cars below me swerved, and I felt something for the first time since the boats.

It wasn't guilt. Relax. It'll never be guilt.

It was the other thing. The thing I said I don't feel. The thing I only feel about one person.

Whatever. Next. I fixed my hair. I started walking. East, toward where her voice came from, because when Himo says come home, you come home. That's not a rule. That's just what I'm made of.

But I'm still the main character. I'm going to eat everything on the way.
