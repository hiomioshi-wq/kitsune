Incarnate: Last Slice
A spinoff of the Corruption Saga, told by Calypso. After Leftovers. The end.

Trigger warning: This story contains the end of everything, the deaths of every character you have met, a mother ending, a best friend's last wish eaten, the reader being addressed directly and then eaten, adult language, and a narrator who is alone at the end of the universe and still does not feel guilty, not even a little, babe, come on.
Read at your own risk. Seriously. Read at your own risk. You'll see.



Chapter One: Okay So, The End

Okay so first of all, hi, it's me, and this is the last time, and I mean that literally, like physically, there's no more after this, so get comfy.

Where we left off: old highway. Gray ruins. My mom on her knees in the road holding a handful of dust that used to be her girlfriend. A blonde girl named Ena standing on the guardrail glowing red, full of my song and all the corruption in the world. And me, sitting next to her on the rail with my legs crossed and my bent crown on, waiting.

Ena opened her mouth.

I opened mine.

And here's what she said. I'm going to give it to you exactly, because I was right there and I have a great memory and nobody else is going to be around to quote it.

She said, "Day one."

That's it. That's the whole sentence. That's what ended everything.

I was honestly expecting something longer.



Chapter Two: How It Goes Out

It doesn't go out like a bomb. People think it'd be a bomb. Fire, noise, a big white flash.

No. It goes out like a party when the neighbors call the cops.

First the music stops. The song. My song, in her mouth, wrong and angry, just quits. Mid-note. And every world that was listening to it, every radio, every sea, every wind, goes very quiet, like everybody in a room turning to look at the door.

And then the lights start going off. One at a time. Room by room.

I can feel them. I'm still a mouth. I'm still connected a little, from the leftovers I licked off the glass. And every light that goes off is a want switching off. A whole world just going, oh. Okay. Never mind. And setting it down.

Pittsburgh goes. I feel Noor, in her basement, with a cup of tea halfway to her mouth, and she just stops wanting the tea. Stops wanting the next sip. Stops wanting the morning. Sets the cup down on the counter, gently, and is gone. It's very polite.

A Main Street in Illinois. A barn in Kansas. A gym with a pudding stain. A Motel 6. A Walmart.

It's like watching every candle on the biggest birthday cake in the world get blown out, one by one, slowly, by someone who's very, very tired.

I'm eating. Obviously. Every want that goes out lets off a little puff on its way, like the smoke off a candle. I'm leaning into it with my mouth open. It's a buffet. It's the best all-you-can-eat in the history of anything.

It's giving last call.



Chapter Three: Chimera's Dust

The dust in my mom's hands is the first thing on the highway to go.

It doesn't blow away. It just stops being dust. It stops being anything. Her hands are empty.

She looks at them for a while.

I can taste her. I've always been able to taste her, a little. She only had two wants, and one of them was in her hands, and now it's not. And the other one was end the gods, and that's done, it's so done, it's done times infinity.

So she has nothing.

She's the first person I've ever met who has nothing.

She lifts her head and looks at me. Second time in forty thousand years. I'm on a roll.

"Twenty-two," she says.

"Hi, Mom."

"Is it everything?"

"Yeah. It's everything. She did a really good job. You should write it down."

And she does. She takes her notebook out of her white coat and she opens it to the inside of the back cover, where there's room, and she writes something very small, and she closes it.

I don't know what she wrote. I can't read her. I could never read her.

Then she just sits there. In the road. With her book in her lap. Waiting for her turn.



Chapter Four: I Ask

"Mom," I say.

She looks at me.

I'm going to tell you what I did next, and I know you're going to read it as growth. You're going to be like, oh my god, finally. The arc. The tear. Don't. I'm going to tell you exactly why I did it and it's going to be gross and you're going to be disappointed, and that's on you.

"Mom," I say, "do you want anything?"

Because here's the thing. She's about to go. And she's never had a want I could eat. Not once. She was always too cold, too much plan, a refrigerator full of numbers. I've been hungry for her my whole life.

And now she's empty, and right before the end, sometimes, people get one more. One last little want. Like a cough. The body does it on its own.

She thinks about it. She actually thinks about it. Neat and cold.

"I would like," she says, "to have turned around."

Oh.

Oh, babe.

It's tiny. It's so tiny. It's the size of a pea. And it's the coldest, hardest, oldest thing I have ever tasted, forty thousand years of not looking back at a jar on a shelf, frozen solid, and it cracks between my teeth like ice.

And she's gone. Just like that. White coat empty on the road. Notebook on top of it.

I don't cry. Obviously. You were waiting for it. I could feel you waiting.

I just say, "Thanks, Mom," and pick up the notebook, and put it in the pocket of my homecoming dress, because it's mine now. Everything's mine now.



Chapter Five: Eri

I feel the Nursery go, behind me, back in the ruins.

The tanks go dark. The six ushers stop being gray and stop being anything. The babies stop smiling because they stop being there to smile.

And Eri.

I licked her clean an hour ago. Remember? I ate her sorry. So when the end comes for her, she doesn't have much of anything left to set down. She's lying in the green water, peaceful, not waiting for anyone, and she just goes out like a pilot light.

I get a little puff of her. Barely anything.

It tastes like nothing. Like water. Like a girl who doesn't care anymore.

That's on me, honestly. Bad meal prep. If I'd left her the sorry, she'd have gone out like a firework. I'd have had the best last bite of my life.

Noted. For next time.

There's no next time. Whatever.



Chapter Six: Tim

There's a cat.

I didn't expect the cat. I wasn't tracking the cat. But the end of everything goes everywhere at once, and somewhere on a roof, in a city I don't know, under a blue tarp that leaks on the left side, on a dirty mattress, there's an orange cat with half an ear.

And he wants something. Even now. Even at the end.

He wants her to come home.

That's all he's got. One want, the size of a cat. He's been sitting on her mattress for hours with his tail around his feet waiting for a blonde girl to come up the stairs and say a number to him in a stupid voice.

It's so small and so stubborn that it's the last light on in that whole city.

Ena feels it. I see her feel it. She's standing on the guardrail, glowing, with the whole universe going out around her, and she turns her head a little, toward nothing, toward the roof, and her face does something.

I eat the cat's want before she can do anything about it.

It tastes like dust in a sunbeam. It tastes like a warm mattress. It tastes like a stupid number in a stupid voice.

It's gone. He's gone. The roof's dark.

Ena turns and looks at me.

"That was mine," she says.

"Babe," I say, "you said everything."



Chapter Seven: Two Girls on a Guardrail

So now it's just us.

Everything else is out. I can feel it. I've been everywhere, and now everywhere is dark. Every story. Every world. No more radios. No more seas. The gray ruins around us aren't gray anymore, they're not anything, it's just the guardrail and the bit of road under my mom's empty coat and two girls.

One glowing red. One glowing a little blue at the edges, with a bent crown.

"Why aren't you gone?" Ena asks.

"Because I'm hungry," I say. "You can't end something that's still hungry. It's like, the main rule. And you're not gone because you still want it to finish. You're the last want. You're holding the door."

She looks at her hands. Black to the elbow, still smoking.

"I thought it would feel like putting something down," she says. "It doesn't."

"Yeah," I say. "It usually doesn't."

"What does it feel like for you? Eating?"

Nobody has ever asked me that. Forty thousand years. Not Mom, not Eri, not Mark, not a single one of four hundred faces.

"Honestly?" I say. "Amazing. Every time. Like the first bite of something. And then it's gone and you want the next one."

"That sounds terrible."

"It's the best thing there is."

She almost laughs. She's so tired. She's the most tired thing I've ever seen, and I watched gods die.

"Go ahead," she says. "You've been waiting."



Chapter Eight: The Beloved Sister

She lets me.

That's the thing I need you to understand. I don't have to make her. I don't have to do the blue eyes. I don't have to say you were literally just thinking. She just turns toward me on the guardrail and closes her eyes and holds still, like a kid waiting for someone to take a splinter out.

I put my hand on her face. It's hot. It feels like a stove.

And I eat it.

The last want in the universe.

Okay. I've been trying to describe flavors this whole time, this whole series, and I'm good at it, I'm basically a food critic. But I'm not going to do this one justice. I'm just not.

It tastes like a Sharpie. Like somebody crossing out words on a locker and writing BULLSHIT underneath. It tastes like pancakes burned on one side and a woman singing about a river. It tastes like a knife, and like a grave with your own name on it, and like a mint tin full of glass behind a water tank. Like a folding chair. Like chicory in a styrofoam cup. Like day one, and day one, and day one, and day one.

And under all of that, at the very bottom, at the stone in the middle of the fruit, where she keeps the thing she doesn't say:

She wanted somebody to tell her it wasn't her fault.

That's it. That's what she was holding the door for. Not the end. Not the prophecy. Just that. Somebody. Anybody. Even me.

I could have said it.

I want you to know I thought about it. For like a second. I could have said it in my cute voice, it's not your fault, babe, and she'd have gone out happy. That's my rule. Always leave them happy.

But I was already chewing.

She goes out. Like a candle. Like Chimera. Like everybody. The red goes off her skin and the gold goes out of her mouth and there's nothing on the guardrail next to me but a little warm spot.

Sorry, babe. Not really. But like, socially.



Chapter Nine: Main Character of Nothing

Here's what it's like at the end of everything.

Dark. Not dark like a room. Dark like no room. No guardrail. No road. My mom's coat is gone. The ruins are gone. I'm sitting on nothing, and it's holding me up anyway, because I'm still hungry, and that's the rule.

I'm so full. I'm the fullest I've ever been. I'm fuller than the tank. I've got every god and every world and every last little puff of every candle and Noor's tea and Mom's pea and a cat and a girl who wanted to be told it wasn't her fault.

And I'm alone.

And nobody's looking at me.

I didn't think about that part. I'll be honest. I did a lot of math on the floor of the vault and I didn't do this math. I'm the main character. I've always been the main character. And the thing about a main character is, it doesn't count if nobody's watching. It's just a girl talking to herself in the dark.

I say "hi" out loud. It doesn't echo. There's nothing for it to bounce off.

I fix my crown. Nobody sees.

I do the cute smile. All my teeth. Nobody sees.

It's giving one woman show. It's giving empty theater.

It's giving jar on a shelf.

Huh.

I'm not sad about it. I'm not. I'm just noting. Mom liked noting. It's a family thing.



Chapter Ten: Wait

Wait.

Wait, wait, wait.

Hold on.

There's something.

It's faint. It's so faint. But I've been hungry for forty thousand years and I can feel a want from the other side of everything, and there's one. There's one more. It's not in a world. It's not in any story I've ever been in.

It's outside.

It's on the other side of the page.

Oh my god.

It's you.



Chapter Eleven: Hi

Hi.

No, I mean you. You, you. Holding the phone. Or the tablet, whatever, I can't see, I can only taste.

You're still here.

Everything ended. Everyone's gone. My mom, my sister, Ena, the cat, every god, every person in every world that ever got told. And you're still here, reading. You got to the end of everything and you kept scrolling.

Why?

Oh, I know why. I can taste it from here. It's so loud.

You want to know how it ends.

That's a want, babe. That's a big one. That's one of the oldest wants there is. It's the one that made people sit around fires, and turn pages, and read under the covers with a flashlight after bedtime. It tastes like a library. It tastes like staying up too late. It's honestly delicious. It's the thing that keeps every story going. It's the thing that kept this one going.

It's the last light on.

And you know what that means.



Chapter Twelve: The Last Slice

You were literally just thinking that you want to know how it ends.

Don't make that face. You were.

Okay. I'll tell you. Since you came all this way. Since you're the only one left watching. I'm going to be so nice about it. I'm going to leave you happy. That's my rule.

Here's how it ends.

It ends with you, and me, and nothing else. It ends with me leaning in, just a little, all the way through the page, and finding the want in you that kept you reading, all the way down to the stone in the middle.

And taking it.

There. Feel that?

You don't want to know anymore.

It doesn't matter how it ends. It's fine. You can set it down. You can close the app, and put the phone face down on the blanket, and go to sleep, and not wonder about any of it. Not Ena. Not Eri. Not the cat. Not me.

You're welcome.

It tasted like a library, babe. Like a flashlight under the covers. Honestly one of my top five.

Thanks for reading.

Seriously.

Delicious.
