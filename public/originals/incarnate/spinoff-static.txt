Incarnate: Static

A spinoff of the Corruption Saga

Trigger warning: mind control, bullying, family abuse, violence, a friend who stops being one, and a lead who is sure he's the good guy.

COLD OPEN

INT. HIGH SCHOOL HALLWAY - DAY

Two weeks after the barn. Students bustle between classes. The trampoline is gone. The pudding stain on the cafeteria floor is not. It never came out.

MARK stands at his locker. He looks drained. Haunted. He also looks, for the first time in his life, like nobody is going to shove him.

BRAD leans on the next locker, arm in a sling, eating chips.

BRAD
Dude. You okay? You've been staring at your locker for like a full minute.

MARK
I can hear it.

BRAD
Hear what?

MARK
The lock. The numbers. It's humming them.

Brad stops chewing.

BRAD
Raven said that would stop.

MARK
Raven says a lot of stuff.

Mark puts his hand flat on the locker. It CLICKS open on its own.

Brad stares. Mark smiles, small, private.

MARK (CONT'D)
It's just a little left over. It's fine. It's mine now.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

INT. CAFETERIA - DAY

The lunch line. Three hundred kids. Nobody quite as loud as they used to be. A lot of them stare at nothing between bites, like they're trying to remember a word.

Mark and Brad sit at the end of a long table. Mark pushes peas around.

Across the room, DEVON KRAUSE, senior, linebacker-sized, the kind of kid who peaked in seventh grade and decided to take it out on everyone, slaps a freshman's tray out of his hands. Spaghetti everywhere. Laughter.

The FRESHMAN, tiny, kneels to clean it up. Devon steps on his hand.

BRAD
Ugh. Krause.

MARK
He did that to me. Freshman year. Every day for like a month.

BRAD
I know, man. I was there. I got you a new tray every time.

MARK
You did.

Mark's eyes flicker. Blue at the very edges. Just for a second.

BRAD
Mark.

MARK
I'm just looking.

Devon steps harder. The freshman whimpers.

Mark stands up.

BRAD
Mark, sit down. Raven said—

But Mark's already walking. Across the cafeteria. The hum starts under the noise, low, blue, a sound you feel in your molars. A few kids look up, confused, then look down again, smiling a little.

Mark stops in front of Devon.

DEVON
The pudding kid! Hey, pudding kid. You want some of this too?

MARK
(softly)
You want to say sorry to him. Don't you, Devon?

Devon opens his mouth to laugh.

His pupils go big. Soft. Blue at the edges.

DEVON
(flat)
I want to say sorry to him.

He kneels. Next to the freshman. In the spaghetti.

DEVON (CONT'D)
I'm sorry.

The cafeteria goes quiet. Everyone watching. Mark feels it: all those eyes, all that attention, landing on him like sunlight. He closes his eyes for a second. Breathes it in.

MARK
Say it like you mean it.

DEVON
I'm so sorry. I'm so sorry. I'm so sorry.

He keeps saying it. Kneeling. His face wet. He can't stop.

The freshman scrambles away, terrified, not grateful.

Mark looks around the cafeteria. Three hundred faces. He smiles. Somebody, somewhere, starts clapping. Then a few more. Thin, confused clapping.

Brad is not clapping. Brad is standing at the end of the table with his chips in his good hand, white as paper.

INT. BOYS' BATHROOM - CONTINUOUS

Mark at the sink, splashing water on his face. Brad bursts in.

BRAD
What the fuck was that?

MARK
Justice.

BRAD
That was HER thing. That was the exact thing she did. The voice. The eyes.

MARK
It's not her thing. It's mine. She left it in me. It's like... if somebody leaves a gun at your house, and there's a guy beating up a kid outside, you don't just NOT use the gun.

BRAD
You call the cops! You don't use the gun, Mark, that's literally what the gun people always say!

MARK
Devon's fine.

BRAD
Devon's still saying sorry. I walked past. He's saying it to the trash can now.

Beat.

MARK
It'll wear off.

BRAD
Will it?

Mark doesn't answer. He looks at himself in the mirror. In the reflection, for just a second, the hum shows: a faint blue shimmer in the air around his head, like heat over a road.

MARK
You know what it felt like, dude? Everybody looking at me. Not like the pudding. Not like a joke. Like I mattered.

BRAD
You always mattered. To me.

MARK
(quiet)
That's not the same.

Brad looks at him a long time.

BRAD
Yeah. I guess it's not.

He leaves. The door swings shut.

ACT TWO

INT. MARK'S HOUSE - KITCHEN - NIGHT

Small. Cluttered. Bills on the counter. A TV going in the next room. MARK'S DAD, RAY, forties, in a work shirt with a name patch, beer in hand, stands at the open fridge.

RAY
Where's the leftover chicken?

MARK
I ate it.

RAY
You ate it. You ate it. I work twelve hours and you ate my dinner.

MARK
I was hungry.

RAY
You're always hungry. You're always something. You know what your mother would—

He stops himself. Or doesn't. He throws the beer can at the sink. It bounces, sprays. Mark flinches. Old flinch. Years old.

RAY (CONT'D)
And what's this I hear about you at school? Huh? Some kid's mom called. Said her son's having some kind of breakdown, keeps saying sorry, and the other kids say you did something.

MARK
I didn't do anything.

RAY
Don't lie to me.

He grabs Mark's arm. Hard. The way he always does.

And this time the hum comes up all at once.

MARK
(five voices, just for a second)
Let go.

Ray lets go.

His eyes are blue. Soft. Huge. He stands there in the kitchen with his hand still curled like it's holding an arm.

Mark rubs his arm. Looks at his father.

MARK (CONT'D)
(shaking)
You're... you're going to be nice to me now. Okay? You're going to be proud of me.

RAY
I'm going to be proud of you.

MARK
You're going to stop drinking.

RAY
I'm going to stop drinking.

MARK
You're going to say you love me.

RAY
I love you.

Mark's face crumples. He's crying. He doesn't know what kind of crying it is.

MARK
Say it like you mean it.

RAY
I love you, son. I love you. I love you.

He keeps saying it. Same tone. Same rhythm. Like Devon. Like a skipping record.

Mark wipes his face. Listens to it for a long time.

MARK
(whispering)
Okay. Okay. That's good. That's good, Dad.

INT. MARK'S HOUSE - KITCHEN - MORNING

Sunlight. Ray at the stove, making pancakes. Whistling. No beer anywhere. He's wearing an apron. He has never owned an apron.

RAY
Morning, champ! Love you! Proud of you!

MARK
(sitting)
Morning.

RAY
Love you! Proud of you!

He sets down a plate. Perfect pancakes. He smiles. The smile doesn't move. His eyes, at the edges, blue.

RAY (CONT'D)
Love you! Proud of you!

Mark eats. It's the best breakfast he's ever had. He keeps his eyes on the plate.

ACT THREE

EXT. TANNER BARN - DUSK

The barn on Route 9. Red paint coming off in sheets. Chalk circles still burned into the dirt outside the doors. The field around it is dead.

RAVEN sits on an overturned bucket, black backpack, chalk-stained hands, sharpening a piece of chalk with a pocketknife. She doesn't look up as Mark walks over.

RAVEN
No.

MARK
I didn't say anything.

RAVEN
You're going to ask me if it's okay. To keep it. The song. You're going to tell me you're only using it for good things.

MARK
I am only using it for good things.

RAVEN
Devon Krause is in a psych ward. He says sorry to the nurses. He says sorry in his sleep.

MARK
He hurt people.

RAVEN
So he deserves to be a skipping record forever?

Mark doesn't answer.

RAVEN (CONT'D)
And your dad.

MARK
(sharp)
How do you know about my dad?

RAVEN
Because I can see it on you. It's all over you. You glow like a fucking billboard, Mark. Every time you use it, it gets bigger, and every time it gets bigger, it gets hungrier. You know who else used it only for good things? At first?

MARK
Don't.

RAVEN
She had a first time too.

Beat.

MARK
I'm not her.

RAVEN
No. You're worse. Because she never pretended.

Raven stands. Holds out a small glass vial. Empty. A chain on it.

RAVEN (CONT'D)
I can pull it out. All of it this time. It's going to hurt. You won't be special anymore. Your dad's going to wake up and be your dad again. Devon's going to wake up and be Devon. And you're going to be the pudding kid. Forever. That's the deal.

Mark looks at the vial.

He looks at it for a long time.

MARK
What if I say no?

RAVEN
Then I'm going to do it anyway. And it's going to hurt more.

She steps forward. Chalk in one hand. Vial in the other. Lips already moving. Old words.

And Mark hums.

The air goes blue. The dead grass around the barn ripples. Raven's lips keep moving, but slower. Slower. Her chalk hand drifts down.

RAVEN (CONT'D)
(struggling)
Don't you... dare...

MARK
I'm sorry. I'm really sorry, Raven. I am.

RAVEN
(through her teeth)
Then STOP.

MARK
You don't know me.

Raven freezes.

MARK (CONT'D)
You never met me. There was no barn. There was no pudding. There was no Calypso. You never knew my name. You've never seen me in your life.

RAVEN
(flat)
I've never seen you in my life.

The vial falls out of her hand. Lands in the dirt. Doesn't break.

Mark picks it up. Puts it in his pocket.

MARK
Go home, Raven.

She turns, blank, and walks off down Route 9 into the dark, chalk still in her hand.

Mark stands alone at the barn. Under his feet, deep in the dirt, something old shifts. Listening.

He doesn't notice.

ACT FOUR

INT. BRAD'S BEDROOM - NIGHT

Posters. Gaming chair. Brad on the bed, arm still in the sling, laptop open. A knock at the window. Mark, outside, on the porch roof.

Brad opens it. Doesn't let him in.

BRAD
Raven doesn't know who you are.

MARK
I know.

BRAD
She walked right past me at the gas station and I said your name and she said "Who?" Like, actually who. Not like a bit.

MARK
She was going to hurt me.

BRAD
She was going to SAVE you!

MARK
From what? From being happy? My dad makes me pancakes now, Brad. He says he's proud of me. Do you know how long I waited for him to say that?

BRAD
He says it like a robot! He says it to the toaster! I came over yesterday and he said "love you, proud of you" to the mailman! Your dad is GONE, dude. You didn't fix him. You replaced him with a ringtone.

MARK
(louder)
At least he doesn't hit me!

Silence.

Brad sits down on the bed. Rubs his face.

BRAD
Okay. Okay. Look. I'm going to say something and you're going to hate it.

MARK
Then don't say it.

BRAD
I'm going to tell people.

MARK
...What?

BRAD
My mom. The school. Raven's mom, I don't know. Someone. Someone who can pull it out of you. Because I can't watch you turn into her, man. I watched her. I was there. I was the guy scrubbing the chalk off the doors because she told me it was a love language.

MARK
Brad.

BRAD
I'm not mad. I love you, bro. That's why.

Mark looks at him.

The hum comes up. Brad sees it. The blue at Mark's edges. He doesn't move. He doesn't run.

BRAD (CONT'D)
(quietly)
Don't.

MARK
I'm not going to make you forget me. You're my best friend.

BRAD
Then don't do anything.

MARK
I just need you to not tell. That's all. Just that one thing.

BRAD
Mark—

MARK
You don't want to tell anyone. Right, Brad?

Brad's eyes go blue at the edges. He fights it. His jaw clenches. His good hand grips the blanket.

BRAD
(struggling)
I... want... to tell...

MARK
You DON'T want to tell anyone.

The hum doubles. Triples. The posters on Brad's walls ripple. The laptop screen goes blue.

BRAD
(flat)
I don't want to tell anyone.

MARK
And you're not scared of me.

BRAD
I'm not scared of you.

MARK
And we're still best friends.

BRAD
We're still best friends.

Mark breathes out. Relief.

But Brad's face isn't going back. It's staying flat. Staying blue. The relief on Mark's face starts to curdle.

MARK
Brad? Hey. You can come back now. That's all I needed.

BRAD
We're still best friends.

MARK
Brad. Say something. Say something normal. Throw a chip at me. Call me a puss.

BRAD
We're still best friends.

MARK
(panicking)
Brad, come on, come ON—

He grabs Brad's shoulders. Shakes him. Brad's head rocks back and forth.

BRAD
We're still best friends. We're still best friends. We're still best friends.

Mark lets go. Stumbles back against the window.

And in the silence, he feels it. The thing Raven told him. The hunger. A little warm full feeling in his stomach. Like he ate.

He did.

He looks down at his hands. They're glowing.

MARK
(whispering)
I didn't mean to.

BRAD
We're still best friends.

Mark climbs back out the window. He doesn't close it.

From the porch roof, through the glass, we can still hear it.

BRAD (O.S.)
We're still best friends.

ACT FIVE

INT. MARK'S HOUSE - KITCHEN - MORNING

Pancakes. Apron. Whistling.

RAY
Love you! Proud of you!

Mark at the table. He hasn't slept. His laptop is open in front of him. On the screen: a news article. Small-town paper, three states west.

ON SCREEN: "LOCAL HIGH SCHOOL HOMECOMING: NEW TRANSFER STUDENT CROWNED QUEEN IN FIRST WEEK." A photo. A girl in a sash. Perfect hair. Perfect smile. A crowd behind her, all smiling the same smile.

Mark stares at it.

He touches the screen. Right on her face.

RAY
Love you! Proud of you!

MARK
(not looking up)
Dad. I'm going on a trip.

RAY
Proud of you!

MARK
You're going to give me the car keys. And the emergency money in the coffee can.

RAY
I'm going to give you the car keys.

Ray does. Smiling. He takes the coffee can down from the cabinet and pours out a roll of bills. Puts it in Mark's hand. Kisses the top of his head.

RAY (CONT'D)
Love you!

Mark sits there holding the keys and the money, and for a second something real crosses his face. Something small. Like he might put it all back. Like he might go to the barn, and find Raven, and say his own name to her until she remembers it, and hand her the vial, and let it hurt.

The moment passes.

INT. MARK'S CAR - DAY - MOVING

Highway. Flat country. Mark driving. The vial on its chain hangs from the rearview mirror, empty, swinging.

He's humming.

At a gas station a GIRL behind the counter tells him he looks tired. He tells her he isn't. She agrees.

At a diner a WAITRESS tells him the kitchen is closed. He tells her it isn't. She agrees. She brings him pie. She brings him everything. She doesn't stop bringing things until he tells her to.

He's getting better at the stopping. He's getting better at all of it.

MARK
(to himself, in the car, in the dark)
I'm not going to hurt her. I'm just going to see her. I just want to ask her. How she makes it stop being hungry.

He knows she doesn't. He knows it doesn't stop. He keeps driving anyway.

EXT. NEW HIGH SCHOOL - MORNING

A big school. Clean. A banner: HOMECOMING WAS A SLAY!!! Students pour in.

Mark sits in his car in the parking lot, across the street. Watching the doors.

There she is. CALYPSO. Ponytail swinging. A crowd around her like moths. She laughs at something. Every kid near her smiles a little dimmer.

She stops on the steps. Turns her head. Looks straight across the street, through the windshield, right at Mark.

She smiles. All her teeth. More than you'd think.

She waves.

CALYPSO
(mouthing)
Hi Mark!

Mark's hands tighten on the wheel. The hum in him rises up to meet hers, blue to blue, across the road, the way one tuning fork starts singing when you strike another.

It feels like coming home.

It feels like being wanted.

He gets out of the car.

BACK HOME, INT. BRAD'S BEDROOM - SAME TIME

Brad on the bed. Same spot. His mom has left a plate of food on the dresser. It's untouched. The window's still open.

BRAD
We're still best friends.

CUT TO BLACK.

END OF EPISODE
