Incarnate: Remade
A spinoff of the Corruption Saga. Book One, from inside Mark's head.

Trigger warning: This story contains mind control, losing yourself, hurting your best friend, folk horror, body horror, adult language, and a narrator who keeps something he should have given back.
Read at your own risk.



Chapter One: Hi Mark

She said hi to me.

That's where it starts. That's where all of it starts, if you want to know. Not the pudding. Not the barn. Not the static. B Hall, Monday, 11:42, between second and third period, Calypso walks by in her uniform with her ponytail doing the swing and she looks right at me, at me, Mark Ellison, the kid whose dad throws beer cans, the kid who eats lunch in the library most days, and she smiles and she goes:

"Hi Mark!"

And something goes into me.

I didn't know that's what it was. Not then. I thought it was a crush. I thought it was the biggest crush in the history of crushes. My whole chest went hot and my face went hot and my ears rang. I said "H-Hi Calypso" like an idiot, and she walked away, and I stood there at my locker, and the ringing didn't stop.

It was a hum. Low. Blue. Like a fridge in another room.

Brad elbowed me. "See? She's totally into you, you just need to make a big move!"

And the hum went: yes.

That's the first thing it ever said to me. Yes. And I thought it was me thinking it.



Chapter Two: The Plan

Brad's plan was insane. Trampoline. Mariachi band. Five hundred gallons of pudding.

I said no. I said it a bunch of times. I said it was over the top, I said we'd get expelled, I said where are we even going to get five hundred gallons of pudding.

But every time I said no, the hum got louder. And every time I thought about Calypso, about her looking at me, about the whole school looking at me while she looked at me, it got warmer. Like standing near a heater. Like when you're freezing and you finally get inside.

I'd been freezing my whole life. You don't know that until somebody turns the heat on.

Brad came over Thursday night with the forty gallons he'd somehow gotten from his cousin who works at a Costco. I didn't ask. He was weird that week. Kept saying things twice. Kept staring at the cafeteria in the middle of conversations, mouthing numbers like he was measuring it. He wrote SO BIG on his arm in Sharpie. When I asked him what that meant, he looked at it like he'd never seen it before.

I should have noticed. I noticed. I didn't care. The hum didn't care.

That night I lay in bed and I couldn't sleep, and the hum was right there in the dark with me, and for the first time it said something longer.

You're going to be so special.

And I cried. I don't know why. I just cried, quietly so my dad wouldn't hear, because nobody had ever said that to me. Not even a fridge.



Chapter Three: The Pudding

Friday. Lunch.

I remember the trampoline. I remember the band starting. I remember the bounce, once, twice, and I remember thinking, I'm going to do a flip, I've never done a flip in my life, and then I was doing a flip, my body just did it, like it already knew how.

And I landed in the pudding.

Warm. Brown. Everywhere. In my mouth, my ears, my nose. I came up and I was screaming her name and holding the poster and three hundred people were looking at me.

Three hundred people. Looking at me.

That's what I wanted. Not Calypso. I know that now. I wanted the looking. My whole life nobody looked at me, and when they did, it was because I had a bruise they were trying not to ask about. And now three hundred people were looking at me like I was the only thing in the room.

And then she stood on her chair.

And then she sang.

I don't know how to describe the song. I've tried. I've tried for weeks. It's not a sound. Your ears don't do anything. It goes in through your teeth and your spine and the backs of your eyes, and it's beautiful, and it's beautiful the way the edge of a cliff is beautiful when you're standing on it and part of you wants to step off.

I was in the pudding when she sang. I was in it up to my chest. And the song hit the pudding and went into the pudding and the pudding went into me, every pore, every inch.

Everybody else got a splash.

I got a bath.



Chapter Four: remadereborntransfigured

Saturday morning I woke up and I could hear everything.

The hum wasn't in the other room anymore. It was in me. It was me. My blood was humming. My bones were humming. When I opened my eyes the ceiling of my bedroom was breathing, slow, in and out, and I wasn't scared. I thought it was the most beautiful thing I'd ever seen.

I could hear my dad downstairs. Not his voice. Him. His wanting. It came up through the floor like heat. He wanted a beer. He wanted my mom back. He wanted me to be somebody else. He wanted that so bad it was almost loud.

I could hear the neighbors. The lady next door wanted her daughter to call. The kid across the street wanted a dog. The guy at the end of the block wanted to die, a little, just a little, every morning, and then didn't.

I could hear the whole town.

And I could hear her. Calypso. Across town. Her hum, my hum, the same key, like two tuning forks. And hers was so much bigger. Hers was an ocean and mine was a puddle that had fallen out of it. But it was the same water.

She opened the doors of perception. That's what I told Brad later. I told him I could see the rhythms of the universe. I told him I was remadereborntransfigured, all one word, because it felt like one word, because it felt like everything happening at once.

I want to be honest about this part, because nobody else is going to be.

It felt incredible.

Everybody thinks being mind-controlled feels like being trapped. Like being a puppet. It doesn't. Not at first. At first it feels like being the most yourself you've ever been. Every bad feeling just slides off. Every worry. Every flinch. It's like taking off a backpack you've been wearing your whole life. The hum takes all of it. The hum eats it. And what's left is just you, and the beautiful sound, and the breathing ceiling.

I didn't want her to stop.

That's the thing I never told Raven. I didn't want to be saved. Nobody asked me if I wanted to be saved.



Chapter Five: Brad

By Sunday it was getting bigger in me. Faster than I could keep up with.

I couldn't tell anymore which thoughts were mine. I'd think I want some cereal and the hum would say yes and I'd think, did I want cereal or did it? I'd look at the wall and the wall would ripple, and I couldn't tell if that was the hum or me doing it. I made a light bulb burst just by being annoyed at it. I made the dog next door sit down in the middle of the street. I made my laptop screen go blue and stay blue.

Brad came over. Brad always comes over.

He was different too. Clearer. His eyes were plain brown and he was looking at me the way you look at a dog that's foaming at the mouth.

"Dude, you're freakin' me out here," he said. "I think that chick did something to you."

"Of course she did!" I was so happy. I was so happy he noticed. "She opened the doors, Brad! She showed me the rhythms of the universe! I am—"

And I said the word. My word.

And Brad stood up and said, "Okay, that's it, I'm getting Raven."

Raven.

The hum heard that name and something in it went cold. A flash. A feeling that wasn't mine at all. Fear. Calypso's fear, coming down the line from across town like a jolt down a wire. She was scared of Raven. And the second I felt her be scared, the hum turned it into something else.

"No," I said. "The Raven Girl, she's stuck in the illusions! Lost in the static! But I can take you to the other side, Brad! I can—"

And then Calypso was in my room.

I don't remember the door. She was just there, sweet, smiling, and she said two words.

"Mark. Pin him."

And I did.



Chapter Six: Pinning

I need to tell you what it felt like, because I've never said it out loud.

It felt good.

Not like I was being made to do it. Like I wanted to. Like I'd always wanted to. Brad, flying back into the closet door, sticking there, his feet off the ground, his arms out, shouting, and I could feel him in the song like a fly in a web, and I could feel exactly how much pressure it would take to crush him.

Brad. Who gave me half his Lunchable in second grade. Who let me sleep on his floor for a week when my mom left. Who threw chips at me and called me a puss and got me forty gallons of pudding because I liked a girl.

I held him there and I liked it.

I said, "Don't fight it, Brad. The puzzle is finally taking shape," and it was my voice and four other voices, and one of them was hers, and one of them was older than her, and one of them, I swear, was mine from a long time from now, from the future, from a version of me that had kept doing this.

That's the part that scares me. Not that she made me do it. That some of me already knew how.

Brad was crying. Up on the door. Crying and yelling my name. And the hum ate it. Every bit of it. His fear. His love for me. His wanting me to be okay. It came off him like steam and I breathed it in and it tasted like nothing I'd ever had.

It tasted like being the one who throws the beer can.

Then the window broke, and a girl with chalk came through it, and everything went white.



Chapter Seven: The Barn

I don't remember getting to the barn. I remember being in it. I remember Raven's circles, chalk inside chalk inside chalk, smoking under me. I remember being on my knees. I remember her pulling.

She was pulling the song out of me. Like thread out of a sweater. Like a splinter. She had a little glass vial on a chain around her neck, and she was chanting, and the blue was coming out of my eyes and my mouth and my fingertips and going into the vial in a thin bright stream.

And it hurt. God, it hurt. It wasn't like having something taken out. It was like having something ripped out that had already grown roots. Every inch of it that left, I felt the backpack coming back. The weight. My dad. The beer can. The library at lunch. Being nobody. Being the kid with the bruise.

The cold coming back in.

And then she came. Calypso. Through the barn door, with her face open, with the song so loud the walls went soft, and Raven was screaming and Brad was screaming and the barn was crawling with things from under the dirt.

And the vial broke against her and she screamed and got bigger and lunged.

And I stood up.

The story says I stood up to protect my friends. That's what Raven tells people. That's what Brad told his mom. Mark stepped in front of her. Mark held out his hand. Mark made a shield. Mark said, "You did nothing for me but use me. Now it's my turn to protect my friends."

I did say that. I did do that. It's true.

But it's not the whole truth.



Chapter Eight: What I Kept

Here's the whole truth. Nobody knows this. Not Raven. Not Brad. Not even Calypso.

When Raven was pulling the song out of me, right before Calypso came in, I felt the last of it going. The very last thread. The bottom of the glass. And I had a choice. I could feel it there, a little blue knot, deep down, right under my heart, the very first bit she ever put in me. Hi Mark. The first yes.

And I grabbed it.

I don't know how. I just did. The way you grab a railing when you trip. I closed around it, inside, and I held on, and Raven's pull went right past it and never noticed, and the vial filled up with everything else, and I let it. I let her take all of it except that.

I kept one piece.

That's what the shield was made of. Not her song. Mine. The piece I stole back. I made a shield out of it to protect my friends because I'd just lied to them, and some part of me knew, and wanted to make it up to them right then, all at once, in one big hero moment.

And it worked. Calypso shrank. She ran. She went out a knothole like a wet blue cat. Raven sat down in the chalk and cried. Brad laughed, one big crazy laugh, and hugged me so hard my ribs creaked.

"You did it, bro," he said into my shoulder. "You did it. You're you again."

"Yeah," I said. "I'm me again."

The little blue knot under my heart hummed.

I want you to know, I told myself I'd give it back. That night, in my bed, with my dad snoring downstairs and the ceiling not breathing anymore, just a ceiling, I told myself: tomorrow. Tomorrow I'll go find Raven and tell her she missed a piece. She'll pull it. It'll hurt. I'll be okay.

But the ceiling was just a ceiling. And the town was quiet. I couldn't hear anyone wanting anything. I couldn't hear anything at all. And the cold was all the way back in, the whole backpack, every pound of it, and I lay there and I thought: I can't. I can't go back to that. Not all the way. Not after knowing.

Just a little. Just to keep warm. It's so small. It's nothing.



Chapter Nine: Two Weeks

Two weeks later I was standing at my locker and I could hear the lock humming its own numbers.

I put my hand on it. It clicked open.

Brad was next to me with his arm in a sling and a bag of chips, looking at me like he'd seen a ghost.

"Raven said that would stop," he said.

"Raven says a lot of stuff."

And I smiled. Small. Private. Because I knew something he didn't. I knew the little blue knot under my heart wasn't little anymore. It had been eating. Quietly. Every day. Every time somebody looked at me in the hall, every time the lunch lady said hi, every time my dad stopped at the bottom of the stairs and wanted, just for a second, to come up and say something and didn't. It ate all of it. It was growing. It was mine.

You know what I'll say, later, to Brad, in a bathroom, after a kid named Devon gets on his knees in spaghetti? I'll say: if somebody leaves a gun at your house, you don't just NOT use it.

That's a lie. Nobody left it. I picked it up. I held it in the dark while the girl who saved me was pulling everything else out, and I closed my hand around it on purpose, and I never told her.

People say she controlled me. Calypso. That I was under her song. That none of it was my fault.

For a weekend, maybe. For a weekend, it was her.

Everything after that was me.

It's just a little. It's so small.

It's mine.
