Incarnate: Main Character
A spinoff of the Corruption Saga

Trigger warning: This story contains mind control, body horror, the eating of minds, manipulation, violence, and a narrator who thinks all of that is fine. She is not fine. Do not take notes from her.
Read at your own risk.



Chapter One: Hi Mark!

Okay so first of all, I did not ask to be this iconic. It just happened. It's literally a curse.

Hi. It's me. Calypso. You know me. Everybody knows me, that's kind of the whole point of me. Cheer captain, homecoming court three years running, the girl whose name gets written on bathroom stalls in a nice way for once. I have perfect hair because I decided I have perfect hair. I have a 4.0 because I looked Mr. Duggan dead in his sad little eyes in September and said, "You think my essays are amazing, right?" and his pupils went big and soft and blue and he said, "Yes," and that was that. Done. Locked in. Academic weapon.

I am also, technically, a cosmic force of hunger older than your planet's crust. But like. Details.

Let me explain the hunger thing because people get so weird about it. You eat food, right? You don't feel bad for a sandwich. You don't go to the sandwich's funeral. I eat attention. I eat wanting. I eat that little warm buzz that comes off you when you look at somebody and go, wow. And if I'm really hungry, and I'm always kind of really hungry, I eat the part of you that makes the buzz. The whole part. Down to the floor. After that you're just kind of a smiley shell that says "Yes" a lot.

Is that dark? Maybe. Is it giving apex predator? Also yes. Periodt.

So anyway, Monday. I'm walking down B Hall in my uniform with my ponytail doing the swing, the good swing, and I clock these two boys at the lockers. Mark and Brad. Mark has been staring at me since freshman year like I'm a sunset he isn't allowed to look at directly. It's honestly kind of sweet. It's also, and I need you to understand this, so loud. His wanting is so loud. It comes off him like heat off a parking lot. Most people want me in a background way, like elevator music. Mark wants me in a full orchestra way.

I have been saving him. Like a dessert.

So I do my little wave and I go, "Hi Mark!" super sweet, and he goes "H-Hi Calypso," and the buzz coming off him almost knocks me over. Delicious. Ten out of ten. No notes.

Brad elbows him. Brad is a golden retriever who learned to talk and immediately used it for evil. I can hear him going on about a plan. Trampoline. Mariachi band. Five hundred gallons of pudding.

Five hundred gallons of pudding.

I stop walking. I actually stop. Because babe. That's a grand gesture. That's a whole cafeteria of people watching one boy lose his mind over me in public. Do you know how many people that is? Do you know how much wanting fills a room when a whole school is watching someone be desperate?

That's not a crush. That's a buffet.

I turn around and smile at Brad. Just at Brad. I let it slide out of me a little, the song, a soft blue hum under the hallway noise that nobody hears with their ears.

"You should totally do the pudding thing," I tell him. "It's so romantic. Like, lowkey genius."

Brad's eyes go blue at the edges. "It's so romantic," he says. "Lowkey genius."

"Bet," I say, and I flip my ponytail and I go to second period, and I eat a little nibble off the substitute because I'm peckish, and she forgets her own middle name for the rest of the day. Whatever. She wasn't using it.



Chapter Two: Rules For Being Me

People think I don't have rules. I have so many rules. I'm basically a lawyer.

Rule one. Never eat anyone all the way at school. If too many people at one school start walking around saying "Yes" with blue eyes, the adults get weird, and then somebody calls somebody, and then a priest shows up, or worse, a girl like Raven.

Rule two. Always leave them happy. A happy shell is a quiet shell. If you leave somebody hollowed out and sad, they cry, and crying makes noise, and noise makes questions. If you leave them smiling, everyone just thinks they found Jesus or got really into running.

Rule three. Never, ever let anyone hear the whole song unless you're done with the whole town.

I've been doing this a while. Like, a while while. Before this school there was a school in Ohio. Before Ohio there was a girls' academy in Switzerland in like 1890 that had a tragic "fever." Before that, a fishing village that just sort of stopped. Before that, boats. So many boats. Sailors were a whole era for me. Sailors loved to want things. The ocean was basically a drive-thru.

And before the boats, before your planet's water even had salt in it, there was the Nursery, and a lot of screaming, and a woman named Himo with cold hands who made me on purpose and then tried to throw me away. But I don't talk about that. Literally not my vibe. Next.

The point is, I've done the high school thing like forty times, and I'm good at it, and I'm going to be good at it for forty more, because the thing about teenagers is they want everything so hard. Every single day. They're like little gas stations of wanting. You can fill up anywhere.

Mark Tuesday: walks past my locker four times. I count. I don't even look up. Four little sips.

Mark Wednesday: tries to say something to me in the lunch line, panics, says "Nice pudding," about the cafeteria pudding, which is not even nice. I let him see me laugh at it, like it was cute. His whole chest lights up. Gulp.

Brad Wednesday: I pass him a note in chem that just says "the cafeteria is so big lol." He reads it and his eyes go blue and he writes "SO BIG" on his own arm in Sharpie and starts measuring the room in footsteps during lunch. Icon. Unwell. Love that for him.

Thursday a girl in my squad, Kenzie, says my routine is "kind of mid" in front of everybody. She says it as a joke. She's smiling. It's fine.

It's not fine.

I wait until after practice. In the locker room. I sit down next to her on the bench while she's untying her shoes, and I lean my head on her shoulder like we're besties, and I go, really soft, "You think I'm the best captain this team has ever had, right?"

"Yes," she says.

"And you'd do literally anything for me."

"Yes."

"Say it like you mean it, girlie."

"Anything," Kenzie says, and her eyes are blue all the way across now, no white at all, and I take a little more than I meant to, because she called me mid, and her face goes slack and pretty and empty, and she smiles at the lockers for a while.

She came back mostly. She's quieter now. She does a lot of yes. Her mom thinks she's finally maturing.

Rule one, technically still intact. I didn't eat her all the way. You can't prove I did.



Chapter Three: Raven Is Such A Hater

So there's this girl.

There's always a girl. Every school, every century. In Switzerland it was a scullery maid who kept garlic in her apron for some reason (wrong monster, babe, but cute effort). In Ohio it was a theater kid with a tarot deck who almost got me with a candle. Here it's Raven.

Raven is goth but like, real goth. Not aesthetic goth. She doesn't post. She doesn't have a story. She has a black backpack covered in chalk symbols and she smells like burnt sage and pennies, and the first day of junior year she looked right at me across the courtyard and didn't want me even a little.

Do you know how rare that is? It's like walking into a bakery and smelling nothing.

I tried the song on her once, early. Just a little. She was at the vending machine and I walked up behind her and hummed and said, "You like my hair, right?"

She didn't even turn around. She put her dollar in and said, "Your hair is a symptom."

A SYMPTOM. Of what, Raven. Of being beautiful? Bffr.

So we have this thing now, me and Raven. We don't talk. We just know. She watches me at pep rallies with her arms crossed like a bouncer. I smile at her across the cafeteria with all my teeth, which is a lot of teeth, more teeth than you'd think, if I stop paying attention for a second. She chalks little symbols on the doorframes. I have Brad scrub them off. Brad loves scrubbing them off. I told him it was a love language.

Wednesday night I'm in my room with my real face on for a minute, just to breathe, because the human face is cute but it's like wearing skinny jeans for a thousand years. My real face is more of a whole situation. It opens. Long story. And I get a feeling like someone's watching, and I look at my window, and there's chalk on the outside of the glass.

On the outside. Second floor.

It's a little circle with a line through it and a word underneath in some language older than me, which, rude, because nothing's older than me, but it's close. It's the word for no.

Okay. Okay okay okay.

I put my cute face back on. I open the window. I lean out into the October dark and I say, so the whole street can hear if it's listening, "RAVEN, I KNOW YOU'RE IN THE BUSHES, BESTIE, AND THAT'S SO EMBARRASSING FOR YOU."

The bushes rustle. Footsteps run off.

Then I sit on my bed and I laugh until I'm doing the other laugh, the old one, the one that sounds like a lot of water going down a very deep drain, and the neighbor's dog starts barking and doesn't stop until morning.

It's fine. I'm not scared of a girl with chalk. I'm scared of nothing.

That's not true. I'm scared of exactly one thing and her name is Himo and she's not here. So I'm scared of nothing. Math.



Chapter Four: The Pudding

Friday. Lunch. The vibes are immaculate.

Brad did it. Brad actually did it. I don't know how. I didn't ask. When I say "make it happen," I don't do follow-up questions, I'm not a project manager. There's a trampoline in the middle of the cafeteria. There's a mariachi band, four guys in full charro suits who look like they've been hired and then mildly kidnapped. And there's the pudding. Not five hundred gallons, okay, maybe more like forty, in a kiddie pool and a bunch of those big orange coolers, chocolate, already going a little warm in the fluorescent light.

The whole school is in there. Three hundred and twelve kids. I counted. Plus lunch ladies. Plus Coach Ferris. Plus Mr. Duggan, who still thinks my essays are amazing.

And everybody is looking at Mark.

Mark is standing on the edge of the trampoline holding a poster that says CALYPSO WILL YOU GO TO THE WINTER FORMAL WITH ME in glitter glue. His hands are shaking so bad the glitter's falling off like snow. His face is the color of a stop sign. He wants this so much. He wants me so much. And three hundred and twelve people are watching him want it, and they're cringing and laughing and feeling sorry for him and feeling glad it isn't them, and every single one of those feelings is coming off them like steam off a pot.

Oh, I'm so hungry. I'm so hungry I can feel my real mouth trying to open behind my cute one.

The band starts playing. Mark bounces. He bounces once, twice, he does a flip, an actual flip, he's committed, I respect it, and he lands in the pudding pool with this huge brown splash that hits the whole front row, and he comes up dripping and holding the poster over his head and screaming "CALYPSO!" like a soldier in a war movie.

The whole room gasps. Then the whole room laughs. Then the whole room looks at me.

And that, that right there, that's the moment. Three hundred and twelve people looking at one girl. All that attention aimed at one spot like a magnifying glass at an ant. It's the best feeling in the universe. It's better than the boats.

I stand up on my chair. I put my hand on my heart. And I do something I promised myself I wouldn't do at this school.

I let it out.

Not all of it. Rule three. But a lot. More than Switzerland. The song comes out of me like a tide coming in, low and blue and gorgeous, a sound that isn't a sound, a sound that goes in through the bones instead of the ears, and every light in the cafeteria flickers, and the mariachi band stops playing because their hands just stop, and three hundred and twelve pairs of eyes go blue at the edges.

"Mark," I say, and my voice is about five voices right now, "that was literally the most romantic thing anyone has ever done."

"It was the most romantic thing," the cafeteria says. All of them. At once.

"Yes," I say. "Yes, I'll go with you."

Mark makes a noise like his soul left and came back. And I eat.

Not the whole room. Just a sip from everybody. Just the top. It's like skimming cream. A little bit of wanting from every kid in that room, the lunch ladies, Coach Ferris, Mr. Duggan, the band. Somebody's crush on someone. Somebody's dream of being a nurse. Somebody's love for their little brother. Somebody's homesickness. I don't pick. I just drink. And they all stand there in the pudding-splattered light, smiling, happy, a little dimmer than they were.

Rule two. Leave them happy.

I'm a girl of my word.



Chapter Five: Mark Is Glowing Now, Which Is Kind Of My Fault

Okay so. Small problem. Teeny.

When I sing that loud, the song splashes. It's like pudding, honestly. If you're in the front row, you get some on you. And Mark wasn't in the front row, Mark was in the pool. Mark was the pool. Mark got a whole face full of me.

By Saturday Mark's eyes are blue. Not edges-blue. Full blue. And he's not hollow. That's the thing. He's the opposite of hollow. He's full. He got the song in him and instead of draining him, it filled him up, and now he's walking around town humming it.

It's my song. He's humming MY song. Like a cover. Without asking.

Brad texts me (Brad texts me all the time now, he's very loyal, it's giving emotional support animal): "yo mark is acting weird. he says he can see the rhythms of the universe. is that a formal thing"

I text back "lol he's just excited 💙"

Brad texts "ok!!" and then "he says I can come to the other side with him" and then "is that a formal thing"

I don't answer that one.

Here's what I know and Brad doesn't. When a human gets a mouthful of the song and doesn't go hollow, it's because they've got something in them that wants back. Some little hunger of their own. Mark wanted me so hard for so long that there was a hole in him exactly my shape, and the song went in and filled it and now it's growing. Mark's turning into something. Something like me. Something small and new and hungry and mine.

Is that a problem? Some people would say that's a problem.

I say it's a glow-up.

Sunday afternoon I walk over to Mark's house. His mom lets me in because I tell her she wants to let me in. He's in his room with the curtains shut, sitting cross-legged on his bed, and the walls around him are doing this thing, this wobble, like heat over a road, like reality is a waterbed and he's lying on it. His laptop is open and the screen is just blue. Brad's on the floor in the corner, sitting very still, because Mark told him to sit very still.

"Calypso," Mark says, and it's two voices, his and a tiny echo of mine. "You opened the doors. You showed me. I'm remadereborntransfigured." He says it like that, one word. He's so proud of it.

"Aw," I say. "That's so cute. I love that for you."

"I can bring Brad," he says. "To the other side. I can bring everybody."

Brad, from the floor, through his teeth, eyes very wide, very not-blue: "Calypso, I think something's wrong with him."

That's when I realize Brad isn't under. Brad was under, a little, from all my nudges. But something's cleared him out. He's looking at me with plain brown human eyes, and he's scared, and for the first time since freshman year he's looking at me without wanting a single thing from me except for me to go away.

Someone got to him. Someone with chalk.

"Mark," I say, very sweet, "pin him."

Mark doesn't even move. The air moves. A big blue boom goes off in that little bedroom, a slam of song so loud the posters fall off the walls, and Brad flies back into the closet door and sticks there, spread out like a bug on a windshield, shouting, his feet off the ground.

Mark is breathing hard. His eyes are blazing. Five voices are coming out of him now, old and new, layered. "Don't fight it, Brad. The puzzle is finally taking shape."

Wow. Okay. Main character energy. I'm actually kind of proud? It's like watching your kid take their first steps, if your kid was taking them on someone's ribcage.

That's when the window breaks.



Chapter Six: The Barn

You know what, I'm going to skip some of this, because Raven got a couple hits in and I don't need that in my story. That's not canon for me. Long story short, she came through the window with a bag of salt and a lot of Latin, and she grabbed Brad off the door and Mark by the collar, and there was this huge flash, and then I was alone in a bedroom with a bunch of fallen posters and a mom downstairs yelling "Is everything okay up there?"

"Yes," I said, and the mom said "Yes," and stopped yelling.

But I'm not dumb. I know where they went. There's only one place in this town a girl like Raven would take two boys to do witch stuff. The old Tanner barn out on Route 9, with the red paint coming off in sheets and the field around it that never grows anything right. I've known about that barn since the day I enrolled. There's something under it. Something old that got buried out there before the town existed, something a little bit like me, a hunger in the dirt.

It's honestly a great venue.

So I go Sunday night. I don't drive. I walk, and the dark walks with me, and every house I pass, the people inside get up from their couches and go stand at their windows and watch me go by, smiling, because I'm humming. I'm humming the whole way. Rule three is getting a little blurry for me, if I'm being so honest right now.

The barn doors are open. There's candlelight inside. Chalk everywhere, circles inside circles inside circles. Raven's in the middle of it, chanting, hands moving like she's knitting something out of air. Brad's in a corner holding a baseball bat like it'll help. And Mark's in the middle of the biggest circle, on his knees, with blue light pouring out of his eyes and his mouth and his fingertips, and Raven's pulling it. Pulling my song out of him like thread out of a sweater, winding it into a little glass vial on a chain around her neck.

My song. Into a little bottle. Like perfume. Like a souvenir.

Babe, no.

"Greetings, little Raven," I say from the doorway, and I don't use my cute voice. "I was wondering when you'd arrive to bear witness."

She looks up. She doesn't scream. Points for that. Brad screams, though. Brad screams enough for everybody.

And then I let my face open.

I'm not going to describe it. You've seen it if you've seen it, and if you haven't, keep it that way. Let's just say it goes sideways and then it goes back and there's a lot more mouth than there used to be and some fronds, honestly, and it's not my best angle but it is my truest one.

"My children are finally transcending their frail mortality," I say, out of all of it. "Their minds broken open to be reborn in ecstatic rapture. Tell me, can you hear the symphony?"

And I sing.

Not a little. Not a sip. The whole thing. The town-ender. The boat-sinker. The Switzerland Special.

The barn shakes. The candles go blue. The chalk lines start to smoke. The walls of the barn start to move, and I mean move, the old wood going soft and wet and crawling, tendrils of whatever's under the ground coming up to listen, because it's my song and it's the most beautiful thing in any universe and everything that hears it wants to come closer.

Raven claps her hands over her ears. Her face scrunches up. Blood comes out of her nose.

"No," she says. "I won't let you take this world!"

Cute. "You think," I say, "you can defy a god?"



Chapter Seven: Okay So This Part Didn't Go Great

So Raven throws the vial.

The little glass bottle full of my own song, the stuff she pulled out of Mark, she rips it off her neck and she throws it at me and it hits me in the chest and shatters.

And it burns. It burns like nothing has burned since the Nursery. Because it's not just my song in there. It's my song after it went through Mark. After it sat inside a stupid, shaking, sixteen-year-old boy who wanted something so hard. It came back out of him tasting like him. Like a human. Like wanting me but also wanting his mom to be okay and wanting to pass driver's ed and wanting Brad to stop calling him a puss. All mixed in. It's my own song but it's been through a person, and I can't digest a person's whole heart all at once, it's too much, it's too many things, it's like swallowing a fistful of glass.

I scream. The barn screams. Every window in the county breaks. (Probably. I didn't check.)

And then, because I'm me, because I'm literally the hunger, I eat the pain too.

The black stuff eating me starts to pull back together, into me, and I get bigger. I get so much bigger. I fill up the barn. My head hits the rafters. I'm laughing, I'm laughing, I'm the size of the whole sky, Raven is on her back in the chalk with her nose bleeding and Brad is just sitting down now, done, so done, and I lean down over them with more teeth than anything has ever had.

"You have only made me stronger," I say, and I mean it. I lunge.

And Mark steps in front of her.

Mark. My Mark. My dessert. My little glow-up. He stands up out of the chalk, with blue still dripping out of his eyes, and he holds out one hand and a shield comes up around the three of them, shimmery, blue. My blue. Made out of my song.

"You dare defy me," I say, "after all I have done for you?"

And Mark, little pudding Mark, looks right up at me, at the real me, at the mouth, and doesn't want me at all.

"You did nothing for me but use me," he says. "Now it's my turn to protect my friends."

That's the part that gets me. Not the shield. The not-wanting. It hits like a door slamming in my face. Every bit of wanting that boy ever had for me, years of it, the whole parking lot of heat, just gone, turned off. And the hole in him that was shaped exactly like me is suddenly shaped like them. Raven, who he barely knew a week ago. Brad, who's useless. Them.

Him choosing them makes me so small so fast I feel like a balloon letting go.



Chapter Eight: Lowkey Not My Fault

Here's what I want on the record.

I could've eaten him on Monday. Literally Monday. At the lockers. One "Hi Mark!" with a little extra on it, and that boy would have been a happy, empty shell in the B Hall by second period, and none of this would have happened. No pudding. No glowing. No barn. No shield.

I didn't, because I was saving him. Because I wanted the big moment. Because I wanted the whole room watching. Because I wanted to be looked at.

So some people would say this was my fault. Some people would say that if I were capable of a single thought that wasn't "me," I'd have seen it coming. Some people (Raven) actually did say that, out loud, while I was shrinking, and she said it with a lot of swear words, and I will be thinking about her for a long time in a not-cute way.

I don't think it's my fault. I think Mark was a hater the whole time, deep down. I think Raven groomed him into being a hater. I think Brad should never have been allowed to buy forty gallons of pudding as a minor. I think this school had bad energy from the start. Next.

When it was over I was about the size of a cat. I got out through a knothole in the barn wall. I spent the night in a drainage pipe under Route 9, being a small blue wet thing that hummed to itself and didn't feel bad. I want to be clear about that, too. I didn't feel bad. Not about Kenzie. Not about the three hundred and twelve kids who all lost a little something in the cafeteria and will spend the rest of their lives feeling like they forgot a word that was right on the tip of their tongue. Not about the substitute's middle name. Not about the boats.

You don't feel bad about the sandwich.

I felt hungry. That's all I ever feel. It's honestly kind of peaceful.



Chapter Nine: New Semester, New Menu

It takes me three weeks to get my face back. Cute face, I mean. The real one's always there.

I spend the three weeks in the drainage pipe, then in a culvert, then in the walls of a Waffle House off the interstate, eating little bits off the night shift. A trucker's love of country music. A waitress's plan to go back to school. It's fine. It's survival food. It's giving gas station sushi.

Then I take a bus west.

I don't buy a ticket. I tell the driver I already bought one. He agrees with me. Everybody always agrees with me. That's what makes me so easy to be.

There's a new school in a new state. Clean floors. Big cafeteria. A huge banner in the hallway that says HOMECOMING IS COMING!!! with three exclamation points. I walk into the front office in a new uniform with a new swing on my ponytail, and the secretary looks up at me, and I lean on the counter and I smile and I say, "Hi! I'm new. You have all my transcripts already, right?"

Her pupils go big and soft and blue.

"Yes," she says.

"And I'm, like, the perfect transfer student."

"Yes."

"Say less," I say, and I take my schedule, and I walk out into the hallway.

It's passing period. Hundreds of kids. Hundreds of little gas stations of wanting, buzzing, glowing, totally unprotected. A boy by the trophy case drops his books when I walk by. A girl in a band hoodie forgets what she was saying mid-sentence. I can hear all of it. The crushes. The dreams. The fear. The homesick. The hungry.

Somewhere in this building there's going to be a girl with chalk. There always is.

I'm going to find her first this time. That's the lesson. That's growth. Look at me, being so self-aware.

I'm not going to stop, though. Obviously. Why would I stop? Stopping is for people who feel bad, and I've told you like nine times, I don't. I'm the main character. Main characters don't stop. They just get a new season.

A boy by the water fountain is staring at me like I'm a sunset he isn't allowed to look at directly.

Aw. He's so loud.

I wave.

"Hi!" I say.

Delicious.
