Incarnate: Last Rites
A spinoff of the Corruption Saga. During Main Character, Uncut.

Trigger warning: This story contains the end of gods, death, wanting to die, a love that excuses everything, the abandonment of everyone else, heavy adult language, and a narrator who finally gets what she wants and lets the world pay for it.
Read at your own risk.



Chapter One: Oh, Fuck

I was in the middle of a sentence when the song hit me.

I was sitting on a broken highway in the Gray Abyss, which is where I live now, because nobody else fucking will, telling a dog with a cat's head about the time I set a whole moon on fire because it looked at me funny. The dog was being very polite about it. Good dog. Weird head. And I was right at the good part, the part where the moon tries to apologize, and then the song came up through the asphalt and into my spine and every bone in my body went quiet.

And I said, "Oh, fuck."

And then I said, "Oh, Himo, you didn't."

She did. Of course she did. She always does.

My name's Chimera. You don't know it. Nobody does anymore. Over the centuries people worshipped me, followed me, praised me, loved me, called me a lot of names. A lion with a goat and a snake stuck on it, that was a Greek thing, they never got me right. I was a god of patchwork. Of things put together that weren't supposed to go together. Of hybrids and monsters and scars that heal crooked. A goddess of making do.

I'm old. Older than most. I survived the first war because I'm stubborn and ugly and made of spare parts, and corruption's a typo, and you can't typo something that was a mess to begin with.

And right now a song was coming up out of the bottom of the world and telling me, in the sweetest voice I've ever heard, that I wanted to end.

And the worst part, the fucking worst part, was it was right.



Chapter Two: What It Feels Like

You want to know what it's like? Being a god for a hundred thousand years and then hearing that?

It's like being told you can finally sit down.

That's all. That's the whole feeling. You know when you've been standing all day, on concrete, in bad shoes, and somebody pulls out a chair? That. Every muscle going, oh. Oh, thank god. Except the chair is the end of you, and the day was a hundred thousand years, and the shoes were every war, every lover, every world you watched burn, every name you outlived.

You want to end, the song sang. You want to end.

Yeah, kid. Yeah. I fucking do.

I sat there on the highway with the cat-dog licking my hand and I could feel them going. All over the multiverse. The other old ones. The ones Himo didn't get in the first war, the ones who learned to resist, the ones who hid. I could feel them like candles in a big dark room, and I could feel them going out. One. Then another. Then ten.

A god of rivers, who I used to go dancing with, three dimensions over. Gone. She went smiling.

Conquest. Took off her crown. I felt it hit the ground.

A god of doorways who made the best tea in the first war. Wait, no, he died ages ago. Himo got him first. Eleven seconds. She wrote down the time.

Fuck. Okay. I'm getting confused. That's the song. It makes everything soft.

I got up.



Chapter Three: Walking

I walked. That's what I do when I'm upset. I walk and I swear.

I walked down through the Abyss, through the rubble from both wars, through roads stacked on buildings and traffic signs stuck in the dirt like flags and a field of bathtubs, which, I'm sorry, what the fuck, who did the bathtubs. Down. Toward the Nursery. Because that's where she'd be. That's where she always is.

And the song walked with me.

Every step, it got sweeter. Every step, I got more tired. I'd look at a pile of rubble and think, that's a good place to lie down. I'd look at a puddle and think, that's a nice place to end. The cat-dog followed me for a while and then it lay down in the road and closed its eyes and went still, smiling, and I didn't stop for it, because if I stopped I wouldn't start again.

"Fuck you," I told the song. "Fuck you, fuck you, fuck you."

It doesn't care. It's not listening to you. It just wants you to listen to it.

That's her kid, the one singing. The blue one. Twenty-two. I met her once in the first war, when she was a jar on a shelf, humming. I told Himo that thing was going to be trouble. I told her, put it in the ocean or put it out of its misery, but don't you dare put it on a shelf and leave it there and never turn around. You make a thing that's nothing but wanting, and you don't give it a single fucking thing to want except you? You're building a bomb.

She put it on the shelf.

She never turns around. That's her whole thing.



Chapter Four: Eri

On the way down I passed a demon crawling out of a drainage pipe.

One eye. Scarred to hell. Mind demon. Himo's ninth. I knew her too. Eri. She was dragging herself on her elbows through the rubble with her legs all fucked up, like something had held her by them very hard and then let go. She looked up at me with her one eye and it was blue at the edge, just a little, and getting less blue as I watched, like she was fighting it out.

"Chimera," she said. "Chimera. She's in the tank. The kid. Twenty-two. Himo put her in the tank. You have to get it open. You can still stop it. If you break the glass, the song goes out of the throat and back into her, it's just one girl again, it's just one hungry girl, we can deal with that, we can—"

"Where's Himo?"

Eri stopped. "What?"

"Where is she?"

"Chimera, the TANK. Every god is—"

"I know what every god is doing," I said. "I can feel them going, kid. I'm one of them. Where is she?"

Eri looked at me for a long time. Mind demon. She was in my head. I let her. Nothing in there she hadn't seen before. Nothing anybody hadn't.

"Oh," she said quietly. "Oh, you idiot."

"Yeah."

"She walked out. Out the back. Toward the old highway. She left. She's not even watching it. She never fucking watches."

"Thanks."

I stepped over her.

"CHIMERA!" she screamed after me. "CHIMERA, THE TANK!"

I didn't go to the tank.



Chapter Five: Always Her

Okay. Story time. You want to know why I'm like this?

We were young once. Gods are young once. Not long. Ten thousand years, maybe. A blink. Before the great hall, before the war. I was the patchwork goddess and she was the goddess of making, and we were supposed to be rivals, because making and making-do are supposed to hate each other. She made perfect things. I made things out of what was left.

And she came to my workshop one day to sneer at it, and saw a thing I'd made, a little bird out of a fish and a moth and a scrap of a dead star, and it was ugly, it was so ugly, and it sang. Badly. Off-key.

And she laughed.

Himo laughed. I've heard it four times in a hundred thousand years. That was the first.

"It's terrible," she said. "Why does it sing?"

"Because it wants to," I said. "Things want to. That's the whole trick. You make them perfect, they don't want anything. You make them out of scraps, they want everything."

She looked at me like a problem on a whiteboard.

And then she looked at me like something else.

We were together for a long time after that. Badly. The way gods are. Too long and too much. I loved her and she loved me and she never once said it, because she was writing do not get attached on the first page of every notebook even back then, and I was writing her name on the first page of mine.

Then she watched a god fold a solar system in half like a napkin, and she went home, and she made corruption.

And I've been chasing her ever since.



Chapter Six: The Old Highway

She was sitting on the guardrail.

The old highway at the edge of the Abyss, the one that goes up, the one that used to lead somewhere before both wars knocked it sideways. She was sitting on the guardrail with her white coat on and her notebook in her lap and a hole in her chest stitched with black thread. Looking out at nothing. Gray ruins forever.

She didn't turn around when I came up behind her.

"Chimera," she said.

"You stupid fucking bitch," I said.

"Yes."

"You put your kid in a tank."

"Yes."

"And you walked away. While she's singing. While she's ending everything. You didn't even stay to watch."

"I've seen enough of it."

I sat down on the guardrail next to her. It creaked. We're both heavy, in the ways that count.

The song was so loud out here. Everything humming. The ruins humming. The sky humming. Somewhere, a god of mountains went out, and the mountains didn't notice.

"How long?" I asked.

"For the gods? A week. Maybe two. There aren't many left." She opened the notebook. Numbers. Neat columns. Names, with lines through them. "After that, she'll keep going. I designed her that way. She's wanting. She can't stop."

"And the people? The ones with no stories? The mortals?"

Himo was quiet.

"She'll eat them too," she said. "Eventually. They won't mind. She leaves them happy."

"You said you were doing this for them. The mortals. The little ones. That's what you said in the great hall. So the gods would stop hurting them."

"I did," Himo said. "I was."

"So that's a lie now?"

"No." She closed the notebook. "It's just math that didn't work."



Chapter Seven: The Tank

Here's the thing. Eri was right.

I could go back. I could walk back down into the Nursery, past all the tanks, past the ushers, to the big one, the throat. And I could put my fist through it. I'm a goddess of scraps and scars. I'm the best in any universe at breaking a perfect thing. It would take one punch. The glass would go, and the fluid would go, and the song would go back into one hungry girl in a crown, and she'd be just a monster again instead of the voice of everything. And the gods that were left would get to stay. And the mortals would get to stay. And she'd be a problem, sure, she'd be a terrible problem, but she'd be a problem the size of a high school instead of a problem the size of existence.

I knew that. I knew it sitting on the guardrail. I could feel the tank behind me like a stone in my back.

And I could feel the song. Telling me I wanted to end.

And I could feel Himo's shoulder against mine.

She hadn't moved away. That's the first time in a hundred thousand years she didn't move away.



Chapter Eight: Asking

"Himo."

"Yes."

"Turn around."

She didn't.

"Himo. Look at me. Just once. I'm going. You know I'm going. The song's in me. It's good. It's so good, kid. I've been tired for so long. I want to sit down. I want to finally fucking sit down. And I'm going to, soon. In an hour. A day. And I want you to look at me first. One time. The way you looked at that ugly bird."

She looked at the ruins.

"If I look at you," she said, "I'll want you to stay."

"Yeah."

"And if I want you to stay, I'll have to go back and break the tank."

"Yeah."

"And then all of it was for nothing. The war. The Nursery. The prophecy. The girl with the blonde hair. Her parents. Nine. Twenty-two. All of it. For nothing. Because I got attached."

"Yeah," I said. "That's what it would mean."

She sat there for a long time.

And I sat there with her, and I didn't get up, and I didn't go back to the tank, and I knew exactly what I was doing.

I was choosing.

Not the gods. Not the mortals. Not Eri crawling through the rubble on her elbows screaming my name. Not the blue girl in the tank, singing herself into nothing for a mother who walked away. Not the ones who'll get eaten next, the ones with no stories, the little ones she said it was all for. Not the world.

Her.

I was choosing to sit on a guardrail next to the woman who did all of it, and let it happen, if it meant she'd stay next to me until I went.

That's the worst thing I've ever done, and I set a moon on fire.



Chapter Nine: Last Rites

She turned around.

Not all the way. Just her head. She turned and she looked at me. And her face did a thing I've only seen it do four times. Five, now.

She didn't laugh. It wasn't that. But it was close. Her eyes went wet, and she didn't stop them, and she reached over with one cold hand and put it on my scarred one on the guardrail.

"It's terrible," she said. "Why does it sing?"

And I laughed, and I cried, and I told her, "Because it wants to, you stupid bitch. Because it wants to."

The song got louder. I let it. I could feel myself going soft at the edges. Like a scar finally fading after a hundred thousand years. Like a patchwork coming apart at the seams, one stitch at a time, gently. It didn't hurt. It was so good. It was the best feeling I've ever had.

Down in the Nursery, the tank kept singing. Nobody broke it.

Out in the rubble, Eri kept screaming. Nobody came.

Somewhere far away, in other worlds, other gods went out. Candles on a cake.

And Himo held my hand.

She opened her notebook with her other hand. The first page. The brown one. LOG 0. Do not get attached. And under it, where she'd written I didn't, back when the girl with the blonde hair put a knife in her, she picked up her pen and crossed it out.

And she wrote, in handwriting I'd know anywhere, small and neat and humming:

Note: I did. Every time.

"There," she said.

"There," I said.

I'm going now. It's soft. It's so soft. I can feel my scars letting go, one by one, every crooked thing I ever was, unstitching. I can't see the ruins anymore. Just the gray, and her hand, and a little ugly bird somewhere far off, singing off-key.

She's still holding on.

She's still looking.

I never thought I'd get her to turn around. Took the end of everything.

Worth it.

Fuck, kid. It was worth it.
