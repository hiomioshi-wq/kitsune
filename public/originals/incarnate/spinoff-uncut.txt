Chapter One: Okay So, Road Trip

Chapter Three: The Jar

Chapter Fourteen: Switzerland, 1891

I think about Adelheid sometimes. I think about her when I'm scared.

It's neat and cold and humming like a refrigerator. It's Plan C, all the steps, all the numbers. It's: Twenty-two goes in. Twenty-two sings. Twenty-two dissolves into the voice. Signal propagates. Gods end. Note: do not get attached.

And under that. Way under. The way Noor's brother was under everything.

A jar on a shelf. A little blue hum. Coming through the glass every day. And Mom, at her lab bench, with her back to it. Every day. Not turning around.

Because if she turned around she'd have wanted to keep it.

Eri lets go. The bridge snaps.

"She loved you," Eri says. "She loved all of us. She threw us away anyway. That's what she does. She'll do it again. Help me. Together we can end her. Right now. You and me. She can't hear your song, she doesn't want anything but the plan, but she can hear me, and if you hold her, I can go in and—"

"Nine," Mom says, very calmly, not turning around. "Twenty-two can't hold anyone. She only knows how to eat."

And I look at Mom's back. Her white coat. Her cold hands on the glass.

She knows me so well.



Chapter Thirty: Strategic Decision, Part Two

So here's the situation, babe.

Option one: Team up with Eri. End Mom. Walk out of the Nursery. Go back to the sea. Go back to schools. Do my thing forever. Free. Main character of whatever school I'm at.

Option two: Do what Mom says.

And here's what I know, because I'm not dumb, I'm literally forty thousand years old. Eri's right. If I go in that tank, I'm gone. I'll be the song. But I won't be me. Nobody's going to be looking at me. They're going to be looking at the voice.

And also: Mom never turned around. She heard me humming through the glass every single day for decades and she never once turned around.

And also: she just did.

Twice. Today. She looked at me twice. She said I got big. She said she watched.

I turn to Eri.

"You're so right, bestie," I say, sweet, and Eri relaxes, just a little, just for a second, because even mind demons want something, even mind demons want help.

And I sing at her.

Not at her mind. Her mind's armored, she's a mind demon, it's like singing at a bank vault. At her want. Her want is easy. It's so loud. It's been loud for ten thousand years. She wants Mom to be sorry. She wants Mom to turn around and look at her and say I'm sorry, Nine. That's all. That's her whole thing. She's been carrying it so long it's worn grooves in her.

I don't eat it. I turn it up.

Eri's one eye goes blue at the edge.

"Mom's going to say sorry to you," I tell her. "If you just stand still. If you just wait. She's going to turn around and say she's sorry."

"She's going to say she's sorry," Eri whispers.

"Stand very still."

She stands very still.

"Nursery," says Mom, not turning around. "Take Nine."

The six gray ushers come in from the hall and take her by the arms. She doesn't fight. She's standing very still. She's waiting for sorry. Her face looks so young.

They carry her out.

I'll tell you something since nobody's here. She got away, later. Eri always gets away. She's in another story somewhere, doing her thing, hating Mom. That's not my problem. That's lore.

When she's gone, Mom finally turns around.

She looks at me. Third time.

"Good," she says.

And I'm going to be honest. That one word went into me like the first technician did. Back in the lab. My first meal. It made me want to cry.



Chapter Thirty-One: Say It

"Okay," I say. "I'll do it."

Mom nods. She's already moving. Opening valves. Turning dials. The tank hums. The throat inside it twitches.

"One condition," I say.

She pauses with her hand on a lever.

"Say it."

"Say what?"

"You know what." I'm doing a voice I've never done. Not the cute one. Not the drain one. Something smaller. "Say it like you mean it."

She looks at me for a long time.

Here's the thing about my power. I can hear what people want. I can't hear if they're lying. I've never needed to. Everybody tells me the truth eventually, or what I want them to say, and those are the same thing.

I can hear what Mom wants right now. Clear as a bell. Cold as ice.

She wants me in the tank.

That's all.

"I love you, Twenty-two," she says.

And I can't tell. I can't tell if it's true. I'll never be able to tell. That's the joke. Forty thousand years of making everyone say exactly what I want, and the one time it matters, I have no idea.

I decide it's true.

That's the decision. Some people would say it's the wrong one. Eri, for example. Siren. Raven. Mark, probably, if Mark were anything other than dirt in Kansas right now. They'd say she's using me. They'd say it's the oldest trick there is. They'd say I'm doing to myself exactly what I did to every single person in this book.

I don't care.

"Thank you," I say.

And I climb up the ladder on the side of the tank.



Chapter Thirty-Two: The Whole Song

The fluid's warm. Not cold like the Nursery tanks. Warm like the sea at the Jersey Shore when I first walked in. Like my childhood bedroom.

The throat takes me in. It doesn't hurt. It's like sinking into a hot tub. Like a hug. I feel my edges go. My fingers. My face. All four hundred faces I've ever worn. The homecoming crown floats off my head and drifts up through the fluid and bumps against the lid of the tank.

And then I'm not a girl anymore.

I'm a voice.

And the voice opens.

Babe. Babe. You have no idea.

I'm everywhere. I'm in every story. I'm in every dimension that ever got told. I can feel all of them at once, like a sky full of radios. And in every one of them, there's something old and bored and immortal sitting on a throne, and every one of them, every single one, is suddenly listening.

To me.

A god of fire on a burning mountain stops what she's doing and turns her head.

A Horseman with a crown, Conquest, on a white horse, in a world she was about to make kneel, pulls on the reins.

Famine, at the bottom of a red canyon, holding the restored world together, looks up.

A foul-mouthed old god named Chimera, wearing a body made of scars, sitting on a broken highway, stops mid-sentence and goes, "Oh, fuck. Oh, Himo, you didn't."

And all of them. All of them. Looking at me.

Every eye in creation.

I've never been so seen.

And I sing the song Mom wrote for me. You want to end. You want to end. You want to end. And they listen. And they want it. They want it so much. It comes off them like steam off a planet. Ten thousand gods, finally, after forever, wanting something new. Wanting to stop.

And I eat it.

Oh my god.

Oh my god it's the best thing I have ever tasted.

Forget Adelheid. Forget Noor. Forget the boats. This is the wanting of things that have lived forever, finally wanting to die, and it tastes like every star going out at once. It tastes like the end of the longest night. It tastes like Mom's cold hands.

The god of fire puts down her fire.

Conquest takes off her crown.

The first one goes out. Then the next. Like candles on a cake.



Chapter Thirty-Three: Main Character Of Everything

I can still see the Nursery. A little. From inside the tank. Through the glass, the way I used to see the lab from my jar.

Mom's standing there. She's writing in her notebook. Recording the numbers. Gods ending, one by one, by the dozen, by the hundred. Her plan working. Finally. After everything.

She doesn't look up at the tank.

She's got her back to it. Like before. Like the jar.

And I can hear her think it, as she writes. Neat and cold and humming.

Note: do not get attached.

And she closes the notebook. And she puts on her coat. And she walks out of the vault door, past the tanks, past the ushers, into the dark, without turning around.

Leaving me singing.

I could be sad about that. Some people would be. Some people would finally, right here at the end of the book, after all of it, feel something. Some growth. A lesson. A tear.

Not me, babe. I told you on page one. No character growth. Don't even ask.

Because here's the thing she doesn't get. Here's the thing nobody ever gets about me.

She thinks she's using me. She thinks I'm a loudspeaker for her plan. And I am. Sure. But I'm also hungry. I'm always hungry. And right now, for the first time in forty thousand years, every single thing in every single universe is looking at me.

And when the gods are gone, babe, I'm not going to stop.

There's so much else that wants things. People. Planets. Stars. Demons. Nursery babies, someday, maybe, if I sing long enough. Mom.

Every story that ever got told.

I've got time. I'm the voice of everything now. I'm going to eat it all. Slowly. With technique. Leaving them happy. Always leave them happy. That's one of my rules. I still have rules. I'm basically a lawyer.

The crown's still floating at the top of the tank. Bumping against the lid. Glinting in the green light.

I hum. It goes out through every world at once. Every radio. Every phone. Every wind. Every sea.

In a basement in Pittsburgh, a girl named Noor hums along while she makes tea and doesn't know why.

On a Main Street in Illinois, two thousand people hum along on a parade route.

In the ruins, a woman with a borrowed face hums along, getting it wrong.

In a Motel 6, in a Walmart, in a gym, in a barn, in a cafeteria with a pudding stain that never came out.

Everyone.

Hi, everyone.

It's me.

You're welcome.
