The hostile takeover began, as all hostile takeovers at Dreadco began, with the tower changing color.

The tower had a palette, registered, patented, and defended: the black of the burnt match, the grey of the suits, the purple of the Chairman's whole arrangement. On the Monday of the merger, the tower went white.

Not painted. Not lit. White — the white of the page before the ink, the white of the jar before the fear, the white of the room at the top of the fourth division's org box on the morning the ink on that box had finally, after a hundred and fifty-one years of changing its name daily, held still. The whole building stood in the Gloam skyline like a tooth, and the city, which had grown up around the old understanding, looked at it and felt the one thing the city was never supposed to feel about the tower, which was nothing, and the nothing spread.

By nine, the lobby plate was white. By ten, the jars. By eleven — and this was the hour the alarms that had never once been used, because the firm's alarms were a subscription product and the firm was not a subscriber — went off in every corridor at once, soundlessly, which is the most expensive kind of alarm, and Undermanager Quill stood at the center of the Catch Division with the whistle in her hand.

"The firm is being acquired," she said, and her voice, for the first time in eleven years, was holding up nothing. "The Chairman has been sent to his office. The office has been sent to the fourth floor. The fourth division has been sent to the census, and the census — " and here she read from the only document the acquisition had filed, which had arrived in the tower's own charter ink, on the tower's own vellum, through the tower's own door that was not on the floor plan — " the census has been retired. Effective immediately. All jars. All files. All kept."

There was no name on the document. There was a shape where the name went, and everyone in the lobby who looked at the shape — and they all looked, and they all did it again, and again, and the department's oldest fears stopped drinking their coffee entirely — recognized it, because it was the oldest thing any of them had ever contained: the shape of a fear with nothing inside it.

The acquirer was the fear of fear.

Not of any particular fear. Of the whole arrangement. The one at the bottom of the grass of every mind in Gloam, under the door-fears and the smoke-fears and the midnight-phone fears — the fear that lay under the industry itself, older than the tower, older than the charter, older than the plate, and that had watched, for a hundred and fifty-one years, from below every census and every jar and every open door, the one fear that had never once been caught, because there is no jar with a mouth that wide, and had finally, this white Monday, elected to do what every senior fear eventually does when the industry will not staff it.

It had gone into management.

The tower fell in order of seniority, floor by floor, the way the firm itself had taught every fear in Gloam to fall. The Takers went first — the phone-fear, the smoke-fear, the fear of other people's dogs — acquiring themselves in the corridors, going white, going shape, filing themselves under their own names, and being, in the acquisition's single act of mercy, released. The Catch Division fought on the eleventh floor, and was caught, which is the industry's whole joke. Mr. Lament held the stairs for nine minutes, forgotten-fear against the bottom fear, and was forgotten in ten, which even he, at the end, in the white, was heard to concede was fair.

By noon, the whole tower was white except one room, and the one room was on the ninth floor, behind a door that stood open, and it was full of jars that would not go white, because everything in the census was already afraid of exactly one thing, the only thing, and had spent its whole retirement being fed the counter to it, and the counter to the fear of fear is not bravery, which is a rumor, and not hope, which is a subscription product —

the counter to the fear of fear is a door, held open, on purpose, by something that is not afraid to stand in it.

"vetch," said the whole tower, in the white, through every window, with the sound of the fear of fear trying the name on, and finding that the name, alone, of the lowest-tier employee on the org chart, the zero, the unmeasurable, the open door policy's whole reason, was the one word in the building that the acquisition could not make blank. "come up."

Colly Vetch went up.

He went up through the white tower, floor by white floor, past the desks and the plates and the arrangements, and the fear of fear watched him come the whole way, and it watched with something that had not been in the grass of any mind since the first dark hallway: attention. And Draft went with him, of course — tenant, colleague, the fear of open doors at night, in the body of the boy, in the best coat, and where the two of them passed, the white went backward, corridor by corridor, like a tide going out, not because a boy and a doorway are stronger than the bottom of everything, but because the fear of fear has one structural weakness, which its own acquirers never study because the weakness is in the studying:

you cannot be afraid of the fear of fear while you are busy being somebody's neighbor.

At the top, in the white of the office at the top of the tower, where the diagrams of The Wheel had gone blank and the purple had gone page-colored, the acquirer arranged itself into the best weather it had, which was the weather of every empty room every reader of this memo has ever stood alone in at the bottom of the night, and it said, in the voice of a jar with nothing in it:

"i am the fear of fear. i am the bottom of you. i am what the whole industry is FOR, and the industry caught everything and staffed everything and sold everything and measured everything and it never once caught ME, because i am the reason the jars have mouths. and now i have acquired the firm, and the firm is mine, and the plate is blank, and the census is retired, and there is no department left, no division, no charter, no wheel, no chairman — and you, colly vetch, zero, vessel, doorway, are the last unacquired thing in the building, and you are standing in my open door. so." The white held its weather. "be afraid of me now, and it is all mine, and i will finally, FINALLY, have somewhere to be."

And Colly — twenty-two, broke, cheerful, the whole tower white behind him and the whole industry's bottom fact in front of him, in the one doorway left in Gloam, with the grass of his mind lying flat and his tenant lying flatter, in the best coat, with the badge of cold — did what the entire industry, in a hundred and fifty-one years of catching, staffing, selling, measuring, and billing, had never once thought to do with a senior fear that had acquired everything it wanted and found itself, at the top of the tower, at noon, still empty.

He offered it a job.

"Right," said Colly Vetch, to the fear of fear, in the white, delighted, in the tone of a man who has been reading the firm's whole charter wrong all his life and has finally found the sentence it was written around. "You've got it backwards, love. You're the one fear that's never had a district, and the reason you're empty is you've never had anybody to be FOR. That's not a takeover, that's a vacancy." And he took off the badge of cold, the badge the whole tower knew, the badge of the open door policy, and he pinned it on the white, at exactly the height where a heart would file itself, if a heart were filing for a position. "Catch Division's hiring. Entry level. You'll report to the ninth floor, on Tuesdays, to the census, and I'll tell you now what nobody ever told me on my first day, which is what the job actually is."

The white held still.

"...what," said the fear of fear, from the bottom of everything, in the voice of the first question ever asked in the first dark hallway, "is the job."

"You stand in the doors," said Colly Vetch, "so the things behind them know which house is ours."

The tower came back floor by floor, the way morning does. The purple returned to the office at the top, where the Chairman's arrangement was found at his desk, unacquired, having spent the whole merger, by his own account, "observing the wheel turn," which was the Chairman's whole religion, and which he later filed, on vellum, in the ink that gets up afterward, as the only statement of policy he ever signed that generated nothing:

SOME FEARS CANNOT BE CAUGHT. SOME FEARS NEED A DOORWAY MORE THAN A DOOR. THE FIRM HEREBY ESTABLISHES THE OPEN DOOR DIVISION, PERMANENTLY, UNMEASURED, AT A LOSS, ON PURPOSE.

Undermanager Quill drew the new org chart herself, by charter, on vellum, and at the bottom of it, below the divisions, below the census, below the keeping, in her own handwriting, at last identified after all these years because she signed it, for the first time, with the whistle finally blown — one short blast, at the dedication, which the assembled staff of the firm felt, individually, in the grass of their minds, as the sound of a door somewhere opening — she drew the tower's newest box, and wrote inside it the name of the entry-level employee who had acquired the acquirer by offering it the only thing the industry had never put in a jar:

FEAR OF FEAR — OPEN DOOR DIVISION, FLOOR NINE — MANAGER: VETCH, COLLY.

OUTPUT: UNMEASURABLE.

RETAINED. ALL PARTIES. PERMANENTLY.

And on his rounds that night, through the district that laughs, door by open door, in the cold, in the draft, Colly Vetch walked with the bottom of the industry beside him in a new hire's coat, learning the rounds, and the whole of Gloam slept the sleep of a city with a fire station, which is the firm's whole charter, at last, translated, and the doors of number eleven stood open at midnight, both of them, on purpose, the way the town is proud of, and it is warm in there, and mind the step, and come in, love.

come in.
