Dreadco measured everything. That was the firm's whole theology, boiled down to a single stained-glass window in the Chairman's private chapel of management theory: a thing that can be measured can be billed, and a thing that can be billed can be forgiven, and the Chairman had built an empire on the difference between the two.

Colly's first performance review fell due at the end of his first quarter, and it fell, as all reviews did, on the last Friday, in the Evaluation Room, which was the only room in the tower with windows, because the firm had learned by 1911 that staff performed measurably better under observation, and had then learned, by 1913, that the observation itself was worth more than the performance, and had adjusted the windows accordingly.

The metrics of the Catch Division were simple, ancient, and posted in the lobby on a brass plate the size of a door, where the Chairman's grandfather had bolted it, and where it had outlived four dynasties of management theory, a war, and a committee:

PRODUCTIVITY: SCREAMS PER ANNUM, PER EMPLOYEE, BY DISTRICT.

Colly Vetch's number, at the end of his first quarter, was zero.

Not low. Zero. It was, Undermanager Quill said, at the meeting where the number was unveiled, a number she had not seen in the history of the brass plate, and she had been reading the brass plate for eleven years, and there were people buried in the tower's foundations with higher numbers.

"The boy produces no fear," said Mr. Lament, from the end of the table, with the satisfaction of a rival presenting the kill to the pack. "None. His district is the safest three streets in Gloam. I have had him followed — professionally followed, at my own expense, out of concern for the firm — and I can report that on Tuesday of last week he was observed helping an old woman across a road that has been a reliable earner for this department since 1888."

"He walks the streets at night," said the fear of phone calls after midnight, who was sitting in the corner on the phone, to the fear of the smell of smoke, who was not listening, which for both of them was the job. "He walks them OPENLY. The door-fear walks around LOOSENED, like a badge. The whole arrangement is an embarrassment to the industry."

"His fear is not underperformed," said Quill, sharply, reading the file. "The fear is excellent. Six assessments, six streets closed, three evictions of squatter fears, one district-wide weather event of cold. Draft is the most productive junior intake on the plate. The problem is the vessel. The fear does the work, and then the boy undoes it, at street level, personally, with — " she consulted the file, and her voice, for the first time in eleven years, showed the ceiling it had been holding up — " — with what the assessors describe as CHATTING."

There it was. The report's most damning paragraph, filed by three different professional assessors who had each gone out into Colly's district to watch the anomaly and had each come back with the same account, in the same tone of a naturalist watching a new species cooperate with its own extinction:

The vessel walks his rounds. The fear opens. The streets go cold, and the doors go dark, and the district produces, per the plate. And then the vessel — the vessel comes back along the same street, in the morning, and it talks to the people, and it says things like "cold last night, wasn't it" and "mind the draft in number eleven, I'll see to it" and the people — the crop, the stock, the per-annum — the people laugh. The people LIKE him. The tenants of number eleven have started leaving the door open on PURPOSE, for the company. We have measured. The fear of open doors at night is running, in its home district, at the highest output in its recorded history — and the district's scream rate is zero, because the entire district has stopped being afraid of it, and started being PROUD of it, in the way that a town is proud of a fire station.

The evaluation room was quiet, in the way rooms go quiet around a number nobody has a box for.

It was at this point that the Chairman's interest, which had been circulating the meeting in the form of a memo that everyone had read and nobody had acknowledged, became a presence, which at Dreadco meant that the lights in the Evaluation Room changed to the Chairman's private color, a purple that had been patented, and that a door at the back of the room — a door that was not on the floor plan, and was, the whole firm knew, the Chairman's whole entire method — swung open by itself.

"send him to me," said the door, in the Chairman's voice, which was the sound of a thunderclap that had gone to business school. "monday. nine sharp. alone."

The department filed out in order of seniority, which put Mr. Lament first and Colly last, and Colly stopped at the door, and looked back at the room, and at Undermanager Quill, who had not moved, and who was looking at the brass-plate number, and at the file, and had taken out her pen.

"What," said Colly, "should I tell him?"

Quill looked up, and the ceiling, for one moment, was held by something other than her shoulders, and it came down to the only question she had ever asked the firm, in eleven years, and she asked it now, quietly, over the pen:

"Tell him the truth, Vetch. It's the one thing we've never sold."

Monday came, at nine, and the door at the back of the Evaluation Room, and behind the door the office at the top of the tower, all purple patent and diagrams of The Wheel, and the Chairman, who was not, in any account that survived him, a fear at all, or a man, or a department, but was, by every witness's best description, an ARRANGEMENT — an arrangement of coat and smile and the general weather — sitting behind a desk with Colly's whole file on it, and Colly's whole number on the brass plate beside it, which had been unscrewed from the lobby and carried up forty flights by two porters who had never spoken of it since.

ZERO, said the plate, to the Chairman's office, in the Chairman's presence, in the Chairman's own metal.

"zero," said the Chairman, tasting it, and the coat arranged itself around a cup of something black, and the smile, which had been to business school too, attended. "zero screams. a fear at record output. a district PROUD of its dread. do you know, vetch, what my grandfather would have done with you?"

Colly, standing alone in the purple, with the inside of his head gone very still and flat and quiet, the grass of him lying down, said that he did not.

"he would have put you in a jar," said the Chairman, "and sold the jar as a paperweight."

The office held its weather.

"but my grandfather," said the Chairman, "built a firm that measures everything, and i have inherited the measurement, and here is mine: in one hundred and fifty-one years, this tower's fears have lost this city to nothing. dreads rise, dreads fall, we catch them, we staff them, we sell them back. and the city has never once, in a century and a half, been BRAVE at us. you are the first. i have read your file three times and i cannot find the product. so." The cup went down. The smile attended itself. "tell me what you are for, colly vetch, or be a paperweight."

And Colly — twenty-two, broke, cheerful, standing in the purple at the top of the only career in Gloam that had ever wanted him, with the oldest fact of the industry lying flat in the grass of him, going still — said the true thing, the one that was not for sale, the one he had been carrying since the elevator on his first day, when a bored fear in a jar had decided to keep him:

"I'm for the doors, sir," he said. "all the open ones. I don't close them. I stand in them."

The Chairman's office considered its first brave employee for a long, patented moment.

"keep doing that," said the Chairman, at last, and the purple warmed half a degree, which was, from the top of that tower, the whole of the applause. "and vetch — " the door at the back was already opening, the audience over, the file already being carried down forty flights to a filing cabinet that had a drawer, everyone later swore, prepared in advance — " stand where i can see you. i am keeping the plate."

The plate, minus its zero, went back to the lobby that afternoon.

Undermanager Quill, at her desk, filed the outcome under the only heading she had ever trusted, and screwed the pen together, and the Catch Division went back to the streets, and on the brass plate, in the place of honor, where the district numbers ran, one entry appeared in Quill's handwriting, which the department read on their way in the next morning, and which nobody, in the entire history of the firm, ever requested be amended:

VETCH, COLLY — OPEN DOORS, NIGHT — OUTPUT: UNMEASURABLE. RETAIN.
