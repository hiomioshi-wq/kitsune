There was a day on the Dreadco calendar, marked in the lobby, on the brass plate, and in the private purple of the Chairman's own office, that no employee at any level had ever been able to get abolished, and it came, this year, as it came every year, on the second Friday of the dark quarter, and it was called:

TAKE YOUR HUMAN TO WORK DAY.

The origin was a charter clause, written in 1871 by the Chairman's grandfather in the margins of the founding document, in the ink that gets up afterward, and the clause read: THE STAFF SHALL BE PERMITTED, ANNUALLY, TO PRESENT THEIR ORIGINS TO THE FIRM, LEST THE FIRM FORGET WHAT IT IS FOR, which every subsequent administration had interpreted in the manner most convenient to itself, and which had settled, by long attrition, into the current observance: every fear on the payroll, once a year, brought in the human it had originally come from — its first landlord, its first believer, the person who had, by being afraid at the right moment in the right hallway, started the fear's whole career — and the human was fed, watered, shown the tower, and returned by midnight, and the practice was loved by exactly one faction of the firm, which was the fears, and tolerated by the rest, and had never once, in a hundred and fifty years, gone according to any plan ever filed for it.

Colly Vetch's human was his grandmother, Talla Vetch, and the problem with Colly's human, from the firm's point of view, was that she was not afraid of anything, and the problem with that was that it meant Draft had, technically, no origin on file, and had never had one since the intake on the eleventh floor, which made Colly, on the org chart of the observance, the only vessel in the tower registered to a fear that had made itself.

"you are taking the woman who raised you," said Draft, on the morning of, in the hallway of Colly's head, where the tenant had been pacing since dawn. "to work. at the firm. where we keep the census, and the jars, and the fourth division, and the thing in the basement that i now report to on tuesdays. this is the worst plan of a career that has included standing in a doorway for the chairman."

"She asked," said Colly, in the mirror, putting on the good coat, the one with the badge of cold, the one the whole district knew. "She's been asking since the review. She wants to meet you."

The tower at ten o'clock on Take Your Human to Work Day was, and no other sentence will do it, a workplace. The lobby had sandwiches, by the ton, by Basket, who had outdone a career of outdoing. The fears had come in their best weather — the fear of phone calls after midnight had put on a ringtone — and the humans, several hundred of them, the tired, the brave, the elderly, the very young, stood around the lobby in their coats looking up at the brass plate, and the plate, on this one day, was always covered with a cloth, by tradition, by charter, because the plate could not be seen by the crop, and this was the whole observance, and the one thing the day was actually for, in the grandfather's ink: LEST THE FIRM FORGET WHAT IT IS FOR.

The humans were shown the catch floor, under escort. They were shown the jars, which hummed politely. They were introduced, one by one, department by department, to the fears they had personally founded, and this, every year, was the whole terrible heart of the day: the fear of other people's dogs meeting, over sandwiches, the man whose neighbor's dog it had been; the fear of the smell of smoke meeting the old cook whose stove it was born over; a hundred small, unbillable reunions, and the fear standing there, in its best weather, in front of the only person in Gloam who had ever really known it, and the human looking at it, most years, with the one thing the firm could not generate at any price, which was recognition.

Talla Vetch, eighty-one, of the third floor of number eleven, was shown everything, and thanked everyone, and was, by one o'clock, the most popular human in the lobby, on the strength of a competence the firm had no metric for and the fears had no defense against, which was that she talked to everyone the way her grandson talked to a street: openly, in the morning, on purpose.

At two she met her grandson's tenant.

The meeting was held, at Draft's insistence, which was the first time the tower had heard the fear insist on anything, in the Evaluation Room, under the windows, in the presence of Undermanager Quill, who had come with the clipboard strapped and the whistle unblown, and the department, which had gathered in the corridor outside on the grounds that the windows were observation and observation was the job.

The fear of open doors at night, in the body of the boy it kept, stood up from the desk, and faced an eighty-one-year-old woman in a good coat, and there was nowhere in a hundred and fifty-one years of the tower's paperwork a form for what the fear was about to find out, and the fear knew it, and stood there anyway.

"you are the grandmother," said Draft, out of Colly, in a voice like a hallway, but softened, at the edges, at the doorframe. "you raised the boy from four. there is a file. i have read it. you are the reason he is — whatever he is. and so i have a question, and the firm has no form for it, and i am asking it anyway." The voice went through the room like cold under a door. "he says the doors are OPEN, on his rounds, all of them, all night, in the cold, and he stands in them. and the district laughs, and the plate says zero, and i have been in a jar, and i have been bored, and i have been billed, and this is the best job a fear ever had, and it is all, all of it, running the wrong direction through this entire industry. so tell me." The windows held their observation. The corridor held its breath. "what IS he for? what did you make him for? because whatever it is, i am it now too, and i would like to know what i am."

Talla Vetch looked at the doorway standing politely in her grandson's body, and the whole Catch Division listened through the wall, and she did what Vetches do, which is answer a question about a door honestly.

"His mother," she said, "was afraid of everything. Every night of her life, every door in every hallway, and she died of it — not of the fear, love, but of never once having stood still long enough to let anything come through. And I raised him after, and I made a decision about him on the day he was four years old and I found him standing in the front doorway at midnight, in the draft, with the whole dark outside, because he had heard something in the hall and instead of shutting the door he had OPENED it further, to see, and he was not afraid, and I could have — " and here the whole corridor, and the whole Evaluation Room, and the weather in the windows, attended — " I could have taught him to be. It is the first thing every parent teaches. It is the first thing I taught his mother. And I decided, that night, in the doorway, that this one I would teach the other thing."

She looked at the fear her grandson had become, and the recognition, the unbillable thing, the thing the whole day was chartered for, moved through the Evaluation Room like the draft moving through number eleven, and the fear of open doors at night, senior tenant, record output, employee of the open door policy, stood still and let it come through.

"i was the fear in a hallway like that," it said, quietly, to the woman who had not taught it to her boy. "for a hundred and fifty years. everyone shut. everyone, always, shut."

"I know," said Talla Vetch. "That's why we leave it open now, love. So the ones like you know which house is ours."

The observance concluded at midnight, per charter, per tradition, per the cloth coming off the brass plate at the stroke of it, and the plate, that year, showed what it always showed — screams per annum, by district, by employee — with one entry at the bottom in the unidentified ink, and the humans went home through the revolving doors into the dark of Gloam, escorted, fed, recognized, and the firm stood around its lobby afterwards, as it did every year, holding the annual inventory of what it was for, and finding it, this year, and filing it.

Draft filed the interview itself, personally, in the census, under its own heading, in the tower's one drawer that fed on purpose:

ORIGIN, FOUND. RETAINED. BOTH PARTIES.

And Colly walked his grandmother home, at midnight, through the district that had stopped being afraid, door by open door, in the cold, in the best coat, and at the door of number eleven she stopped, and kissed her grandson's cold forehead, and said, to the both of them, in the draft, the sentence that the tower would spend the next fiscal year failing to measure:

"Come in, love. Mind the step. It's warm in here."
