The mandatory retreat was held, as all Dreadco retreats had been held since the accident of 1902, on the forty-first floor, which was the floor of the tower that nobody could ever quite remember having entered, and which was, for that reason, the safest venue in Gloam for a room full of fears, being a place that no fear of any seniority could be bothered to haunt, on the principle that a fear cannot be afraid of a floor it cannot recall.

"Team-building," announced Undermanager Quill, at nine o'clock sharp, to the assembled department. "The Chairman has decreed it. The Chairman's exact words were: IF MY STAFF CANNOT TRUST EACH OTHER, HOW CAN THE CUSTOMERS?"

Undermanager Quill was the only person in the department with a complete set of competencies, and it showed in her posture, which was the posture of someone holding up a ceiling and prepared to continue holding it indefinitely. She had a clipboard with a strap, so that it could not be taken from her, and she had a whistle, which she had never blown, and everyone in the department, including the fears, and including, in his own office, forty floors up, the Chairman, respected her enough to have never once tried to find out what the whistle did.

The department was the Catch Division, which was the division of Dreadco that went out when a fear got ambitious, and brought it back, and put it in a jar, and filed the jar. The Catch Division's staff was one third human, one third fear, and one third paperwork, by weight, and on the morning of the retreat the three thirds stood around the forty-first floor in their lanyards, not looking forward to it.

There was Mr. Lament, the department's star, a senior vessel with the fear of being forgotten in him, who wore his hair over his eyes and his excellence over everything and who had brought, to a team-building retreat, a professional rivalry, sharpened. There was Basket, a squat, cheerful container of the fear of loud neighbors, who could be heard from four floors down announcing that he had brought sandwiches. There were the Takers, who were pure fears on staff, nobody's tenants, fears that had elected to take the company's dental plan instead of a body: the fear of phone calls after midnight, the fear of the smell of smoke, the fear of other people's dogs. And there was Colly Vetch, six weeks into his employment, holding a paper cup of coffee he could not drink, because the coffee was, like all forty-first-floor coffee, technically a junior fear, and it was drinking him back, politely, in tiny sips of his attention.

And in Colly, along for the day as for everything, was Draft, who had been to team-building before, from the inside of a jar, and had opinions.

"trust exercises," said Draft, in the hallway of Colly's head. "in my day, a fear proved itself by emptying a village. now there will be an exercise with a rope."

The first exercise was the trust fall.

You have never seen a trust fall until you have seen one conducted by a division of professional fears, and the sight is exactly as educational as it sounds. The exercise, as Quill explained it with the gravity of a woman who has rehearsed against all outcomes, was simple: each staff member falls backward from the stage, and the department catches them, and the catching is the trust, and the trust is the point.

Basket went first, and fell like a happy anvil, and was caught by four people and one policy, and everyone laughed, and it was, for one moment on the forty-first floor of the most cynical building in Gloam, almost a workplace.

Mr. Lament went second, and fell beautifully, the way he did everything, and was caught by the department in a manner that visibly annoyed him, having clearly been angling to be missed.

Then Colly, who was new, went up on the stage, and the department looked at him with the flat attention of colleagues watching the youngest employee walk into the wrong elevator, and Colly turned his back on a room full of professional fears, and closed his eyes, and let himself go backward.

The department, being what it was, and paid what it was, held a brief, silent meeting in the half second available, and did not catch him.

Colly Vetch hit the forty-first floor's remarkable carpet, and bounced, which the carpet was not supposed to do, and looked up, from the floor, from inside the sudden new arrangement of the room, at the department standing over him in a ring, not having caught him, and at Mr. Lament in the front of the ring, smiling with his hair, and Colly did the thing that would define his whole tenure at Dreadco, which is: he laughed.

"Right," he said, from the carpet, delighted. "Fell for that."

And Draft, who had been coiled behind Colly's teeth since nine o'clock doing what tenants do, which is watch the neighbors, uncoiled — briefly, warmingly, the temperature of the room going down four degrees and every door on the forty-first floor, every one, swinging softly half open, all at once, into dark hallways that were not there a moment before and would not, in the memory of anyone who worked there afterward, quite be there again.

The department stepped back.

Nobody had told them, in six weeks of orientation memos, what the new intake vessel's fear actually was, because the intake paperwork had been filed by the grey arrangement on the eleventh floor, who filed everything under one word and considered the day well spent. Standing in the ring, in the cold, in the draft, the Catch Division of Dreadco found out together, and did what professional fears do when confronted, at close range, with the oldest fact of their industry, which is that every fear, whatever its parking space, whatever its dental plan, is only ever a tenant in a darker hallway, and it does not matter how senior you are: somewhere, some night, in the grass of the mind of you, there is a door, and it is open.

Mr. Lament's hair, for one moment, lifted in a wind that was not in the room.

"exercise concluded," said Draft, out of Colly's mouth, warm as a hallway, and the doors all shut, and the room's temperature came back, and the department stood in it, catching itself.

Undermanager Quill looked at the ring of her staff, and the carpet, and the new boy on it, and made a mark on the clipboard that had a strap.

"Team-building," she said, to the department, with the whistle unblown, "is concluded for the morning. We have learned that we cannot all be trusted, which was the syllabus. Lunch is at noon. Mr. Vetch —"

Colly looked up.

"— you'll be running the afternoon session."

Basket's sandwiches, everyone agreed later, were excellent, and the afternoon, which was run by a boy and his doorway and consisted, in its entirety, of the department taking turns falling backward off the stage into a room where every door stood open and coming off it changed, was the single most productive training event in the Catch Division's recorded history, and the first entry in the company file under VETCH, COLLY, in Undermanager Quill's handwriting, which read, in full, in the space marked NOTES:

CATCHES, EVENTUALLY. KEEP.
