BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY

NON-CANON: “WHAT DO YOU WANT”

This story is not canon. It is an alternate continuation of Episode 22, written for fun, and it does not count as part of the series’ continuity.

Previously: Yuki sat in the hundred-and-forty-fourth chair and took the scythe's real job, which is opening. The last 144 humans said "we let it happen" one at a time. Dr. Halvorsen got her daughter out of the dark. Lux woke up and asked a very old woman in a grey coat what she had done to her school. There are thirty-seven days of warmth left.

Content warning: Rated R. Strong language, grief, mass panic, a hundred and four thousand people learning they are dead, and one question asked a hundred and four thousand times.

TEASER

INT. BLACKWOOD — THE GREAT HALL — NIGHT

The common room of the dead, wider than it was. The walls have been pushed back by something that doesn't care about floor plans, and in the new space a hundred and four thousand people lie sleeping on mattresses, sofas, coats and each other. Pyjamas, prom dresses, hoodies, a man in a full bear costume, a girl in a wedding veil. Their breathing is one slow ocean.

At the centre of the room, on a folding chair somebody found, sits Yuki. Grey cardigan, wet shoes, the scythe across her knees. She has been sitting there for nine hours. She has not slept. She has watched every face.

Around her, quietly, a ring of figures: Lux with her arms folded, still in her sleep-crumpled dress. Velvette in black, one hand on the heel-knife, eyes on the door. Sasha with a legal pad and a pen she isn't using. Kevin, translucent Kevina at his shoulder, holding a very small cardboard box that is, for once, silent. Behind them, at the far wall, stiff as a row of nervous coats, the hundred and forty-three.

Simone stands at the front of them. She has taken her grey coat off, finally, and is holding it in both hands like a flag she doesn't know whether to surrender.

LUX
(quietly, not looking at her)
You're certain.

YUKI
(not looking up)
No.

LUX
(a dry breath)
Marvellous. Then I'll be certain for both of us. Where do you want me?

YUKI
(a tired smile)
Near.

LUX
Near I can do.

Yuki stands. Her knees crack. She looks round the ring of faces, and for a second she is just a nineteen-year-old in a cardigan who has to say something very large.

YUKI
(very low)
I don't know how to do this.

KEVIN
(gently)
Nobody does it for the first time. They do it badly, and then it's the second time.

YUKI
(a laugh)
That's the worst advice I've ever heard.

KEVIN
(shrugging)
It got me through a funeral.

Yuki takes a breath. She raises the scythe, not as a weapon, as a lamp. The blade lights, softly, white-gold, the colour of a very old kitchen at four in the morning.

She walks forward into the sleepers.

EPISODE TWENTY-THREE: “WHAT DO YOU WANT”

ACT ONE

INT. THE GREAT HALL — CONTINUOUS

Yuki kneels beside the first sleeper: a boy of sixteen in a school blazer with a tear in one sleeve. His mouth is slightly open. He has a bruise yellowing under one eye.

She touches his shoulder.

YUKI
(very gently)
Hey.

He doesn't stir.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(softer)
Hey. Can I talk to you?

The scythe hums. She presses the flat of the blade, very lightly, to the floor beside him, and the light runs out of it along the boards like water finding a crack.

The boy opens his eyes.

BOY
(a croak)
Is it morning?

YUKI
(a smile that costs her something)
Sort of.

BOY
(sitting up, rubbing his face)
Am I late?

YUKI
No.

BOY
(frowning at the bruise on his own arm, then at her)
Have I… who are you?

YUKI
I'm Yuki.

BOY
(hesitating)
The cardigan girl.

YUKI
(a faint laugh)
Yeah.

BOY
(a small, polite, terrified voice)
Did something happen?

Yuki lets the question sit. She looks at him. She looks past him, at the hall full of sleeping people, and at the ring of her friends and the hundred and forty-three pale strangers standing against the wall with their hands folded.

YUKI
(quietly)
I need to tell you something. It's going to be bad.

BOY
(very still)
Okay.

YUKI
And then I need to ask you something. And whatever you say, I'm going to listen.

BOY
(a long breath)
Okay.

YUKI
(steadying)
Your name's Daniel.

BOY
(startled)
Yes.

YUKI
You've been here a while. You've been happy, mostly.

BOY
(guarded)
It's a good school.

YUKI
(gently)
It's not a school.

A silence.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(carefully)
Daniel. Do you remember how you got here?

The boy's face begins to change. Not fear, not yet. Something worse: the slow dawning blankness of someone feeling for a stair that isn't there.

BOY
(whispering)
I was… I was in a…

He stops.

BOY (CONT’D)
I can’t remember my house.

YUKI
(very low)
I know.

BOY
(touching his bruised eye)
I… my dad…

His breath starts to shorten.

YUKI
(her hand on his arm, steady)
Daniel. Look at me. Look at me. You're safe. Nobody's going to hurt you again. That's the one thing I can promise.

BOY
(a thin, frightened sound)
Again?

Yuki closes her eyes for a moment.

YUKI
(opening them)
You died, Daniel.

The boy goes absolutely still.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(gently, every word placed)
You died a long time ago, and it was a bad way, and it wasn't your fault. And somebody built a place so you wouldn't have to be on your own in it. But they didn't tell you the truth. And that was wrong. So I'm telling you now.

A long, long silence.

The boy stares at her. His mouth works. His eyes are enormous. Behind him, in the hall, nobody breathes.

BOY
(a whisper)
That's not funny.

YUKI
(very quietly)
I know.

BOY
(shaking his head)
That's not… I'd remember.

YUKI
(gently)
You don't. You remember being cold in a doorway.

The boy stops shaking his head.

His face does something terrible. It folds. It falls apart in layers, like a wall of snow: first the fear, then the knowledge under the fear, then the grief under the knowledge, and finally, at the very bottom, the plain, small, bewildered face of a sixteen-year-old who has been told something he already knew.

BOY
(barely audible)
There was a doorway.

YUKI
(softly)
Yes.

BOY
(the first tear)
It was so cold.

YUKI
(her own eyes filling)
I know.

The boy folds in half. He puts his face on his knees and sobs, great, wrenching, helpless boy-sobs, and Yuki gathers him in without a word, scythe and all, and holds on while he comes apart.

Behind her, very quietly, Lux unfolds her arms.

LUX
(under her breath)
Oh, darling.

VELVETTE
(a low whisper)
Don't.

LUX
(not looking away)
I'm not.

VELVETTE
(her voice rough)
You are.

Lux wipes her face with the back of her wrist, savagely.

INT. THE GREAT HALL — LATER

The hall is waking.

It doesn't happen all at once. It goes through the sleepers like wind through wheat: one man sits up, then four girls, then a hundred, then a thousand. The sound builds from a sigh into a murmur into a tide. A hundred and four thousand people, in pyjamas and prom dresses and bear costumes, waking in a room that is suddenly much too large and finding a girl in a grey cardigan standing on a table with a glowing scythe.

She is shaking. She doesn't know where else to stand.

YUKI
(raising her voice, cracking)
Hi.

A ripple. Faces turning.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(louder, over a thousand mutters)
Hi. Everyone. Can everyone hear me?

SOMEONE
(from the back)
Who the hell is that?

SOMEONE ELSE
It's Yuki! It's the Reaper's friend!

YUKI
(a thin laugh)
I'm the Reaper.

Silence, sudden and total.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(her voice small, then growing)
I'm the new one. The old one's gone. She's… she's at peace. And before she went she gave me something to do, and I'm very sorry, because it's the worst thing, and I'm going to do it anyway.

She looks down at the sea of upturned faces.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(steadying)
Everyone here is dead.

For a second, nothing.

Then a girl near the front laughs, high, brittle, delighted.

GIRL
(laughing)
Oh my God, is this a bit?

YUKI
(gently)
It isn't.

GIRL
(still laughing, but her eyes are getting round)
Is this for Madison? Is this a Madison thing?

YUKI
(very quietly)
Madison's dead too.

The laugh stops.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(louder)
All of us. Every one of us. I'm so sorry. We died in a lot of different ways, and they were all bad, and it wasn't your fault, and this place was built to catch us. But the people who built it didn't tell you, and they should have, and I'm telling you now.

A hundred and four thousand people look at her.

Then someone, somewhere, makes a small, high, broken sound.

Then another.

Then the whole room.

It isn't a scream. It's worse. It's the sound of a hundred and four thousand people understanding something at the same time: a vast, rising, ragged chord of breath, of gasps, of names, of Mum and Dad and Kitty and oh God and no, no, no.

The hall breaks.

INT. THE GREAT HALL — MOMENTS LATER

Chaos. People on their feet, people on their knees, people clinging to each other. A boy in a bear costume is trying to rip off his own head and can't find the zip. A woman in a wedding veil has walked straight into a wall and is standing with her forehead against it, whispering a man's name. Two thousand people are rushing the doors. Somebody, somewhere, is shouting a flight number.

Sasha, at the edge of it, in her grey suit, is hanging on to a pillar.

SASHA
(shouting to nobody, hoarse)
There's no flight! There's no flight!

VELVETTE
(grabbing her arm)
Sash!

SASHA
(white-faced)
I wrote the Student Code. I wrote forty-one clauses on how to run this place and not one of them is about this.

VELVETTE
(hard)
Then write a forty-second.

SASHA
(a frantic laugh)
In what, crayon?

Lux comes through the crowd like a ship through weed, flatly, imperiously, with a path opening before her that nobody can explain.

LUX
(to nobody, to everybody)
Sit. Down.

A hundred heads turn.

LUX (CONT’D)
(the full Lux, a voice trained on boardrooms and funerals)
I am not going to ask twice. I am going to count to three and then I am going to be very cross. Sit.

A pause. Some of them sit. It spreads. By the time she has finished glaring at the third row, the nearest five thousand are cross-legged on the floor.

Lux looks at them. She looks, for a moment, as though she might cry.

LUX (CONT’D)
(softly, not for the crowd)
God. I hated being obeyed.

VELVETTE
(under her breath)
You loved it.

LUX
(a ghost of a smile)
Slightly.

On the table, Yuki has crouched, white, trembling, shaking.

YUKI
(to Lux)
It's not working.

LUX
(quietly)
It is. They're just screaming.

YUKI
(a ragged whisper)
That's not what I wanted.

LUX
(gently)
You told five thousand children they were dead. What did you want, a round of applause?

YUKI
(a wet laugh)
I wanted them to be okay.

LUX
(very softly)
I know. That's the problem with the truth. It never asks if you're ready.

She puts one hand flat on Yuki's back.

LUX (CONT’D)
(very low)
You still have to ask.

Yuki closes her eyes.

YUKI
(a breath)
I know.

She opens them and stands.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(clear, shaking, loud)
I'm going to ask you something. I'm going to ask every one of you, one at a time, and I'm going to listen to all of it. It might take a while. We've got thirty-seven days.

A rustle.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(her voice breaking)
I only want one thing. I want you to tell me the truth. Not the polite thing. The real thing. What do you want?

Silence.

It is not the silence of agreement. It is the silence of a hundred and four thousand people discovering that nobody has ever asked them that.

END OF ACT ONE

ACT TWO

INT. THE GREAT HALL — HOURS LATER

Yuki is sitting on the floor against a pillar, with a girl's head in her lap. She is hoarse. Her cardigan is soaked through. Around her, in a widening ring, Kevin, Kevina, Velvette, Sasha and a dozen volunteers from among the dead are moving through the hall with paper cups of water, with tissues, with a patience that has no words.

The girl in her lap is ten. She is wearing a unicorn onesie. She has been crying for an hour and has finally started to hiccup.

CHILD
(hiccuping)
I want my mum.

YUKI
(stroking her hair)
I know.

CHILD
(a small, flat voice)
Is she dead too?

YUKI
(carefully)
I don't know.

CHILD
(after a long time)
I hope not.

YUKI
(her throat working)
Me too.

CHILD
(a whisper)
But I want her to be here.

YUKI
(her face crumpling)
I know.

She holds the child. She cannot do anything else.

Behind them, at the edge of the light, a very old woman in a grey coat is standing. She has been standing there for two hours. She has not spoken. Simone watches the girl in the unicorn onesie and her face is the face of a woman looking at the sun.

Yuki sees her.

YUKI (CONT’D)
(not unkindly)
You can come over.

Simone doesn't move.

SIMONE
(hoarse)
I don't think I'm allowed.

YUKI
(gently)
Who's going to stop you?

SIMONE
(a thin, awful smile)
Nobody. That's what's wrong with me.

She crosses the floor with a slowness that is almost shy and stops at the edge of the ring, with her coat in her hands.

The child looks up. She sees the face. She sees the age in it, and the tiredness, and something in the eyes that no ten-year-old has words for.

CHILD
(a whisper)
Are you somebody's nan?

Simone laughs. It is a broken sound.

SIMONE
(her eyes filling)
Yes. A very long time ago.

CHILD
(tentatively)
You look sad.

SIMONE
(a small, wet laugh)
I am.

CHILD
(considering)
You can sit if you want. Yuki's got room.

Simone looks at Yuki.

Yuki nods.

Simone lowers herself to the floor, slowly, joint by joint, until she is sitting cross-legged beside the child in the unicorn onesie with her back against a pillar. She is six hundred years old. She looks, for the first time in the series, like somebody's grandmother.

CHILD (CONT’D)
(fascinated)
You've got a coat with a hole.

SIMONE
(touching the missing button)
It's a button.

CHILD
(frowning)
Where is it?

SIMONE
(very quietly)
It came off a long time ago. I never sewed it back.

CHILD
(after a moment, practically)
I can sew.

SIMONE
(startled)
Can you?

CHILD
(proud)
Miss Okafor taught me. In art.

Simone stares at her.

SIMONE
(a whisper)
Did she.

CHILD
(a nod, solemnly)
I'm very good.

SIMONE
(her voice shaking)
I should think you are.

The child holds out her hand. Simone, as if in a dream, takes off the grey coat and gives it to her.

The child examines it. She picks at the frayed place where a button used to be. She chews her lip.

CHILD
(frowning)
I haven't got a needle.

YUKI
(very softly)
I can find you one.

CHILD
(brightening)
Can you?

YUKI
(a faint smile)
I'm the Reaper. I can find anything.

She reaches into her cardigan pocket, and from it, with a small shiver of gold, comes a needle and a reel of thread, grey wool-coloured, exactly the right grey.

Simone stares at it. Her mouth is open.

SIMONE
(barely audible)
That was in my mother's tin.

YUKI
(gently)
I know.

The child threads the needle with her tongue between her teeth. She finds a button, a plain, flat, slightly chipped grey one from the same pocket, and sews it on, crookedly, slowly, with enormous concentration, while the hall roars and sobs around them and an old woman watches every stitch.

CHILD
(biting the thread)
There.

She holds up the coat.

On the front, beside the others, in a thick wobbly line of grey wool, a button.

Simone takes it. She puts it against her face and breathes in, and then, without a sound, with her whole body, she begins to cry.

The child pats her knee.

CHILD (CONT’D)
(kindly)
It's okay. It's only a button.

SIMONE
(into the wool)
Yes.

CHILD
(matter-of-fact)
You can have a cuddle if you like.

Simone makes a sound that is not a word.

She leans over, and the little girl in the unicorn onesie puts both arms round the neck of the oldest woman in the universe, and holds on, and neither says anything at all.

Over their heads, Yuki closes her eyes.

INT. THE GREAT HALL — DEEP NIGHT

The hall is quieter.

Not calm. Never calm. But the screaming has run its course, and what's left is the low, exhausted, human murmur of a hundred thousand people sitting on the floor in the dark and talking. Small groups. Pairs. A boy teaching a girl a card game with a deck made of cardboard. A man reading aloud from a book that doesn't exist. A choir of thirty humming something, softly, a tune with no name.

At the far end, near the doors, Kevin is sitting on a mattress with Kevina, passing a mug between them. The box is on the floor between them. It has opened a flap at the top, shyly.

BOX
(very small)
Is it over?

KEVIN
(gently)
Not yet.

BOX
(after a pause)
I had a bad feeling about the weather.

KEVINA
(smiling)
It was the end of the world.

BOX
(sniffing)
I said I had a feeling.

Kevin laughs. Beside him, Kevina puts her translucent head on his shoulder, and for a moment the two of them are just two dead people and a box, listening to the choir.

Across the hall, Velvette sits on the edge of a table with her boots swinging, a paper cup in one hand and the other on Lux's shoulder. Lux, sitting on the floor with her back against Velvette's knee, has her eyes closed. They are very tired.

VELVETTE
(low)
You did well.

LUX
(not opening her eyes)
I told five thousand people to sit down.

VELVETTE
(a faint smile)
That's what I said.

LUX
(a dry breath)
I'm so frightfully good at being listened to.

VELVETTE
(very softly)
You are.

A pause.

LUX
(eyes still closed)
Velvette.

VELVETTE
Mm.

LUX
(carefully)
She said nine lives.

VELVETTE
(a breath)
Yes.

LUX
(quietly)
And thirty-seven days.

VELVETTE
(very evenly)
Yes.

LUX
(opening her eyes)
I'd like to spend them badly.

VELVETTE
(a short laugh)
Define badly.

LUX
(looking up at her, a flash of the old self)
I'd like to eat something I shouldn't, in a bed I haven't paid for, with a woman who has threatened a Dean.

Velvette laughs. It cracks across the hall like a plate.

VELVETTE
(shaking her head)
You absolute lunatic.

LUX
(softly)
Mm.

VELVETTE
(leaning down, her forehead against Lux's)
That can be arranged.

They stay like that.

At the other end of the hall, under the great windows, where the grey sky is going, very slowly, pink, Yuki has finished.

The last of the hundred and four thousand has told her what they want. She has heard every one. She sits on the table with her head in her hands and the scythe lying across her knees, white-gold, humming faintly, like a kettle that has finally come to the boil.

A shadow falls across her.

She looks up.

Simone is standing there, with the coat on again, and a new button.

SIMONE
(quietly)
Thank you.

YUKI
(wiping her face)
I don't think you should be thanking me.

SIMONE
(a thin smile)
I'll thank who I like.

She sits beside Yuki on the table. For a while neither of them says anything. Out in the hall, the choir has found a second verse.

YUKI
(finally)
It's every kind of thing.

SIMONE
(softly)
Yes.

YUKI
(a long breath)
Some of them want to go home. Some of them want to stay. A lot of them want their mums. A lot of them want somebody to say sorry, and a lot more just want somebody to be in the room. There's about nine thousand who want to be angry at you.

SIMONE
(a faint smile)
Good.

YUKI
(a wet laugh)
You keep saying that.

SIMONE
(softly)
Because it's true.

YUKI
(looking at her)
And a hundred and four thousand of them said one thing, in all of it.

SIMONE
(very still)
What?

YUKI
(gently)
They said they didn't want it to be for nothing.

Simone closes her eyes.

SIMONE
(barely audible)
Ah.

YUKI
They said if the warmth's real, if it's what keeps you alive, then they don't mind. Not if it's honest. Not if they get to choose.

SIMONE
(her eyes still closed)
That's not what I expected.

YUKI
(quietly)
No. Me neither.

SIMONE
(a long, ragged breath)
Nine thousand of them want me hanged, and the rest want to help.

YUKI
(a faint smile)
That's people.

SIMONE
(opening her eyes)
It is, isn't it.

A silence.

YUKI
(carefully)
But I can't make that decision.

SIMONE
(turning)
Which?

YUKI
(her voice steady now)
Whether they stay. Whether they keep the warm going. Whether you do. I'm the Reaper, I'm not a god. I can open a door. I can't tell a hundred and four thousand people what to walk through.

SIMONE
(very quietly)
Then who can?

Yuki lifts her hand, and points.

Across the hall, at the long table where Kwabena and Marisol and Anselm and the twins have been silently taking down names, a very small figure in a pink cardigan has risen to her feet and is standing on a chair with her hands cupped round her mouth.

PEONY
(a high, wavering, absurd voice)
EXCUSE ME!

Every head turns.

PEONY (CONT’D)
(trembling, brave, bright red)
I'd like to call a vote!

A long, long, bewildered silence.

Then, from the second row, a single, deep, disbelieving voice:

DANIEL
(the boy in the blazer, standing)
Seconded.

END OF ACT TWO

ACT THREE

INT. THE GREAT HALL — DAWN

The hall has been arranged. It has taken the whole of the night, and it has been done by hand: chairs and sofas and tables dragged into rings, rows, a vast soft amphitheatre in the shape of the Round Room, a hundred and four thousand seats, each one different, each one salvaged from a house or a school or a life. The chair on the innermost ring is empty. It has a cushion.

At the centre, on a table, with a candle, stands Simone.

She is alone. The hundred and forty-three have been seated among the rest, at random, mixed in: Anselm next to a girl in a hijab, Dr. Halvorsen next to a boy in a football shirt, Old Ruth on somebody's knee. There is no stage. There is no dais. She is the one who stands because she's the one who has to.

Yuki sits on the inner ring in the cushioned chair, with the scythe laid across her knees. Lux and Velvette sit just behind her. Kevin has put the box on his lap and is holding its flaps shut, gently, as you would a nervous cat.

SIMONE
(her voice carrying, level, a voice that has run a thousand assemblies)
There is one item.

The hall is silent.

SIMONE (CONT’D)
(steadily)
You've been told the truth. You've heard what we are, and what we took, and what it bought. You've heard that we have thirty-seven days.

She looks round the great hall.

SIMONE (CONT’D)
(quietly)
I'm not going to make a speech. I've made too many. I'm going to ask a question.

She takes a breath.

SIMONE (CONT’D)
(very clearly)
Do you want the warmth to go on?

A silence you could hang a coat on.

SIMONE (CONT’D)
(carefully)
I'll tell you what it means. It means the door stays open. It means the Ark lives. It means some of you, the ones who burn, go on feeding it. Not in the dark rooms. Not in terror. We've closed the First Room, and every room like it. The harvest, from today, is only what's given.

A murmur.

SIMONE (CONT’D)
(louder)
It will not be enough. We will go out, one by one, over a very long time, and we will be nothing, and the door will close. It will take years. I don't know how many. That is the truth.

She spreads her hands.

SIMONE (CONT’D)
(quietly)
Or you can say no. Tonight. And the Ark goes dark in thirty-seven days, and we go out with it, a hundred and forty-three of us, and you go on a little longer, and then you don't. Either way. Nobody makes you. Nobody decides for you. I'd like you to know that.

She looks down.

SIMONE (CONT’D)
(very low)
I'd like you to know that I'm sorry.

Silence.

Then, from the ring, a voice. A small one.

CHILD
(the unicorn onesie, standing on her chair)
Can we have a vote with hands?

Simone, startled, laughs.

SIMONE
(her voice breaking)
Yes. I think we'd better.

CHILD
(solemnly)
I'll count.

SIMONE
(a whisper)
You do that.

She steps down off the table.

Yuki stands. In the cushioned chair, with the scythe at her side, she lifts her hand, and her voice carries, clear and breaking and nineteen:

YUKI
Everyone who wants the warmth to go on, put your hand up.

A long, long pause.

Then, in the dawn light, slowly, a wave goes through the hall.

Hands.

One. Ten. A thousand. Row after row, ring after ring, rising out of the dark like a field of tall grass. A girl in a wedding veil. A boy in a bear costume. A man in a bathrobe. Daniel in his torn blazer. A grandmother in a headscarf. Hands, and hands, and hands.

Not all.

Not nine thousand. Not nearly: nine thousand sit, arms folded, jaws tight, eyes on Simone like knives.

But the rest.

The child in the unicorn onesie, standing on her chair, counts under her breath, with her whole face, one finger at a time, until she gives up and shouts:

CHILD
(shrieking)
A LOT!

A wild, wrecked laughter goes through the hall, a hundred thousand people laughing at once through their tears.

Simone does not laugh. She is looking at the hands, at the unbroken field of them, at the faces under them.

SIMONE
(a whisper)
Why?

Nobody hears.

She says it again, louder, and her voice cracks across the hall like a plate.

SIMONE (CONT’D)
(her whole body shaking)
Why? Why would you? After what we did.

For a moment, nobody answers.

Then, from somewhere in the middle, one hand lowers, and a voice, a girl's, cool, clear, clipped, carries up out of the crowd.

LUX
(standing, one hand on a chair, in the first row of the second ring)
Because you asked.

Simone turns.

LUX (CONT’D)
(not smiling, perfectly steady)
That's the whole of it, darling. You came in and sat down, and you asked what we wanted. In all your six hundred years, you never once thought to do that.

SIMONE
(barely audible)
I was afraid of the answer.

LUX
(softly)
Yes. People always are.

She looks round at the field of hands.

LUX (CONT’D)
(her voice carrying)
And the answer is, we'll help. Not for you. Not because you've earned it. Because it's cold, and we've all got a hundred and forty-three people in it, and nobody should have to go out in the dark alone.

She lifts her chin.

LUX (CONT’D)
(very quietly)
That's the only thing the dead know.

The hall is silent.

And then, slowly, at the back, one of the sitters with folded arms, a tall man in a leather jacket, nine thousand strong in his sullen, furious, grieving silence, uncrosses his arms and puts up his hand.

Another.

A girl in a black dress.

A very old man in a cardigan.

The hands rise among the folded arms, row by row, in a slow, uneven, reluctant wave, until the whole great hall of a hundred and four thousand people is a forest of raised hands, and not one is still.

Simone sinks to her knees on the floor of the hall.

She puts her face in her hands.

Nobody says anything. Nobody needs to. All around her, in the half-light, the dead are lifting their hands to say yes.

Beside her, kneeling, in a grey cardigan, a nineteen-year-old with a scythe lays one hand on her back.

YUKI
(very softly)
Congratulations on your promotion.

Simone makes a sound that might be a laugh.

SIMONE
(muffled, tear-stained)
Oh, God. Don't.

YUKI
(a faint smile)
Your probation begins now.

INT. THE ARK — THE LATTICE — LATER

The cathedral of lamps.

It has changed. A hundred and four thousand points of light on walls that rise out of sight, but they are not burning as they were. They are steady. They are quiet. They are, somehow, warmer: a soft gold glow like a house at night with the curtains drawn.

At the foot of the pillar, in a grey coat with a new button, Simone stands with her hand flat against the light.

In the doorway behind her, the hundred and forty-three. Beside them, in a very large pyjama top, with a small brown dog under her arm, a girl of seven.

SOFIA
(whispering, awed)
Is that where the warm comes from?

DR. HALVORSEN
(gently, behind her)
Yes.

SOFIA
(a long considering pause)
It's quite nice.

DR. HALVORSEN
(a wet laugh)
I think so.

SOFIA
(solemnly)
I think the dog likes it.

BISCUIT, in her arms, wags once.

Simone lowers her hand from the pillar.

She turns. She looks at the hundred and forty-three, the pale, thin, patched, haunted, extraordinary faces, in the gold light. Her hand goes to the button on her coat.

SIMONE
(very quietly)
Well.

TOBIAS
(from the front, in his huge tracksuit)
Well what?

SIMONE
(a faint smile)
Has anyone got any ideas?

TOBIAS
(blinking)
About what?

SIMONE
(softly)
About how we do this properly.

A long, long silence.

Then, from the back, a dry, upright voice:

KWABENA
I should like to propose a rota.

The hundred and forty-three groan, as one.

OLD RUTH
(cackling)
God help us, he's going to want minutes.

KWABENA
(with dignity)
There will be minutes.

And the whole room, helplessly, laughs.

Simone laughs with them. It is a shaky, wet, startled sound, and it goes on much longer than she means it to, and by the end she has to hold on to the pillar.

OUTSIDE, high above the Lattice, through a seam in the roof that has never been there before, a single point of white light has appeared in the black.

Nobody is looking at it.

It stays.

EXT. BLACKWOOD — THE RING ROAD — DAWN

Yuki stands at the edge of the world.

Behind her, the white wall of the school, the great hall of the dead, a hundred thousand voices going quietly about the business of being alive. In front of her, the salt plain, the grey sky, the nothing.

She is not afraid of it any more. That's the strange thing.

She lifts the scythe, and for the first time, uses it as it was made to be used. Not to cut. To open.

The sky parts.

It parts like a curtain, along a seam, from top to bottom, and through it comes a light she has never seen: not gold, not white, a deep, unhurried blue, the colour of the hour before dawn in a country she has never visited. There is grass under it. There is wind. There is, very far away, the sound of a hundred thousand birds.

A door.

Not an exit. An option.

She looks at it for a long time.

SCYTHE
(very quietly, from her hand)
Are you going to?

YUKI
(a long breath)
Not yet.

SCYTHE
(gently)
No?

YUKI
(a faint smile)
I think I want to be there when the last one's ready.

SCYTHE
(after a moment)
That could be years.

YUKI
(softly)
I've got time.

She closes the door. It folds shut with a sound like a page turning.

She stands there for a little longer, in the early light, with her hand on the scythe.

Then she turns, and walks back to the school.

In the hall, in the gold dawn, a girl in a sleep-crumpled dress and a girl in black are sharing a croissant that does not exist. A boy named Daniel is learning to play cards. A girl in a unicorn onesie is sewing a button on a dog.

And on the glass of the great window, in the condensation, in a neat, round, tired hand with a little loop on every p, someone has written:

THANK YOU FOR ASKING.
WE'LL TAKE IT FROM HERE.
— S.

FADE OUT.

END OF EPISODE TWENTY-THREE.
