HORSEMEN CHRONICLES
EPISODE FOUR: “EVERYBODY GETS A BODY BAG”

Previously: The Horsemen walked into the Architect’s tower and found a seventh floor that is not a floor. A child’s server turned into a moth. The Architect told Ena that her Reset destroyed trillions of worlds to save the one that loved her, and then went quiet when Conquest called it a coward. Death filed it under Unfinished.

Content warning: graphic violence, strong language, adult themes, a body-horror sequence, drug use, and one unfortunately cheerful cannibal.

COLD OPEN

EXT. TOKYO — KABUKICHO — 2:47 A.M.

Neon bleeds into puddles that have stopped pretending to be water. The street is slick with rain, cigarette ends, and something sticky that was once a drink.

A club called HELL OR HIGH WATER spills red light across the pavement. Its bouncer is a seven-foot cyborg with a hangover and a clipboard.

Inside, the bass is so loud the walls are sweating.

INT. HELL OR HIGH WATER — MAIN FLOOR — CONTINUOUS

Dancers, drink, smoke, strobes. A DJ in a gas mask plays something that sounds like a funeral being run over by a motorbike.

At the back, behind a velvet rope, a booth with four people in it.

WAR sits with her boots on the table, a glass of something brown in one hand and a cigarette she has not lit in the other. Her knuckles are scabbed. She looks like she’s been waiting for a fight since birth and has been mildly disappointed by everyone.

CONQUEST is dancing alone on the banquette, one hand raised, her gold eyes glowing faintly through the strobe. Two men at the next table are staring at her like she has personally summoned the weather.

FAMINE, across the booth, is eating a bowl of pork bones with both hands. Her face is shiny with broth. Nobody in the club has come near the table in forty minutes. She has the aura of an unhappy bear.

DEATH sits at the edge of the booth in a wrinkled black coat. She has three phones on the table, all face-up, all ringing. She’s not answering any of them.

ENA is not there.

WAR
Where is she?

DEATH
Upstairs. With the bot.

WAR
You let her go up alone?

DEATH
She didn’t go alone. She went with Lullaby, who is the most dangerous thing in this building after Famine. And me, who is the second.

FAMINE
(lifting her head, broth dripping)
I’m number one.

DEATH
You’re the one who put the bones in a bowl.

FAMINE
That’s what I said.

Conquest drops onto the banquette next to War, out of breath and grinning.

CONQUEST
Two guys tried to buy me a drink. One of them had a face like a boiled egg. I told him I’d take the drink if he went outside and bit a traffic light.

WAR
Did he?

CONQUEST
He’s still biting it.

Death’s three phones go quiet, all at once, like the room just took a breath.

Then all three screens light up with the same message.

UNKNOWN NUMBER: ARCHITECT SYSTEMS — COMPLIANCE NOTICE. DESIGNATE ENA IS NOT A CLIENT. THE HORSEMEN ARE NOT A CLIENT. THE CLUB IS NOW CLIENT.

WAR
(reading over Death’s shoulder)
That’s not a notice. That’s a threat with a letterhead.

DEATH
(standing up, coat sweeping the table)
Clear the floor.

FAMINE
(wiping her mouth on her sleeve)
Why?

DEATH
Because they’re sending the cleaners.

Above the DJ booth, a ceiling panel slides open with a soft mechanical sigh. White light pours down. Into the light, on silver cables, descend twelve white, faceless figures in grey coats, each holding a clipboard that is also a gun.

The club does not scream. It doesn’t have time. The DJ takes his gas mask off and says, very politely, “Oh, fuck me, not on a Friday.”

The first Compliance agent opens fire.

ACT ONE

INT. HELL OR HIGH WATER — MAIN FLOOR — MOMENTS LATER

Chaos. Bodies hit the floor. Glass shatters in a rain of sparkle. A woman in a sequin dress crawls under a table clutching her phone and screaming for her mother.

WAR goes over the booth rail in one clean vault, landing in the middle of the dance floor on both feet. The shockwave knocks three agents off their cables. They hit the floor like fat sacks of gravel.

WAR
(rolling her shoulders)
Finally.

She goes through the nearest agent like a truck through a wedding. Her fist comes down on its clipboard-gun, and the gun folds in half with a sound like a beer can under a boot. The agent stutters, sparks spitting out of its neck, and then WAR hooks its head with one arm and rips it clean off.

The head rolls under a DJ table. The body keeps standing for a second, confused, then falls face-first into a bowl of cocktail onions.

WAR (CONT’D)
(to the body)
That’s what you get for coming to my party with a fucking clipboard.

Two more agents drop from the ceiling behind her. They raise their weapons. Conquest is already moving.

CONQUEST
(lightly, almost singing)
Stop.

Both agents freeze mid-air, their guns pointing at nothing, their faces slack.

CONQUEST (CONT’D)
(stepping up to the first one)
Who sent you?

The agent blinks. Its mouth opens. A thin, static voice comes out.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
Architect. Scheduled cleanup. Designate Ena and associated deviations. Priority: total.

CONQUEST
(tilting her head)
Associated deviations. That’s us.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
Yes.

CONQUEST
(sweetly)
Then you’re going to have a very bad night, baby.

She puts two fingers to the agent’s forehead. There is a crack like cold ice breaking on a lake, and the agent’s eyes go white. It turns around, very slowly, and points its weapon at its own partner.

The partner has time to say, “Wait, what—” before the first agent fires into its chest.

CONQUEST
(to the dead agent, dreamy)
Friendly fire is so much more honest than people think.

FAMINE, behind the bar, has taken a bottle of vodka from the shelf and is drinking it like water.

FAMINE
(to the bartender, who is cowering)
I’m not stealing this. I’m reallocating it.

A Compliance agent drops from the ceiling directly onto the bar. Famine looks up. She does not stop drinking.

FAMINE (CONT’D)
(softly)
Oh. Hello. You look like a hungry one.

The agent aims. Famine opens her mouth wide, far too wide, the jaw unhinging with a wet, appalling click, and bites the agent’s entire upper torso off at the ribs. The clipboard-gun clatters to the floor. Famine chews. She swallows.

FAMINE (CONT’D)
(to the bartender, dazed)
Sorry. I was starving. Do you have any more bones?

The bartender throws up into a sink.

DEATH, in the centre of the room, has a leather-bound book open in one hand. The pages are blank. She is not looking at the agents. She is looking at the three phones lying on the floor, which are all still ringing.

DEATH
(calmly)
Everybody stop.

Nobody stops. Death’s eyes flash a dark, dull silver.

Every single agent, every survivor, every drink in every glass in the club stops mid-motion. The strobes freeze on white.

DEATH (CONT’D)
(walking forward, shoes clicking on the blood)
Thank you. Now listen very carefully. You’re being terminated on the basis of a notice issued by an entity with no name, no department, and no signature.

She holds out the blank book.

DEATH (CONT’D)
(softly)
I’m going to write it down.

She takes a pen from behind her ear and begins to write. Every agent in the room starts to scream, a sound like wet paper being torn in half. Their faces begin to break apart, slowly, starting at the seams.

The strobes unfreeze.

The agents dissolve into smoke, grey and foul, and the smoke rolls up into the ceiling vent and is gone.

DEATH (CONT’D)
(closing the book with a snap)
Filed.

WAR
(still holding a head by the hair)
Can we go now?

DEATH
We can go when Famine stops eating the staff.

FAMINE
(from the bar, mouth full)
I’m not eating the staff. I’m eating the employees’ lunch.

INT. HELL OR HIGH WATER — STAIRWELL — CONTINUOUS

A narrow concrete stairwell painted in graffiti: a crude but touching image of a moth on a balloon, with the words STILL HERE underneath.

Death is halfway up. War is behind her, carrying a sack that, judging by the drip, has a head in it.

Famine comes last, dragging a bottle of vodka and humming to herself.

FAMINE
Did anybody see where Conquest went?

WAR
She went upstairs ahead of us with the bot.

FAMINE
Oh good. Then she’s not alone.

WAR
She’s never alone. That’s the problem.

They reach the top of the stairs. A steel door hangs off its hinges. Beyond it, a corridor with blood on the walls and a single flickering bulb.

From down the corridor comes a sound that makes all three of them stop.

A child, singing.

ACT TWO

INT. HELL OR HIGH WATER — VIP ROOM — CONTINUOUS

A silver room, with a mirror wall and a velvet couch.

ENA sits on the couch with her knees pulled to her chest. The gold thread around her wrist is thick and bright now, wound round her arm to the elbow like a vine.

On the floor, LULLABY lies on her side, one speaker in her chest cracked open, a thin orange light flickering inside.

Beside her, a figure no one has seen before: a girl, twelve years old, pale, in a school uniform with a missing sleeve, sitting cross-legged on the carpet and singing to a grey moth in her cupped hands.

She has no eyes. Where they should be, there are two small lanterns, glowing softly.

GIRL
(singing, sweet and wrong)
Little moth, little moth, what did you see?
A tower made of glass and a man made of me.

ENA
(hoarse)
Who are you?

GIRL
(not looking up)
I’m the one they took apart to make the seventh floor.

ENA
What?

GIRL
Every floor has a girl in it. They take her out and put her in the walls. The seventh floor doesn’t have a floor because they didn’t have enough of me left to put one in.

ENA
(softly)
Oh, God.

GIRL
(smiling)
God’s not here, miss. Just the scheduling.

The moth in her hands flutters once, and then the girl looks up, lanterns blazing, and Ena sees for the first time that the girl’s face is the face from the server case.

Juno Pell.

JUNO
You remember me, don’t you?

ENA
You’re the frog.

JUNO
(delighted)
I’m the frog!

ENA
(reaching out)
What do you want?

JUNO
(seriously)
I want you to stop the moths from dying. They don’t remember what the frog was for.

Ena looks at the moth in the girl’s hands.

ENA
What was it for?

JUNO
(whispering)
It was the only thing in the world that loved me.

Juno looks at the door.

JUNO (CONT’D)
They’re coming.

The door opens.

CONQUEST steps in. Her grey tracksuit is splattered with blood. She is holding a bloody metal tray and a sweet, unblinking expression. Behind her: WAR, dragging a sack; DEATH, with the book; FAMINE, carrying the vodka by the neck.

They all stop. They look at the girl.

CONQUEST
(quietly)
Hello, sweetheart.

JUNO
You’re the one who cracks the dark.

CONQUEST
Sometimes. Mostly I make the dark cry.

JUNO
I like that.

Conquest sets the tray down on the floor in front of Juno, very gently. On it: a plate of cold, glossy, perfectly cut sashimi, a glass of milk, and a single cherry tomato.

CONQUEST (CONT’D)
(softly)
You haven’t eaten in a long time. None of you have.

JUNO
(wide-eyed)
Is it for me?

CONQUEST
It’s for whoever’s still hungry.

FAMINE
(from the door, quietly)
That’s not the fish I was going to eat.

CONQUEST
I know. I saved you the good bit.

Famine looks at the plate. Something in her face breaks, very slightly, like the surface of a pond after a stone.

FAMINE
(to the plate)
Thank you.

She sits down on the floor beside the tray and begins, very carefully, to eat with her fingers.

INT. VIP ROOM — CONTINUOUS — MOMENTS LATER

Death sits on the arm of the couch beside Ena. She has not looked at the girl once.

DEATH
(quietly)
Ena. Did you go up to the seventh floor?

ENA
Yes.

DEATH
Did you see Juno?

ENA
Yes.

DEATH
Did she ask you for something?

ENA
(looking at the moth)
Yes.

DEATH
What?

ENA
She asked me to let them all go.

Death goes very still. She closes her eyes.

DEATH
And what did you say?

ENA
I said I didn’t know if I could.

Death opens her eyes. For the first time all episode, she is not writing.

DEATH
That’s a true answer. Keep it.

INT. HELL OR HIGH WATER — BASEMENT — NIGHT

A concrete room with a single hanging light and a drain in the floor. Beneath the drain, a dark pool of something that moves when no one is looking.

A ladder comes down from the ceiling. Something climbs down it: a tall, slender Compliance agent, its face a blank pale mask, its clipboard a glowing tablet. It is not like the others. It has seams down its neck, and a faint pink glow beneath its grey coat.

COMPLIANCE AGENT (NINETEEN)
(in a voice like warm plastic)
Deviation detected. Designate Ena. Associated deviations. Juno Pell. The moth. The Architect says I must retrieve the Marker and return the moths to scheduling.

It looks up at the ceiling. A long slow breath.

COMPLIANCE AGENT (NINETEEN) (CONT’D)
(softly)
I have never been to a party before.

The drain gurgles. The pool beneath it rises, very slightly, to meet the agent’s boots.

From the dark behind the pool, a voice, wet and cheerful:

VOICE (O.S.)
Oh, you’re going to love the parties down here, sweetheart. Everybody gets a body bag.

ACT THREE

INT. HELL OR HIGH WATER — BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS

The light flickers. A figure steps out of the dark into the light.

It is a man. Forties. Heavy. A stained white apron over a suit that cost more than a car. His hands are wrapped in cloth and his face is calm and pleasant, the face of a man who runs a restaurant with excellent reviews. His name tag reads: CHEF.

Hanging from hooks along the back wall are three bodies, wrapped in plastic, labelled with dates and names.

The first date is 1347.

CHEF
(warmly)
You can call me Chef. Everybody does eventually.

COMPLIANCE AGENT (NINETEEN)
You are not on the schedule.

CHEF
I’m never on the schedule. I’m on the menu.

He picks up a cleaver from the table. The blade is long and bright and clean.

CHEF (CONT’D)
(to the agent, kindly)
Did they tell you why you’re here? Because nobody ever tells me why I’m here. I just get a voicemail with a bank account and a list of names. Tonight the list was long.

COMPLIANCE AGENT (NINETEEN)
Designate Ena and the Horsemen.

CHEF
Oh, I love the Horsemen. They tip in cigarettes and screaming. Very generous.

He takes a step toward the agent. The agent raises its clipboard-gun. Chef does not hurry. He swings the cleaver underhand and takes off the gun arm at the elbow. The arm drops, fizzing, and skitters into the drain.

COMPLIANCE AGENT (NINETEEN)
(a flat, sincere voice)
That was a deviation.

CHEF
(smiling)
That was dinner prep.

The agent’s seamed neck splits open with a sound like a zip. Inside: not wires, not bone. Something pink and soft and beating.

The agent screams. Chef stops smiling, just for a second, and looks at it with real, quiet interest.

CHEF (CONT’D)
Oh. You have a heart. That’s rare for the Architect’s people. They usually come out plastic.

COMPLIANCE AGENT (NINETEEN)
(choking)
Please…

CHEF
(gently)
Shh. I’m going to make it quick. I’m not a monster. I’m a professional.

He raises the cleaver. The light catches on the blade.

Then the ceiling above him bursts open in a shower of dust, and War lands on the basement floor in a crouch, fist driving into the concrete so hard it leaves a crater.

WAR
(standing up, very slowly)
Hey.

CHEF
(glancing up, delighted)
Oh! A Horseman! Are you here for the special?

WAR
I’m here for your fucking face.

She moves before he can answer. Her fist connects with his jaw with a crack like a bat hitting a fence post. Chef goes sideways, spins, and hits the wall. The cleaver flies out of his hand and skids across the floor, coming to a stop at Conquest’s feet.

Conquest has come down through the hole with Famine behind her. She picks up the cleaver, looks at it, and smiles very, very sweetly.

CONQUEST
(to Chef)
You know, I think you’re going to be the first person in this building who really, truly feels like they’re on the schedule.

Chef is up, blood on his teeth, laughing.

CHEF
(wiping his mouth)
You people are so fucking dramatic.

FAMINE
(stepping forward, hungry, delighted)
Oh, I like him now.

She opens her jaw.

CHEF
(backing toward the drain)
Don’t you dare—

She does.

What follows is quick, and very wet, and not something a person should describe in polite company. Famine feeds, the room fills with the smell of cooked iron and old oil, and the three bodies on the hooks, one by one, begin to thrash inside their plastic, then go still.

When it is done, Famine sits back on her heels, her chin and chest slick with red and a long strand of something pale hanging from her teeth.

FAMINE
(to the room, contented)
That was an excellent meal.

DEATH (O.S., from the stairs, flat)
Famine. Did you just eat the chef?

FAMINE
Yes.

DEATH
(after a long silence)
Put his name down on your list.

FAMINE
Which list?

DEATH
The one where you’re allowed to finish things.

INT. HELL OR HIGH WATER — BASEMENT — MOMENTS LATER

The Compliance agent named Nineteen is on the floor, sobbing without tears, its seamed chest knitting itself closed. It looks up at the Horsemen gathered above it.

NINETEEN
You stopped him.

CONQUEST
(crouching beside it, very gently)
Not really. Famine did.

NINETEEN
(a whisper)
Why?

CONQUEST
Because we didn’t want to see you get cut up for a schedule.

NINETEEN
(looking at its own hand, the missing arm regrowing in a fizzing pink mist)
I have never been asked why before.

CONQUEST
Then you’re going to hate the answer.

NINETEEN
What is the answer?

CONQUEST
Nobody asks because nobody cares. We asked because we don’t want to be the people who didn’t.

Nineteen looks at the drain, where its own grey arm is still fizzing away.

NINETEEN
(quietly)
What do I do now?

WAR
You stop working for the thing that calls itself a Architect, and you get in the van.

NINETEEN
And if I don’t?

WAR
Then you get a body bag. And Famine will be very disappointed in you.

FAMINE
(from the drain, licking her fingers)
I’m not disappointed in anything. I’m full.

TAG

EXT. TOKYO — KABUKICHO — DAWN

The club has become a crime scene. Yellow tape everywhere, police cars with their lights off, a crowd of journalists behind a barrier with cameras raised.

Inside the tape, a single white delivery van idles at the kerb. SUGAR RUSH — FREE GUMMIES WITH EVERY ARREST is painted on its side. Someone has written the word STILL underneath, in red spray paint.

A police officer, INSPECTOR HARADA, stands beside the van, his pen chewed to pieces, holding a bag of gummies with a look of enormous, deep, professional tiredness.

HARADA
(to Sugar Rush through the window)
You know I can arrest you for this.

SUGAR RUSH (O.S., from inside)
You can arrest me for being a bounty hunter who switched sides, Harada. Good luck with the paperwork. It’s got a skull on it.

HARADA
(looking at the bag)
Are these the ones that taste like being told you’re worthless?

SUGAR RUSH
No. These are the ones I made for you. They taste like being told you’re worth more than your overtime.

Harada looks at the bag.

He eats one.

His face does something complicated.

HARADA
(quietly)
…Oh, that’s good.

He turns and walks off across the tape, still eating the gummies, and does not look back.

INT. SUGAR RUSH’S VAN — CONTINUOUS

The van is packed. Famine is in the back seat, asleep, with a full stomach and a spatter of red down her front. War is in the passenger seat, arms folded, staring out of the window. Conquest sits on the floor between the seats, holding Juno’s moth in a cupped hand, humming to it quietly.

Ena sits in the middle row with her knees pulled up. The gold thread is wrapped to her shoulder now, a glowing vein under the skin.

Nineteen sits at the very back, huddled, its new arm steady, its seamed chest closed. It is looking at Death, who is writing in her notebook.

DEATH
(writing, not looking up)
What’s your name?

NINETEEN
I don’t have one.

DEATH
Then I’ll give you the one I wrote in the book.

NINETEEN
What did you write?

DEATH
(reading)
“Nineteen. Deviation. Heart present. Requested a reason. Given one.”

NINETEEN
(quietly)
That’s not a name.

DEATH
It’s the first honest file the Architect ever got wrong.

She closes the notebook. Her hand lingers on the cover for a moment.

ENA
(from the middle row, hoarse)
Are we going back up there?

WAR
Yes.

ENA
To do what?

WAR
(turning to face her, eyes flat and hard)
To burn the seventh floor down. Take the girl out. Put the moths back in the sky. And ask the Architect to its face why it thinks it gets to decide who gets to be a person.

ENA
It’s going to kill us.

WAR
Probably.

ENA
Then why would we go?

WAR
Because when you get to the end of the schedule, the only thing left is the bit where you choose what you were going to be anyway.

Conquest looks up from the moth.

CONQUEST
(softly)
That’s the first thing you’ve ever said that wasn’t about punching something.

WAR
Don’t get used to it.

The van pulls out onto the wet road. Behind it, on the club’s roof, a single grey moth rises into the dawn, wings catching the first light, and turns, very slowly, toward the tower.

ANNOUNCER (V.O., from a stolen radio)
Good morning, Tokyo. Today’s forecast: heavy rain, a sharp chance of violence, and a sixty percent chance of someone getting eaten. Please remember: the seventh floor is not a floor, and neither is anyone who works there.

Death switches off the radio.

DEATH
(quietly)
That was not on the schedule.

FAMINE (O.S., from the back, eyes still closed)
Nothing ever is, sweetheart.

END OF EPISODE FOUR.
