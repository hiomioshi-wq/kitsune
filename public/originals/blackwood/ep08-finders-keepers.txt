BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY
EPISODE 8 — “FINDERS KEEPERS”

TEASER

INT. MORTALITY SERVICES — MORNING

KEVIN stands in front of the office door patting every pocket he owns.

He has eleven pockets. He pats them in order. Then in a different order, as if the order were the problem.

YUKI TANAKA watches from her desk over a coffee she has not had time to drink.

YUKI
What.

KEVIN
I can’t find my keycard.

YUKI
You’re inside.

KEVIN
I came in behind the Reaper.

YUKI
So you’re a fucking tailgater now.

KEVIN
I need it for the fridge.

The office fridge has a card reader. A laminated sign above it reads:

STAFF ONLY. THE YOGURT KNOWS.

YUKI
Check Lost and Found.

KEVIN
Where’s Lost and Found?

YUKI
Under the east stairwell.

Kevin goes very still.

KEVIN
The hungry stairwell.

YUKI
It’s been fed. It’s fine on Tuesdays.

KEVIN
It’s Wednesday.

YUKI
Then walk fucking quickly.

INT. EAST STAIRWELL — CONTINUOUS

The stairwell breathes.

Not dramatically. The way a large dog breathes while pretending to sleep near a sandwich.

Kevin creeps down the steps.

At the bottom: a small wooden door with a brass plate.

LOST & FOUND.
CLAIMS 9–11. NO EXCEPTIONS. NO, NOT EVEN YOU.

Beside it, brand new, a tablet on a stand. A friendly interface glows:

WELCOME TO LOST & FOUND 2.0!
POWERED BY CAMPUS IT.
SPEAK YOUR LOSS.

Kevin leans toward it.

KEVIN
Um. Hi. I’m lost without my keycard.

The tablet chimes.

LOST ITEM LOGGED: KEVIN.
STATUS: FOUND.

The little wooden door opens.

A brass hook on a long arm reaches out, gently, politely, and takes Kevin by the collar.

KEVIN
Oh no.

The door closes.

A paper ticket slides out of the brass slot and drifts to the floor.

It reads:

ITEM 40,117.
KEVIN (ONE).
CONDITION: FAIR.

The stairwell exhales, a little jealous.

CUT TO TITLE:

BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY
EPISODE 8 — “FINDERS KEEPERS”

ACT ONE

INT. MORTALITY SERVICES — TWENTY MINUTES LATER

The GRIM REAPER reads the claim ticket at arm’s length, the way she reads anything that has been on the floor of that stairwell.

GRIM REAPER
Condition fair.

YUKI
That’s generous.

GRIM REAPER
Where is he?

YUKI
On a shelf. Presumably.

GRIM REAPER
We have twelve intakes this morning and a man from 1340 who still thinks he can negotiate.

The PRINTER coughs out a single sheet.

PRINTER

YUKI

PRINTER

GRIM REAPER

YUKI
Everyone knows where his desk is. It’s the one with the plant he keeps apologizing to.

GRIM REAPER
Coat.

INT. EAST STAIRWELL — LOST & FOUND DOOR — MOMENTS LATER

Yuki raps on the wooden door.

A hatch slides open at eye level.

Inside, behind a counter, stands DORIS.

Doris is a coat rack. Bentwood. Nineteenth century. Wearing a cardigan, a lanyard, and the expression a coat rack would have after one hundred and thirty years of other people’s mittens.

DORIS
Ticket.

Yuki holds up the ticket.

DORIS
Claims are nine to eleven.

YUKI
It is ten past nine.

DORIS
Then you’re in luck. Which is also in the back, if anyone wants it. Some girl left it in 1974.

YUKI
We’re here for Kevin.

DORIS
Describe the item.

YUKI
He’s a person.

DORIS
Everything’s a person to somebody. Describe him.

Yuki opens her mouth.

Nothing comes out.

She looks at the Reaper.

The Reaper looks at the ceiling.

GRIM REAPER
He’s… staff.

DORIS
Height.

GRIM REAPER
Standard.

DORIS
Hair.

GRIM REAPER
Present.

DORIS
Distinguishing features.

A very long pause.

YUKI
He says “sorry” to the printer.

PRINTER, O.S., from three floors up
HE SHOULD!

Doris closes the hatch halfway.

DORIS
That describes a lot of people.

YUKI
It really fucking doesn’t.

DORIS
Come back with a description. Or a receipt.

YUKI
A receipt for Kevin.

DORIS
Proof of purchase. Proof of ownership. Proof of prior relationship. I’m not handing a man to anyone who knocks.

GRIM REAPER
Mortality Services employs him.

DORIS
Employment is a rental.

The hatch snaps shut.

The stairwell makes a sound like a stifled laugh.

Yuki kicks the bottom step.

The step kicks back.

INT. IT HELP DESK — LATER

MINA THREAD is eating cereal from her promotional mug.

The six floating windows orbit her head. One of them shows a spreadsheet growing faster than a spreadsheet should.

Yuki slams the ticket on the counter.

YUKI
What the fuck is Lost and Found Two Point Oh.

MINA
Oh, good. You found it.

YUKI
It found Kevin.

MINA
That’s the idea. It’s a smart catalogue. Natural language intake. You say what you lost. It logs it. When the item turns up anywhere on campus, it auto-returns it to the owner.

YUKI
Kevin said he was lost without his keycard.

Mina chews.

MINA
Ah.

YUKI
Ah?

MINA
It might be parsing “I’m lost” as an intake.

YUKI
Might be?

Mina swivels a window around.

LOST ITEMS LOGGED SINCE LAUNCH: 4,211.

YUKI
How many of those are objects?

MINA
Ninety.

YUKI
And the rest?

Mina scrolls.

MINA
“I’m so lost in this lecture.” “I’m lost, where’s Building C.” “Lost my mind.” “Lost my train of thought.” “Lost the plot.” “Lost track of time.”

YUKI
Where are they?

MINA
Well, the people are mostly fine. The cupboard only has room for one at a time, so it’s holding Kevin and queueing the rest.

YUKI
And the trains of thought?

Somewhere across campus, a train whistle.

MINA
Being returned.

YUKI
To who?

MINA
Their owners. That’s the second half of the feature. Auto-return. Anything anyone has ever reported lost on this campus, since records began, gets delivered back.

YUKI
Since records began.

MINA
The old paper ledgers were scanned last month. Very thorough job. I’m proud of it.

YUKI
When did records begin?

Mina checks.

MINA
1611.

Yuki puts both hands flat on the counter.

YUKI
Turn it off.

MINA
Can’t mid-cycle. It’s returning. If I stop it now, everything in transit gets lost again and it logs that as a new loss and starts over.

YUKI
How long is a cycle?

MINA
Until everything’s home.

Through the window behind her, a delivery van marked BLACKWOOD RETURNS — WE FOUND IT! drives across the quad and over a professor.

The professor sits up holding a returned umbrella.

PROFESSOR
I lost this in 1998.

He opens it. A pigeon from 1998 flies out.

EXT. LUX AND VELVETTE’S PENTHOUSE TERRACE — SAME TIME

LUX lounges in white silk beside a pot of tea that costs more than a Mortality Services salary.

VELVETTE sharpens a silver chain link against another silver chain link.

The doorbell rings.

A RETURNS COURIER stands on the terrace holding a parcel and a clipboard.

COURIER
Delivery for Velvette.

VELVETTE
I didn’t order anything.

COURIER
It’s a return. You reported it lost in—

He checks.

COURIER
Third grade.

Velvette goes very still.

She opens the parcel.

Inside, wrapped in tissue paper: a small, crude, beautiful knife made from a sharpened plastic ruler.

A label in a teacher’s handwriting reads:

CONFISCATED. ASK FOR IT BACK AT THE END OF TERM.

Velvette lifts it out like a holy relic.

VELVETTE
Gerald.

LUX
Darling.

VELVETTE
They took Gerald.

LUX
You named it Gerald?

VELVETTE
I was eight. He looked like a Gerald.

She holds the plastic knife to the light. A tear she will deny later shines in one eye.

VELVETTE
I asked for him back on the last day of term. Mrs Pruett said “What knife?” to my fucking face.

The courier hands Lux a second parcel.

COURIER
And one for you, madam.

Lux takes it with two fingers.

Inside: a pink plastic diary with a tiny brass lock. A sticker on the front says PRIVATE!!!! in the handwriting of a nine-year-old with ambitions.

Lux closes the box very quickly.

VELVETTE
What is it?

LUX
Nothing.

VELVETTE
What is it?

LUX
A clerical error.

Velvette snatches the box.

Reads the sticker.

Grins like a shark at a pool party.

VELVETTE
Oh, I am going to fucking read this.

LUX
You will do no such thing.

VELVETTE
I will do it aloud.

LUX
I will have you killed.

VELVETTE
You’d have to find someone who’d win.

Lux takes a sip of tea with tremendous dignity.

LUX
Sasha. Now.

INT. BLACKWOOD CAFETERIA — SAME TIME

FAMINE sits at the counter.

The LUNCH LADY slides her a coffee.

A courier sets down a small clay pot sealed with wax.

COURIER
Return for Famine.

FAMINE
I’ve never lost anything in my fucking life.

COURIER

He squints.

COURIER

The smell that comes out is ancient. Rich. Lentils, onions, something that hasn’t been grown since the Bronze Age.

Famine inhales.

Her eyes fill.

FAMINE
Ur. Riverside. I put this down to go and wither a harvest and when I came back some fucking shepherd had taken it.

LUNCH LADY
That’s four thousand years old, honey.

FAMINE

FAMINE

LUNCH LADY
Don’t you dare tell the health inspector.

FAMINE
I am the health inspector of the soul.

LUNCH LADY
That’s not a job.

FAMINE
It was in Ur.

ACT TWO

INT. LOST & FOUND — INSIDE THE CUPBOARD — CONTINUOUS

The cupboard is much bigger on the inside.

Not magically bigger. Just badly measured, for four hundred years, in a way the building finds funny.

Shelves stretch into the dark: umbrellas, single gloves, a hundred thousand pens, a suit of armor with a note reading STILL IN IT, three violins, a pram, a canoe.

Kevin sits on a high shelf between a box of mittens and a stuffed owl.

A paper tag hangs from his wrist.

He looks… peaceful.

The OWL turns its head.

OWL
First time?

KEVIN
On a shelf? Yes.

OWL
You get used to it. Nobody asks you to do anything.

Kevin considers this.

KEVIN
Nobody asks you to do anything?

OWL
I’ve been here since a taxidermy class in 1952. Not one meeting.

Kevin leans back against the mittens.

They’re very soft.

KEVIN
Oh.

Music begins: a music box, then a soft shuffle beat, then a whole cupboard of lost objects humming along.

“FINDERS KEEPERS”

DORIS
Every glove is one of a pair.
Every scarf was somebody’s care.
Every key was someone’s door.
Nobody wants them anymore.

Finders keepers. Losers weep.
The shelves are high. The dark is deep.
Leave your name and leave your claim.
They never fucking come again.

The objects join in.

A SINGLE GLOVE
I was on a hand at a football game.
I fell in a puddle. Nobody came.

AN UMBRELLA

Turns out the categorical imperative is “can’t.”

THE SUIT OF ARMOR, muffled
I’M STILL IN HERE.

DORIS
We know, Gareth.

THE SUIT OF ARMOR
SINCE THE FENCING CLUB, DORIS.

DORIS
We all have our stories, Gareth.

Kevin, very quietly, begins to sway.

KEVIN
No inbox. No queue.
No form, no review.
No printer that hates me,
Nobody for me to do.

OWL
Welcome to the shelf.

KEVIN
I could live on the shelf.

ALL OBJECTS
Finders keepers! Losers weep!
Lay down your tag and go to sleep!
Nobody comes, and nobody calls,
And that’s the best thing about walls!

Doris looks up at Kevin.

She almost smiles. On a coat rack, this is mostly the hooks.

DORIS
You’re the first one in thirty years who hasn’t screamed.

KEVIN
I scream inside.

DORIS
That’s why I like you.

INT. LUX AND VELVETTE’S PENTHOUSE — LIVING ROOM — LATER

SASHA reads the Lost & Found bylaws.

They are bound in calfskin and have been amended by hand in eleven centuries of ink.

POPPY sits on the arm of the sofa eating macarons from a returned box she is fairly sure wasn’t hers.

Velvette has Gerald tucked into her chain like a corsage.

Lux holds the diary in her lap with both hands. The lock is intact. For now.

SASHA

SASHA

VELVETTE

SASHA

POPPY

SASHA

Closes it.

LUX

VELVETTE

POPPY

YUKI

Somewhere, a bass line.

A boy is wearing a traffic cone.

YUKI

MINA
Since 1611.

YUKI

MINA

YUKI

ACT FOUR

Article Four.

Five eleven in shoes, five ten without, because the left heel of his right shoe is worn down from the way he stands when somebody’s about to ask him to do something. Brown eyes, but they go green in the fluorescent light by the fridge, which is the only light he stands under because he’s always at the fucking fridge. Mug says WORLD’S OKAYEST EMPLOYEE, which he bought for himself. Hums the same four notes of a song he’s never told anyone the name of. Apologizes to the printer every morning. Not allergic to cats but says he is because a cat once looked at him. Is the only person in this building who has ever said thank you to me for a form.

YUKI
Then fucking do it.

MINA
One problem.

LUX

Takes what?

Whatever’s closest and least wanted.
