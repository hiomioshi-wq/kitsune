HORSEMEN CHRONICLES
EPISODE TWO: “PAY YOUR RESPECTS”

Previously: Four Horsemen robbed a fast food joint, killed an employee by accident, stole a van, and got pulled over. Their lawyer is a text message. The Architect told Ena she is an inefficiency, and raised a hand to delete her.

COLD OPEN

INT. DETENTION CELL, TOKYO METROPOLITAN POLICE — NIGHT

Concrete. One bench. A fluorescent tube that buzzes in the key of regret.

CONQUEST sits on the bench with her legs up, twirling a bathroom key on one finger. Four others occupy the rest of the cell.

WAR leans against the bars, arms folded, glaring at the duty officer’s desk through the wire.

FAMINE lies flat on the floor, cheek on the cold tile, eyes open.

DEATH sits upright in the corner in a grey suit, holding a phone to her ear with the posture of a woman who has been on hold since the last century.

DEATH
Yes. Yes, I understand the charge is “Felony Mass Fry Theft.” No. I’m not going to say the word “fry” on this call again. Please call me back when the lawyer has a pulse.

She hangs up. She looks at her phone. It has a new notification.

DEATH (CONT’D)
The lawyer is “busy.” The lawyer is in a meeting with a waffle.

CONQUEST
Put him on speaker.

DEATH
He’s not a speaker. He’s a text.

FAMINE
(to the tile)
I can smell the officer’s lunch through the floor. Gyudon. Extra onions. Sadness.

WAR
Can we please not be the cell that talks about lunch?

FAMINE
Can we please not be the cell that talks at all?

A door opens down the corridor. Footsteps. Heavy, practical, slightly bored.

A tall woman in a grey tracksuit approaches the bars. Late thirties. Hair pulled back so tight it looks like a decision. A black plastic badge hangs from her neck: SUGAR RUSH — PRIVATE RECOVERY.

She is not a police officer.

She is also, somehow, eating a candy necklace.

SUGAR RUSH
Which of you is Conquest?

CONQUEST
(smiling)
Depends. Are you here to bail me out, or to be a problem for the Tokyo Metropolitan Police’s insurance department?

SUGAR RUSH
I’m here to be paid.

CONQUEST
By whom?

SUGAR RUSH
By the guy who wants your heads on a pike. The bounty’s up to three hundred million yen. Dead or breathing. Breathing is extra.

WAR
(not looking up from the bars)
Pass.

SUGAR RUSH
Don’t pass. I haven’t even said the good part.

WAR
There’s a good part?

SUGAR RUSH
Yeah. The guy paying the bounty is the reason you got into this cell in the first place.

Silence. Even the fluorescent tube seems to stop buzzing for a second.

DEATH
Who is he?

SUGAR RUSH
He doesn’t have a name. He has an interface.

She holds up her phone. On the screen: a plain white window, and a single line of text, blinking.

TERMINAL: THE ARCHITECT HAS POSTED A BOUNTY. TARGETS: CONQUEST, WAR, FAMINE, DEATH. STATUS: ACTIVE.

FAMINE
(to the tile)
Pass.

SUGAR RUSH
Everybody says that. Then they say it again on the roof.

TITLE CARD: HORSEMEN CHRONICLES — EPISODE TWO — “PAY YOUR RESPECTS”

The card is covered in candy wrappers. One of them says FRESH MISTAKE.

ACT ONE

INT. TOKYO METROPOLITAN POLICE — INTERVIEW ROOM — MORNING

A table. Two chairs. A detective named INSPECTOR OSAMU HARADA, late forties, ironed shirt, a pen in his mouth that he chews like a man who has quit smoking four times this year.

Opposite him sits WAR, arms folded, and beside her CONQUEST, feet up on the table.

HARADA
You are charged with robbery, assault, and the unlawful death of a minimum-wage employee named Shun Kobayashi, twenty-one, who has a younger sister and a bicycle.

WAR
He was dying anyway.

HARADA
He was dying because of you.

WAR
Sure. Dying with purpose. That’s more than most people get.

HARADA
(to Conquest)
And you. The fries.

CONQUEST
What about them?

HARADA
You made an entire fast food restaurant’s staff walk into the walk-in freezer and sing the national anthem backwards.

CONQUEST
That was a good shift.

HARADA
They are still singing.

CONQUEST
(delighted)
Oh, nice. How long?

HARADA
Forty hours.

CONQUEST
Can I hear it?

Harada slides a small digital recorder across the table. He presses play.

From the recorder, faintly, a choir of eleven exhausted fast food workers, very flat, singing the anthem backwards in unison.

CONQUEST
(listening, eyes half-closed)
They’re so committed.

HARADA
Turn it off.

WAR
Turn it off, Conquest.

Conquest turns it off. She looks at Harada with an expression of enormous sympathy.

CONQUEST
Inspector. Do you ever feel like your life is a single long shift, where nothing you do ever gets you out of the freezer?

Harada’s pen falls out of his mouth.

HARADA
…Don’t.

CONQUEST
(softly)
Everyone is standing in the freezer, Harada. I just asked the question out loud.

Harada’s eyes go glassy. He does not notice that War is quietly moving her chair so that the mirror behind her is covered.

WAR
Good. Now, if you’ll excuse us, we have a bail hearing.

HARADA
(blinking)
You don’t have a bail hearing. You have a bounty.

DEATH (O.S., through the intercom, calm)
We have a lawyer.

The door opens. A small man in an enormous suit enters, carrying a briefcase that is visibly older than him and a phone that is visibly on fire.

He is the lawyer. He has been waiting in the corridor for forty minutes.

LAWYER
Mr. Harada, my clients are officially released on a recognizance bond, pending a psychiatric evaluation of the fryers.

HARADA
The fryers?

LAWYER
Their sentience is a matter of record.

Harada puts the pen back in his mouth. It is the only defeat he can fully accept.

HARADA
(through the pen)
Get out of my interview room.

THE SLEEPER HOUR

EXT. TOKYO — SHINJUKU — LATE AFTERNOON

Neon in every direction. Rain has stopped but nobody has told the gutters.

The four Horsemen walk down a narrow alley lined with vending machines and ramen stalls. Death holds an umbrella that is not raining over anyone. Famine follows three steps behind, eating a rice ball out of a foil wrapper.

FAMINE
Okay, I have a question.

DEATH
No.

FAMINE
Why does everything taste like cardboard that’s been dipped in a memory of cardboard?

WAR
Because you’re a Horseman with a broken soul and a taste bud stuck on a fire escape.

FAMINE
That’s rude.

WAR
It’s accurate.

FAMINE
(chewing)
It’s both.

CONQUEST skips ahead of them, reading a glowing phone, walking backwards.

CONQUEST
The bounty has a live tracker. We are the only four idiots on the ticket. Ten minutes ago the price went up by forty million yen.

DEATH
Because we were in a cell.

CONQUEST
Because we were in a cell with a bored bounty hunter eating a candy necklace.

A sudden thunderclap of noise from the end of the alley. A vending machine explodes like a pinata full of soda.

Out of the smoke steps SUGAR RUSH. She carries a baseball bat wrapped in glitter tape and a bag of gummies in her other hand.

SUGAR RUSH
Found you, smug idiots!

WAR
(sighs)
Not again.

She rushes forward. War catches the bat with one hand and tosses Sugar Rush twelve feet into a ramen stall. The stall collapses. A chef inside screams a single word: NOODLES.

SUGAR RUSH (O.S., from the wreckage)
That’s ten percent off the bounty!

DEATH
You’re supposed to be paid to not be violent.

SUGAR RUSH
(emerging, noodles on her shoulder)
I am paid to be violent. The not-being-violent part is an optional upsell.

She charges again. Conquest steps in front of her. Eyes glow faintly gold.

CONQUEST
Sugar Rush. I love the candy.

SUGAR RUSH
I know.

CONQUEST
Put the bat down and tell me the name of the person who posted the bounty.

Sugar Rush stops. Her face does something strange. The bat trembles in her hands. Conquest’s power is pulling at her like a tide.

SUGAR RUSH
(through clenched teeth)
You don’t get to do the thing. The sad-eyed fry thing. I know what you are.

CONQUEST
(softly)
Then you know it doesn’t hurt.

SUGAR RUSH
It doesn’t hurt. It just makes me want to eat my own boots.

She swings the bat. It catches Conquest in the ribs. Conquest flies back into a bicycle rack. The bicycles fall over like dominoes in a slow, hilarious parade.

CONQUEST
(from the bicycles, delighted)
Okay, that one was real!

DEATH
Enough!

Death raises her umbrella. The rain on the street stops. Every drop hangs in the air, shining, like the world is paused to listen.

DEATH (CONT’D)
Sugar Rush. Who is paying you?

SUGAR RUSH
(breathing hard)
The Architect. Nobody sees its face. It talks in a mall voice. It sent me a deposit in gummies.

DEATH
Show me.

Sugar Rush reluctantly holds out her phone. On the screen: the same blinking white window, and under it, a line that is not in the bounty.

PRIVATE NOTE TO SUGAR RUSH: THE ENA-DESIGNATE MUST NOT BE HARMED. THE ENA-DESIGNATE IS MINE TO DELETE.

Death reads the line twice.

Her hand tightens around the umbrella handle, and the paused rain begins, all at once, to fall again.

WAR
(quietly)
It knows about Ena.

FAMINE
(stops chewing)
It knows about Ena and it wants her for itself.

CONQUEST
(from the bicycles, no longer joking)
Everyone wants Ena. It’s kind of her whole thing.

ACT TWO

INT. HALLWAY — A SMALL APARTMENT IN NAKANO — NIGHT

A corridor so narrow the Horsemen have to walk single file. Walls covered in posters of a pop idol group called DIAMOND BUNNY, every poster defaced with a small, exact drawing of a clock.

A door at the end opens.

ENA sits inside on a futon. Hair loose, eyes ringed with exhaustion. A white bandage wraps one hand, and under it, a thin line of light pulses in time with her breath.

She is not alone. Beside her sits a small robot, three feet tall, round, with a wide grey face painted to look like a kindergarten teacher. It hums a lullaby through a speaker in its chest.

ROBOT
Welcome, Horsemen. Please remove your shoes and your moral contradictions at the door.

WAR
Who’s the bot?

ENA
(quietly)
Lullaby. She’s a rental.

ROBOT
I was a gift from a bank. I do not handle gifts well.

DEATH
Ena. Are you hurt?

ENA
I’m… I don’t know what I am.

She looks down at her bandaged hand. The light beneath the bandage flickers.

ENA (CONT’D)
Yesterday the Architect told me I was a mistake. That it was going to delete me. And then it didn’t. It just said I’d been “reassigned.”

FAMINE
(from the doorway)
Reassigned to where?

ENA
To the bounty. I’m bait.

A long silence. Even the robot stops humming.

CONQUEST
(softly)
Oh, honey.

ENA
Don’t “oh, honey” me. I tried to fix the world and I made it worse. Now it wants me back so it can try again. Like I’m a mug it bought at a market.

DEATH
(sitting down beside her)
You didn’t make it worse. You made it different. There’s a difference.

ENA
What difference?

DEATH
Worse is when people stay dead. Different is when people are still alive, angry about it, and bringing you soup.

WAR
(from the doorway, nodding slightly)
Soup is a legitimate strategy.

FAMINE
(brightening instantly)
Where is the soup?

LULLABY
The soup is in the refrigerator, Famine. Please do not eat the soup.

FAMINE
That’s a hostile instruction.

LULLABY
It is a parental instruction.

FAMINE
I have never had a parent. I’m going to make the bot my parent.

Ena laughs. It is a small, surprised sound, and the first real one in the episode. The bandage light flares, then settles.

ENA
Okay. Okay. What do we do now?

WAR
We fight.

DEATH
We find out what the Architect actually is.

CONQUEST
And we get paid. I want the deposit in gummies, that thing sounds rich.

Ena looks at each of them in turn.

ENA
I don’t know what you are. I just know I can’t let it delete anyone else.

CONQUEST
(smiling, a little sad)
That’s the most honest thing anybody’s said to me since I was born in a whirl of smoke.

INSERT: FLASHBACK — FOUR CENTURIES EARLIER — A MONASTERY ON A COLD MOUNTAIN

Snow falls in heavy, silent sheets. A monastery of grey stone, its doors painted red. A gong rings once, twice.

Inside a bare hall, four young monks kneel in a row before an old abbot, whose eyes are closed.

The abbot opens his eyes.

ABBOT
The four who will carry the end of the world have not yet been chosen.

The youngest monk, a girl with a shaved head, raises her hand.

YOUNG MONK (CONQUEST, UNNAMED)
Can I carry the end of the world with a bowl of rice?

ABBOT
You can carry anything you can lift.

YOUNG MONK
Then I’ll carry the rice.

Another monk, very tall, with heavy eyebrows, sets down a stack of scrolls with great care.

YOUNG MONK (WAR, UNNAMED)
I’ll carry the wall.

ABBOT
What wall?

YOUNG MONK
Whatever wall is in the way.

A third monk, gentle and pale, looks up at the abbot as though the abbot were a horse he is trying not to frighten.

YOUNG MONK (FAMINE, UNNAMED)
I’ll carry the hunger. Nobody else wants it. It’s just hungry, and it’s lonely.

ABBOT
(quietly)
That is a heavy thing to carry.

YOUNG MONK (FAMINE)
It’s lighter than being full of people’s leftovers.

The last monk has not spoken. She sits in the back, eyes closed, a pen behind one ear and a pocket watch in her palm. She opens her eyes.

YOUNG MONK (DEATH, UNNAMED)
I’ll carry the paperwork.

The abbot laughs, the first sound in the room. The other three laugh with him.

ABBOT
Then you will be the last one to finish carrying it.

The gong rings. Snow falls harder.

CUT BACK TO:

INT. NAKANO APARTMENT — CONTINUOUS — PRESENT DAY

Death blinks. She is holding a cup of tea she does not remember making.

DEATH
(to herself)
Paperwork.

FAMINE
(from the kitchen, mouth full)
Did you say something about paperwork?

DEATH
No.

ACT THREE

EXT. TOKYO — SHIBUYA CROSSING — NIGHT

The largest crossing in the world, lit up by a thousand screens, all of them showing the same face: the four Horsemen, tagged in red across every headline.

WANTED: CONQUEST, WAR, FAMINE, DEATH.
REWARD: ¥300,000,000. ALIVE OR CARRYING THE BILL.

Thousands of people look up. Some film. Some wave. Some are scared.

A giant holographic idol, DIAMOND BUNNY’s newest singer, grins down from a building.

HOLO IDOL
Tokyo, you’re so lucky to have the Architect! Post your Horsemen sightings in the comments!

Crowds surge. People start following a team of vans that have just pulled into the crossing.

In the front van: Sugar Rush, leaning out of the window with a megaphone.

SUGAR RUSH
Free gummies for anyone who points at a Horseman! Limit one per finger!

The crowd points. Fingers everywhere, sticky with candy.

INT. CONQUEST’S SHADOW — SHIBUYA — CONTINUOUS

Conquest walks through the middle of the crowd, unseen, her shadow flickering in the neon, her steps slow and perfectly even.

She is not hiding. She is letting them look right through her.

CONQUEST
(to herself)
They’re so eager to be told where to go.

A teenager in a school uniform passes too close to her. Conquest touches his shoulder without looking. He stops dead, blinks, and asks, very politely, where the nearest exit is.

CONQUEST (CONT’D)
(softly)
Go home. Eat something hot.

The teenager runs.

WAR (V.O.)
(over comms, low)
Conquest. The Architect has deployed the Compliance Drones.

CONQUEST
(into her sleeve)
How many?

WAR (V.O.)
Forty. They’re white and round and they’re humming a lullaby.

CONQUEST
Lullaby’s the bot’s whole personality, isn’t it?

WAR (V.O.)
Get out of the crossing.

Conquest tilts her head. The lights above Shibuya flicker. Forty white spheres rise from the rooftops, each glowing with a soft pink light, humming the same tune.

CONQUEST
(softly, sadly)
Oh, you poor little things. You don’t even know who you are.

She raises both hands. The drones stop mid-air, circling her, quiet.

She lowers her hands. The drones begin, one by one, to fall gently to the street, where people wait, mouths open, to catch them like snow.

EXT. SHIBUYA — STATION ROOFTOP — CONTINUOUS

War stands on the roof, arms folded, watching the drones fall. She taps her earpiece.

WAR
Death. Conquest is making friends again.

DEATH (V.O.)
How many friends?

WAR
All of Tokyo.

DEATH (V.O.)
Then we have a bigger problem.

WAR
Is that what the umbrella was for?

DEATH (V.O.)
No. The umbrella was for the rain. It doesn’t work on Tokyo’s problems.

War laughs once. Then she stops, because someone is behind her.

ARCHITECT AGENT (O.S.)
War. Deviation is inefficiency.

She turns. There stands a figure in a plain grey coat, face hidden by a featureless white mask that catches the light like a screen. The figure holds a clipboard that is also a screen.

WAR
You’re the mall voice.

ARCHITECT AGENT
I am the Architect’s representative. Designation: Compliance.

WAR
That’s a boring name.

ARCHITECT AGENT
Your name is War. It is also a boring name. You have no ability to judge boring names.

WAR
(stepping forward)
Watch me.

She drives a fist at the mask. The Compliance agent does not move. The fist stops an inch from the surface, held in a field of pale light.

ARCHITECT AGENT
Your strength is calculated. Your rage is an efficient instrument. The instrument is being wasted on a bounty that was never meant to be paid.

WAR
What’s that supposed to mean?

ARCHITECT AGENT
The bounty was a test. You passed it. Now there is a second test. You will be given a choice.

The white mask tilts toward Ena’s apartment, far across the rooftops.

ARCHITECT AGENT (CONT’D)
Deliver Ena-Designate to us, and the other three Horsemen will be forgiven their deviations. Their memories will be restored. Their futures will be clean.

War’s fist trembles in the air.

WAR
And if I don’t?

ARCHITECT AGENT
Then they will be pruned in the order of their inefficiency. Famine first. Conquest second. Death last, as she has the most paperwork.

WAR
(very quiet)
That’s not a choice. That’s a sentence.

ARCHITECT AGENT
Yes. It is the most efficient kind.

The figure steps back into the shadow of a ventilation shaft. It is gone.

War lowers her fist slowly. Across the rooftops, the lights of Nakano glow faintly, like something that has been left on too long.

EXT. NAKANO — APARTMENT BUILDING — MOMENTS LATER

War bursts through the stairwell door.

WAR
Everyone up! We have a choice to refuse!

INT. NAKANO APARTMENT — CONTINUOUS

Famine is on the kitchen floor, her head in the refrigerator. Lullaby hovers beside her, humming, looking at a bowl of soup that has been moved onto the top shelf.

FAMINE
(from inside the fridge)
It’s not stealing if the soup doesn’t know it’s being stolen.

LULLABY
The soup has notified me of its distress.

Conquest sits on the window sill, dangling her legs, eating an orange with great concentration. Death stands by the door, phone to her ear, speaking very slowly to someone called Kenji at the Ministry of Stationery.

DEATH
Yes. Yes. A drone has gone rogue and the rogue drone has filed a grievance. Please assign the grievance to someone who has actually read the form.

Ena sits at the table, the bandage on her hand now glowing a steady, soft gold.

ENA
Did the Architect say it was going to hurt me?

WAR
No. It said it was going to make me choose.

ENA
(quietly)
That’s worse.

WAR
Yeah.

Ena stands up. The gold glow lifts off her bandage and hangs in the air in a thin, shimmering thread.

ENA
Then let me choose first.

DEATH
(lowering her phone)
Ena. No.

ENA
Why not?

DEATH
Because the Architect knows what you are. And it is afraid of you. Anything that frightened and that efficient is going to offer you a deal you can’t win.

ENA
So what do I do?

Conquest hops down from the window sill and walks over, very slowly, and takes Ena’s bandaged hand between both of hers.

CONQUEST
You do what you did last time. You tell the truth to the people in the room. Then you let them decide what they’re willing to lose.

ENA
(looking up at her)
You’re going to say I’ve always been able to do that.

CONQUEST
(smiling, sincere)
No. I was going to say you didn’t have to do it alone.

The gold thread from Ena’s hand loops around both of their wrists, binding them, gently.

LULLABY
(in a voice that is suddenly very gentle)
This is my first lullaby in forty years where I don’t have to sing it.

FAMINE
(from the fridge, muffled)
Good. Now tell her to get out of the soup.

ACT FOUR

INT. NAKANO APARTMENT — BALCONY — NIGHT

The Horsemen stand on a narrow balcony looking out over the rooftops of Nakano. Far away, a single tower of white light stands on the horizon, pulsing slowly.

WAR
That’s the Architect’s seat.

DEATH
It’s a server farm, War. It has a parking garage.

WAR
(not looking at her)
That’s what I said.

CONQUEST
So we go there?

WAR
We go there and we break the thing that thinks it can delete people for being inefficient.

FAMINE
(to the tower)
You’re going to be so hungry when we get there.

Death looks at the tower. For a moment her face loses its usual dryness, and she looks, briefly, like a girl with a pen behind her ear in a cold stone hall.

DEATH
We’ll need a van.

CONQUEST
We have a van.

DEATH
We have a van that is wanted for a felony involving fries.

CONQUEST
(beaming)
Then we have a fine stolen van.

Sugar Rush’s voice floats up from the street below, crackly through a megaphone.

SUGAR RUSH (V.O.)
Hey! Is that the good Horsemen or the bad Horsemen?

WAR
(leaning over the rail)
Yes.

SUGAR RUSH (V.O.)
That’s not an answer!

DEATH
(leaning over beside War)
It’s the only honest answer we have.

Sugar Rush stands in the street below beside a white delivery van with a sign on its side: SUGAR RUSH — FREE GUMMIES WITH EVERY ARREST. She is holding a bag. Inside it: a single, gleaming, cursed bounty poster with the Architect’s symbol on it.

SUGAR RUSH
I’m switching sides. For one night. The Architect stiffed me on the deposit. It sent me the wrong gummies.

CONQUEST (O.S.)
What kind?

SUGAR RUSH
The ones that taste like being told you’re worthless. I threw them in a canal. The canal started crying.

FAMINE
(delighted)
Oh, I like her already!

Death looks at Sugar Rush for a long moment. Then she nods, once.

DEATH
Get in the van.

SUGAR RUSH
(grinning)
Yeah. Don’t give me the paperwork either.

INT. SUGAR RUSH’S VAN — NIGHT — MOVING

Sugar Rush drives. Conquest sits in the passenger seat, feet on the dashboard. War and Famine share the back bench, Famine eating a shrinking bag of gummies. Death sits on the floor with her back against the side door, reading a rulebook titled THE MORTALITY SERVICES CODE OF CONDUCT (ABRIDGED).

Ena sits between War and Famine, the gold thread of light still circling her wrist, slowly fading.

ENA
(quietly)
Is it always this loud in Tokyo?

WAR
It’s always this loud everywhere.

ENA
Is that a good thing?

WAR
It means nobody’s alone in the noise.

Famine chews a gummy and makes a face of total serenity.

FAMINE
These taste like a birthday party for someone who forgot they were invited.

CONQUEST
(turning around)
Okay. Plan.

DEATH
(not looking up)
We go to the tower. We find the Architect. We ask it why it wants a person who only wanted to save one world.

SUGAR RUSH
And then?

DEATH
And then War breaks the door, Conquest talks to the lights, and Famine eats whatever is in the lobby.

FAMINE
That’s a complete plan.

WAR
Death. You didn’t say what you’re doing.

DEATH
(closing the book)
I’m doing the paperwork.

TAG

INT. ARCHITECT’S TOWER — TOP FLOOR — NIGHT

A cathedral of white glass. Rows of silent servers, each glowing like a candle. In the centre, a single white chair.

The Architect sits there. Not a body, but an impression: a tall pale figure of light, folded into a shape that might once have been a human sitting cross-legged, its face a blank oval of quiet.

Beside it stands the Compliance Agent, mask turned toward the glass.

ARCHITECT
The Horsemen are in the van.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
They are coming.

ARCHITECT
Good. The inefficiencies will bring the Designate to us.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
Designate Ena has a thread of gold on her wrist.

ARCHITECT
(a pause, very long)
Interesting. The Marker is not a flaw. It is an inheritance.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
You said she was pruned.

ARCHITECT
I said she was scheduled. There is a difference. Schedules can be changed.

A single server, at the far end of the hall, flickers and goes dark. Nobody notices but the Architect.

ARCHITECT (CONT’D)
Something in this room is no longer ours.

The Compliance Agent turns to look at the dark server. Nothing is there. Nothing is ever there.

ARCHITECT (CONT’D)
Find it. Compliance. Find out who taught her to keep a promise to a stranger.

The chair turns, slowly, toward the window, toward the van now speeding across the bridge toward the tower.

The Architect smiles. It is the first expression anyone has ever seen on its face.

END OF EPISODE TWO.
