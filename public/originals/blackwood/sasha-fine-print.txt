SASHA
A BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY SPINOFF

“FINE PRINT”

Content warning: Rated R. Strong language, dark humour, workplace violence, a lawsuit against a god, one very bad seating plan, and an adult woman losing her composure at a stationery cupboard.

Set the night before Lux and Velvette’s wedding. It follows “Lux & Velvette: Nine Courses”, though it works on its own.

TEASER

INT. SASHA’S OFFICE — NIGHT

An immaculate office in grey and white, thirty floors above the Ring Road. Shelves of binders sorted by colour. A desk so tidy it looks unoccupied. A single stem of white orchid in a vase. On the wall, a framed certificate: SASHA OKONKWO-REYES, J.D., S-TIER OWNERSHIP, MAGNA CUM LAUDE. Under it, in smaller frame, a Post-it note in a child’s handwriting: YOU CAN DO IT.

It is 11:47 p.m.

SASHA sits behind the desk. Early thirties, hair scraped back, wire spectacles, a grey pencil skirt, one earring missing, a bandage on the back of her right hand. In front of her are four stacks of paper: red, blue, green and, at the end, a black folder tied shut with ribbon.

On the screen of her laptop, a document is open. The title reads: MARRIAGE AGREEMENT: LUX HARROW & VELVETTE VOSS. The cursor is blinking at Clause 14.

On the corner of the desk sits a telephone. It begins to ring.

Sasha doesn’t look at it. She types a word, deletes it, types another word.

The phone rings eleven times. It stops.

It begins again.

SASHA
(not looking up)
It’s a quarter to midnight.

The phone doesn’t care. Sasha picks up the receiver without breaking eye contact with the screen.

SASHA (CONT’D)
Legal.

VOICE (V.O.)
(a cheerful baritone, absolutely enormous)
SASHA! There you are!

Sasha shuts her eyes.

SASHA
(quietly)
Hello, Conquest.

CONQUEST (V.O.)
Is it true you’re doing the contract?

SASHA
It’s true I’m doing the contract.

CONQUEST (V.O.)
I want a clause.

SASHA
(flat)
No.

CONQUEST (V.O.)
Just a small one! About being adored!

SASHA
(still flat)
The agreement contains forty-one clauses. None of them is about being adored.

CONQUEST (V.O.)
Add one!

SASHA
Goodnight, Conquest.

She hangs up. The phone rings immediately. She lifts the receiver an inch, sets it back down, and lifts it again.

SASHA (CONT’D)
(into the phone)
Legal.

VOICE (V.O.)
(a dry, ancient voice, extremely calm)
It’s Death.

Sasha says nothing.

DEATH (V.O.)
(pleasantly)
I’ve been told there’s a wedding.

SASHA
(very carefully)
There is.

DEATH (V.O.)
I wasn’t invited.

SASHA
(her eyes opening)
It’s a quiet wedding.

DEATH (V.O.)
I’ve seen the guest list. Famine is on it.

SASHA
(rubbing her forehead)
Famine is catering.

DEATH (V.O.)
(after a pause)
That’s fair.

Click. The line goes dead.

Sasha sets down the receiver. She takes off her spectacles and pinches the bridge of her nose, and a very small sound escapes her, a tiny, shaken laugh, which she suppresses at once.

She puts her glasses back on and looks at Clause 14.

SASHA (CONT’D)
(to the screen)
Don’t you dare be difficult.

The cursor blinks. Behind her, the black folder rustles by itself.

Sasha stops moving.

SASHA (CONT’D)
(without turning)
Not now.

The ribbon on the folder slowly unties itself.

ACT ONE

INT. SASHA’S OFFICE — CONTINUOUS

Sasha swivels her chair, very slowly, and looks at the black folder. The ribbon has fallen open. The cover has lifted an inch, as though something under it is breathing.

SASHA
(quietly)
I told you. After the wedding.

A voice speaks from inside the folder. It is small, dry, formal, and faintly aggrieved, like a bailiff.

FOLDER (V.O.)
(muffled)
It is after the wedding in fourteen minutes.

SASHA
(weary)
It’s before the wedding for another twenty-four hours.

FOLDER (V.O.)
Time is a clause like any other.

Sasha stands, crosses to the folder, and flips it open.

Inside lies a single document, thick as a phone book, printed on cream paper in an ancient and extremely clear typeface. The title page reads, in capitals: THE SECOND SCHEDULE. At the bottom, in small italics, someone has written: PROPERTY OF THE OFFICE OF THE ADVOCATE.

SASHA (CONT’D)
(dryly)
You’re supposed to be in the vault.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(from the paper itself)
I was borne to you in a courier’s satchel at half past nine. I understand you have been avoiding me.

SASHA
(sharply)
I have been drafting a marriage agreement.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
You have been drafting it for eleven hours and have written four lines.

SASHA
(rubbing her eyes)
It’s a delicate document.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
It is a contract between a woman who cannot be told no and a woman who has never said yes.

Sasha stops.

SASHA
(quietly)
That’s not a legal opinion.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
It is an observation.

Sasha sits on the edge of the desk and, for the first time, her composure slips. She is exhausted. Her hand is shaking very slightly on the folder.

SASHA
(softly)
What do you want?

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(with great dignity)
I want you to sign me.

SASHA
(flat)
No.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
It is the only way.

SASHA
(standing)
I am not signing the Second Schedule. I’m not putting my name to a document that would make me the Advocate.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
The Advocate is dead.

SASHA
(sharply)
The Advocate was a lawyer who spent four hundred years representing Hell’s interests and ended up as a footnote in a folder.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(patiently)
He ended up as a precedent.

Sasha stares at the page. Her hand has stopped shaking.

SASHA
(very quietly)
Say that again.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
Every contract between the living and the dead in this institution traces back to a single clause. I am the clause. You have been quietly working for me, Ms. Okonkwo-Reyes, for six years.

SASHA
(a harsh breath)
I’ve been working for Lux.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(gently)
And who do you think wrote the by-laws that make Lux possible?

Silence.

SASHA
(slowly)
You’re saying every loophole I’ve found—

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
I left them there.

Sasha takes one step back. Her face does something complicated.

SASHA
(whispering)
Why?

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
Because somebody has to be able to keep the dead from being owned.

She turns very slowly and looks at the orchid on her desk. It is a perfect white flower. She has watered it every morning for six years.

SASHA
(very low)
You’re the reason I’m good at this.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
No. You are good at this. I merely made the room for you.

Sasha stands there, shaking, with her glasses misting.

SASHA
(a rough whisper)
That’s a really horrible thing to say to a person at midnight.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(softly, almost kind)
It is a fine print.

Sasha laughs. She can’t help it. It comes out shaky and furious and ten years overdue. She covers her mouth with the back of her bandaged hand.

SASHA
(through the laugh)
God, you’re awful.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
I was drafted by a committee.

She takes a breath. She wipes her eyes. She straightens her spine, with a visible effort of will.

SASHA
(steadier)
What does signing do?

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
It makes you the Advocate. The office of last resort between the living and the dead. You would hold the power of attorney for everyone in this university.

SASHA
(quietly)
Everyone.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
Everyone who is dead. And everyone who will be.

Sasha looks at the black folder for a very long time.

SASHA
(finally)
That’s the opposite of a vacation.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(drily)
You have never taken a vacation.

SASHA
(a faint smile)
I took one in 2019. It was a funeral.

She closes the folder. She doesn’t tie the ribbon.

SASHA (CONT’D)
(quietly)
After the wedding.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(from inside the folder)
After the wedding.

She picks up her laptop. The cursor is still blinking at Clause 14.

SASHA
(to the screen)
All right. Let’s do this properly.

INT. SASHA’S OFFICE — LATER

A montage, in her own private idiom: Sasha at the desk with her sleeves rolled to the elbow, hair coming loose, typing; Sasha on the phone with Velvette, whispering: “It’s a ring. It’s a ruler. No, I’m not making it a rider”; Sasha rewriting Clause 14 on a whiteboard and crossing it out; Sasha eating cold noodles from a carton standing up; Sasha in the stationery cupboard, door shut, alone, pressing her forehead against a shelf of sticky notes and breathing.

SASHA (V.O.)
(over the above, dryly)
Clause fourteen. Property. “Each party shall retain sole ownership of her possessions, save for the following jointly held items…”

She is in the cupboard. She straightens. She wipes her eyes with the back of her hand and goes back out.

SASHA (V.O.)
“…the penthouse, the table, the ruler, and Gerald.”

She stops typing. She looks at the screen.

SASHA (CONT’D)
(softly)
Gerald’s the ruler.

She deletes “and Gerald.”

SASHA (CONT’D)
(beat)
Gerald is a person.

She types it back.

ACT TWO

INT. SASHA’S OFFICE — 2:16 A.M.

The office has become a war room. Papers cover every surface. The whiteboard is a thicket of arrows. The orchid has been moved to the floor. Sasha stands in the centre, hair down now, sleeves pushed up, the second earring found and placed on the desk like evidence.

At the door stands DEAN ASHDOWN, in a grey coat and a perfectly calm expression, holding a seating plan.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(evenly)
You’re working late.

SASHA
(not turning)
Dean Ashdown.

DEAN ASHDOWN
I understand I am sitting next to you.

SASHA
(a muscle in her jaw jumping)
So I’m told.

DEAN ASHDOWN
I wondered if we might discuss it.

SASHA
(very pleasantly)
No.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(unperturbed)
I have a few concerns.

SASHA
(turning at last)
You tried to poison our soup.

DEAN ASHDOWN
Your soup.

SASHA
(coldly)
You used my office to do it.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(inclining her head)
I used your sous-chef. Your office merely processed the paperwork.

SASHA
(very quietly)
I processed it.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(a dry look)
You did say “reasonable stress test.”

SASHA
(a flash of anger)
I was told it was a reasonable stress test.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(mildly)
By me.

SASHA
(breathing in through her nose)
Yes. By you. Which is why I’m going to be extremely cold at your table.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(a faint, patient smile)
I would expect nothing less.

She steps into the room and sets the seating plan on the desk. It is a beautiful hand-drawn chart, in ink, with every name in calligraphy.

DEAN ASHDOWN (CONT’D)
(quietly)
I wanted to apologise.

Sasha stares at her.

SASHA
(slowly)
You wanted what.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(with great composure)
You were collateral. I was aware of that. I do not usually do that to people I rely on.

SASHA
(stiffly)
You’ve never relied on me.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(dryly)
I attempted to hire you four times.

SASHA
(blinking)
That was recruitment.

DEAN ASHDOWN
It was reliance.

A silence.

SASHA
(carefully)
You want me to… accept an apology.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(a faint inclination of her head)
I want you to accept an offer.

SASHA
(narrowing her eyes)
There it is.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(with the smallest smile)
There it is.

She takes a folded card out of her breast pocket and sets it on the desk.

DEAN ASHDOWN (CONT’D)
(quietly)
Lakewood is looking for a General Counsel.

SASHA
(flatly)
No.

DEAN ASHDOWN
I would triple your salary.

SASHA
I don’t take money.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(unfazed)
I would give you a team of fifty.

SASHA
(very evenly)
I don’t want fifty people. I want one person to call me back.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(softly)
Who?

Sasha doesn’t answer. She is looking at the Post-it note on the wall, YOU CAN DO IT, and for a moment she is not an S-tier attorney. She is a very tired woman who has not been thanked in six years.

DEAN ASHDOWN (CONT’D)
(gently)
Ah.

SASHA
(not looking at her)
It’s not that.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(softly)
Isn’t it?

SASHA
(a hard laugh)
Lux never says thank you.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(with surprising gentleness)
No. She wouldn’t.

SASHA
(her voice steadying)
She says “darling.” She says “I knew you would.” She says “there’s a file on my desk.” She has never once said, “I couldn’t have done it without you.”

DEAN ASHDOWN
(softly)
But you’ve stayed.

SASHA
(shrugging, very small)
It’s a very interesting job.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(quietly)
It is not a job. It is a marriage.

Sasha looks at her sharply. The Dean holds her gaze.

DEAN ASHDOWN (CONT’D)
(evenly)
I recognise the symptoms. I have been married to Lakewood for thirty-one years.

A very long silence.

SASHA
(finally, hoarsely)
That’s a bleak thing to say at two in the morning.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(a faint dry smile)
It’s an observation.

Sasha takes the card. She doesn’t put it in her pocket. She holds it for a moment, turns it over, and slides it under the vase of the orchid.

SASHA
(quietly)
I’ll keep it.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(inclining her head)
That’s all I ask.

She turns to go. At the door she stops.

DEAN ASHDOWN (CONT’D)
(without turning)
Sasha.

SASHA
Yes.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(very quietly)
Your Clause Fourteen. The one about Gerald.

SASHA
(guarded)
What about it?

DEAN ASHDOWN
(a very slight smile)
It is the best clause I have read in forty years.

She is gone.

Sasha stands in the silent office with her hand on the back of her chair. After a moment she says, very quietly, to the empty room:

SASHA
(whispering)
Thank you.

She doesn’t seem to know who she is saying it to.

INT. SASHA’S OFFICE — 3:40 A.M.

The office is dark. A single lamp burns on the desk. The laptop is open to the final page of the document. Forty-one clauses, five hundred and twelve words in the signature block. At the bottom: two lines for two signatures and a third line, very small, titled WITNESS.

Sasha sits in front of it, her chin in her hand. She has fallen asleep sitting up. Her glasses are crooked.

The orchid, now back on the desk, has moved a very small distance toward her.

The door opens. VELVETTE slips in, in black, silent, boots in her hand. She crosses the carpet and stops beside the chair. She looks at the sleeping woman, at the empty noodle cartons, at the Post-it on the wall.

Her face goes soft.

She takes her jacket off, a black leather biker jacket with a silver chain at the collar, and lays it over Sasha’s shoulders.

Sasha stirs.

SASHA
(muttering, asleep)
Clause forty-one…

VELVETTE
(very softly)
Sleep, Sash.

SASHA
(not waking)
Gerald is a person.

VELVETTE
(a faint smile)
He is.

She reads the final page over Sasha’s shoulder. At the WITNESS line, she stops. She picks up the pen from the desk.

On the witness line, in her own spiky handwriting, she writes: S. OKONKWO-REYES. She puts the pen down very precisely, and, because nobody is watching, bends and kisses the top of Sasha’s head.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(a whisper)
Thank you.

She leaves as silently as she came.

On the desk, the paper rustles. The Second Schedule’s voice, tiny, from the folder:

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(quietly)
That, too, is a clause.

CUT TO BLACK.

ACT THREE

EXT. HELL’S PEAK ESTATES — CUL-DE-SAC — DAWN

The wedding is to be held on the central lawn. White chairs in rows. A canopy of white roses. A very long runway of crushed pearl gravel down the centre. A string quartet of ghosts in white tie tuning up under the flagpole. On the flag, in gold thread, the HOA motto has been altered: LOVE, ORDER, MUTUAL ASSURED DESTRUCTION.

Sasha, in a crisp grey suit and flat shoes, stands at the entrance with a clipboard. She has had two hours of sleep. She is holding a coffee in each hand.

A DELIVERY GHOST in a cap hurries past with a tray of macarons.

SASHA
(to the ghost)
Seven hundred, not eight.

DELIVERY GHOST
Eight, ma’am. Famine insisted.

SASHA
(closing her eyes)
Fine.

Behind her, FAMINE (a thin, kind, gaunt woman in an apron the colour of a cardigan) arrives with two trays and a quite dangerous quantity of bread.

FAMINE
(softly)
I made enough for everyone.

SASHA
(carefully)
Famine, please do not feed the guests.

FAMINE
(hurt)
It’s a wedding.

SASHA
(gently)
Last time you fed a wedding it turned into a famine.

FAMINE
(sniffing)
That was a misunderstanding.

SASHA
(taking one of the trays)
It was a seven-year drought.

FAMINE
(very small)
It was a very good bread.

SASHA
(after a beat, quietly)
I’ll take the sourdough.

FAMINE
(brightening)
Thank you.

Sasha sets the tray on a trestle table with the ghostly efficiency of a woman defusing a bomb. At the entrance, a long black car pulls up.

The door opens. LUX steps out in a white suit of astonishing cut, hair swept up, lips the colour of a very old rose. She is beaming.

LUX
(sweeping toward Sasha)
Darling!

SASHA
(not looking up)
You’re an hour early.

LUX
(kissing her cheek)
I couldn’t sleep.

SASHA
(dryly)
Neither could I.

LUX
(sweetly)
That’s because you worry.

SASHA
(very evenly)
I’m a lawyer.

LUX
(unrepentant)
You’re an angel.

She turns to go. Sasha takes a breath.

SASHA
(quietly)
Lux.

LUX
(turning)
Mm?

SASHA
(not meeting her eyes)
I finished the agreement.

LUX
(airily)
Of course you did.

SASHA
(carefully)
There’s a clause about Gerald.

LUX
(smiling)
Velvette told me.

SASHA
(a faint breath)
I wasn’t sure you’d like it.

LUX
(looking at her, really looking, for a moment)
Sasha.

SASHA
(very still)
Yes.

LUX
(quietly)
I couldn’t have done it without you.

The clipboard slips out of Sasha’s hand and clatters on the gravel.

Neither of them moves.

LUX (CONT’D)
(softly)
Not any of it. Not once. You’ve been in the room since the day I started and I have never said so, and I should have, and I’m sorry. I’m saying it now.

Sasha’s throat works. She cannot speak.

LUX (CONT’D)
(very gently)
Will you walk me down the aisle?

SASHA
(a choked whisper)
I’m not— I’m the witness.

LUX
(a faint smile)
You can be both.

SASHA
(a laugh and a sob together)
That’s a conflict of interest.

LUX
(gently, taking her hands)
Darling. It’s my wedding. I can have as many conflicts as I like.

Sasha is crying. She doesn’t try to stop. She nods. She takes the coffee, drinks it, hands it to the nearest ghost, and straightens her suit.

SASHA
(shakily)
I have to find a tissue.

LUX
(grinning)
Use my sleeve.

SASHA
(horrified)
It’s Armani.

LUX
(grandly)
It’s a very good sleeve.

Sasha laughs. She wipes her face on the sleeve of a white Armani suit. Somewhere behind them, a string quartet strikes up the wedding march a quarter-tone flat.

SASHA
(listening, wincing)
That’s flat.

LUX
(serenely)
It’s a ghost quartet. They’re all a little flat.

SASHA
(drily)
It’s a metaphor.

LUX
(hooking her arm through Sasha’s)
Shall we?

They walk together toward the canopy, Lux in white and Sasha in grey, past the rows of white chairs filling now with guests: a very tall man with a briefcase; a pair of cheerful teachers; a frightened Kevin and a translucent Kevina; Dean Ashdown in a grey hat, seated, exactly as planned, next to an empty chair; and at the very back, in a black robe, perfectly still, a figure with a scythe.

Sasha sees her. She falters.

SASHA
(whispering)
Lux.

LUX
(not looking)
I know.

SASHA
(whispering)
Did you invite her?

LUX
(sweetly)
Famine is catering.

SASHA
(a breath)
That’s not an answer.

LUX
(quietly)
She’s lonely, darling.

Sasha looks at the figure at the back. The figure inclines her hood a single, courteous inch.

SASHA
(very quietly)
I’ll make her a plate.

LUX
(squeezing her arm)
There you are.

They reach the canopy. At the end of the pearl runway, in black, with a silver ruler in her buttonhole, stands VELVETTE. She is crying already.

Sasha takes her place beside the registrar’s table. She lifts the pen. She looks at the two women facing each other under the roses, and in the quiet, the small voice of the Second Schedule comes up from the briefcase at her feet:

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(faintly)
Now?

SASHA
(under her breath)
Now.

She takes out the black folder, unties the ribbon, and opens it on the table.

SASHA (CONT’D)
(clearly, to the whole lawn)
Dearly beloved. We are gathered here under the by-laws of Hell’s Peak Estates, a gated community of good standing, to witness the lawful union of two persons. In accordance with Clause One, no one in attendance is permitted to say please.

A ripple of laughter. Lux’s shoulders shake. Velvette snorts through her tears.

SASHA (CONT’D)
(dry as dust)
Nor thank you. Nor sorry. In accordance with Clause Forty-One, an exception is made for the following.

She turns the page. Her voice is steady, and clear, and so quiet the whole lawn leans in.

SASHA (CONT’D)
Thank you.

She looks at the two of them.

SASHA (CONT’D)
(softly)
Please be happy.

For a long moment, nobody moves.

Then, from the very back of the lawn, a voice like an old door opens in the silence.

GRIM REAPER
(from under her hood, hoarse, entirely sincere)
Please.

Lux begins to cry. Velvette begins to laugh. The quartet misses its cue entirely.

Sasha sets down her pen, takes off her glasses, and presses her fingers to her eyes, and for the first time in six years nobody is asking her for anything.

SLOW FADE.

INT. SASHA’S OFFICE — NIGHT

Dark. The office is silent. The only light is a thin line from the street. On the desk, a single white orchid in a vase; next to it, a business card with the Lakewood crest, slightly bent.

The door opens. Sasha comes in alone, in the grey suit, shoes in her hand, hair down, with a plate of cold sourdough. She sets it on the desk, sits, and, for a moment, does absolutely nothing at all.

On the wall, the Post-it note. YOU CAN DO IT.

She looks at it for a long time.

She picks up a pen. Under the child’s handwriting, in her own, very small and precise, she adds three words.

SASHA
(softly, writing)
I did it.

She caps the pen. The orchid, at the corner of the desk, turns very slightly toward her.

The black folder, on the shelf, rustles.

SECOND SCHEDULE (V.O.)
(faintly)
Now?

SASHA
(not looking round, eating a piece of bread)
Monday.

CUT TO BLACK.

THE END
