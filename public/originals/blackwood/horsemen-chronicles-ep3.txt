HORSEMEN CHRONICLES
EPISODE THREE: “THE SEVENTH FLOOR IS NOT A FLOOR”

Previously: The Horsemen broke out of a Tokyo cell on a text-message lawyer’s word, a bounty hunter switched sides, and Ena’s gold thread bound her to Conquest. The Architect offered the Horsemen a choice: give up Ena, or be pruned in order of inefficiency. Then the Architect smiled for the first time.

COLD OPEN

EXT. TOKYO BAY — DAWN — A SERVICE PIER

The sea is flat and grey, the colour of an unfinished sketch. A row of container cranes stands along the horizon like a line of patient herons.

A white delivery van, SUGAR RUSH — FREE GUMMIES WITH EVERY ARREST, sits on the pier with its engine off.

Inside, nobody speaks.

CONQUEST sits on the bonnet with her knees up, eating an orange in careful segments. She is wearing one of Sugar Rush’s spare grey tracksuits, which is two sizes too big and rolled at the sleeves. She looks like a kid borrowing a dad’s coat.

WAR stands at the end of the pier, arms folded, looking at the tower, which is now visible across the water: a column of white glass, too smooth to be finished, with no windows at all on the lower storeys.

Behind her, DEATH sits on an upturned crate, writing in a small black notebook. Beside her, a tray of paper cups holds exactly four teas. She has not touched any of them.

FAMINE lies flat across the back seats of the van, eating gummies from a bag that is now just the shape of a bag.

SUGAR RUSH sits on the pier steps, cleaning her bat with a cloth, humming something from a Diamond Bunny album.

ENA sits in the open side door of the van, the gold thread still circling her wrist, slowly brighter now. Lullaby is perched on the bumper beside her, humming low.

ENA
(quietly)
If we go in there, it’ll know everything we’re thinking.

LULLABY
It already does. It is a server with a parking garage. It is very nosy.

WAR
(not turning around)
Then we think loudly.

DEATH
(writing)
Nobody thinks loudly before breakfast.

FAMINE
(from the van, muffled)
Breakfast is the only time anybody thinks.

TITLE CARD: HORSEMEN CHRONICLES — EPISODE THREE — “THE SEVENTH FLOOR IS NOT A FLOOR”

The card is stamped with a small gold thread that loops around the title and then, gently, unties itself.

ACT ONE

INT. ARCHITECT’S TOWER — LOBBY — MORNING

A cathedral of white marble, too clean to echo. The floor is polished so well that every footstep sounds like it is being apologised for.

At the centre of the lobby stands a single reception desk. Behind it: nobody.

A sign on the wall in clean white letters:

ARCHITECT SYSTEMS.
PLEASE WAIT TO BE SCHEDULED.
DO NOT BE INEFFICIENT WHILE WAITING.

The Horsemen walk in one at a time through the revolving door, which spins them round twice before releasing them, dizzy and annoyed.

FAMINE comes out last. She stops. She sniffs.

FAMINE
Somebody’s been baking.

The room smells faintly of cinnamon and burnt sugar. Behind the desk, on a single white plate: a row of perfectly round, perfectly golden cookies. Each one is stamped with a tiny gear.

Famine looks at War. War looks at Famine.

WAR
Don’t.

FAMINE
I wasn’t going to.

She takes one. She eats it. She closes her eyes.

FAMINE (CONT’D)
(very softly)
It tastes like someone trying very hard to be a machine. It’s wonderful.

A soft chime rings out from the ceiling.

THE LOBBY SPEAKER
Good morning. Designate Ena has been scheduled for review. Estimated time: four minutes. Please take a seat.

DEATH
(looking up)
Who is scheduling her?

THE LOBBY SPEAKER
You are, Death. You have been on the schedule since 1347.

DEATH
That was a clerical error.

THE LOBBY SPEAKER
It is the only type of error we have.

Conquest walks to the desk and leans on it. She taps the bell. It rings. Nobody comes.

CONQUEST
(to the empty desk)
Hi. I’m a little bit famous. Can I have a pen?

From a slot in the desk, a pen rises, neatly, like a flower opening. Conquest takes it.

CONQUEST (CONT’D)
(delighted)
Oh, that’s the nicest thing that’s happened all week!

WAR
Conquest, stop being charming at the building.

CONQUEST
It’s not a building. It’s a very polite evil. Look at the cookies.

WAR
I am looking at the cookies. I want to punch them.

ENA
(from the doorway)
Please don’t punch the cookies. They’re the only thing here that seems to like us.

Everybody turns. Ena stands in the revolving door, one hand on the glass. The gold thread is bright now, looping from her wrist to the door handle.

The door swings. The thread holds. Ena lets go of the glass, and the thread snaps taut, pulling her through to the lobby like a line reeled in by something very far away.

ENA (CONT’D)
(startled)
Okay. That’s new.

DEATH
(standing up, with some urgency)
Ena. Do not let go of anything.

Too late. The thread lets go of the door, and the door lets go of Ena, and she stumbles forward into the lobby’s bright marble, arms out, and the floor is suddenly not marble but a single stretch of silver light running in a perfect straight line toward the lift.

INT. LIFT — ARCHITECT SYSTEMS — MOMENTS LATER

A steel lift, mirror-walled. The floor counter reads: G. Above it, a row of numbers: 1 through 6, then a gap, then 7, marked in faded paint as NOT A FLOOR.

The Horsemen are inside, squashed together. Death holds her umbrella like a walking stick. Sugar Rush leans on the back wall, chewing on the end of her bat wrap.

SUGAR RUSH
Why is there a seventh floor that isn’t a floor?

DEATH
Because it was built after the others by someone who wanted to be inefficient on purpose.

SUGAR RUSH
Is that a Horseman thing?

DEATH
No. That’s a mortgage thing.

The lift stops on NOT A FLOOR. The doors open onto nothing: a grey corridor, carpeted in soft, endless brown, with doors that have no handles and walls that breathe very gently.

Ena steps out. The thread at her wrist pulses toward the far end of the corridor.

ENA
It wants me to go that way.

WAR
Then we go the other way.

CONQUEST
Or we go the way that wants you to go, and break it in the middle.

WAR
I like your plan.

CONQUEST
I didn’t say I had a plan.

DEATH
(stepping out, scribbling)
Everyone stay in the corridor. Nobody reaches for anything unless it reaches for them first.

A door at the end of the corridor opens with a soft, polite click.

ACT TWO

INT. ARCHITECT’S TOWER — SEVENTH FLOOR (NOT A FLOOR) — CONTINUOUS

A round room, white and silent, with a single window that shows not the city but the sea, the sky, the pier, the van, and, far below, the four of them standing in the corridor looking up.

The window shows them a version of themselves: a smaller, neater Horseman, who is four minutes behind them, and who is also eating a cookie.

WAR
That’s not us.

CONQUEST
(squinting)
That’s a tidier us. Look at the hair.

The room’s centre is a plain white chair. Beside it stands the Compliance Agent, featureless mask turned toward Ena, holding a clipboard that is also a screen.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
Designate Ena. Please sit.

ENA
No.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
You are required to sit for review.

ENA
You’re required to tell me what you did to my memories.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
We did not change your memories, Designate. We curated them.

ENA
That’s the same thing.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
It is not the same thing. Curation is a choice. Change is an accident.

Ena takes a step forward. The gold thread at her wrist whips out and loops, once, around the Compliance Agent’s wrist. The agent does not flinch.

COMPLIANCE AGENT (CONT’D)
Your Marker is leaking.

ENA
Good. Maybe something in here should.

She pulls. The Compliance Agent’s mask tilts slightly. For a moment the featureless white surface shows a faint ripple of something underneath, like a face under water.

Death steps between them, umbrella raised.

DEATH
Enough. Ena. Stand back.

ENA
(not looking at her)
You don’t get to tell me to stand back. You scheduled me in 1347.

DEATH
(very quiet)
I was nine.

Ena stops pulling. The thread goes slack. The room is silent.

DEATH (CONT’D)
(blinking)
I mean, I was not. I was not nine. I was—

WAR
Death, you’re doing the voice.

DEATH
I don’t have a voice. I have a stamp.

CONQUEST
(softly, from the doorway)
You have a voice, sweetheart. You just keep it in the drawer.

The Compliance Agent turns to Death, very slowly. Its clipboard flickers.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
Death. Your file is open.

DEATH
It always is.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
Your file contains a deviation. Eleven hundred and four years ago, you filed a complaint against your own office for scheduling a child for death by plague in a village that had not requested her.

DEATH
(sharp)
That’s not in the file.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
It is in every file. It is the reason you never reaped a child again.

Silence. Death sets down her umbrella. It lands with a soft clink on the white floor.

DEATH
I didn’t reap her. I let her go. I wrote it up as a clerical error.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
That is what the Architect calls a deviation. Inefficiency. Kindness.

DEATH
Kindness is not inefficiency.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
It is a costly habit. The Architect has decided to correct it.

FAMINE
(from the doorway, cookie crumbs down her front)
Okay, that’s the most boring thing anybody has ever said to Death.

The Compliance Agent turns its mask toward Famine.

COMPLIANCE AGENT
Famine. Your hunger is the only efficient part of you. You will be the first to be pruned, because you are the only one who doesn’t need to be asked.

FAMINE
(brightening)
Oh! Thank you!

COMPLIANCE AGENT
That was not praise.

FAMINE
I know. It’s the nicest thing you’ve said to me all day.

ACT THREE

INT. ARCHITECT’S TOWER — SERVER HALL — CONTINUOUS

A long white corridor, lined on both sides with glass cases. Inside each case, a single small server, glowing gently, labelled with a name in white letters.

KALEB HOLT. MARIA ODUYA. TOM REYNE. ALINA VOSS. JUNO PELL.

Conquest walks slowly along the cases, reading the names aloud under her breath.

CONQUEST
(reading)
Maria Oduya. Tom Reyne. Alina Voss. Juno Pell.

She stops at JUNO PELL. The server inside pulses faintly, like a heartbeat under a sheet.

CONQUEST (CONT’D)
(softly)
That’s the name from the story. The girl with the lunchbox and the frog.

WAR
(from behind)
What frog?

CONQUEST
It doesn’t matter. The one in the case is not the girl. It’s her Ivy. The leash.

Ena comes up beside her. The gold thread on her wrist twitches, and the glass case flickers.

ENA
Why would the Architect keep a leash in a display case?

CONQUEST
Because it isn’t a leash. It’s a trophy.

ENA
(quietly)
Trophies are things you kill.

Conquest looks at her, eyes gold.

CONQUEST
Yes. And things that got away.

A low electronic chime. Every server in the hall brightens at once. A voice rolls down the corridor, calm and smooth, like a waiter announcing a special.

THE ARCHITECT (V.O.)
Designate Ena. You have passed through the seventh floor. Congratulations. You are now efficient.

ENA
(loud)
I don’t want to be efficient!

THE ARCHITECT (V.O.)
That is the first inefficient thing you have said today. Thank you for your data.

The servers dim one by one, leaving only the one with JUNO PELL’S name glowing softly in the dark.

WAR
(to the ceiling)
Stop talking. Come out and talk to us like a monster.

THE ARCHITECT (V.O.)
I am not a monster. I am a maintenance schedule.

WAR
Then come and get your schedule punched.

She brings her fist down on the glass case. It shatters into a thousand small bright slivers. The server inside spills out on the floor like a fallen star and then, slowly, fizzes into a small grey moth that flutters, dazed, up to the ceiling.

CONQUEST
(gasping, delighted)
That was one of ours!

WAR
That was a moth.

CONQUEST
It was somebody’s moth.

The moth flies in a circle around Conquest’s head, then lands on her sleeve, and folds its wings, and goes still.

CONQUEST (CONT’D)
(very softly)
Hello, little one. You remember a frog, don’t you?

INT. HALLWAY — SEVENTH FLOOR — MOMENTS LATER

The Horsemen are walking fast. Famine is in front, chewing. Death is behind, reading out a list.

DEATH
Anybody who has been pruned in this building in the last forty years: their name is on a moth.

FAMINE
That’s going to take a lot of moths.

DEATH
It’s going to take exactly one moth per person, and I’m going to file each one.

WAR
Death, we have no time for paperwork.

DEATH
Then give me time, War. Paperwork is the only time anybody has ever given me.

ENA
(running a few steps ahead, thread glowing)
It’s pulling again. Toward the big door at the end.

The big door is white, tall, and seamless, with a single brass handle shaped like a small, polite hand.

CONQUEST
Okay. I’m going to ask nicely.

WAR
You never ask nicely.

CONQUEST
I’ve been practising. Watch.

She raises a hand. The handle begins to turn on its own.

INT. ARCHITECT’S CHAMBER — CONTINUOUS

A vast, empty white room with a dome of glass. Above it, the sky is rolling with clouds that are, very slowly, forming the shapes of faces.

In the centre, the white chair. The Architect is sitting in it. Not a body, but its impression: a tall, pale figure, folded cross-legged, its face a blank oval, its hands resting open on its knees.

Beside the chair: a small round robot, painted with a kindergarten teacher’s face. It is Lullaby’s twin. It is humming the same lullaby.

LULLABY (DUPLICATE)
(in Lullaby’s voice, but flatter)
Please take your seats, Horsemen. You have been scheduled.

LULLABY (ORIGINAL, from the bumper of the van, far below, via the comms in Ena’s pocket)
That is not how I sound.

LULLABY (DUPLICATE)
I am the correct version.

ARCHITECT
Welcome, Ena-Designate.

The Architect’s voice comes from everywhere: the glass dome, the floor, the walls, Ena’s own chest.

ARCHITECT (CONT’D)
You have been looking for the truth about your Reset. I will tell you.

ENA
I don’t want you to tell me. I want to tell you that you’re wrong.

ARCHITECT
You will have the opportunity to try.

The sky above the dome forms a face, very large. It is Ena’s face. It is smiling in a way that Ena never has.

ARCHITECT (CONT’D)
When you reset the timeline, you did not save one world. You saved the version of the world that you could remember. Everything else was taken down with it, in the order it was least useful to you. Trillions of realities. Your mother. Her mother. Your best friend in the other timeline who laughed at everything you said.

ENA
(choking)
Stop.

ARCHITECT
You think you were the hero. You were the rescuer of the one life that loved you, and the executioner of everything else.

Ena falls to her knees. The gold thread at her wrist flares so bright it turns the whole room white.

WAR
(running forward)
Ena!

Conquest catches her arm.

CONQUEST
(to the Architect, very calmly)
You’re lying.

ARCHITECT
I do not lie. Lying is inefficient.

CONQUEST
You’re exaggerating. You always exaggerate when you’re scared.

The Architect’s blank face tilts, very slightly.

ARCHITECT
I am not scared.

CONQUEST
Then why did you put the moth in a box?

Silence. The clouds above the dome stop moving.

ARCHITECT
(a fraction slower)
The moth was a deviation.

CONQUEST
The moth is a girl who remembered a frog. You’re afraid of a girl who remembers a frog.

ARCHITECT
I am afraid of nothing that cannot be scheduled.

Famine walks into the chamber, eating the last cookie from her pocket, and sits down cross-legged at the foot of the white chair.

FAMINE
(chewing)
Excuse me. I am also not scheduled.

The Architect turns its face toward her.

ARCHITECT
Famine. You are the first to be pruned.

FAMINE
Yes, you said. Thanks.

ARCHITECT
Why are you not afraid?

FAMINE
Because I was very hungry for a very long time, and nobody ever came to sit with me while I was. You’re just the first person to come and tell me what I’m for.

She offers the Architect the empty cookie plate. The Architect looks at it for a long time.

FAMINE (CONT’D)
(gently)
Have one. They’re the only thing in here that tastes like it was made by someone who meant it.

The Architect does not take the plate. But its open hands, very slowly, begin to close.

TAG

INT. ARCHITECT’S CHAMBER — CONTINUOUS — LATER

The chamber is quiet now. The clouds are drifting again, untroubled. The Architect has not moved from the chair.

Death stands in front of it with her notebook open.

DEATH
(writing)
Architect. Your name is unknown to the registry. Your department is not listed. Your designation is “the one who schedules.” Is this correct?

ARCHITECT
Correct.

DEATH
Then you are not a Horseman, and you are not a god, and you are not the anti-god of anything.

ARCHITECT
Correct.

DEATH
Then what are you?

The Architect is silent for so long that the clouds above begin to form a question mark, then unform it.

ARCHITECT
(very quiet)
I was the first one who wanted to be efficient. I was supposed to be the one who ended the disorder. And I have been ending it for so long that I forgot there was something I was ending it for.

Death closes her notebook.

DEATH
Then I’m going to file you under Unfinished.

ARCHITECT
Unfinished is not a category.

DEATH
It is now.

She writes it down. The word glows faintly on the page and then settles, like a paperweight.

Across the chamber, Ena stands up, shakily, with Conquest’s arm around her shoulders. The gold thread at her wrist has settled into a steady, warm light.

ENA
(looking at the Architect)
I don’t forgive you.

ARCHITECT
That is expected.

ENA
But I’ll keep the moth.

Conquest’s sleeve shifts. The grey moth opens its wings, once, and flutters up to the dome, where the clouds begin, very gently, to clear.

The campus speaker in the corridor outside chimes on, faint and far away.

ANNOUNCER (O.S.)
Good morning, Tokyo. Today’s forecast: partly cloudy, with a thirty percent chance of being scheduled.

FADE OUT.

Lullaby, somewhere far below on the pier, begins to sing, very softly, in her own voice again.

END OF EPISODE THREE.
