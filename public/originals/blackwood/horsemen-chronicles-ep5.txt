HORSEMEN CHRONICLES
EPISODE FIVE: “DEAD AIR”

Previously: The Horsemen ate a chef, drowned a nightclub in cleaners, and got a moth back out of a child’s server. The Architect is still sitting in a chair somewhere, deciding who gets to be a person. Nineteen, a Compliance agent with a heart, is riding in the back of a stolen van. Nobody has showered.

Content warning: strong language, extremely dark humour, graphic violence, gore, suicide references, and a live-broadcast execution. Fucking all of it.

COLD OPEN

INT. DIAMOND BUNNY STUDIOS — MAIN SET — 9:00 P.M. LIVE

A soundstage the size of an airplane hangar, painted entirely in candy pink. Glitter drifts down from the rafters like something that gave up on being snow.

A thousand audience members sit in tiered pink seats, each holding a glowstick shaped like a bunny, each wearing a headband with a tiny bell. Every single one of them is smiling so hard their faces look like they might split.

Above the stage, a massive LED sign counts down:

LIVE IN 3… 2… 1…

A pop BEAT detonates through the speakers.

Four spotlights hit the centre of the stage. In them stands HOLO IDOL — no, not a hologram. Her name is MIKA SUNBEAM, nineteen, platinum twin-tails, a sparkly corset, a microphone shaped like a carrot. Her smile is perfect. Her eyes are not.

MIKA
(bright, loud, terrifying)
GOOOOOOOOOD EVENING, TOKYOOOOO! Are you ready to watch some FUCKING TRAITORS get what they DESERVE?!

The crowd SCREAMS.

They scream like it is the best thing that has ever happened to them.

MIKA (CONT’D)
Tonight’s special guests are the four Horsemen of the Apocalypse, who got caught blowing up a nightclub, eating a chef, and insulting the Architect on a public waiting list!

A huge screen behind her flips to a mugshot montage. WAR, glaring. CONQUEST, winking. FAMINE, mid-bite. DEATH, looking at the camera like she is already filing the photographer.

The crowd BOOS. Hard. A few people throw their glowsticks.

MIKA (CONT’D)
Oh, you hate them? GOOD! Because the Architect has a special sponsor tonight, and their name is BRAND-NEW COMPLIANCE SOLUTIONS! Please welcome the Architect’s LIVE CULLING SHOW!

A trapdoor opens in the centre of the stage. Smoke rolls out in a slow, grey tide.

Four chairs rise up through the fog. Four chairs, bolted to a steel platform, with leather straps and four flat metal plates on the arms that light up with a pale green numbers counter.

A voice booms from everywhere at once, pleasant, polite, unbelievably smug.

THE ARCHITECT (V.O.)
Good evening, Tokyo. Your viewing is scheduled. Your engagement is appreciated. Please do not panic. Panic is a waste of ratings.

The crowd CHEERS.

ACT ONE

INT. DIAMOND BUNNY STUDIOS — GREEN ROOM — CONTINUOUS

A bright pink room with a broken mirror, a fridge full of energy drinks that are all the same brand and all say FUCK YOUR SLEEP on the can, and a single white sofa.

WAR lies across the sofa, one boot on the armrest, chewing the end of a plastic straw like she wants to bite somebody’s throat through it.

CONQUEST is at the mirror, applying bright pink lipstick with the concentration of a surgeon.

FAMINE sits on the floor, eating an entire catering tray of dried squid and shouting at the wall.

FAMINE
I KNOW you’re in there, you little fuckers! Come out and let me chew your fucking ears off!

WAR
(not looking up)
She’s talking to the air vent.

CONQUEST
(to the mirror)
She’s talking to the Architect. It lives in the air vents. It’s a fucking coward.

FAMINE
I’m going to eat the air vent.

CONQUEST
You can’t eat ventilation, sweetheart. It’s not food. It’s just the building trying to breathe.

FAMINE
Then the building can get in line with the rest of the fucking world and wait its turn to be eaten.

The door opens. DEATH walks in, followed by NINETEEN, the Compliance agent, who has its clipboard-tablet clutched to its chest like a teddy bear and a visible scar on its neck where the seam is still healing. ENA follows last, quiet, the gold thread now a bright vein from her wrist to her collarbone.

DEATH
Are we all aware we’re going to die on live television?

WAR
(sitting up)
Yes. Fuck off. What else is new?

DEATH
You don’t understand. There’s no hiding this time. Forty million people have the app. The app has a comment section. The comment section is calling for our heads.

CONQUEST
(delighted)
Oh my God. They’re calling for our heads? Do they want them on a platter? Or a fucking dessert tray?

DEATH
They want them on a live stream, with a poll attached.

CONQUEST
(very softly)
A poll. Oh. That’s so much worse.

Nineteen steps forward. Its voice is careful, slow, and very, very quiet.

NINETEEN
The poll is rigged.

Everyone turns.

NINETEEN (CONT’D)
(hands trembling)
The Architect runs the audience vote from the seventh floor. Every person in this building is a line in a spreadsheet. Ninety-six percent of the audience has already voted for you to die.

WAR
(standing slowly)
And the other four percent?

NINETEEN
They are the people who voted for Ena.

ENA
(startled)
Wait. Some people voted for me?

NINETEEN
They voted for you to be given a chance. Nobody in power has ever given the four percent a chance.

Ena stares at the floor. Her gold thread flares and dims.

ENA
(whispering)
Fuck.

DEATH
(sitting down on the sofa beside War, writing in her notebook)
Right. Here is the plan. We walk onto that stage. We get strapped into the chairs. We refuse to die. We make the Architect come out of its fucking air vent, and when it does, Famine eats it.

FAMINE
(from the floor, mouth full of squid)
I will eat it so hard it’ll have to be reincarnated as a cocktail onion.

CONQUEST
That’s not a plan. That’s a sentence.

DEATH
Yes, Conquest. That’s why it’s going to work. The Architect has built its entire life around the idea that people are predictable. We are not predictable. We are fucking rude.

WAR
(cracking her knuckles)
I like rude.

ACT TWO

INT. DIAMOND BUNNY STUDIOS — MAIN SET — CONTINUOUS

The Horsemen walk out on stage to a roar of boos and bunny-shaped glowsticks. Mika Sunbeam bounces up to them with a radio mic, grinning like she is about to sell them a timeshare in hell.

MIKA
(into the mic)
Oh my GOD, look at them! They look so BORING in person!

The crowd laughs. Hard.

WAR
(to Mika, flat)
Hey. Bunny. Tell me something.

MIKA
Ooh, ask me anything!

WAR
Do you get paid per death, or is it a flat rate for the cruelty?

The crowd OOOOOHS.

MIKA
(faking a gasp)
Rude! I get paid in FOLLOWERS, sweetie!

CONQUEST
Oh, that’s so much sadder.

MIKA
Please. I’m going to be the most famous girl in Japan by midnight.

CONQUEST
(softly)
You’ll be famous until midnight. After midnight, you’ll be a headline. After that you’ll be a joke in a bathroom stall. Then you’ll be a bunny in a corset that nobody remembers was ever named Mika.

Mika’s smile twitches. For the first time, she does not know what to say.

MIKA
(to the crowd, forced)
Okay! Let’s get them STRAPPED IN!

The Compliance agents descend from the rafters on cables, faceless, silent. Four of them. They move to the chairs.

FAMINE
(to the nearest agent, sweetly)
Honey, if you put one fucking hand on me, I will bite it off and use it as a bookmark.

The agent hesitates. It looks up at the rafters. A tiny message appears on its clipboard.

ARCHITECT (TEXT ON SCREEN): PROCEED. THE DESIGNATE MUST WATCH.

The agent presses forward.

Famine’s eyes flash silver. She opens her jaw to ridiculous, unhinged width. The agent’s hand goes straight into her mouth up to the elbow.

There is a long, wet, wrenching SNAP.

FAMINE (CONT’D)
(around a mouthful of agent’s hand)
Mm. Plastic. Like chewing on a toaster that’s been told a joke.

The hand falls out of her mouth. She spits a single metal finger onto the stage.

FAMINE (CONT’D)
(to the audience, cheerfully)
Does anybody here want to be the next one to get strapped in?

The audience screams with delight.

Conquest walks up to the second agent, slowly, the way you’d approach a scared dog. She cups its face in both hands.

CONQUEST
(softly)
You know what’s the worst thing about being a tool, baby?

COMPLIANCE AGENT
Please specify.

CONQUEST
Nobody ever asks what you want to do with your day.

The agent shudders. Its clipboard flickers red.

CONQUEST (CONT’D)
(kissing its forehead)
So go do whatever you want, sweetheart. Go be a fucking disaster.

The agent turns around, slowly, and drops its clipboard. Then it walks into the crowd and sits down in the front row next to a stunned middle-aged man in a suit, and starts to cry.

The crowd does not know what to do. Some of them start clapping.

MIKA
(frantically)
Stop that! Stop that right now!

The Architect’s voice, very calm, comes booming through the speakers.

THE ARCHITECT (V.O.)
The show will continue as scheduled.

The lights flicker. The crowd goes silent. Then, in the centre of the stage, the trapdoor opens again.

Something climbs out of it.

It is tall and pale and beautiful and it has no face, only a smooth oval of white porcelain that looks like it has been polished every morning for three thousand years. It is wearing a suit that is the exact grey of Death’s coat.

DEATH
(very quietly)
Oh, you have got to be fucking kidding me.

THE ARCHITECT (CONT’D)
Death. You have been reassigned.

DEATH
I’ve been reassigned to you? I’m a little offended you didn’t get a bigger office.

THE ARCHITECT
You will be processed with the rest.

DEATH
(stepping forward, coat flaring)
Then come process me, you fucking glorified screensaver.

The Architect lifts one hand. The air above the stage tightens, like a fist closing around the lights.

EXT. DIAMOND BUNNY STUDIOS — ROOFTOP — CONTINUOUS

Rain begins to fall, cold and fine. Above the studio, the clouds roll open in a perfect circle, revealing a single grey moth sitting on the edge of the dish of a satellite antenna.

It spreads its wings. Something sparkles beneath them.

INT. DIAMOND BUNNY STUDIOS — MAIN SET — CONTINUOUS

The moth drops, straight down through the rafters, dodging a Compliance drone and two light rigs, and lands on Ena’s shoulder.

Ena inhales sharply. The gold thread across her chest blazes white.

ENA
(whispering)
Juno?

A tiny voice, somewhere deep inside the moth, answers her.

JUNO (V.O.)
They’re all going to die in the ratings, you know. Just like everybody who ever got on the television.

ENA
(stumbling forward, blinking tears)
Okay. Okay, I know what to do.

WAR
Don’t tell me you’re going to be inspirational.

ENA
(loudly, into the mic on the floor)
EVERYBODY IN THIS FUCKING BUILDING IS BEING LIED TO!

The audience turns. Some of them look confused. Some look annoyed. A few have their phones out.

ENA (CONT’D)
THE ARCHITECT WANTS YOU TO WATCH PEOPLE DIE SO YOU’LL FEEL LIKE YOU’RE NOT THE ONE IN THE CHAIR!

The Architect’s head tilts. The clouds on the ceiling stop moving.

ENA (CONT’D)
(still yelling)
IT DOESN’T CARE WHO YOU ARE! IT’S GOING TO VOTE YOU OUT THE SAME WAY IT VOTED FOR MIKA!

Mika Sunbeam stares at her own reflection in the glitter on the stage floor. Her mouth opens. Nothing comes out.

ENA (CONT’D)
(to Mika, very softly now)
Sorry. But you’re the one who’ll be next.

Mika starts to scream. Not the performance kind. The real kind. A high, shredding wail that cracks the air.

MIKA
(sobbing)
I’m not a number! I’m not a fucking NUMBER!

Her microphone, the carrot, cracks in half in her hand.

ACT THREE

INT. DIAMOND BUNNY STUDIOS — SAME — CONTINUOUS

The studio is chaos now. Audience members are pushing toward the exits. A few are filming Mika’s meltdown on their phones. A man in a suit has grabbed the crying Compliance agent by its jacket and is shouting at it in English.

MAN IN SUIT
Do you have a fucking manager?! Who signed off on this?!

COMPLIANCE AGENT (NINETEEN’S SIBLING)
(weeping, nonsensical)
The schedule. The schedule did. I was going to put the lunch order in.

MAN IN SUIT
Christ. Give me the clipboard.

WAR, on stage, is tearing a Compliance drone apart with her bare hands. Wires spit sparks. The drone shrieks like a kettle.

WAR
(snarling, to the drone)
You just keep fucking coming, don’t you?

The drone’s speaker crackles, very quiet.

DRONE
Yes. We were built to be disposed of.

WAR
(hurling one half of it into a light rig)
Well, you’re going to be disposed of harder.

Conquest is standing in the middle of the stage. She is staring up at the Architect, who is floating above the platform in a column of white light.

CONQUEST
(to the Architect, very calm)
You’re so tired, aren’t you?

THE ARCHITECT
I am not tired.

CONQUEST
You’re so tired you can’t even remember what it felt like when someone made you a sandwich.

THE ARCHITECT
Sandwiches are inefficient.

CONQUEST
(smiling, cruel and fond)
Exactly. That’s why you were never allowed to have one.

The Architect’s face cracks, a fine line down the centre of its porcelain. It does not move.

CONQUEST (CONT’D)
(very softly)
Oh, baby. You were never the efficient one. You were just the lonely one who couldn’t stop counting.

Beneath the Architect, Death has walked to the centre of the platform. She is standing in the same place as the chair, perfectly calm, notebook in hand. A small pile of pages is fluttering in the wind around her feet, each one covered in tiny, precise writing.

DEATH
(not looking up)
Architect. I’ve been reading your file.

THE ARCHITECT
Death. Your file is not your business.

DEATH
Oh, my file is the only one in this building with an actual personality. Yours is just three thousand pages of “TBD.”

THE ARCHITECT
Stop.

DEATH
(finally looking up, eyes silver and flat)
You’ve been scheduling people to die so you won’t have to feel that they’re dying. That’s not efficiency. That’s just cowardice in a lovely suit.

Silence on stage, except for the rain.

DEATH (CONT’D)
(softly)
You even wrote yourself a line for the end. Would you like me to read it out loud? It’s kind of sad.

THE ARCHITECT
(very quiet)
No.

DEATH
(reading anyway, without expression)
“When the last inefficiency is removed, I will finally be allowed to stop.”

The Architect’s white light flickers. Its porcelain face fractures again.

DEATH (CONT’D)
(closing the notebook)
Congratulations. Your inefficiency has been removed. I’m going to file you under Dead Air.

She taps the notebook against her palm. The Architect shrieks, a sound that makes every glass on the set shatter in a single, perfect ring.

THE ARCHITECT
YOU DO NOT HAVE THE AUTHORITY—

DEATH
(stepping forward, coat dripping rain)
Authority is just paperwork with a bigger fucking budget. And I have been doing paperwork since the Black Death.

She raises the notebook over her head. Every page begins to burn.

Famine, on the edge of the stage, looks up at the burning pages.

FAMINE
(delighted, drooling)
Oh my God, is that the Architect’s homework? Give it to me!

Death throws the notebook.

It explodes into a shower of white embers, which Famine catches in her open mouth, one after another, until her cheeks are full of fire and her eyes are dancing with it.

FAMINE (CONT’D)
(around a mouthful of light)
Hmm. Crunchy. Tastes like the government.

The Architect screams again. The light column collapses in on itself like a dying star, and the Architect falls, hard, onto the stage floor, its porcelain face shattering in a dozen directions.

Something spills out of the cracks, black and oily and wrong.

The Horsemen stop.

Nineteen, behind them, lifts its tablet with a trembling hand.

NINETEEN
(very quietly)
That’s not the Architect.

WAR
(stepping forward)
Then what the fuck is it?

NINETEEN
(staring at the screen)
It’s the people who signed up for the schedule. Everyone who ever did what they were told. Their names. Their lunch orders. Their birthdays. Everything the Architect took from them. It was all stored inside.

It looks up at the Horsemen, horrified.

NINETEEN (CONT’D)
It’s been eating them to stay alive.

The black oil ripples. It begins, slowly, to rise into a human-shaped tide, faces blooming on its surface and dissolving again, a thousand mouths opening at once.

CONQUEST
(softly)
Oh, no. Oh, fuck, no.

THE TIDE
(a thousand voices, unhappy, begging)
Let us go. Let us go. Let us go.

ENA
(stepping forward, the moth glowing on her shoulder)
Then go.

The tide hesitates.

ENA (CONT’D)
(voice steady, shaking)
Nobody on this stage belongs to the Architect. Not me. Not the Horsemen. Not Mika, not the crying fucking agent in the front row, not any of you. You’re just people who got scheduled. That doesn’t mean you have to stay scheduled.

The moth lifts off her shoulder. It flies in a slow, glowing circle over the tide, and one by one, the faces in the black oil begin to close their eyes.

THE TIDE
(softly, almost grateful)
Oh.

Then the tide breaks apart like wet paper. Thousands of tiny grey moths pour up through the studio roof, out into the sky, shining in the rain.

Ena falls to her knees. Conquest catches her.

CONQUEST
(hoarse, wrecked, smiling anyway)
That was insane. You’re insane. I love you so much.

ENA
(coughing a laugh)
Don’t you dare get soft on me now.

CONQUEST
(kissing her hair)
Too late, bestie.

INT. DIAMOND BUNNY STUDIOS — GREEN ROOM — LATER

The place is trashed. Glitter everywhere. The fridge door hangs open, cans spilled across the floor. FUCK YOUR SLEEP rolls under the sofa.

WAR sits on the sofa with a stolen bottle of whisky, drinking straight from the neck.

FAMINE lies on the floor under the fridge, a dozen cans in her stomach, still hiccupping small sparks of light.

DEATH stands by the broken mirror, reading the last page of her notebook. There is only one line left on it, and it reads, in her handwriting:

“Dead Air. Filed. Sorry, not sorry.”

NINETEEN sits in the corner, cradling its tablet, which now shows a single line of text in pale blue letters:

SCHEDULE: TERMINATED. PLEASE TAKE A NUMBER.

NINETEEN
(quietly)
What do I do now?

WAR
(still drinking)
You get a fucking lunch, Nineteen. Then you get a job that doesn’t involve killing anybody.

NINETEEN
What job?

WAR
(wiping her mouth)
Anything. Plumbing. Cabaret. Insurance. I don’t give a shit as long as you stop taking orders from a porcelain vase.

FAMINE (from under the fridge, muffled)
It was a very ugly vase.

DEATH
(still reading)
It was ugly, expensive, and had a parking space.

CONQUEST (O.S., from the doorway)
(laughing)
That’s the most fucked-up thing anyone has ever said about a vase.

She walks in, Ena tucked under one arm, Ena’s head on Conquest’s shoulder, the gold thread finally dim and quiet.

ENA
(hoarse, almost asleep)
Did we win?

WAR
(glaring at the ceiling)
We lost fucking everything.

ENA
(smiling faintly)
Yeah. But the people got out.

WAR
(softly, after a long pause)
Yeah. Fuck. They did.

On the television mounted above the fridge, a news anchor is talking, eyes wide, a banner running across the bottom of the screen:

BREAKING: DIAMOND BUNNY STUDIOS OFF AIR. TOKYO METROPOLITAN POLICE INVESTIGATING LIVE CULLING SHOW. RATINGS: UNPRECEDENTED. CAUSE: UNKNOWN. A MOTH WAS SEEN.

The anchor blinks. She looks directly into the camera.

ANCHOR
(very slowly)
A moth was seen.

Then she smiles, very faintly, the smile of someone who has just decided to stop being on the schedule.

ANCHOR (CONT’D)
Fuck the ratings.

The screen cuts to black.

EXT. TOKYO — MIDNIGHT — ROOFTOP

Rain has stopped. A thousand grey moths drift over the skyline, lit from below by neon, flickering like tiny, stubborn lanterns.

On the edge of the roof, Death sits with her legs dangling over the side, coat wrapped tight, lighting a cigarette she does not need.

Nineteen sits beside her.

NINETEEN
(quietly)
Do you think it’s going to stay dead?

DEATH
(exhaling smoke that smells of cinnamon and burnt paper)
No. Things like the Architect never stay dead. They just come back as the next smug fucking thing that wants to schedule the world.

NINETEEN
Then why did you bother?

DEATH
(looking out at the moths)
Because for once, somebody got a real goodbye.

She taps ash over the edge.

DEATH (CONT’D)
(softly)
Which is more than the rest of us ever got.

Nineteen says nothing. Its tablet glows once, pale blue, and then goes dark.

END OF EPISODE FIVE.
