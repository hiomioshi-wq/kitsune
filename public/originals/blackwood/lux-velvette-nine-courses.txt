LUX & VELVETTE
A BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY SPINOFF

“NINE COURSES”

Content warning: Rated R. Strong language, adult relationships and sexual content (not graphic), alcohol, knife violence, poison, one fully justified dismemberment, and a great deal of honesty. Both leads are adults. No children are in danger, but one remembers being small.

Set in the penthouse, ten years after they met. Spoiler-light: nothing here depends on the rest of the series, though the rest of the series will look different afterwards.

TEASER

INT. LUX AND VELVETTE’S PENTHOUSE — DINING ROOM — NIGHT

A very long table laid for two. White linen, black candles, two settings at opposite ends, nine glasses at each place, and between them forty feet of polished walnut that Lux had flown in specifically so that nobody could ever lean across it. The room has floor-to-ceiling windows. Beyond them, the whole campus of Blackwood lies in the dark like a model of itself, lit in gold along the Ring Road.

On the wall, in a frame, a menu in calligraphy: NINE COURSES. FOR V. TEN YEARS.

At the far end of the table sits LUX, platinum hair swept up, wearing a floor-length gown the colour of dry champagne, backless to the base of her spine. She holds a glass of something pale and cold. She has been sitting very straight for forty minutes.

The door at the other end of the room opens.

VELVETTE comes in. Black silk shirt, open at the throat, black trousers, the silver chain at her collar with a small plastic ruler hanging from it, hair freshly shaved at the sides, boots with the heel blade retracted. She stops in the doorway, looks at the table, at the candles, at the frame, and exhales through her nose.

VELVETTE
You didn’t.

LUX
(not turning her head)
I did.

VELVETTE
You promised no performance.

LUX
It isn’t a performance, darling. It’s a dinner.

VELVETTE
There’s a menu in a frame.

LUX
(a faint smile)
It’s a very good frame.

Velvette crosses the room. Her boots are quiet on the marble. She reaches Lux’s end of the table, stops behind her chair, and puts two fingers under Lux’s jaw to turn her face up.

Lux lets her.

VELVETTE
(quietly)
Hello.

LUX
(just as quietly)
Hello.

VELVETTE
You’re wearing the dress.

LUX
You said it was a war crime.

VELVETTE
I said it should be tried at The Hague.

LUX
(lightly)
They declined jurisdiction.

Velvette bends and kisses her, once, slowly. Lux’s glass tilts. A bead of wine runs down the side and neither of them notices.

When Velvette straightens, her eyes have changed.

VELVETTE
(too casual)
There are eleven people on the roof.

LUX
(still looking up at her)
Twelve.

VELVETTE
I counted eleven.

LUX
(sweetly)
The twelfth is in the lift, wearing a waiter’s jacket and carrying a tray with a bomb under the dome.

A very small silence.

VELVETTE
(flat)
It’s our anniversary.

LUX
(quite calmly)
Yes.

VELVETTE
Ten years.

LUX
I did mention that on the card.

VELVETTE
(drawing a long breath)
Somebody is going to die very slowly.

LUX
(lifting her glass)
Several somebodies, I should think. Shall we start with the amuse-bouche?

The lift chimes.

ACT ONE

FIRST COURSE — AMUSE-BOUCHE

INT. PENTHOUSE — DINING ROOM — CONTINUOUS

The lift doors open. A WAITER steps out: young, handsome, bland, in a white jacket, carrying a silver tray with a dome. His smile is professional and his hands are slightly too steady.

WAITER
Good evening, ladies. Your first course.

He crosses the room, sets the tray on the table between the two plates, and lifts the dome with a flourish.

On a white plate lies a single oyster on crushed ice, with a lemon wedge. Next to it, a small clock, ticking.

WAITER (CONT’D)
(brightly)
Fresh from the Aegean.

Velvette looks at the oyster. Lux looks at the clock.

LUX
(mildly)
How long?

WAITER
(still smiling)
Ma’am?

LUX
How long on the clock, darling.

WAITER
(after a beat)
Four minutes.

LUX
(to Velvette)
He’s very good. He’s only sweated through one shirt.

VELVETTE
(not looking at him)
Who sent you?

WAITER
(politely)
I can’t say.

VELVETTE
(pleasantly)
That’s fine.

She picks up the oyster fork. She looks at it, looks at the waiter, and, with one flat movement of her wrist, rotates the fork once in the air. Something folds out of the handle with a soft metallic click, and what was a fork is now four inches of thin grey blade with a hook on the end.

WAITER
(the smile goes)
Ah.

VELVETTE
(conversationally)
Four minutes is a long time to talk.

WAITER
(backing up a step)
I’m not authorised to—

Velvette is across the table before he finishes the word. There is a scraping of linen, a short hard sound, and the waiter finds himself against the wall with the fork-blade resting delicately under his left ear and Velvette’s forearm across his collarbone.

VELVETTE
(very softly)
Who. Sent. You.

WAITER
(whispering)
The… the Dean of Lakewood.

LUX
(from her seat, not turning)
Marguerite Ashdown?

WAITER
(a little strangled)
She says it’s nothing personal. She says it’s only a… a hostile acquisition of your… your marriage.

Lux sets her glass down. For the first time that evening, her voice goes cold enough to frost the table.

LUX
(slowly)
She said what?

WAITER
(miserably)
She said it’s a merger.

Silence.

VELVETTE
(very evenly)
I’m going to cut off your hands.

WAITER
(panicking)
I’m just the delivery!

VELVETTE
(over her shoulder, to Lux)
Do you want the oyster?

LUX
(quietly)
No.

VELVETTE
(to the waiter)
Then you’re going to eat it.

WAITER
(realising)
No, please, it’s—

Velvette takes the oyster from the plate, tips it into his mouth with the same hand, and clamps his jaw shut. The clock ticks. The waiter’s eyes go wide, and then wider.

On the table, the clock reaches zero.

Nothing happens. The waiter swallows.

WAITER (CONT’D)
(weakly)
The bomb is a cover.

VELVETTE
(flatly)
I know.

WAITER
(more weakly)
The oyster is the poison.

VELVETTE
I know that too.

She lets him go. He slides down the wall, clutching his throat, going grey in the face.

LUX
(not unkindly)
There’s an antidote in the left pocket of your jacket, darling, in the breast pocket, in a little silver vial. Dean Ashdown always did like a tidy sabotage.

The waiter stares at her. He fumbles in his pocket with a shaking hand, finds the vial, uncorks it and drinks. His colour returns slowly.

WAITER
(hoarsely)
How—

LUX
(rising, gliding toward him)
Because I taught her that trick.

She crouches in front of him in the champagne gown, tilting her head.

LUX (CONT’D)
(softly)
Go back to Marguerite. Tell her the first course was delicious. Tell her my wife and I are not for sale, not for merger, not for acquisition, and certainly not for a sensible price. And tell her I’ll send my reply in the morning.

WAITER
(hoarse)
What reply?

LUX
(gently)
You’ll see.

She smiles. It’s the smile that eats continents.

He bolts for the lift. The doors close on him.

Velvette watches the numbers fall, folds the fork back into a fork, and sets it neatly on the tablecloth.

VELVETTE
(quietly)
“A merger.”

LUX
(returning to her chair, perfectly composed)
Don’t.

VELVETTE
She called our marriage—

LUX
(sitting)
I know what she called it.

VELVETTE
(a muscle working in her jaw)
I wanted to go to the roof and hang her from the aerial.

LUX
(sipping her wine)
Second course first.

Velvette stares at her. Then, slowly, helplessly, she laughs. It cracks across the room like a dropped plate. She drops into her chair at the far end of the table, puts both elbows on the walnut, and covers her face.

VELVETTE
(into her hands)
This is exactly what I wanted for our anniversary.

LUX
(softly)
I know.

VELVETTE
(lifting her head, still laughing)
I’d have been disappointed with a quiet night.

LUX
(a very small smile)
You would have been bored.

VELVETTE
(shaking her head)
I’d have been bored.

The smile fades from both their faces at the same moment, very slowly.

For a second there is only the sound of the candles.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(quietly)
Hey.

LUX
(not looking at her)
Yes?

VELVETTE
You’re sitting forty feet away.

LUX
(lightly)
It’s a long table.

VELVETTE
You had it made that way.

LUX
(a pause)
I did.

VELVETTE
(gently)
Why?

Lux looks down at her glass. Her thumb moves once around the rim.

LUX
(carefully)
It’s a very good table.

Velvette watches her. She doesn’t press.

The lift chimes again, somewhere beneath them, going down.

SECOND COURSE — THE SOUP

INT. PENTHOUSE — KITCHEN — MOMENTS LATER

A kitchen the size of a small ballroom: steel, white tile, copper pans in rows, a long island scrubbed to a dull shine. Pots are steaming on the range. A dozen ghostly servants in white move through it carrying trays, who now stop, all at once, and look at the doors.

The kitchen doors burst open. Velvette strides in, carrying the tray with the soup tureen.

VELVETTE
(to the kitchen at large)
Who made this?

A small, round head chef, a GHOST in whites, steps forward with a tea towel over one shoulder.

CHEF
(trembling)
Ma’am?

VELVETTE
The soup.

CHEF
(quavering)
Consommé, ma’am. Eight hours.

VELVETTE
(setting it down)
Taste it.

CHEF
(horrified)
Ma’am, I would never taste a dish before it was—

VELVETTE
(softly)
Taste. It.

The chef takes a spoon with a shaking hand, dips it in, tastes, and goes very still.

CHEF
(whispering)
Oh.

VELVETTE
What do you taste?

CHEF
(small voice)
Arsenic. Almond. A little… cyanide.

VELVETTE
How long have you worked for us?

CHEF
Nine years, ma’am.

VELVETTE
And in nine years, have you ever made a bad soup?

CHEF
(horrified at the thought)
No, ma’am.

VELVETTE
(sitting on the edge of the island)
So who got into my kitchen?

The chef looks at the ghostly kitchen staff. Eleven pale faces look at the floor.

CHEF
(whispering)
Sasha’s office sent a new sous.

Velvette closes her eyes for a moment.

VELVETTE
(very quietly)
Of course she did.

CHEF
(wringing the tea towel)
She said it was a favour. She said it was the Dean who— it was an “introductory secondment.”

VELVETTE
(opening her eyes)
Where is the sous?

The chef points a trembling finger at a corner of the kitchen. A young GHOST in a too-large apron stands against the wall with his hands up, eyes enormous.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(walking toward him)
Name.

SOUS-CHEF
(tiny)
Benedict.

VELVETTE
Benedict, did you poison our soup?

SOUS-CHEF
(very fast)
I was told it was a seasoning.

VELVETTE
By who?

SOUS-CHEF
By a woman in a very nice grey suit who said it was a “reasonable stress test.”

Velvette stops in front of him. She looks at him for a long moment, then puts a hand on his shoulder, gently.

VELVETTE
(quietly)
Benedict. Go and get my wife a very clean spoon.

SOUS-CHEF
(blinking)
That’s… all?

VELVETTE
(still gently)
That’s all. And Benedict.

SOUS-CHEF
Yes, ma’am?

VELVETTE
The next time somebody in a nice grey suit offers you a reasonable stress test, you come and tell me. I’ll show you what a reasonable stress test looks like.

SOUS-CHEF
(fervently)
Yes, ma’am.

He bolts. The chef pats Velvette’s arm.

CHEF
(softly)
Ma’am… if I may.

VELVETTE
(tired)
Yes?

CHEF
(carefully)
She’s sitting out there in a very beautiful dress and she has been practising her speech for six weeks.

Velvette looks at him.

VELVETTE
(quietly)
What speech?

CHEF
(a very small shrug)
I don’t know, ma’am. But she’s been rehearsing it in the bath.

Velvette’s face does something complicated.

VELVETTE
(after a moment)
She hates it when I know things before she tells me.

CHEF
(gently)
Then I didn’t tell you anything, ma’am.

Velvette huffs a short laugh. She takes the tureen, tips its contents into the sink, and runs the water.

VELVETTE
(to the kitchen)
Everybody out. Sixty seconds. Take the night off.

The kitchen exhales and empties in a rustle of white linen. The chef lingers at the door.

CHEF
(quietly)
Happy anniversary, ma’am.

He is gone. Velvette stands alone in the steam. She takes the silver chain from her collar and holds it in her palm. The plastic ruler dangles from it, gnawed at one end, a name scratched along its edge in third-grade capitals: GERALD.

She closes her hand around it, breathes out, and puts it back.

VELVETTE
(under her breath)
All right, Gerald. Let’s go and be brave.

She pushes through the doors.

INT. PENTHOUSE — DINING ROOM — CONTINUOUS

Lux is alone at her end of the table, the oyster dish still in front of her. She has refilled her glass. She does not look up when Velvette comes in. Velvette sits.

Neither of them speaks.

VELVETTE
(finally)
Soup’s off.

LUX
(lightly)
Pity. I’d been looking forward to it.

VELVETTE
It was poisoned.

LUX
(not surprised)
By Sasha?

VELVETTE
By the Dean, via Sasha’s office.

LUX
(with the faintest smile)
Sasha is going to have a very difficult conversation tomorrow.

VELVETTE
(dryly)
You’re going to let her?

LUX
(a very small smile)
I’m going to let you.

Velvette laughs, quieter this time. The silence that follows it is not entirely comfortable.

VELVETTE
(carefully)
Lux.

LUX
(softly)
Mm.

VELVETTE
Come and sit here.

Lux looks down the length of the table. Forty feet of walnut. The candles flicker between them like a row of tiny, patient eyes.

LUX
(lightly)
I’m very comfortable.

VELVETTE
(not unkindly)
You’re hiding.

LUX
(a flash of ice)
I am not hiding.

VELVETTE
(gently)
You’ve hidden behind this table for six weeks.

LUX
(very coolly)
It’s a dinner.

VELVETTE
(gently)
It’s a barricade.

A very long pause.

LUX
(softly)
Third course.

VELVETTE
(after a moment)
Third course.

THIRD COURSE — THE FISH

INT. PENTHOUSE — HALLWAY — A LITTLE LATER

A long corridor of dark wood and gilt mirrors, lit by candles in sconces. The mirrors show them walking together: Lux in champagne silk, Velvette in black, two reflections on every side, a hundred of them, like a gallery of rival queens. They walk toward the lift side by side and not quite touching.

LUX
(not looking at her)
I counted eleven on the roof.

VELVETTE
You said twelve.

LUX
The twelfth is the waiter.

VELVETTE
So eleven plus a waiter.

LUX
(a very small smile)
Eleven plus a waiter and a clock.

VELVETTE
(drily)
That’s a lot of effort for our anniversary.

LUX
(lightly)
Marguerite was always theatrical.

They reach the lift. The doors are closed. The numbers above them are climbing.

VELVETTE
(very quietly)
Company.

LUX
(also quietly)
Yes.

VELVETTE
How many?

LUX
(listening to the lift)
Six.

VELVETTE
You’re wearing silk.

LUX
(lightly)
I’m wearing silk and a very good grudge.

VELVETTE
(a short breath of laughter)
Stay behind me.

LUX
(cooly)
No.

VELVETTE
(not smiling)
Lux.

LUX
(looking at her for the first time in several minutes)
I have waited ten years to stand beside you at your worst.

The numbers climb. Four. Five.

VELVETTE
(very softly)
You’ve done that before.

LUX
(almost gentle)
Not in this dress.

The lift chimes. The doors open.

Six figures in black tactical gear stand shoulder to shoulder in the lift. Each carries a suppressed weapon. Each wears a small white pin on the lapel: a stylised L, for Lakewood. The one in front, a lean woman with a shaved head, takes a half-step forward.

OPERATIVE
(over a very calm voice)
Mrs. Harrow. Mrs. Voss. I’m instructed to say that—

Velvette throws the fork.

It crosses the corridor in a straight silver line and goes through the operative’s left shoulder, pinning her to the wall of the lift. She makes a sound of pure outrage.

OPERATIVE (CONT’D)
(strangled)
I wasn’t finished!

LUX
(sweetly)
Neither was she.

She lifts her hand. A bangle on her wrist, a plain gold bangle Velvette has seen every day for ten years, slides down to her knuckles and opens. A cloud of pale gold dust blows into the lift with a soft puff.

LUX (CONT’D)
(serenely)
Don’t breathe.

They breathe. They have no choice. Five operatives stagger and lock up, and one by one slide to the floor of the lift with their weapons clattering.

The operative pinned to the wall looks at the dust on her tunic.

OPERATIVE
(feebly)
What is that.

LUX
(taking a step into the lift)
Honey.

OPERATIVE
(blinking)
Honey?

LUX
(crouching in front of her)
Acacia. Fortified. It’s very sweet and it makes your muscles forget what you were doing.

OPERATIVE
(slurring)
That’s… unnecessarily… cruel.

LUX
(smiling)
It’s our anniversary.

Velvette steps in beside her and yanks the fork out of the operative’s shoulder with a wet sound. The operative goes a very pale colour and does not scream.

VELVETTE
(wiping the fork on her sleeve)
Who’s on the roof?

OPERATIVE
(with difficulty)
Four more.

VELVETTE
Armed?

OPERATIVE
(whispering)
Two snipers. Two for— for the extraction.

LUX
(softly)
Extraction of what?

The operative looks at her. Something in her face is almost apologetic.

OPERATIVE
(very softly)
You.

A very small silence.

LUX
(blinking once)
Me.

OPERATIVE
The Dean wants you alive. She— she wants you to sign.

VELVETTE
(her voice dropping to a growl)
Sign what.

OPERATIVE
(whispering)
She said, “the only thing that was ever missing from Blackwood was a better administrator.” She wants Lux to hand over the university and— and walk away. In exchange for— for—

LUX
(softly)
In exchange for?

OPERATIVE
(almost to herself)
For you.

She looks at Velvette. Velvette’s hand tightens on the fork.

VELVETTE
(very evenly)
For me.

OPERATIVE
(barely a breath)
She said you’d come cheaply. She said the leather and the boots were just— just branding. She said you stay with her because she— because she made you.

Silence.

The operative’s eyes slide away from Velvette’s face.

VELVETTE
(still very evenly)
Made me.

OPERATIVE
(whispering)
Because she arranged for you to need her. In first year. The scholarship, the… the roommate, the… the whole—

LUX
(very quietly, very sharply)
That is enough.

The operative stops. The candles in the corridor flicker, though there is no wind.

For a long, long moment nobody moves.

Velvette stands very still in the lift with the fork in her hand and her face unreadable. Lux stands with her back to the mirrors, hands at her sides, and for the first time that evening she is not performing at all.

VELVETTE
(without turning)
Lux.

LUX
(so quietly)
Yes.

VELVETTE
Is it true?

Lux closes her eyes.

LUX
(a whisper)
I’d have preferred to tell you at the cheese course.

Velvette says nothing.

The lift doors begin to close. Velvette puts out a hand and stops them. She steps out into the corridor, and Lux steps out after her, and behind them the doors slide shut on five unconscious operatives and one very slow-bleeding woman with an excellent sense of timing.

In the long mirrors, a hundred of them stand shoulder to shoulder, and none of them are touching.

END OF ACT ONE

ACT TWO

FOURTH COURSE — THE SORBET

INT. PENTHOUSE — TERRACE — NIGHT

A wide stone terrace under a very large sky. Candles in glass globes have been set around the balustrade. Past it the campus glows, and above them the stars are very clear: the kind of night that happens only on the highest floor of the highest spire, when the world feels like a drawing of itself.

Lux stands at the balustrade, her back to the room, her arms folded against the cold. Her gown moves a little in the wind. Velvette stands in the doorway behind her, not coming closer.

There is a small silver tray on the stone table between them: two glasses of lemon sorbet, melting.

VELVETTE
(finally)
Say it.

LUX
(not turning)
Which part?

VELVETTE
All of it.

Lux is quiet for a very long time.

LUX
(softly)
First year. You arrived on a scholarship that didn’t exist.

Velvette doesn’t move.

LUX (CONT’D)
(still to the dark)
I invented it. I wrote the letter, signed it for the Dean, wired the money through three shells and forged the committee’s stamp. I read your file in the admissions office six weeks before term. Your mother had died in March. You were sleeping in a car. You had a place at another school and no way to get there.

VELVETTE
(very quietly)
I remember.

LUX
(a faint, bitter breath)
I’m sure you do.

VELVETTE
(softly)
You gave me the roommate.

LUX
(nodding to the dark)
Priya. Quiet. Kind. A little frightened of everything. I put her next to you because I thought you needed someone who wouldn’t push.

VELVETTE
(a very small, dry sound)
She left in October.

LUX
(with great effort)
I arranged for her to leave in October.

Velvette’s hand tightens on the doorframe.

VELVETTE
(very evenly)
Why.

LUX
(finally turning)
Because by October I wanted you to need me.

Velvette looks at her.

Lux’s face in the candlelight is stripped of every device: the poise, the smile, the little tilt of the head. What is left is a woman of thirty-two with a face she hasn’t made up for anyone.

LUX (CONT’D)
(in a rush, as though it’s costing her blood)
I watched you for a month. In the library, on the stairs, in the corridor. You were so angry. It was the most alive thing I had ever seen. And I thought: I could be anything to her. I could build it. A safe room, a friend, a person who wouldn’t leave. I could give her everything and she would stay.

VELVETTE
(quietly)
And I stayed.

LUX
(her voice cracking very slightly)
And you stayed.

VELVETTE
(a long breath)
For ten years.

LUX
(almost a whisper)
And every night for ten years I have lain awake and wondered if it counts.

Velvette’s face changes.

VELVETTE
(very softly)
If what counts?

LUX
(blinking fast)
If I wanted someone to love me very much, and she does. If I arranged the room she was standing in. Does it count? If I made it?

A very long silence.

It is the question a five-year-old asked a nanny in a nursery the size of most apartments. It is, word for word, the only question Lux has ever really had.

Velvette stands very still in the doorway.

VELVETTE
(finally, almost inaudible)
Is that what the table was for?

LUX
(a very small sound, almost a laugh)
Yes.

VELVETTE
(gently)
Forty feet.

LUX
(nodding)
So that if you looked at me and said no, I’d have time to be on the other side of the room.

Velvette’s breath catches. She walks forward, very slowly. She comes to the stone table and picks up the nearest glass of sorbet. The ice has begun to melt in a pale yellow line down the side.

She sets it down again.

VELVETTE
(very evenly)
Lux.

LUX
(not looking at her)
Mm.

VELVETTE
I knew.

Lux’s head comes up.

LUX
(blankly)
You what?

VELVETTE
(quietly)
I knew by week three.

LUX
(a strangled whisper)
That’s not possible.

VELVETTE
(almost smiling)
Darling, I could smell the invoices from the other end of the corridor.

LUX
(helplessly)
You never said.

VELVETTE
(shrugging, a small gesture)
It didn’t seem to matter.

LUX
(a harsh whisper)
It didn’t matter?

VELVETTE
(stepping closer)
I was nineteen and I’d been sleeping in a car. A girl I didn’t know wrote a forged letter to say I deserved a place. I read it three times. I found the stamp. I found the shells. I knew exactly what you’d done.

LUX
(voice small)
And you stayed.

VELVETTE
(very softly)
Lux. Nobody had ever lied to me to help me before.

Lux stares at her. Her mouth opens and nothing comes out.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(gently, taking another step)
Everybody lies to hurt. You were the first person who ever lied to me to keep me alive. I thought, well. That’s a monster. And she’s on my side.

LUX
(a whisper)
That isn’t— that doesn’t—

VELVETTE
(a faint smile)
It counts.

LUX
(shaking her head slowly)
You can’t know that.

VELVETTE
(very quietly)
Priya wrote to me.

Lux goes absolutely still.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(softly)
Four years ago. She’s a vet now, in Lisbon, two kids, a dog called Gerald. She said she never knew why she left. She wanted to say thank you. She said she’d been miserable in that room and something told her to go, and she’d found out she was happier in Portugal.

LUX
(barely audible)
You never told me.

VELVETTE
(a very small shrug)
You’d never asked.

Lux presses her hand to her mouth. Her eyes are brimming. She looks, for the first time in the entire series, entirely undefended.

LUX
(through her fingers)
Vel.

VELVETTE
(closing the last step between them)
I chose you.

LUX
(a ragged breath)
You didn’t know what you were choosing.

VELVETTE
(with almost savage tenderness)
I knew exactly what I was choosing. I chose the worst person I’d ever met and I made her my family. And I’d do it again. I’d do it tonight, in the dress, with eleven snipers on the roof.

Lux makes a sound that is half a laugh and half a sob.

LUX
(shaking)
Four snipers.

VELVETTE
(almost smiling)
Four snipers.

She takes Lux’s face in both her hands. Her thumbs wipe away the tears. They are both shaking very slightly.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(gently)
You’re an idiot.

LUX
(a wet laugh)
I’m a monster.

VELVETTE
(very softly)
That too.

She kisses her.

It is not like the kiss in the dining room. This one is slow and desperate and entirely without audience. Lux’s hands come up and fist in Velvette’s shirt, pulling her closer, and the sorbet melts on the table, and the stars turn above them like a great slow wheel.

When they finally part, Lux rests her forehead against Velvette’s.

LUX
(a whisper)
I really did have a speech.

VELVETTE
(a breath of laughter)
I heard.

LUX
(furious at the chef, softly)
He told you.

VELVETTE
(shamelessly)
He told me you’d rehearsed it in the bath.

LUX
(shaking her head)
I’m going to boil him.

VELVETTE
(grinning)
Do the speech.

LUX
(helplessly)
Now?

VELVETTE
(gentle)
Please.

Lux closes her eyes. She takes a breath. When she opens them, she is looking at Velvette, only at Velvette, and the guards have come off every part of her face.

LUX
(very quietly)
When I was five, I asked my nanny a question. I said, if I want someone to like me very much, and they do, does it count, if I made them?

VELVETTE
(still, listening)
Yes.

LUX
(softly)
She never answered. She thought about it for a very long time. She died when I was nineteen and I never asked her again.

VELVETTE
(very gently)
Lux.

LUX
(continuing)
So I built a world to find out. I built a school. I built a house with a hundred rooms and a long table, and a thousand schemes, and not one of them ever answered it. Not once. Because none of it was you.

She takes Velvette’s hands.

LUX (CONT’D)
(her voice breaking and steadying)
You’re the only thing in my entire life that I didn’t arrange.

Velvette’s breath stops.

LUX (CONT’D)
(smiling through tears)
I tried. God knows I tried. But you walked in with your boots and your bad language and your ruler on a chain, and you said no. You said no to everything. Every bribe, every favour. Every time I reached for a lever, you broke it off in my hand and told me to go to hell.

VELVETTE
(a rough whisper)
I remember.

LUX
(a laugh)
And I thought: well. That’s it. That’s the one thing I can’t make.

She lifts Velvette’s hand to her mouth and kisses the knuckles.

LUX (CONT’D)
(very softly)
So that’s the answer. It counts. It counts because you could have left, and you didn’t, and nobody arranged it.

Velvette is crying. She is not making any sound about it; the tears simply run down her face.

VELVETTE
(a whisper)
That was a good speech.

LUX
(a wet laugh)
I had a better ending.

VELVETTE
(gruffly)
Oh, yeah?

LUX
(reaching into the bodice of her gown)
It involves a ring.

She takes out a small black box and holds it out on her palm.

Velvette stares at it.

VELVETTE
(a whisper)
We’re already married.

LUX
(smiling)
In a cul-de-sac. In a parallel universe. Where I run the HOA and you grow tomatoes.

VELVETTE
(laughing wetly)
That’s a lovely universe.

LUX
(softly)
I’d like it in this one.

Velvette takes the box. She opens it.

Inside, nested in black velvet, is a ring made of plain silver, with a tiny band of plastic set into it, scored along one side with a child’s capital letters, G E R A L D.

Velvette makes a sound like a person being hit.

VELVETTE
(a hoarse whisper)
You took it out of my chain.

LUX
(gently)
I borrowed it. He’s been in the workshop for six weeks. I had him set in silver by a jeweller who thinks I’m very strange.

VELVETTE
(laughing and crying)
You’re very strange.

LUX
(soft, almost shy)
Is that a yes?

VELVETTE
(wiping her eyes with the back of her wrist)
Lux, I would like to point out that you have just proposed to me with a confiscated ruler.

LUX
(with great dignity)
I proposed with a ruler that is part of you.

Velvette laughs, completely undone. She takes the ring out of the box and holds it up in the candlelight, turning it. The plastic gleams.

VELVETTE
(quietly)
Yes.

LUX
(a breath)
Yes?

VELVETTE
(smiling)
Yes, you absolute lunatic.

She slides it onto her finger. It fits perfectly.

Lux stands there, shaking, completely lost for words for perhaps the first time in her life.

Velvette pulls her in. They kiss again, against the balustrade, in the cold and the candlelight, and the sky spins around them.

The sorbet has become a small yellow lake.

FIFTH COURSE — THE INTERVAL

INT. PENTHOUSE — BEDROOM — LATER

A bedroom of dark velvet and pale linen. The curtains are drawn. A single lamp burns on the nightstand. The bed is enormous and very rumpled.

They are in it.

The scene is quiet. Lux lies with her back against the headboard in a silk slip the colour of ink, her hair loose for the first time that evening and spilling over her shoulders in a pale tangle. Velvette lies with her head on Lux’s stomach, shirtless, tattoos moving as she breathes. A small silver ring glints on her left hand.

Lux is idly combing her fingers through the undercut, over and over.

LUX
(softly)
You didn’t have to say yes.

VELVETTE
(sleepily)
I know.

LUX
(a pause)
I’d have understood if you said no.

VELVETTE
(a breath of laughter)
You would have spent three weeks pretending you hadn’t asked.

LUX
(smiling)
Four.

VELVETTE
(drowsy)
Four.

A long, comfortable silence. Outside, very faintly, there is the sound of a helicopter circling.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(without moving)
Is that Marguerite?

LUX
(listening)
Probably.

VELVETTE
(lazily)
She’s going to be very annoyed.

LUX
(pleasantly)
She’s going to be extremely annoyed.

VELVETTE
(turning her face into Lux’s skin)
Do we have to do anything about it?

LUX
(a slow smile)
Eventually.

VELVETTE
(mumbling)
How eventually?

Lux looks down at her. Something very soft crosses her face.

LUX
(quietly)
Vel.

VELVETTE
Mm.

LUX
Come here.

Velvette lifts her head. They look at each other for a long moment in the lamplight.

Then Velvette moves up the bed, slowly, deliberately, and takes Lux’s face in both hands, and kisses her. It is unhurried and sure, the kiss of a woman who has nowhere else to be and has finally stopped waiting for permission. Lux’s fingers slide into her hair. Lux makes a low sound in the back of her throat, and Velvette answers it.

The slip slides off one shoulder. A hand finds the curve of a hip. The lamp casts their shadows enormous on the curtains, two shapes becoming one, and the shapes move in the dark velvet like an argument that has finally ended.

Lux’s breath comes ragged. Velvette’s voice is rough with something very close to prayer.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(against her skin)
Tell me.

LUX
(barely audible)
Yes.

VELVETTE
(murmuring)
Say it.

LUX
(a gasp, almost laughing)
Vel, there are snipers.

VELVETTE
(not stopping)
Let them watch.

Lux laughs, helplessly, and the laugh turns into something else, and the lamp goes out.

We hold on the dark window. Out in the night the helicopter circles, circles, and finally veers away with the wounded dignity of an aircraft that has been publicly ignored.

CUT TO:

INT. PENTHOUSE — BEDROOM — LATER

The lamp is on again. The bed has become a ruin of linen. They lie tangled together, the sheet barely covering anything, Lux on her back with one arm flung over her head, Velvette on her side with a hand resting low on Lux’s stomach, both of them sweaty and wrecked and smiling like people who have gotten away with a crime.

LUX
(hoarsely)
That was not on the menu.

VELVETTE
(grinning into the pillow)
It was a surprise course.

LUX
(a breath of laughter)
We’re not doing the other four, are we.

VELVETTE
(lazily)
I’d like the cheese.

LUX
(sleepily)
I’m not getting up for the cheese.

VELVETTE
(lifting her head)
You’d get up for the cheese if it was on fire.

LUX
(smiling at the ceiling)
That’s different.

LUX (CONT’D)

Lift.

VELVETTE

Four snipers and a helicopter.

LUX
(reaching for her robe)
She’s very persistent.

VELVETTE
(swinging out of bed, pulling on her shirt)
She’s a menace.

(wrapping the robe around her)
She’s a Dean.

SIXTH COURSE — THE ROAST

INT. PENTHOUSE — DINING ROOM — A LITTLE LATER

The candles have burned low. The long table is unchanged: nine glasses at each place, linen, the two untouched plates. At the far end, behind a very large silver tureen, sits DEAN MARGUERITE ASHDOWN, sixties, grey suit, grey pearls, grey eyes, a face like a closed filing cabinet. She is calmly buttering a roll.

Behind her, along the wall, stand four armed operatives in Lakewood black. They do not look entirely sure of themselves.

Lux and Velvette enter side by side from the hallway in robes and half-buttoned shirts. Lux has her hair down. Velvette’s feet are bare. Neither of them looks like she has been interrupted. They look like they were about to leave a party and have just remembered something.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(without looking up)
Good evening, ladies.

LUX
(sweetly)
Marguerite.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(spreading butter)
I hope I haven’t arrived at an inconvenient time.

VELVETTE
(drily)
You’ve arrived at the cheese course.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(a thin smile)
I took the liberty of seating myself. Your sous-chef was most obliging.

LUX
(taking her seat at her end of the table)
He’s a ghost. He’s very easily led.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(biting into the roll)
I have always admired your staff.

Velvette goes to her own place at the far end and sits. She picks up a knife, looks at it, and sets it down again.

VELVETTE
(pleasantly)
You sent a man with a bomb under an oyster.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(calmly)
It was a diversion.

VELVETTE
You sent a poisoned soup.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(a small shrug)
A second diversion.

VELVETTE
(politely)
You sent six operatives, four snipers, a helicopter, and a quite unforgivable pun about a merger.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(composed)
I’m a woman of many talents.

LUX
(quietly)
What do you want, Marguerite?

The Dean puts down her roll. She folds her napkin once, precisely, and for the first time looks directly at Lux.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(in a perfectly level voice)
I want what I’ve wanted for twenty years. I want to run the best university in the world. And you, my dear, are standing in my way.

LUX
(dryly)
Blackwood isn’t the best university in the world.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(very precisely)
It’s the most interesting. That is a great deal more dangerous.

LUX
(leaning back)
You’ve been trying to buy me out since I was twenty-three.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(evenly)
You’ve been trying to burn me down since you were twenty-two.

LUX
(smiling)
We’ve always had a very honest relationship.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(coolly)
That’s why I’m here. I find I no longer enjoy the game.

LUX
(softly)
Marguerite.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(looking at the ring on Velvette’s hand)
Congratulations, by the way.

A short pause.

VELVETTE
(narrowing her eyes)
How did you—

DEAN ASHDOWN
(mildly)
I have a very good telescope.

LUX
(very quietly)
You were watching.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(unruffled)
My dear, I’ve been watching you for ten years. I know exactly how much you love her. It is the single most valuable fact in the entire institution.

The room goes cold.

LUX
(softly)
Careful.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(continuing, quite gently)
I was not going to hurt her. I want to be perfectly clear. The bomb was a prop. The poison was a sedative. The operatives were not to fire on either of you. This was never an assassination.

VELVETTE
(flatly)
Then what was it?

DEAN ASHDOWN
(turning to her)
A negotiation.

LUX
(slowly)
You wanted to see what I’d do.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(inclining her head)
I wanted to see what you’d do if it came to a choice. Between her and Blackwood. I wanted to know which one you’d sacrifice.

Silence.

VELVETTE
(very quietly)
And?

DEAN ASHDOWN
(with something almost like respect)
I watched you hold a woman you’d known for twenty minutes against a wall over a joke about your marriage. And I watched Lux set fire to a corridor over the word “merger.” I think that is my answer.

LUX
(coldly)
You’ve wasted a very good dinner.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(rising)
No. I’ve saved myself a very costly war.

She nods to the operatives. They lower their weapons, all four at once.

DEAN ASHDOWN (CONT’D)
(buttoning her jacket)
I’ll withdraw the offer. I’ll withdraw the pressure on the Board. I’ll withdraw my men and I’ll never mention a merger again.

LUX
(suspicious)
What’s the catch?

DEAN ASHDOWN
(a very small, very dry smile)
I want an invitation to the wedding.

A very long silence.

LUX
(absolutely deadpan)
You must be joking.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(mildly)
I’m a very good guest.

VELVETTE
(deadpan)
You tried to poison our soup.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(unrepentant)
It was a very good soup.

Lux looks at Velvette. Velvette looks at Lux. For a moment neither of them says anything.

Then Lux starts to laugh. She laughs very quietly at first and then not quietly at all, her head back, the robe slipping off one shoulder, and Velvette puts her face in her hand and her shoulders shake, and the Dean stands watching the two of them with an expression of faint, careful patience, like a woman who has just won a very small war and isn’t entirely certain what she has won.

LUX
(wiping her eyes)
Marguerite, darling.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(dryly)
Yes.

LUX
(still laughing)
You can sit next to Sasha.

DEAN ASHDOWN
(a very slight inclination of her head)
I would be honoured.

She turns and walks out. The operatives follow in a row. The lift doors close behind them. The numbers fall. At the table, the two women sit in the guttering candlelight and listen to the helicopter, very faintly, leaving for good.

ACT THREE

SEVENTH COURSE — THE CHEESE

INT. PENTHOUSE — DINING ROOM — LATER

Dawn is a thin grey line along the edge of the windows. The candles have burned down to stubs. The table is a wreck of linen, glasses and unfinished plates.

At the far end of the table, Lux and Velvette sit side by side on the same side of it. At some point in the last hour, one of them has pulled a chair out of position, and nobody has moved it back. They are eating cheese off the same board with their fingers.

Lux is in her robe, hair wild, makeup gone. Velvette is in her shirt, ring catching the light. A very large, very ugly blue cheese sits between them, and a bottle of port, and a long, slow silence of the sort that only follows an enormous event.

VELVETTE
(finally)
That was the worst anniversary of my life.

LUX
(licking her thumb)
It was the best.

VELVETTE
(considering)
It was both.

LUX
(smiling)
That’s marriage, darling.

VELVETTE
(after a beat)
Is it?

LUX
(very softly)
I’ve been informed.

They eat. A bird begins to sing, tentatively, somewhere on the roof.

VELVETTE
(without looking at her)
I have something to say.

LUX
(quietly)
Please don’t. I’ve had my fill of speeches.

VELVETTE
(a faint smile)
It’s a short one.

LUX
(carefully)
Go on.

Velvette sets down her knife. She wipes her hands on a napkin. When she speaks her voice is low and entirely unadorned.

VELVETTE
When I was nineteen I thought love was a thing that happened to people on the other side of a window.

Lux says nothing. She doesn’t move.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(continuing)
I thought it was for people with houses. Mothers who answered phones. I stopped being angry about it around the time I started sleeping in the car. It just became a fact. Like weather.

LUX
(very softly)
Vel.

VELVETTE
(not looking up)
And then some blonde lunatic forged my scholarship and put a girl in my room and broke her out again when she thought I wasn’t ready.

LUX
(a breath)
That’s—

VELVETTE
(smiling slightly)
I’ve been waiting ten years to say this. Let me finish.

Lux closes her mouth.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(carefully)
You are the worst thing that ever happened to me. You’re vain and manipulative and you’d sell the sun if there was a margin in it. You’ve lied to me about eleven hundred separate things and I’ve counted every one.

LUX
(whispering)
Eleven hundred.

VELVETTE
(a quirk of her mouth)
And twelve. I counted tonight.

LUX
(helplessly)
I’m so sorry.

VELVETTE
(shaking her head)
I’m not finished.

She turns to look at her. Her eyes are very steady.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
You are also the first person who ever built a room for me. You are the only person who has ever stayed up until four in the morning to find out what I was afraid of. You sat on the floor of that flat for three nights when the news about my mother came, and you didn’t say anything. You didn’t fix anything. You just sat there.

LUX
(very quietly)
I didn’t know what else to do.

VELVETTE
(gently)
That’s the point.

Lux’s eyes are full.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(softly)
I don’t care what you arranged. I don’t care what you lied about. Every bad thing you ever did, you did out of a terror so big you built a school around it. And somewhere in the middle of that terror, there was a girl, and she wanted me to be okay.

Her voice cracks.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(a ragged whisper)
Nobody had ever wanted that.

Lux presses the back of her hand to her mouth.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(wiping her face roughly)
So that’s the speech. It counts. It’s always counted. Stop asking.

Lux nods. She can’t speak. She puts her hand on Velvette’s and holds on to it very tightly.

They sit like that for a while with the light growing.

LUX
(finally, hoarsely)
That was better than mine.

VELVETTE
(a damp laugh)
It had a better rehearsal.

LUX
(smiling through tears)
I’m going to cry on the cheese.

VELVETTE
(wryly)
It’s a very good cheese.

LUX
(a sob and a laugh at once)
Stop making me laugh.

VELVETTE
(grinning)
Never.

She puts an arm around Lux’s shoulders and pulls her in. Lux turns her face into Velvette’s neck and stays there. For a long time neither of them says anything at all.

EIGHTH COURSE — THE DESSERT

INT. PENTHOUSE — BEDROOM — MORNING

Full daylight. The curtains are open. Gold light pours across the bed, across the rumpled sheets and the scattered clothing, across two women asleep in a heap.

Velvette is on her stomach, one arm dangling off the mattress, the ring glinting. Lux lies curled against her back, face buried in her shoulder, one hand spread over her ribs as though to keep her from going anywhere.

On the nightstand, a small silver tray has been placed at some point in the past hour by an invisible hand: a pot of coffee, two cups, and a single slice of chocolate cake with a candle in it, unlit. A card leans against the plate. The handwriting is a chef’s.

The card reads: ON THE HOUSE. HAPPY ANNIVERSARY, MA’AMS. — YOUR STAFF.

Velvette opens one eye. She looks at the cake for a long moment.

VELVETTE
(muffled, into the pillow)
Lux.

LUX
(asleep)
Mm.

VELVETTE
There’s cake.

LUX
(not opening her eyes)
Is it poisoned?

VELVETTE

No.

Pity.

You love me.

I loathe you.

Come here.

LUX

I’m not eating cake in bed.

It’s our anniversary.

LUX
(sighing, taking the fork)
It’s a terrible precedent.

LUX (CONT’D)
(with her mouth full)
What.

VELVETTE
(shaking her head, smiling)
Nothing.

LUX
(suspicious)
Say it.

VELVETTE

Nothing. You’re just very easy to look at.

(swallowing)
You’re being sentimental.

VELVETTE
(unrepentant)
I’m allowed. I’m engaged.

LUX
(very drily)
You’re insufferable.

VELVETTE
(kissing her on the temple)
You’re stuck with me.

LUX
(leaning into it, very quietly)
I know.

The light shifts. Far below them, on the Ring Road, the first students are walking to their first class. A bell rings across the campus.

LUX (CONT’D)
(listening)
Sasha will be in at nine.

VELVETTE
(sighing)
I have to cut off her hands.

LUX
(finishing the cake)
Only one.

VELVETTE
(considering)
I could do one.

LUX
(handing her the empty plate)
She’s a very good lawyer.

VELVETTE
(setting it aside)
She sent a poisoned soup into my kitchen.

LUX
(lightly)
She’ll be very contrite.

VELVETTE
(grinning)
She’ll be bleeding.

LUX
(a slow smile)
Then she’ll be contrite and bleeding. It’s the best kind.

They laugh. The laughter fades into a comfortable quiet, two people who have said everything.

NINTH COURSE — THE BILL

INT. PENTHOUSE — DINING ROOM — DAY

The room has been tidied. Daylight streams in over the long table. The nine glasses at each setting have been cleared; the forty feet of walnut have been shortened by thirty-eight. A pair of very strong ghostly movers are shoving the last of it into the corner, where two chairs now sit side by side at one end of a table that is, for the first time, about the size of a normal table.

On the wall, the framed menu has been taken down. In its place hangs a plain white card in a small steel frame: THE BILL. PAID IN FULL.

Lux and Velvette stand in the doorway in their robes, watching the movers work.

LUX
(softly)
It looks very strange.

VELVETTE
(admiring)
It looks like a table.

LUX
(a faint smile)
I hated it for six weeks.

VELVETTE
(not looking at her)
You can order another one if you want.

LUX
(shaking her head)
No.

She slips her hand into Velvette’s. The ring on Velvette’s finger catches the light.

LUX (CONT’D)
(quietly)
I think I’d like to be able to reach.

Velvette squeezes her hand, hard.

VELVETTE
(husky)
Good.

The movers finish. They pull the chairs together at the new table. One of them, a very small ghost in a cap, looks at the two women, then at the empty chairs, and tips his cap with great dignity before leaving.

Lux goes to the chair on the left. She sits. Velvette goes to the chair on the right. She sits. For a moment they look at each other across a distance of perhaps thirty inches.

Then, at the same moment, without speaking, both of them lean forward until their elbows touch the table, and their foreheads meet in the middle, over the white linen, in the morning light.

LUX
(a whisper)
Happy anniversary.

VELVETTE
(a whisper)
Happy anniversary, you absolute disaster.

The phone on the sideboard begins to ring. Neither of them moves.

It rings eleven times.

Somewhere in the distance, faint and cheerful, a bell tolls for first period.

LUX
(eyes closed, smiling)
That’ll be Sasha.

VELVETTE
(eyes closed, smiling)
Let her wait.

The phone rings. The light moves slowly across the table.

INT. SASHA’S OFFICE — LATER

(sleepy, warm, perfectly pleasant)
Sasha, darling.

(pained)
Lux. Thank God. Marguerite Ashdown is in the lobby and she’s asking for a seating plan.

Put her next to you.

(horrified)
Why would I be—

(from further away, perfectly cheerful)
And Sasha.

(freezing)
Yes?

About the soup.

(very carefully)
I can explain.

Should I bring a tourniquet?

LUX (V.O.)
(a smile you can hear)
Bring a pen. It’s a wedding.

The line goes dead.

Sasha sits perfectly still with the receiver in her hand. She looks at the orchid, at her left hand, at the pen. Very slowly, she opens a drawer, takes out a clean pair of white gloves and a first-aid kit, and puts both in her bag.

SASHA
(to the empty office)
It’s a wedding.

She stands, straightens her jacket, and goes to the door.

CUT TO BLACK.

THE END
