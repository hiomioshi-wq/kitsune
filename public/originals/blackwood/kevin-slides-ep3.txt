KEVIN SLIDES
A BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY SPINOFF

EPISODE THREE: “HELL’S PEAK ESTATES”

Previously: Kevin and Kevina delivered a million dead letters, balanced a ghost’s ledger, and drank tea with the Reaper’s mother, who told Kevin there are two stops left on the route. The next is a very silly place. The last is a very sad one. They’re both home. It depends who’s opening the door.

Content warning: strong language, dark humour, suburban violence, cannibalism-adjacent casserole, passive-aggressive notes, and a lawn that is more dangerous than it looks.

TEASER

EXT. HELL’S PEAK ESTATES — CUL-DE-SAC — SATURDAY, 10:00 A.M.

A cul-de-sac so perfect it looks printed. Twelve identical houses in a gentle horseshoe. White picket fences. Lawns trimmed to a uniform two and a half inches. Sprinklers ticking in unison like a chorus of very polite metronomes.

A sign at the entrance, carved from white stone: HELL’S PEAK ESTATES. A GATED COMMUNITY OF GOOD STANDING. NO SOLICITORS. NO EXCEPTIONS. NO EXITS.

The sun is shining. Birds sing. Somewhere, a radio plays a jingle for a casserole.

On the centre of the central lawn stands a tall flagpole. At its top, flapping gently in the breeze, a flag reads: HOA — ORDER, CLEANLINESS, OBEDIENCE.

A sprinkler pops. A bird falls out of the sky and lands in the geraniums, dead.

A woman in a floral apron walks out of Number Seven with a trowel, picks the bird up by one wing, examines it, and drops it into a small, immaculate bin labelled COMPOST (APPROVED).

She wipes her hands on her apron, nods with satisfaction, and goes back inside.

Silence.

Then, from the sky, a scream.

Kevin and Kevina fall out of nothing, tumble across a lawn, and come to rest in a hedge. The Parcel bounces out of Kevin’s arms and rolls to a stop at the foot of the flagpole.

A sprinkler turns toward them with a soft, menacing hiss.

KEVIN
(muffled, from the hedge)
Is it Saturday?

KEVINA
(muffled, from beside him)
It’s always Saturday.

KEVIN
(muffled)
That’s unsettling.

KEVINA
(muffled)
It’s a gated community.

From the nearest window, a curtain twitches.

The title is printed on a laminated notice and taped to a lamppost. Under it, in small neat type: A VIOLATION HAS BEEN RECORDED.

ACT ONE

EXT. HELL’S PEAK ESTATES — CUL-DE-SAC — CONTINUOUS

Kevin extracts himself from the hedge, picking leaves out of his hair. Kevina floats up behind him, translucent in the bright suburban sunlight, a mop in one hand and a twig in the other.

KEVIN
(looking around)
It’s very clean.

KEVINA
(grimly)
It’s very Lux.

KEVIN
(carefully)
Is that bad?

KEVINA
(whispering)
Kevin. There’s a reason every house is the same colour.

KEVIN
(also whispering)
Because of the HOA?

KEVINA
Because they’re all the same house.

Kevin looks along the cul-de-sac. Twelve houses. Twelve identical porches. Twelve identical doors, each with a wreath that reads WELCOME. On every lawn, a small blue sign: THIS LAWN IS MONITORED.

He picks up the Parcel. It is shivering.

BOX
(very small)
Don’t put me down.

KEVIN
(gently)
I won’t.

BOX
(whispering)
She knows I’m here.

KEVIN
(carefully)
Which she?

BOX
(whispering, miserable)
The one who runs the block.

A shadow falls across the lawn. Kevin turns.

A woman stands at the end of the driveway, haloed by sunlight. White blouse, pearls, a pleated skirt and a pair of gardening gloves in a very pale shade of lilac. A name tag on her lapel reads: PRESIDENT, HOMEOWNERS’ ASSOCIATION.

It is LUX. Her blonde hair is in a perfect bun. She is smiling with a warmth that could freeze a pond.

LUX
(sweetly)
Well. Hello, neighbour.

KEVIN
(dropping the twig)
Hi. Sorry. We were just… landing.

LUX
(gliding forward)
Landing is a very serious violation of Section Four, Paragraph Nine.

KEVIN
(carefully)
Is it?

LUX
(delighted)
“No unscheduled arrivals on the common lawn.”

KEVIN
(small)
I’m so sorry. I didn’t read the paragraph.

LUX
(patting his arm, with a gloved hand)
Nobody ever does. That’s why I wrote it.

She looks at Kevina, who is trying to hide behind a very small bush.

LUX (CONT’D)
(without losing her smile)
And you’ve brought a friend.

KEVINA
(squeaking)
Hello, Mrs. Lux.

LUX
(sweetly)
It’s President.

KEVINA
(whispering)
Of course. Sorry.

LUX
(turning back to Kevin)
Now. What’s in the box?

KEVIN
(clutching it)
Nothing. It’s a personal item.

LUX
(sweetly)
Section Seven. “All packages must be inspected by the Welcome Committee.”

KEVIN
(carefully)
I don’t think that applies to this one.

LUX
(softly)
Kevin.

KEVIN
(stiffening)
How do you know my name?

LUX
(smiling)
It’s on the visitor’s log.

KEVIN
(looking around)
There isn’t a log.

LUX
(gently)
There’s always a log.

A second figure steps out from behind a hedge. Tall. Broad-shouldered. A leaf blower strapped to her back like a flamethrower. A khaki uniform, a sheriff’s badge pinned to the chest, mirrored sunglasses, and a pair of steel-capped gardening boots.

It is VELVETTE. Her badge reads: HOA ENFORCEMENT OFFICER. Under it, a smaller plate: DEPUTY OF THE LAWN.

VELVETTE
(drawling, thumbs hooked in her belt)
I hear we have a trespasser.

LUX
(not turning)
It’s a very polite one.

VELVETTE
(lowering her sunglasses)
Those are the worst.

She looks at Kevin. She looks at the box. She looks at Kevina, who has begun to shake.

VELVETTE (CONT’D)
(gravelly, fond, murderous)
Well, hello, little ghost. You missing a fence?

KEVINA
(whispering)
Please don’t blow me.

VELVETTE
(grinning)
Sweetheart. I’m going to need you to define “blow.”

LUX
(reproachfully)
Darling.

VELVETTE
(shrugging)
It’s a sprinkler season. Everything’s a little wet.

LUX
(to Kevin)
Welcome to Hell’s Peak Estates. We hope you enjoy your stay.

KEVIN
(carefully)
How long is it?

LUX
(beaming)
Indefinitely.

KEVIN
(politely)
I’m afraid I have to go.

LUX
(softly)
That’s the funniest thing anybody’s said all week.

She snaps her fingers. The sprinklers, all twelve of them, turn in perfect unison and aim at Kevin’s feet.

Music starts. A cheery, brassy, deeply sinister jingle played on a ukulele, a vacuum cleaner, and a choir of children who have clearly been told to smile.

“WELCOME TO THE NEIGHBOURHOOD”

LUX
(singing, bright, honeyed)
Welcome, welcome, one and all,
To the finest place you’ll ever fall.
We’ve got lawns, we’ve got fences,
We’ve got rules that make no senses.

VELVETTE
(singing, rough, leering)
We’ve got a pool and a very long fence,
We’ve got a casserole and no defence.
You can try to run, you can try to hide,
But you’ve got to be home by suppertide.

NEIGHBOURS
(from every window, in sugary, dead-eyed harmony)
Welcome, welcome, stay for good!
We’d be so thrilled if you only could!
Welcome, welcome, don’t be shy,
It’s just a place where you’re going to die!

KEVIN
(over the music)
I’m sorry, what was that last line?

LUX
(singing)
Welcome, welcome, don’t you fret!
You can’t get out! You haven’t yet!

Twelve front doors open at once. Twelve neighbours step out onto twelve porches, each smiling the same wide, glassy smile. They wave. They all wave at precisely the same speed.

At the end of the horseshoe, on the porch of the largest house, a thirteenth door opens. A figure steps out. Short. Auburn hair. Pink ribbon. A frilled apron and a platter of cupcakes held like a shield.

It is MADISON. Her name tag reads: WELCOME WAGON. Her smile has gone beyond too wide into the territory of a crime scene.

MADISON
(sweetly, to Kevin)
Bestie!

KEVIN
(whispering, horrified)
Not you again.

MADISON
(rushing across the lawn)
You look like you’ve had the longest day. Have a little treat.

KEVIN
(stepping back)
I’m not hungry.

MADISON
(still advancing)
It’s a cupcake, not a mortgage.

KEVIN
(backing into the hedge)
I really don’t want a cupcake.

MADISON
(softly, sweetly, with a hint of steel)
Kevin. In this neighbourhood, everyone has a cupcake.

LUX
(from behind)
It’s in the by-laws.

KEVIN
(small)
I don’t think I’ve read those by-laws.

VELVETTE
(grinning)
Neither has anybody else. That’s why they work.

Madison holds the cupcake an inch from his face. It glows faintly pink at the edges. A single sugar heart sits on top, trembling, like a tiny, eager eye.

KEVINA
(whispering)
Kevin, don’t.

KEVIN
(whispering back)
I wasn’t going to.

MADISON
(sweetly)
Just one bite.

KEVIN
(politely)
No, thank you.

MADISON
(faltering)
Just a nibble?

KEVIN
(politely)
Please, no.

At the word, the cupcake sags. The frosting droops. The sugar heart slides off the top and lands with a tiny plink on the lawn.

Madison stares at it.

MADISON
(whispering)
It doesn’t work on you.

KEVIN
(carefully)
I’m sorry. I’m very average.

MADISON
(wounded, outraged)
You’re not allowed to be immune! It’s not in the by-laws!

LUX
(coolly)
Madison.

MADISON
(turning, desperate)
He said please.

LUX
(dangerously calm)
I heard.

A long, long silence. Twelve neighbours, twelve smiles, twelve pairs of glassy eyes, all turning to look at Kevin.

VELVETTE
(very softly)
Oh, this is going to be fun.

ACT TWO

INT. LUX AND VELVETTE’S HOUSE — LIVING ROOM — DAY

A showroom living room. White sofa. White carpet. White curtains. A coffee table with a single vase of white lilies. On the mantelpiece, a framed photograph of Lux and Velvette, in matching Christmas sweaters, grinning at the camera. The caption reads: FAMILY.

Kevin sits on the very edge of the sofa, as far from the cushions as possible. The Parcel is on his knee. Kevina has been placed on a footstool, a little to one side, still holding her mop. Her mop has been placed in a vase.

Lux sits opposite, in an armchair, legs crossed, a china teacup balanced on her knee. Velvette stands at the window, leaf blower still strapped to her back, staring out through the venetian blinds.

LUX
(sipping her tea)
Now. Kevin.

KEVIN
(small)
Yes?

LUX
(gently)
You are a guest in our home.

KEVIN
(carefully)
Thank you for having me.

LUX
(sweetly)
It was not a request.

A grandfather clock chimes. Every note is a flat, clinking F.

LUX (CONT’D)
(setting her cup down)
Do you know why we’re so careful about neighbours?

KEVIN
(carefully)
Because of the lawns?

LUX
(delighted)
Because of the lawns.

VELVETTE
(still at the window)
Because of the leaves.

LUX
(nodding)
Because of the leaves, darling.

KEVIN
(small)
What’s wrong with the leaves?

LUX
(leaning forward, voice dropping)
They come from outside.

KEVIN
(carefully)
Isn’t that what leaves do?

LUX
(sweetly)
Not here.

She rises. She walks to the front window and parts the blinds with two lilac-gloved fingers.

LUX (CONT’D)
(softly)
Come and look.

Kevin hesitates. Then he gets up and crosses the room. Lux pulls the blinds apart.

Beyond the glass, past the perfectly trimmed lawn, past the white picket fence, past the pristine street, the world ends.

It does not end subtly. It ends the way a cliff ends. A wall of black, roiling smoke fills the sky from horizon to horizon. Beneath it, a plain of broken concrete and burning wreckage, thousands of twisted shapes, a sea of ash. Lightning forks silently across the clouds. Distant screams, thin as wind.

Kevin stares.

KEVIN
(whispering)
What is that?

LUX
(calmly)
The outside.

KEVIN
(horrified)
Is that real?

VELVETTE
(from behind them)
It’s the Apocalypse, Kevin.

KEVIN
(slowly)
We’re inside it?

VELVETTE
(chewing a toothpick)
We’re the only part still standing.

KEVIN
(carefully)
Why?

LUX
(with a small, proud smile)
Because we have a very good association.

The curtain falls. The room is calm again, bright, clean, white.

KEVIN
(shaking)
I don’t understand.

LUX
(returning to her chair)
Nobody does at first. It takes about three weeks.

KEVIN
(carefully)
Do people leave?

VELVETTE
(grinning)
They try.

KEVIN
(whispering)
What happens to them?

VELVETTE
(stroking the leaf blower)
They get blown away.

KEVIN
(horrified)
By the wind?

VELVETTE
By me.

Kevina, on her footstool, makes a small, wounded sound like a kettle in distress.

KEVINA
(whispering)
Kevin, I think we should go.

LUX
(sweetly)
Oh, but you’ve only just arrived.

She lifts a small silver bell from the side table and rings it once. A door opens at the back of the room. Out comes a maid in a black dress and a white lace cap, carrying a tray of freshly baked rolls.

It is SASHA. Her eyes are glassy. Her hair is in a net. Her name tag reads: STAFF. She walks with the unhurried, hollow grace of someone who has been told that dignity is for other people.

SASHA
(monotone)
Tea is served.

KEVIN
(horrified)
Sasha?

SASHA
(flat)
I’m the help.

KEVIN
(carefully)
Are you okay?

SASHA
(still monotone)
I’m extremely well compensated.

KEVIN
(small)
That doesn’t sound like a yes.

SASHA
(pouring tea, without expression)
I’m obligated to say it’s a yes.

VELVETTE
(chuckling)
She’s on the pension plan. It’s the one where we keep her alive.

KEVIN
(horrified)
That’s not a pension.

VELVETTE
(grinning)
It is if you squint.

LUX
(smiling serenely)
Don’t worry about Sasha. She’s very happy. Aren’t you, Sasha?

SASHA
(flat)
I’m extremely happy.

LUX
(softly)
Say it again.

SASHA
(flat)
I am extremely happy.

LUX
(sweetly)
There. You see?

Kevin looks at Sasha. Sasha looks at Kevin. For half a second, behind her dead, glassy eyes, something flickers. A tiny flash of the real Sasha, furious, trapped, screaming silently through a net of lace.

KEVIN
(very quietly)
I’m so sorry.

SASHA
(not moving her lips)
Please. Get me out.

KEVIN
(carefully)
What?

SASHA
(almost inaudible)
Please.

At the word, a hairline crack runs across the china teacup in her hand. It spreads, widening, with a faint singing crack.

LUX
(sharp)
Sasha. Dish.

SASHA
(blank)
Yes, ma’am.

The cup splits. Hot tea pours over her fingers. She doesn’t flinch.

KEVIN
(horrified)
She’s burning!

LUX
(calmly)
It’s a very good tea.

VELVETTE
(not looking away from the window)
We should probably give him the tour.

LUX
(rising)
An excellent idea. Kevin, darling, put your box in the hallway. We don’t allow luggage in the living room.

KEVIN
(holding it tightly)
I’d rather keep it with me.

LUX
(smiling, the kind of smile you see on knives)
It’s not a request.

BOX
(very quietly)
Don’t.

KEVIN
(carefully)
I’ll take it with me.

Lux’s smile does not move. But the room gets very slightly colder. The lilies on the table droop. The grandfather clock stops mid-tick.

LUX
(softly)
Kevin.

KEVIN
(politely)
Please.

LUX
(gently)
Please what?

KEVIN
(taking a breath)
Please let me keep it.

A long, long silence. The clock starts again. Lux’s smile loosens, very slightly, by a hair.

LUX
(after a moment)
I’ll allow it. For now.

KEVIN
(politely)
Thank you.

LUX
(turning away)
It’s the manners, you see. They’re a weakness.

VELVETTE
(with a glance at Kevin)
They’re an annoyance.

LUX
(not turning)
They’re a different thing.

She leaves the room. Velvette follows, boots thumping. Sasha, still holding the broken cup, stays standing in the middle of the carpet.

Kevin takes a step toward her.

KEVIN
(whispering)
I’m going to come back.

SASHA
(blank)
Of course.

KEVIN
(whispering)
I promise.

SASHA
(not moving)
Promises aren’t in the by-laws.

KEVIN
(smiling a little)
Then I’ll add one.

For half a second, Sasha’s lip twitches.

EXT. HELL’S PEAK ESTATES — CUL-DE-SAC — LATER

A tour. Lux walks briskly down the centre of the street, gloved hand lightly resting on Kevin’s elbow, steering him like a very small, very polite ship. Velvette strolls at the rear, dragging the leaf blower. Kevina trails behind, mop in hand.

The neighbours are now out in force, each on their lawn, trimming hedges, polishing mailboxes, weeding. They don’t look up. They hum in a single, unified, tuneless drone.

LUX
(gesturing)
Number One. Mr. and Mrs. Bell. Marvellous couple. They died in the fire, but they were so keen to stay.

A pair of translucent figures in matching cardigans wave from the porch.

LUX (CONT’D)
Number Two. Ms. Quill. A writer. Fiction, mostly. Poor thing can’t spell.

A small pale woman at a window waves a typewriter.

LUX (CONT’D)
Number Three. The Hargroves. Very nice. Very quiet. Very eaten.

A family of four sit side by side on a sofa, their faces entirely blank, each with a pink sugar heart pinned to their collar.

KEVIN
(looking away)
That’s horrifying.

LUX
(serenely)
It’s a lifestyle.

VELVETTE
(from behind)
You’ll get used to it.

KEVIN
(carefully)
What happens to the ones who don’t?

LUX
(sweetly)
We don’t have those.

VELVETTE
(grinning)
We have fertiliser.

They stop at a small wooden gate at the end of the street, in a high hedge. A sign on the gate reads: THE COMMUNITY GARDEN. KEEP OUT. THIS MEANS YOU.

Beyond the hedge, the faint sound of digging.

KEVIN
(carefully)
What’s in the garden?

LUX
(opening the gate with a flourish)
The future.

The garden is huge. Row on row of neat, well-tended vegetable beds, tomatoes, runner beans, marrows the size of small dogs. A scarecrow in a floral apron stands in the middle, arms outstretched.

Kevin stares at the scarecrow.

It is a person. Pinned to a cross of garden canes, wrapped in sacking, stuffed with straw. A sign hangs round its neck: SCARECROW (RETIRED HOA TREASURER).

The scarecrow lifts its head.

SCARECROW
(whispering, hoarse)
Help.

KEVIN
(horrified)
Oh my God.

SCARECROW
(faint)
I just asked for a receipt.

KEVIN
(stepping forward)
We have to get him down!

LUX
(laying a hand on his arm)
Kevin. He’s in remission.

KEVIN
(shaking her off)
He’s a person!

LUX
(sweetly)
He’s a very good scarecrow. The crows adore him.

A single crow lands on the scarecrow’s shoulder and begins, delicately, to peck.

SCARECROW
(sobbing)
It’s been four years.

KEVIN
(taking a breath)
Please.

The word hangs in the air.

The scarecrow’s ropes go slack. The garden canes crack. The straw bursts open, and the Retired Treasurer falls out in a flurry of sacking, gasping, shaking, alive.

Lux’s face, for the first time, changes. A hairline crack runs across her smile.

LUX
(very quietly)
Kevin.

KEVIN
(kneeling beside the treasurer)
I’m sorry. I couldn’t help it.

LUX
(softly)
You’ve done a very serious thing.

VELVETTE
(unslinging the leaf blower)
Oh, this is going to be great.

KEVIN
(looking up)
I just said please.

LUX
(trembling with something that isn’t anger)
Nobody says please here.

VELVETTE
(grinning)
It’s a fucking violation.

LUX
(whispering)
It’s the only one that matters.

ACT THREE

EXT. HELL’S PEAK ESTATES — COMMUNITY GARDEN — CONTINUOUS

The leaf blower roars to life. Velvette aims it at Kevin like a flamethrower. A gout of orange fire erupts from its nozzle, roaring across the garden bed. Kevin dives behind a trellis. The tomatoes explode.

KEVIN
(shouting)
VELVETTE, PLEASE!

VELVETTE
(shouting)
THAT’S THE WRONG FUCKING WORD!

KEVINA
(floating up, mop raised)
Stop it! You’re going to hurt him!

VELVETTE
(turning the flamethrower)
That’s the idea, ghost.

She fires. The flame passes straight through Kevina, who looks down at herself, unharmed, and then up at Velvette with an expression of enormous reproach.

KEVINA
(flatly)
I’m already dead.

VELVETTE
(lowering the blower)
Oh. Right.

KEVINA
(sharply)
That’s extremely rude.

VELVETTE
(shrugging)
Sorry. Force of habit.

LUX
(from the gate, composed again)
Velvette. Stand down.

VELVETTE
(still aiming)
Lux, he’s a fucking violation.

LUX
(coolly)
He’s a guest.

VELVETTE
(lowering the blower with a pout)
Fine.

LUX
(approaching Kevin, who is huddled behind the trellis)
Kevin, darling. We have a lovely feast tonight.

KEVIN
(peering out)
I’d really rather not.

LUX
(sweetly)
It’s the annual block party. Everyone goes.

KEVIN
(carefully)
What’s on the menu?

LUX
(smiling)
A surprise.

KEVIN
(small)
I’ve had a lot of those today.

LUX
(softly)
This one has a cake.

She holds out a gloved hand. The Retired Treasurer, still shaking, is hauled to his feet by two silent neighbours who have appeared from nowhere. They drag him away.

KEVIN
(standing)
Where are they taking him?

LUX
(fondly)
To be cleaned up.

KEVIN
(slowly)
Cleaned?

LUX
(gently)
He’ll be fine by supper.

She takes Kevin’s arm. Her grip is surprisingly strong.

LUX (CONT’D)
(softly)
You’ll see.

INT. HELL’S PEAK ESTATES — COMMUNITY HALL — EVENING

A long white hall with a polished floor and a stage at the far end. Rows of trestle tables covered in white cloth, each set for thirteen, each with a small vase of lilies. The air smells of cut grass and bleach.

A banner across the stage reads: THE 47TH ANNUAL BLOCK PARTY. SPECIAL GUEST OF HONOUR: KEVIN.

Kevin is at the high table, in a seat of honour, a napkin tucked in his collar, the Parcel clutched in his lap. On his left sits Lux, radiant in a white evening gown. On his right sits Madison, a corsage of pink sugar roses on her wrist.

Kevina is at the far end of the table, bound to her chair with a very long scarf, mop leaning at her side. She has been given a place card that reads: GUEST (DECEASED).

The neighbours file in, twelve of them, all in their Sunday best, all smiling, all humming the same tuneless drone. They sit down at the trestle tables with a single, synchronised scrape of chairs.

LUX
(rising, tapping her glass)
Neighbours. Friends. Associates.

The humming stops.

LUX (CONT’D)
(beaming)
It is my very great pleasure to welcome you to the forty-seventh annual block party.

A round of polite applause.

LUX (CONT’D)
(raising her glass)
It has been an extraordinary year. We have kept our lawns trimmed. We have kept our hedges straight. And we have kept the Apocalypse at bay.

APPLAUSE.

LUX (CONT’D)
(softer, sweeter)
It has not been easy. There have been sacrifices.

A single neighbour at the back, a pale man in a blazer, bursts into tears.

LUX (CONT’D)
(gently)
Mr. Hargrove. We remember.

MR. HARGROVE
(sobbing)
Thank you, Madam President.

LUX
(smiling)
This evening, we have the honour of a guest.

All eyes turn to Kevin.

LUX (CONT’D)
(warmly)
Kevin. Would you like to say a few words?

KEVIN
(standing, with the napkin still in his collar)
Um. Hello.

NEIGHBOURS
(in unison, bright)
Hello, Kevin.

KEVIN
(small)
Thank you for having me.

NEIGHBOURS
(in unison)
Welcome, Kevin.

KEVIN
(taking a breath)
I’m so sorry, but I think there’s been a mistake.

A pin drops somewhere. Every smile freezes.

KEVIN (CONT’D)
(carefully)
I’m not a guest. I’m a courier. I’m delivering this box.

He holds up the Parcel.

A rustle of horror runs through the hall. Several neighbours put down their forks.

LUX
(very quietly)
Kevin.

KEVIN
(politely)
And I’m afraid I’m going to have to go.

LUX
(softly)
You haven’t had dessert.

KEVIN
(carefully)
I don’t think I want any.

LUX
