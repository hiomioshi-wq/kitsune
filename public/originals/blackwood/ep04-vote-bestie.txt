BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY
EPISODE 4 — “VOTE BESTIE”

TEASER

EXT. BLACKWOOD QUAD — MORNING

A folding table. A pink gingham tablecloth. A hand-lettered sign covered in so much glitter it has its own weather:

FREE FRIENDSHIP CUPCAKES!!!
(TAKE ONE, BESTIE!!!)

Behind it stands MADISON.

Auburn hair. Pink ribbon. Pastel pink sweater vest. A smile that is too wide and has been too wide since she was nine.

The cupcakes are perfect. Pink frosting. A little sugar heart on each one. They glow very faintly at the edges, the way a nightlight glows when you are too old to admit you need it.

A STUDENT walks past with a coffee, eyes on his phone.

MADISON
Bestie!

STUDENT
Sorry, I’m late for—

Madison is suddenly in front of him. She did not walk. She is simply there, holding a cupcake at mouth height, like a sommelier.

MADISON
You look like you’ve had the longest week. Have a little treat.

STUDENT
I’m fine, thanks.

MADISON
It’s free.

STUDENT
I’m vegan.

MADISON
So is this one.

It is not. The frosting is mostly butter and intent.

He takes a bite to be polite.

Behind his eyes, something soft and pink turns over.

Madison leans in. Her voice drops, sweet and warm, like syrup pouring itself.

MADISON
You remember, don’t you? First week? When the east stairwell had you by the ankle and I pulled you out by your hoodie strings?

The student blinks.

His eyes fill.

STUDENT
Oh my God.

MADISON
Mm-hm.

STUDENT
Madison. Madison, you saved my LIFE.

MADISON
It was nothing.

STUDENT
It was EVERYTHING. I was halfway down. I could see its tonsils. Stairs don’t even HAVE tonsils.

He hugs her.

He keeps hugging her.

MADISON
Okay. Okay, bestie. Little bit less. Perfect.

He lets go.

He stands beside the table like a guard.

A queue has already formed.

Madison beams down it.

MADISON
Who’s next for a little treat?

The campus speakers crackle.

ANNOUNCER (V.O.)
Good morning, Blackwood University. Nominations for Student Council President close today at noon. The election will be held Friday. Candidates must collect one hundred signatures. Last year’s winner collected ninety-nine signatures and a hostage. Please do not do that again.

Madison freezes mid-hand-off.

The pink glow around the cupcake brightens.

MADISON
An election?

STUDENT
Yeah. You vote for who you like best.

Madison turns slowly toward him.

Her smile widens past the point a human face is legally allowed to.

MADISON
Who you like BEST?

BLACKWOOD UNIVERSITY

A sugar heart lands on the title card.

The title card blushes.

ACT ONE

INT. MORTALITY SERVICES — LATE MORNING

The PRINTER is printing.

It has been printing for an hour.

A paper drift reaches YUKI TANAKA’s knees. She wades through it holding a coffee in a mug that says I DON’T EAT AT WORK.

She picks up a sheet.

NEAR-DEATH EXPERIENCE REPORT.
STUDENT: P. OYELARAN.
INCIDENT: NEARLY EATEN BY EAST STAIRWELL.
RESCUED BY: MADISON.

She picks up another.

NEAR-DEATH EXPERIENCE REPORT.
STUDENT: C. HOLT.
INCIDENT: NEARLY DROWNED IN THE DUCK POND.
RESCUED BY: MADISON.

Another.

STUDENT: T. MBEKI.
INCIDENT: NEARLY CRUSHED BY A GARGOYLE.
RESCUED BY: MADISON.

Another.

STUDENT: F. LAUTNER.
INCIDENT: NEARLY CHOKED ON A FRIENDSHIP CUPCAKE.
RESCUED BY: MADISON.

Yuki reads that one twice.

YUKI
She saved him from her own fucking cupcake.

The GRIM REAPER sits at her desk across the office, reading the same reports in a neat stack. She does not look up.

GRIM REAPER
Nine hundred and twelve.

YUKI
Nine hundred and twelve near-deaths?

GRIM REAPER
In one morning.

YUKI
Did anyone nearly die?

GRIM REAPER
No.

YUKI
You’re sure?

The Reaper finally looks up.

GRIM REAPER
I am Death. If someone nearly dies, I feel a little tug, like a cat pawing a curtain. I have felt no tugs. The curtain is undisturbed.

YUKI
Then why the fuck are they filing reports?

GRIM REAPER
Because they remember nearly dying. When a person remembers a near-death, the form generates itself. It’s a very old system. It was designed for knights.

YUKI
And they all remember Madison.

GRIM REAPER
Every one.

Yuki drops the report back on the drift.

YUKI
It’s the cupcakes.

GRIM REAPER
Probably.

YUKI
It’s definitely the cupcakes. She did it to me in the library. I carried her bags through six stores. I bought her a lamp.

GRIM REAPER
Why a lamp?

YUKI
Because I remembered she pulled me out of a fire during a power cut and I felt like she deserved fucking light.

The Reaper sets down her stack.

GRIM REAPER
Memory isn’t my department.

YUKI
No.

GRIM REAPER
Paperwork is.

YUKI
Oh, no.

GRIM REAPER
Find her. Get her to stop. Every one of these has to be investigated, and if it’s false, closed, and if it’s true, I want to know why I didn’t feel the curtain.

YUKI
And if she offers me a cupcake?

GRIM REAPER
Don’t eat it.

YUKI
I don’t eat at work.

GRIM REAPER
I know. It’s the only thing I like about you.

Yuki looks at her mug.

YUKI
It’s not a fucking slogan. It’s a survival strategy.

INT. PANOPTICON OBSERVATORY — SAME TIME

LUX stands in front of a wall of screens, a crystal goblet of something pink in one hand.

Every screen shows the quad. The queue at Madison’s table is now four hundred people long and loops twice around the fountain.

VELVETTE stands behind her with her arms folded and a stiletto blade extending and retracting from the heel of her boot. SHNK. SHNK. SHNK.

VELVETTE
She’s running for president.

LUX
I know, darling.

VELVETTE
Of the student council.

LUX
I know.

VELVETTE
With fucking cupcakes.

LUX
Isn’t it wonderful?

VELVETTE
It’s a hostage situation with sprinkles.

Lux turns, smiling serenely.

LUX
It’s a field test. I’ve been cultivating her for months. She was always going to want something. I just didn’t know it would be democracy.

VELVETTE
She doesn’t want democracy. She wants to be picked.

LUX
Same thing, if you do it right.

On screen, a boy reaches the front of the queue, eats a cupcake, and immediately starts crying with gratitude.

VELVETTE
If she offers me one of those—

LUX
You’ll say no.

VELVETTE
I’ll put it through her fucking eye socket.

LUX
That’s also a no.

INT. STUDENT UNION — ELECTIONS OFFICE — NOON

A tiny office. A single filing cabinet. A sign: NOMINATIONS CLOSE AT NOON. NO, WE WILL NOT ACCEPT “BUT I FEEL LIKE PRESIDENT.”

Behind the desk sits the ELECTIONS OFFICER, a very old man with a very large rubber stamp.

The door bursts open.

Madison enters carrying a nomination form.

Behind her: one hundred and fifty signatories, all holding hands.

MADISON
Hi! I’m running! Here’s my hundred signatures!

ELECTIONS OFFICER
That’s a hundred and fifty.

MADISON
Some of them signed twice. They really wanted to.

The officer checks the form.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
This one is signed in blood.

MADISON
He insisted.

STUDENT, from the crowd
She saved my LIFE.

The officer sighs and reaches for his stamp.

Madison is suddenly at the desk, holding a cupcake.

MADISON
You look like you need a little treat.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
I’m diabetic.

MADISON
It’s sugar-free.

It is not. It is, in a way, the opposite of sugar-free.

He eats it.

Behind his eyes, something pink turns over.

MADISON
Remember the fire drill? Nineteen seventy-nine? When I carried you down eleven floors on my back?

ELECTIONS OFFICER
…You weren’t born in nineteen seventy-nine.

MADISON
I was very mature for my age.

He considers this.

His eyes fill.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
You were.

He stamps the form so hard the desk cracks.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
Candidate approved.

MADISON
Who else is running?

ELECTIONS OFFICER
The incumbent.

He turns a photograph around.

GRETA VANCE.

Pale. Black bob. Black turtleneck. A face that has not been surprised since 1847. A small badge on her lapel that reads: PRESIDENT (THIRD TERM).

MADISON
Oh, she looks lonely. I’ll fix that.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
She’s a vampire.

MADISON
So?

ELECTIONS OFFICER
She’s on a liquid diet.

Madison looks down at the cupcake in her hand.

For the first time all episode, her smile flickers.

MADISON
…Oh.

Music begins. Bright bubblegum pop, a marching band made of whisks, and somewhere underneath it a pipe organ that sounds like it knows how this ends.

“VOTE BESTIE”

MADISON
Who’s the girl who pulled you from the river?
Who’s the girl who caught you when you fell?
Who’s the girl who found your lost retainer
At the bottom of the wishing well?

STUDENTS
Madison! Madison!
We remember! We recall!

MADISON
And who’s the girl who’s gonna fix the parking?
And put a cupcake in the dining hall?

STUDENTS
Madison! Madison!
She’s the best friend of us all!

She dances down the quad, handing out cupcakes like a parade float handing out beads.

Behind her, a small pink figure pops out of a planter.

It is about a foot tall. It has an auburn ponytail, a pink ribbon, a sweater vest, and a smile far too wide for its tiny face.

A MINI-MADISON.

MINI-MADISON
Bestie!

Then another pops out of a bin.

Another out of a drainpipe.

A dozen.

MINI-MADISONS
BESTIE! BESTIE! BESTIE!

They carry cupcakes over their heads like ants carrying leaves.

MADISON
Vote bestie! Vote me!
Vote the girl you used to be!
Vote the girl you’d die without
And never, ever have a doubt!

STUDENTS
Vote bestie! Vote her!
We cannot even remember
A single day before September
When she wasn’t fucking there!

A STUDENT, mid-dance, frowning
Wait, it’s October.

MINI-MADISON, shoving a cupcake into his mouth
Shh.

STUDENT, swallowing
…It’s always been September.

ALL
VOTE BESTIE!

The number ends with Madison standing on the fountain, arms wide, glitter falling like snow.

A single student at the edge of the quad, who has not eaten anything, quietly backs away into a building.

It is Yuki.

She is carrying a gas-station protein bar she bought herself.

She takes a bite.

It tastes like cardboard and independence.

ACT TWO

INT. DEAN’S OFFICE — AFTERNOON

The DEAN, a tall, nervous man with a nervous moustache, sits behind an enormous desk.

Madison sits across from him, swinging her legs.

DEAN
Miss— Madison, it’s been brought to my attention that you’re handing out food to voters.

MADISON
Not voters. Friends.

DEAN
There are rules about inducements.

MADISON
Oh, I love rules. Rules are like hugs for your behavior.

She puts a cupcake on his blotter.

DEAN
I won’t eat that.

MADISON
That’s fine.

She waits.

He looks at the cupcake.

The cupcake looks at him.

It glows faintly, encouragingly.

DEAN
I— I really won’t.

MADISON
Totally fine.

Silence.

The clock ticks.

The Dean’s stomach rumbles.

He skipped lunch for a budget meeting.

DEAN
…Is it chocolate?

MADISON
It’s whatever you need it to be.

He eats it in two bites.

Something pink turns over.

MADISON
Remember the gala? When the chandelier fell? And I dived across the ballroom and pushed you out of the way?

DEAN, slowly
The chandelier.

MADISON
It nearly got you.

DEAN
It nearly got me. It always looked at me like it wanted my job.

MADISON
And I pushed you.

DEAN
You pushed me.

His eyes fill.

DEAN
You saved my life.

MADISON
And now—

DEAN
And now I’m going to cancel the rule about inducements.

MADISON
You don’t have to.

DEAN
I WANT TO.

He tears a page out of the student handbook and eats it.

EXT. EAST STAIRWELL — CONTINUOUS

The stairwell breathes.

Madison skips down to the top step.

The stairwell considers her.

She holds out a cupcake.

MADISON
Hi, bestie.

The stairwell opens its steps like a mouth.

She drops the cupcake in.

A long, contented chewing sound.

Something pink turns over in the stone.

MADISON
Remember when you were sad and lonely and nobody ever came down you, and I came down you every single day and told you about my classes?

The stairwell is silent.

Then the bottom step, very slowly, wags.

MADISON
Good boy.

A STUDENT, coming down the stairs behind her, freezes.

The stairwell does not bite him.

It politely lets him pass.

STUDENT
It didn’t eat me.

MADISON
It’s my friend.

STUDENT
It eats everyone.

MADISON
Not my friends.

The stairwell, very softly, purrs.

The student looks at Madison.

STUDENT
Can I be your friend?

MADISON
Of course you can.

She hands him a cupcake.

INT. MORTALITY SERVICES — LATER

Yuki stands at the reception desk with a clipboard.

The door opens.

Madison enters with a tray.

Behind her, three Mini-Madisons carry a banner: VOTE BESTIE.

Yuki puts her clipboard down very slowly.

YUKI
No.

MADISON
Yuki! Oh my God, bestie! It’s been so long!

YUKI
It’s been nine days.

MADISON
It feels like forever.

YUKI
You made me buy you a lamp.

MADISON
And it’s SO pretty.

YUKI
I’m still paying it off.

MADISON
Do you want a cupcake?

YUKI
I don’t eat at work.

MADISON
You’re not at work, you’re with me.

YUKI
I am at work. I am literally at my fucking desk.

MADISON
Is your boss here?

The Reaper’s office door opens.

The GRIM REAPER emerges.

Robes. Scythe. Four thousand years of very bad news.

The temperature drops.

The Mini-Madisons hide behind Madison’s ankles.

GRIM REAPER
You’re Madison.

MADISON
Hi! You must be Death. I love your vibe. Very minimalist.

GRIM REAPER
Nine hundred and twelve students believe you saved their lives today.

MADISON
I’m very busy.

GRIM REAPER
None of them were in danger.

MADISON
Not anymore!

GRIM REAPER
Not ever.

Madison holds out the tray.

MADISON
Cupcake?

YUKI
Don’t.

The Reaper looks at the cupcake.

GRIM REAPER
I am Death. Memory is a mortal concern.

YUKI
Don’t.

GRIM REAPER
I have eaten at every wake in Christendom. I have tasted every funeral sandwich since the invention of bread. Nothing has ever changed me.

YUKI
Please don’t.

GRIM REAPER
I want to know what I’m dealing with.

She takes a cupcake.

She takes a bite.

Silence.

Yuki puts her face in her hands.

Something pink turns over somewhere very, very old.

MADISON, very gently
Remember Florence?

GRIM REAPER
…Florence.

MADISON
1348. The plague. You were collecting, and you slipped, and you fell into one of the pits, and you couldn’t get out because your robe got caught on a cart.

GRIM REAPER
I fell.

MADISON
And I came along with a ladder.

GRIM REAPER
You had a ladder.

MADISON
It was a little ladder.

GRIM REAPER
It was a very little ladder.

Her voice cracks.

The scythe lowers.

GRIM REAPER
You saved Death.

MADISON
It was nothing.

GRIM REAPER
It was the Black Death, Madison. It was literally everything.

YUKI
You were not in a fucking pit.

GRIM REAPER
I was in a pit, Yuki. I remember the smell.

YUKI
You remember a cupcake.

The Reaper turns to her filing cabinet.

She pulls out a long, ancient scroll.

At the top: DO NOT COLLECT.

There are only three names on it. One of them is DOT.

The Reaper dips a quill in ink and writes, beneath DOT:

MADISON.

YUKI
No. No, no, no, no.

GRIM REAPER
I owe her a life.

YUKI
She’s a liability!

GRIM REAPER
She’s a friend.

Madison beams.

MADISON
Bestie.

GRIM REAPER
Bestie.

Yuki looks at the two of them.

Looks at the scroll.

Looks at her mug.

I DON’T EAT AT WORK.

YUKI
I fucking hate this school.

ACT THREE

EXT. BLACKWOOD QUAD — NEXT DAY

The gratitude economy.

Everybody owes Madison everything.

And everybody wants to pay.

A boy kneels before her holding out his meal card.

BOY
Take it. You saved my life. Take all my lunches.

A girl holds out her car keys.

GIRL
You pulled me out of the duck pond. Have my Honda.

A professor holds out his tenure.

It is a framed certificate. He is weeping.

PROFESSOR
I wouldn’t have it if not for you.

MADISON
You don’t have to—

PROFESSOR
TAKE MY TENURE.

Madison is now riding in a palanquin carried by eight grateful rowing-team members.

Behind her, a column of students carry her gifts: lamps, kettles, a moped, a goat, a kidney in a cool box with a bow on it.

MADISON
Who gave me a kidney?

A pale student raises his hand weakly from a wheelchair.

STUDENT
You saved my life. You can have one of the bits.

MADISON
I don’t need a kidney!

STUDENT
I don’t need two!

He does.

A Mini-Madison quietly carries the cool box back to him.

The campus has stopped working.

Lectures are empty because everyone is following the palanquin.

The library is empty because the librarians are holding a thank-you vigil.

The cafeteria is empty because the Lunch Lady has cooked a nine-course tribute menu and is serving it exclusively to Madison.

LUNCH LADY
You held my hand in the flood of ninety-three.

MADISON
There was a flood?

LUNCH LADY
There was ME, crying in the flood. And there was YOU.

Madison takes a bite of the tribute menu.

It is extraordinary.

She tries to smile.

The smile is perfect.

It doesn’t quite reach anything.

INT. PANOPTICON OBSERVATORY — SAME TIME

Lux watches the palanquin go by on twelve screens at once.

LUX
Look at her, darling.

VELVETTE
I’m looking.

LUX
Eight thousand people would die for her.

VELVETTE
Eight thousand people think they nearly fucking did.

LUX
Same thing.

VELVETTE
It’s really not.

Lux turns to her.

LUX
Why does it bother you so much?

Velvette stops sharpening.

VELVETTE
Because nobody in that crowd knows her. They know a girl who never existed and pulled them out of a pond that never happened. If she fell in the actual pond, tomorrow, they’d stand on the bank remembering the fake one.

LUX
She chose this.

VELVETTE
She’s nineteen and she’s handing out frosting to be loved. That’s not a choice, Lux. That’s a fucking symptom.

Lux looks at her for a long moment.

Then smiles.

LUX
You’re right.

VELVETTE
I know I’m right.

LUX
That’s exactly why she works so well.

Velvette looks at her.

VELVETTE
You’re a fucking monster.

LUX
You love it.

VELVETTE
I really do. That’s my symptom.

A stage. Two podiums. A banner: PRESIDENTIAL DEBATE. FOOD FREE ZONE.

Somebody has crossed out FREE and written FRIENDLY.

On the left: Madison, with a tray.

On the right: GRETA VANCE, the incumbent. Black turtleneck. A thermos. The air around her is slightly colder and slightly more organized.

In front of them: the entire student body.

Every one of them is wearing a pink rosette.

The MODERATOR, a student, has a pink rosette as well.

MODERATOR
Candidates. Opening statements.

GRETA
Over three terms I have balanced the student budget, reduced stairwell fatalities by twelve percent, and installed a vending machine that only eats people who deserve it. I have a four-hundred-slide manifesto.

MADISON
I saved all your lives.

The crowd erupts.

STUDENTS
SHE SAVED OUR LIVES!

GRETA
She didn’t.

STUDENTS
SHE DID!

GRETA
Name one.

Every single student starts talking at once.

STUDENT 1
The stairwell!

STUDENT 2
The pond!

STUDENT 3
The fire!

STUDENT 4
The OTHER fire!

STUDENT 5
She pulled me out of a vending machine!

GRETA
The vending machine is mine. It’s never once eaten anyone who didn’t try to steal a Twix.

STUDENT 5
…I was going to pay later.

Madison offers Greta a cupcake across the stage.

MADISON
You look like you need a little treat.

GRETA
I’m on a liquid diet.

MADISON
Everyone says that.

GRETA
I am a two-hundred-year-old vampire.

MADISON
That’s so cool. Have a cupcake.

GRETA
I don’t remember you, Madison.

You will.

GRETA
I won’t. I have never eaten anything you’ve made. I will never eat anything you’ve made. In two hundred years, I have known a great many people who wanted to be adored. Most of them died of it.

BOO!

…Pink?

You were wearing it.

The student looks down.

STUDENT
Huh.

“I SAVED YOUR LIFE”

When your date set fire to the train.

She did! She did!

She didn’t.

Madison has an idea.

ACT FOUR

INT. STUDENT UNION KITCHEN — NIGHT

Madison stands at a blender.

She drops in a cupcake.

Then another.

Then blood oranges. Beetroot. Red food coloring. A splash of something from an unlabeled bottle she found in the chemistry department.

She blends.

It comes out deep red. Smooth. Glowing at the edges.

She pours it into a tall glass.

She puts a little sugar heart on the rim.

MADISON
Liquid diet.

Behind her, three Mini-Madisons applaud.

INT. STUDENT UNION — PRESIDENT’S OFFICE — CONTINUOUS

Greta sits at her desk under a single lamp, writing slide four hundred and one.

A knock.

Madison enters with the glass.

MADISON
Peace offering.

Greta looks at it.

GRETA
What is it?

MADISON
Blood orange.

GRETA
I’m a vampire, Madison. I can smell the frosting.

MADISON
Not just frosting. Beets. And whatever was in the chemistry cupboard.

Greta looks at the drink.

It glows. It smells of oranges and iron and something that reminds her, very faintly, of a summer in 1840 before she died.

GRETA
I won’t.

MADISON
Totally fine.

Madison sits down across the desk.

She doesn’t push it.

She just waits.

The clock ticks.

Greta looks at the glass.

GRETA
…I haven’t tasted orange in two hundred years.

MADISON
I know.

GRETA
How do you know?

MADISON
Everyone misses something.

Greta picks up the glass.

She drinks.

Something pink turns over, very slowly, under two centuries of ice.

MADISON
Remember the summer you died?

GRETA, very quiet
Yes.

MADISON
Remember the girl who sat with you? So you weren’t alone?

A long pause.

GRETA
There wasn’t a girl.

MADISON
There was.

GRETA
There wasn’t anybody.

MADISON
There was me.

Greta stares at her.

Her eyes, which have not done anything in two hundred years, fill.

GRETA
There was you.

MADISON
I held your hand.

GRETA
You held my hand.

She sets the glass down.

She closes her laptop.

Slide four hundred and one is never written.

GRETA
I’m withdrawing.

MADISON
You don’t have to.

GRETA
I want to.

MADISON
Will you endorse me?

GRETA
I will do anything you ask.

Madison smiles.

The smile is perfect.

Behind her, very quietly, one of the Mini-Madisons looks at the empty glass and then up at Madison and does not smile.

EXT. BLACKWOOD QUAD — ELECTION DAY

A ballot box the size of a wardrobe.

The queue to vote loops the campus.

Everyone is wearing pink.

Yuki stands beside the ballot box wearing a black tabard that says RETURNING OFFICER (UNDER PROTEST).

The ELECTIONS OFFICER stands beside her, beaming, glittering.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
Isn’t it wonderful? Record turnout!

YUKI
You’ve all been fucking cupcaked.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
She carried me down eleven floors.

YUKI
You’re on the ground floor. Your office has never had eleven floors. Nothing here has eleven floors except the stairwell and it lies.

A voter steps up.

It is the east stairwell.

It has extended a step to the ballot box like a tongue.

It drops in a vote.

YUKI
That’s not a student.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
It’s a building resident.

YUKI
It’s a fucking staircase.

The library statue steps up next.

STATUE
I see her. I vote her.

The vending machine rolls up and dispenses a ballot.

The Reaper arrives, robes immaculate, holding a pink rosette.

GRIM REAPER
I would like to vote.

YUKI
You’re staff.

GRIM REAPER
She saved my life.

YUKI
You were never alive!

GRIM REAPER
Then she saved something adjacent.

She votes.

INT. STUDENT UNION — COUNT — NIGHT

The ballots are counted.

Then counted again.

The Elections Officer reads the result into a microphone with tears in his eyes.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
Total votes cast… eleven thousand four hundred and two.

YUKI
There are three thousand students.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
Some voted more than once. Out of love.

YUKI
That’s fraud.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
That’s friendship.

He takes a breath.

ELECTIONS OFFICER
Madison: eleven thousand four hundred and two. Greta Vance: zero, because she voted for Madison.

The room explodes.

Glitter cannons fire.

Madison is lifted onto shoulders.

MADISON
Oh my God. Oh my God, you guys. You picked me.

ALL
WE PICKED YOU!

MADISON
You actually picked me.

She looks out over the sea of pink, crying, adoring faces.

Somewhere in the middle, her smile goes very still.

Music. A brass fanfare that keeps tripping over its own feet.

“MADAM PRESIDENT”

ALL
Madam President! Madam President!
Glitter on the ballot box!
Madam President! Madam President!
We’d give you our socks!

MADISON
I don’t need your socks.

ALL
Take our socks!

A storm of socks.

Velvette, at the back of the hall, catches one and stabs it.

VELVETTE
Fucking democracy.

ACT FIVE

INT. STUDENT COUNCIL CHAMBER — NEXT MORNING

A long table. A gavel. Twelve council members, all in pink. A towering stack of papers in front of the President’s chair.

Madison sits in the chair.

It is very big.

She is wearing a sash.

She picks up the top paper.

AGENDA ITEM ONE: DRAINAGE BUDGET REVIEW (EAST CAMPUS).

She reads it.

She picks up the second paper.

AGENDA ITEM TWO: AMENDMENTS TO COMMITTEE QUORUM RULES (SUB-PARAGRAPH 9C).

She picks up the third.

AGENDA ITEM THREE: PARKING.

Madison looks up at the council.

They beam back at her, waiting.

COUNCIL MEMBER
What do you think, Madam President?

MADISON
About what?

COUNCIL MEMBER
The drainage.

MADISON
I don’t know anything about drainage.

COUNCIL MEMBER
But you saved my life!

MADISON
That’s not drainage.

COUNCIL MEMBER
It was a lot like drainage. You pulled me out of a drain.

Madison looks at the stack.

It is taller than she is.

She looks at the gavel.

She looks at her sash, which says MADAM PRESIDENT in glitter, and is already shedding.

MADISON
How long is the term?

COUNCIL MEMBER
One year.

MADISON
A year of drainage?

COUNCIL MEMBER
And parking.

Madison sets down the papers.

She reaches into her sweater vest.

She takes out a cupcake.

She looks at the agenda.

She sets the cupcake on the agenda.

Nothing happens.

The agenda remains an agenda.

MADISON
…Ugh.

INT. MORTALITY SERVICES — LATER

Yuki sits at her desk, closing near-death reports.

Each one gets a stamp.

NOT A NEAR-DEATH. A CUPCAKE.

The door opens.

Madison comes in.

She is not carrying a tray.

She is not wearing her sash.

YUKI
No.

MADISON
I’m not here to give you anything.

YUKI
Then why are you here?

MADISON
How do you resign?

Yuki looks up.

YUKI
From what?

MADISON
President.

YUKI
You’ve been president for two hours.

MADISON
It was so boring. Yuki, it was so fucking boring. There’s a drainage budget. There’s a parking committee. Nobody cried. Nobody hugged me. They just asked me about pipes.

YUKI
That’s the job.

MADISON
I didn’t want the job.

YUKI
Then what did you want?

Madison opens her mouth.

Closes it.

MADISON
I wanted the voting bit.

Yuki looks at her for a long moment.

Then she opens a drawer, takes out a form, and slides it across the desk.

NOTICE OF RESIGNATION.

YUKI
Sign there. And name a successor.

MADISON
Anyone?

YUKI
Anyone eligible.

Madison signs.

Thinks.

Writes a name.

Slides it back.

Yuki reads it.

YUKI
The vending machine.

MADISON
It’s a building resident.

YUKI
It eats people.

MADISON
Only the ones who deserve it. Greta said.

Yuki considers this.

It is, she has to admit, a better track record than most councils.

She stamps it.

YUKI
Fine.

Madison turns to go.

YUKI
Madison.

MADISON
Yeah?

YUKI
The Reaper. You put her on her own fucking Do Not Collect list.

MADISON
She’s so sweet about it.

YUKI
Can you take it back?

MADISON
I can’t take memories out. I only put them in.

YUKI
Then put one in.

Madison thinks.

MADISON
What kind?

YUKI
One where you pay her back.

Madison considers.

Smiles.

Not the too-wide one. A slightly smaller, slightly more interested one.

MADISON
Ooh. That’s clever.

YUKI
I process a lot of forms.

INT. GRIM REAPER’S OFFICE — CONTINUOUS

The Reaper looks up from her scroll.

Madison stands in the doorway with a single cupcake.

GRIM REAPER
Bestie.

MADISON
Bestie. Remember Florence?

GRIM REAPER
The ladder.

MADISON
Remember the week after? When I fell in a pit, and you came back with a bigger ladder, and pulled me out, and we said we were even?

Something pink turns over, very gently.

GRIM REAPER
…We were even.

MADISON
Totally even.

The Reaper looks at the scroll.

DO NOT COLLECT.
DOT.
MADISON.

She draws a neat line through MADISON.

GRIM REAPER
Even.

MADISON
Even!

She hands the Reaper the cupcake.

GRIM REAPER
What does this one do?

MADISON
Nothing. It’s just a cupcake.

The Reaper looks at it suspiciously.

She eats it anyway.

GRIM REAPER
…It’s very good.

MADISON
I know.

COUNCIL MEMBER
Motion carried.

Lux.

Darling.

EXT. BLACKWOOD QUAD — FOUNTAIN — SAME TIME

Madison sits on the edge of the fountain.

The quad is empty. Everyone she saved has gone to bed, happy, grateful, owing her everything.

The palanquin sits abandoned on the grass. The goat eats it.

She takes a cupcake out of her vest.

She takes a bite herself.

It does nothing to her.

It never has.

She chews.

MADISON, quietly, to nobody
I’m home.

Nobody answers.

A tiny pink figure climbs up out of the fountain drain, dripping.

A Mini-Madison.

It climbs onto her knee.

MINI-MADISON
Bestie.

Madison looks at it.

Her smile comes back.

Too wide. Perfect. Exactly where it always is.

MADISON
Bestie.

She hands it the rest of the cupcake.

From the east stairwell, a long, low, affectionate purr.

The campus speakers crackle.

ANNOUNCER (V.O.)
Good evening, Blackwood University. Following a record turnout and a resignation of record speed, the new Student Council President is the vending machine outside Building C. It will take questions in the form of exact change. Nine hundred and twelve near-death reports have been closed as cupcakes. The east stairwell is reminded that it is not anybody’s good boy. It is a load-bearing structure.

The stairwell whimpers.

Madison pats the top step.

MADISON
You’re a good boy.

The stairwell wags.

CUT TO BLACK.

MINI-MADISON, O.S.
Vote bestie.
