Cinderbell Academy · Book Two: The Salt-Glass Duel

CHAPTER ONE: WINTER TERM

Snow didn't fall inside Cinderbell Academy, because the volcano kept the cavern at a permanent, dry seventy degrees. Instead, winter term meant Salt-Frost: fine white crystals drifting down from the upper vents, coating the basalt bridges until every crossing sounded like walking on sugar.

It also meant the Inter-House Duels—and a sudden shortage of moonlight ink in Mina's apothecary.

"Someone's watering down our deliveries from the surface," Mina said on the first Monday of term, holding a glass beaker up to the gas lamp. Inside, the ink had separated into pale grey sludge. "Without real moonlight ink, first-years can't seal practice pacts. And guess who just offered to supply the whole freshman class at triple price?"

"The Ash Council heirs," Sera guessed, rubbing her palm. Since the Midnight Gauntlet, the golden bell glyph under her skin stayed warm whenever she was near Vesper—which was always, thanks to the tether.

"Worse. House Thorne's rivals—the Valen twins. If they control the ink supply during Duel Week, half the scholarship kids will forfeit before Round One."

Across the table, Vesper flipped a silver coin over his knuckles. "So we don't buy from them. We brew our own in the Salt-Glass Conservatory."

"The Conservatory's flooded," Mina said. "And guarded by glass-eels."

"Details," Vesper said smoothly. "Sera, bring your boots."

CHAPTER TWO: THE FLOODED CONSERVATORY

The Salt-Glass Conservatory hung from the lowest rib of the cavern, a vast dome of green sea-glass filled with waist-deep mineral water and luminous reeds. Moon-lotuses bloomed along the far catwalk—the exact petals Mina needed to distill pure ink.

"Stay on the stone stepping-pillars," Vesper warned as they slipped through the side grating at midnight. "The eels only strike at ripples."

They made it halfway across before a shadow slid beneath the surface—not an eel, but a tall, familiar figure in a prefect's cloak standing on the central dais, filling a satchel with moon-lotuses.

"Caleb?" Sera whispered.

Caleb Cross froze, his lantern swinging up. For a second he looked genuinely cornered. Then his jaw set. "You shouldn't be in here after curfew."

"Neither should you," Vesper snapped, stepping onto the dais. "Are you working for the Valen twins?"

"I'm working to keep my sister's name off the Expulsion Ledger," Caleb said quietly. He reached into his coat and pulled out a folded slip of red-bordered parchment—an official notice stamped with the Dean's own seal. "She missed two pact-payments in Oakhaven. The Council told me if I starve out the first-year ink supply, they'll tear up her debt."

Sera's chest tightened. "Caleb, if you hand those petals over, twenty scholarship students get expelled instead."

"They're not my sister," Caleb said, and raised his iron staff.

CHAPTER THREE: THE DUEL IN THE GLASS

A duel in Cinderbell Academy wasn't supposed to happen outside the official arena, without referees or salt-circles. Here, every miss cracked the ancient glass floor above a hundred-foot drop.

Caleb struck first, driving the butt of his staff into the water. A shockwave of frozen brine erupted upward, forcing Vesper to flare his soot-wings and lift Sera clear by the waist before she was swept off the dais.

"Stop!" Sera yelled as Vesper banked hard, his wing-tip scraping the dome. "Don't break the glass!"

"Tell him that!" Vesper shot back, hurling a ribbon of dry ember-fire that hissed across Caleb's cloak and forced him onto the narrow stone rim.

Caleb swung back, fast and desperate. His staff caught Vesper's left wing—right along the old notch—and sent both Vesper and Sera plunging toward the dark water.

As they fell, the golden tether between them sang like a plucked harp string. Sera reached out with her marked palm, not to strike Caleb, but to catch the water itself.

Where her hand hit the surface, the brine didn't splash—it crystallized into a flat, shining bridge of clear salt-glass. Vesper skidded across it on one knee, caught his balance, and hooked Caleb's ankle with the edge of his boot, sending the prefect sprawling harmlessly onto the dais.

Silence returned, broken only by the drip of water from the glass dome.

Caleb lay on his back, staring up at the glowing reeds, breathing hard. "Do it, then," he muttered. "Turn me in to Malakor."

Sera knelt beside him and picked up the spilled satchel of moon-lotuses. She took half of the petals for Mina's apothecary, and left the other half in Caleb's bag.

"We're not turning you in," she said. "And we're going to find out why the Dean's seal is on an extortion note."

CHAPTER FOUR: THE VALEN MATCH

By Friday, Mina's freshly distilled moonlight ink had every scholarship first-year back in the Inter-House Duels—and put Sera and Vesper squarely in the quarterfinals against the Valen twins, Orin and Tessa.

The Duel Arena was packed to the rafters. Dean Malakor watched from the high box, his silver teacup steaming.

"Remember," Vesper murmured as they stepped onto the chalk ring. "Tessa throws mirror-illusions. Orin throws heavy ash. Watch the shadows, not their hands."

The gong sounded.

Orin slammed both fists down, raising a wall of choking black cinders, while three identical Tessas sprinted out of the smoke from different angles. The crowd cheered.

Sera closed her eyes for one beat, ignoring the illusions, and listened to the quiet hum of her palm glyph. Two of the Tessas felt empty—cold paper. The third smelled faintly of ozone and cinnamon, like the boiler-room gate.

"Right side!" Sera called, and threw a handful of Mina's crystallized salt-dust straight into the third Tessa's face.

The illusion shattered. Tessa stumbled backward, blinded by glitter-salt, just as Vesper dove through the smoke and pinned Orin's shadow to the chalk ring with a golden tether-spark.

Ten seconds later, the referee's whistle blew.

As they walked off the sand to a roar of stunned applause from the scholarship bleachers, Vesper bumped his shoulder lightly against Sera's.

"Not bad for canvas shoes," he said.

"I'm wearing the leather boots," Sera smiled.

"Even better."

CHAPTER FIVE: THE LEDGER IN THE INK

That night, while the dorms celebrated, Caleb Cross knocked once on Sera and Mina's door. He didn't bring his staff. He brought a magnifying lens and the red-bordered expulsion note.

"Look at the Dean's signature," Caleb said softly, spreading it under Mina's lamp. "I compared it against the official Belltower Registry. The ink is the same, the seal is real—but the handwriting tilts left. Malakor is right-handed."

Mina leaned over the glass. "Someone inside the administration has a stolen seal."

"And they're using it to silence anyone who gets too close to the Belltower," Caleb added, looking straight at Sera's palm. "Every time you use that gold mark, Sera, the Seventh Bell's chains rattle. Whatever's locked up there knows you're here."
