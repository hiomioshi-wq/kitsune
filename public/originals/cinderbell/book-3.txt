Cinderbell Academy · Book Three: The Seventh Bell

CHAPTER ONE: SPRING EXAMS

Spring exams at Cinderbell Academy had two parts: a written test on Pact Law, and a live climb up the seven Belltower spires without touching the outer stairs.

Sera had spent all winter studying Pact Law until her eyes blurred, mostly because Vesper refused to let her fail and ruin his GPA—though by March, neither of them pretended the tether was the only reason he stayed up with her in the library until 3 a.m.

"Clause Fourteen," Vesper recited, balancing a pencil on his nose while sprawled across two armchairs. "A bound demon may not—"

"Harm, deceive, or abandon their signer while the ink holds," Sera finished, yawning. "Wait. What does Clause Fifteen say?"

Vesper lowered the pencil. "Clause Fifteen is redacted in the student edition."

"Not in the Archive copy," Mina said, dropping a dusty, iron-bound folio onto the table between them. She pushed her goggles up, grinning triumphantly. "Caleb let me into the old stacks. Read it."

Sera turned the brittle page. Only three lines were inked in faded gold:

*Clause Fifteen: Should a mortal palm bear the Seventh Seal, no Belltower lock may hold against them—for the Seventh Bell was not silenced to protect the school from the demon below, but to protect the Ash Council from the truth above.*

CHAPTER TWO: THE SEALED SPIRE

They climbed at midnight—Sera, Vesper, Mina, and Caleb, moving silently up the exterior ironwork while the cavern slept below.

Six bells hung in the open belfry, gleaming bronze in the magma-glow. At the very peak, behind a grate of fused obsidian, hung the Seventh Bell. It wasn't bronze. It was pale, hammered silver, wrapped in seven turns of rusted chain.

"Stand back," Caleb murmured, bracing his staff.

Sera stepped up to the grate and pressed her marked palm against the lock.

The golden glyph flared. The rusted chains didn't break—they simply uncoiled like waking snakes and dropped to the stones with a soft clatter. The obsidian gate swung inward.

Inside the small chamber sat a writing desk, a lamp still warm to the touch, and stacks of red-bordered expulsion notices—half-written, with a stolen silver seal beside a cup of cold tea.

Behind the desk stood the copper-horned intake clerk from Orientation Day.

"You really shouldn't have climbed this high," she said, calmly capping her fountain pen.

CHAPTER THREE: THE FORGER'S NAME

"Nadia?" Caleb stared, his staff wavering. "You're the Archivist's assistant."

"I am the daughter of the last Bellkeeper," Nadia said evenly, her gold-rimmed glasses catching the silver bell's light. "Ninety years ago, the Ash Council silenced the Seventh Bell because it rang whenever a Council member broke a mortal pact. My grandfather tried to ring it anyway. They expelled our whole line and erased our house name."

"So you've been forging expulsion orders to blackmail students?" Sera asked, disbelief sharpening her voice. "You went after Caleb's sister!"

"I needed leverage inside the Prefect Guard to get the key to this spire," Nadia replied without flinching. "And now you opened it for me."

Before Vesper could lunge forward, Nadia seized the silver clapper of the Seventh Bell and swung it hard against the rim.

The sound wasn't loud. It was a low, crystalline hum that sank straight into Sera's bones and vibrated through every bridge in the cavern.

Instantly, all six bronze bells below answered in chaotic dissonance. Across the dormitories, windows flew open. Alarms flared crimson.

And from the sealed trapdoor beneath the Seventh Bell, something vast and iron-cold began to rise.

CHAPTER FOUR: WHAT WAS LOCKED AWAY

It wasn't a monster. It was a ledger—ten feet tall, bound in living brass, floating in midair as its pages turned themselves in a wind that didn't exist. Every broken promise made by the Ash Council over ninety years burned across its pages in furious red script.

"Every secret they hid," Nadia whispered, awe softening her face. "Broadcast to the whole school."

Stone footsteps echoed up the spiral stair. Dean Malakor appeared in the doorway—not smiling now, his antlers crowned with pale blue fire.

"Enough, Nadia," Malakor said quietly. "You didn't read the rest of the grandfather's notes, did you? When the Seventh Bell rings, the Ash Council doesn't apologize. They burn the spire down to keep the ledger quiet."

The cavern roof shuddered. High above the spires, the great volcanic vents began to roar, pouring white-hot ash toward the belfry.

"Vesper!" Sera shouted as a stone beam cracked overhead.

Vesper didn't hesitate. He wrapped one arm around Sera, grabbed Caleb's collar with the other, and launched them off the balcony into the dark air while Mina dove onto the zip-line cable beside them.

Behind them, Nadia stood her ground by the floating brass ledger—until a second shockwave shattered the floor, and she vanished into the smoke.

CHAPTER FIVE: THE ASH COUNCIL'S SHADOW

They landed hard on the dormitory roof, coughing ash as the Sixth Bell tolled an emergency curfew.

The Seventh Spire still stood, blackened and smoking, its silver bell silent once more—yet Sera's palm glyph hadn't faded. It burned brighter than ever, pulsing in time with the golden tether on Vesper's wrist.

"The Ash Council is coming tomorrow," Caleb said, staring up at the smoking spire. "Malakor can't stall them after tonight."

"Let them come," Vesper said softly, looking down at the glowing ring on his wrist, then up at Sera. "They think a disgraced third-year and a scholarship mortal can't read a ledger. Let's prove them wrong."
