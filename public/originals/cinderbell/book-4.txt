Cinderbell Academy · Book Four: The Brimstone Choir

CHAPTER ONE: THE AUDITORS ARRIVE

The Ash Council didn't use the boiler-room gate in Oakhaven. They arrived by ripping open a staircase of white marble right through the center of the Ash Atrium ceiling.

Six Auditors marched down in tailored ivory coats, followed by a girl with Vesper's exact black hair, sharper cheekbones, and spotless crimson wings.

Vesper went rigid beside Sera at the breakfast table.

"Sister?" Sera whispered.

"Lyra," Vesper murmured, his voice dropping an octave. "She's the Council's favorite Thorne."

Lyra didn't look at Vesper until she reached the high dais beside Dean Malakor. Even then, her eyes slid right past his notched wing and landed on the golden tether binding his wrist to Sera's. Her smile was polite, cold, and devastating.

"Dean Malakor," Lyra announced to the silent hall, "by order of the Ash Council, Cinderbell Academy is under formal audit. Until the Seventh Bell's sabotage is resolved, all mutual tethers involving mortal clerks are suspended for dismantling."

Sera's breath caught. "Dismantling?"

"It means," Vesper said through clenched teeth, "she's going to cut us apart and lock you back upstairs."

CHAPTER TWO: THE CHOIR CATACOMBS

They had until midnight before the Auditors' tribunal convened.

"There's only one place older than the Belltower," Mina said, spreading an ink-stained blueprint across the apothecary floor. "The Brimstone Choir. Look at the sub-basement map—below the magma lake, there's an echo-chamber where the original founders forged all seven bells."

"Why go down there?" Caleb asked, checking his iron staff.

"Because the founders wrote a counter-clause into the Choir stones," Sera said, pressing her warm palm onto the blueprint. "If the Seventh Bell's ledger was real, the original pact-chords are carved down there. Proof that the Council broke their own laws first."

They slipped down the service shaft behind the kitchens just as the evening bells tolled seven.

The Choir Catacombs weren't hot like the upper cavern; they were cool and resonant, lined with hollow basalt organ-pipes that hummed faintly whenever anyone spoke. Every whisper echoed back three times, slightly out of tune.

At the center of the lowest rotunda stood seven stone lecterns arranged in a circle. Six held bronze tuning forks. The seventh held a cracked iron bell—and standing beside it, wrapped in a dusty archive cloak, was Nadia.

CHAPTER THREE: THE OLDEST CHORD

"You survived," Sera breathed.

"Barely," Nadia said, holding up one bandaged hand. "And I found what my grandfather was really looking for."

She stepped aside from the seventh lectern. Carved deep into the basalt wasn't a list of broken pacts—it was a single, sweeping treaty signed in two hands: one demon founder, one mortal founder.

*Cinderbell Academy shall stand as shared ground,* Sera read aloud, her voice echoing softly through the pipes. *Neither Council nor Crown may expel a keeper of the Seventh Mark.*

"Wait," Vesper said, frowning as he leaned over the stone. "Why did Malakor tell us the Council would burn the spire down?"

"Because he's terrified of what happens if both sides remember this treaty exists," Nadia said. "Look at the signature at the bottom."

Sera looked. The mortal founder's surname was faded, almost worn away by ninety years of soot—but the symbol beside it was unmistakable: a tiny, five-pointed bell-glyph, identical to the burn scar on Sera's right palm.

Before anyone could speak, white marble light flared at the catacomb entrance.

"Charming," Lyra's voice echoed down the stone stairs, flanked by two armed Auditors. "A traitor, a prefect, a mortal, and my little brother playing archaeologist."

CHAPTER FOUR: BROTHERS AND SISTERS

Lyra raised one hand, and the organ-pipes behind them shrieked—a blinding wall of sound that drove Mina and Caleb to their knees.

"Let them go, Lyra," Vesper shouted, stepping in front of Sera with both soot-wings flared wide. "You want the Thorne seat? Keep it. I don't want the Council."

"You never had a choice, Vesper," Lyra snapped, launching a lance of white Council fire. "Father's debt is still on our name. Hand over the mortal girl's palm-seal, and the Council wipes our slate clean."

"That's a lie and you know it," Sera yelled, stepping right up beside Vesper and seizing his tethered hand. "They don't wipe slates—they just add new names to the ledger!"

Together, Sera and Vesper didn't dodge. They struck the seventh stone lectern at the same instant.

The cracked iron bell didn't ring—it *roared*. Every basalt organ-pipe in the catacombs answered in a deep, subterranean chord that shook dust from the ceiling and blew the Auditors' white fire out like birthday candles.

Lyra staggered back against the wall, her crimson wings pinned by the sheer resonance of the Choir. For the first time, the cold mask slipped, and she looked nineteen and exhausted.

"Go," Vesper told her quietly, lowering his hand. "Tell the Council the Seventh Bell has a keeper. And tell them I'm not coming home."

CHAPTER FIVE: THE CRACKED GATE

They climbed back to the Ash Atrium before dawn, carrying Nadia's rubbing of the Founders' Treaty—only to find the hall empty and the magma lake below boiling crimson.

At the top of the glass stairs leading up to Oakhaven Public High, Dean Malakor stood alone, holding a heavy iron key.

"They didn't wait for the tribunal," Malakor said grimly, looking down at the four students. "The Council just sealed the upper vents. If the pressure builds for another twenty-four hours, the boiler-room gate won't just close—it'll blow straight through Oakhaven."
