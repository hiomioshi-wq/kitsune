Cinderbell Academy · Book Five: The Unwritten Pact

CHAPTER ONE: TWO WORLDS BLEEDING

By noon, Oakhaven Public High smelled faintly of cedar smoke and cinnamon.

Sera stood by the rusted mop sink in Sub-Basement Four with Vesper, Mina, and Caleb behind her. Upstairs, the mortal fire department was already checking the radiators because the gym floor had gone mysteriously warm.

"The gate's leaking," Mina said, holding a glass vial of storm-moths up to the pipe. The moths were spinning frantically in tight circles. "If the Ash Council keeps the volcanic vents sealed downstairs, the pressure's going to pop this basement like a cork."

"Then we unseal the vents from inside," Caleb said.

"We can't," Vesper said, staring at the glowing golden bracelet on his wrist. "The Council locked the vent-wheels with a Sovereign Pact. Only a full Council vote—or an Unwritten Pact—can override it."

Sera looked down at her right palm. "What's an Unwritten Pact?"

Vesper hesitated. For the first time since Orientation Day, he didn't have a sharp comeback. "It's a pact where one side breaks an existing gold tether on purpose. The backlash releases enough raw spark to shatter any Council lock—but it tears the demon's wings clean off their tether."

"No," Sera said immediately. "Forget it."

"Sera—"

"Forget it, Vesper. We find another way."

CHAPTER TWO: THE DEAN'S OFFICE

They went straight to Dean Malakor's office—not to ask permission, but to take the iron key he had been holding at dawn.

Malakor didn't stop them. He was already packing a single leather valise, his silver antlers dim and tired.

"The iron key opens the Vent Chamber beneath the magma lake," Malakor said, sliding it across the desk without looking up. "I locked the Seventh Bell ninety years ago because the Council threatened to collapse the caldera on top of Oakhaven if the truth got out. I thought silence was mercy." He finally met Sera's eyes. "I was wrong. Silence just gave them ninety years to sharpen their knives."

"Come with us," Sera said.

"My pact binds me to the school's walls," Malakor replied softly. "If I cross the bridge to the Vent Chamber, I turn to ash. You four are students. The walls haven't claimed you yet."

He handed Sera a blank sheet of heavy, cream-colored parchment—not ash-grey, not red-bordered.

"An Unwritten Pact," Malakor said quietly. "If you have to use it, write what you mean. Not what the Council expects."

CHAPTER THREE: THE VENT CHAMBER

The magma lake was rising, licking at the lower glass walkways as they raced down the spiral ramp. Smoke curled thick and bitter through the air; Mina passed around soaked peppermint cloths so they could breathe.

Inside the Vent Chamber, three massive iron wheels were chained shut with blinding white Council seals. Two Auditors stood guard before the central wheel—and behind them, surprisingly, stood Lyra, her arms crossed, looking pale.

"Step aside," Vesper snapped, soot-sparks flaring around him.

"I'm not stopping you," Lyra said quietly. The two Auditors stared at her in shock. "I'm stalling the rest of the Council upstairs. Just hurry—the caldera roof is already cracking."

Caleb and Mina engaged the two startled Auditors with iron shockwaves and flash-vials of bottled lightning, clearing a path to the central wheel.

Sera and Vesper ran to the white seal. Vesper threw everything he had at it—soot-fire, ember-lances, raw heat. The seal didn't budge.

"It's too thick!" Vesper gasped, dropping to one knee as the chamber shuddered and a fissure split the floor. "Sera, we're out of time! Break the tether!"

Sera pulled the blank parchment from her coat. Her hand was shaking so hard she could barely hold the pen. If she severed the tether, she didn't know if Vesper would survive the backlash.

"Don't look at me like that," Vesper said, catching her free hand—the one with the glowing bell glyph—and pressing it over his own wrist. "You bound me by accident on Day One. Unbind me on purpose now."

CHAPTER FOUR: WHAT SHE WROTE

Sera didn't write a severance clause.

She remembered what Malakor had said: *Write what you mean.*

She pressed her marked palm onto the blank parchment, letting the liquid gold ink surge into the nib, and wrote three crooked, terrible lines in her worst handwriting:

*I release Vesper Thorne from every debt, duty, and shadow of Line Seven. Nothing owns him. If this pact demands a price, split it down the middle—half to him, half to me.*

She signed her name. Vesper caught the pen and signed beneath hers.

The golden tether between their wrists didn't snap. It *detonated*—a blinding sphere of warm, amber light that washed across the Vent Chamber without burning a single hair.

Sera felt a sharp, searing ache across her shoulders, as if she'd fallen ten feet onto flat stone. Beside her, Vesper cried out once—not losing his wings, but watching the ragged notches along his left wing mend in seams of bright, woven gold.

The white Council seals on all three vent-wheels shattered into harmless dust.

Caleb threw his weight onto the central wheel, Mina and Vesper slammed the other two, and with a deafening sigh of steam, the volcanic vents roared open.

CHAPTER FIVE: THE OPEN SKY

Above them, the Ash Atrium ceiling didn't crack—it *opened* cleanly to the night sky above Oakhaven's volcano ridge, letting cool mountain air pour down into the cavern for the first time in ninety years.

Sera sat on the warm floor of the Vent Chamber, staring at her bare wrist. The golden bracelet was gone.

Vesper sat beside her, flexing his newly mended, gold-seamed wings, breathing hard. He looked down at his own empty wrist, then reached over and laced his fingers through Sera's anyway.

"Still terrible handwriting," he whispered.

"Still saved your life," Sera whispered back.
