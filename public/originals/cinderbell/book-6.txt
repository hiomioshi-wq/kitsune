Cinderbell Academy · Book Six: Dawn Over Cinderbell

CHAPTER ONE: GRADUATION EVE

By May, Cinderbell Academy looked nothing like the sealed fortress Sera had walked into nine months ago.

The cavern roof stood open to the real stars, framed by ironwork arches so rain stayed out and fresh mountain wind blew clean across the basalt bridges. Half the Ash Council had resigned after Nadia published the Founders' Treaty to every dorm bulletin board; the remaining half was too busy arguing over back-taxes to bother anyone below Year Four.

On Graduation Eve, the entire student body gathered on the Caldera Terrace—demons, half-fiends, scholarship mortals, and even a few curious Oakhaven townies who had been let in on the secret under a polite non-disclosure pact.

Mina had already secured a full fellowship running the revamped Apothecary Wing—with real moonlight ink delivered straight through the front gate. Caleb stood near the Belltower steps with his sister beside him, both wearing matching prefect pins.

And Sera stood at the parapet in a borrowed crimson blazer over her favorite faded sneakers, watching the sixth bronze bell glint in the starlight.

CHAPTER TWO: THE SEVENTH BELL RINGS

"Nervous?" Vesper asked, stepping up beside her with two mugs of spiced cider. His gold-seamed wings caught the breeze softly.

"About graduating, or about the speech?" Sera took a mug. "Because I'm definitely skipping the speech."

"Malakor won't let you skip it. You're the first mortal Bellkeeper in ninety years."

"I'm an Archive Clerk who spilled gold ink on Line Seven," she corrected, smiling.

Across the terrace, Dean Malakor tapped a silver spoon against his glass. Silence settled gently over the crowd. Behind him, the Seventh Bell hung polished and bright in its central cradle—no rusted chains, no obsidian grate.

"For ninety years," Malakor said, his voice carrying effortlessly across the open caldera, "we taught our students that survival meant hiding beneath stone and signing only what was safe. This year, four students reminded us that a pact is only as honest as the hands that write it."

He stepped aside and gestured toward Sera.

She walked up to the Seventh Bell alone—Wait, no. Halfway there, Vesper fell into step on her left, Mina on her right, and Caleb just behind them. Nobody told them to. They just belonged there.

Sera reached up, wrapped her fingers around the woven crimson rope, and pulled.

CHAPTER THREE: THE SONG OF THE CALDERA

The Seventh Bell didn't shriek or rattle. It sang—a clear, golden note that rolled out over the magma lake, up through the open roof, and across the dark pine ridges of Oakhaven.

All six bronze bells answered in harmony, one after another, until the whole mountain vibrated like a struck harp. Fireworks of bottled lightning—Mina's personal touch—bloomed violet and amber against the stars.

Below, students cheered, tossing mortarboards and glowing spell-papers into the updraft. Even Lyra, standing near the back of the terrace in a plain grey coat, raised her glass once toward Vesper before slipping quietly down the stairs.

CHAPTER FOUR: THE NEW TERM

After the cheering died down and the lanterns burned low, Sera and Vesper sat on the steps of the Boiler-Room Gate—now widened into a real stone archway with a brass lantern hanging above it.

"So," Vesper said, tossing a small brass token from hand to hand. "Summer in Oakhaven. Are you going to teach me how mortal coffee shops work, or am I supposed to guess?"

"Rule one: do not pay with silver teeth," Sera said, leaning her head lightly against his shoulder. "Rule two: we actually have to get jobs if we want iced lattes."

"I can carry heavy boxes," Vesper offered. "And light things on fire."

"Maybe keep the fire part for campouts."

Down in the cavern behind them, the seven bells hung quiet and steady under the open sky, waiting for autumn term. Sera looked down at her right palm—where the tiny golden bell glyph glowed softly, no longer burning, just warm—and smiled.

THE END
