Cinderbell Academy · Book One: The Ash Scholarship

CHAPTER ONE: THE BOILER-ROOM GATE

The door to Cinderbell Academy did not open onto a cobbled courtyard or a grand iron gate. It opened behind the rusted mop sink in the basement of Oakhaven Public High, where the radiators clanked like teeth and the janitor kept a bucket of salt beside the bleach.

Sera Vale checked the brass token in her left hand and the soot-colored burn mark on her right palm. Neither had stopped itching since Tuesday.

"Remember," her aunt had told her at breakfast, sliding a thermos of burnt coffee across the linoleum. "You are there to alphabetize the Brimstone Registry. You do not touch the bells, you do not trade favors with anyone who has more than two shadows, and you do not sign anything in red ink. Black ink only."

"Black ink only," Sera repeated, though she already knew how well she kept promises when something shiny was involved.

She pressed the brass token against the water main. The pipe groaned, split neatly down the seam like an iron pomegranate, and exhaled a wave of dry cedar smoke and cinnamon. Beyond the steam lay a spiral staircase of volcanic glass descending into a cavern the size of a city.

Hanging from the cavern roof by chains as thick as oak trunks was Cinderbell Academy: seven gothic spires of black basalt and stained amber glass, linked by swaying suspension bridges over a lake of slow, luminous magma. Above the tallest spire hung six enormous bronze bells, each engraved with swirling pact-script. A seventh cradle sat empty in the center, wrapped in rusted chains.

Sera swallowed hard, adjusted the strap of her thrifted backpack, and stepped onto the glass stairs.

CHAPTER TWO: BAD HANDWRITING

Intake took place in the Ash Atrium, where three hundred first-year students with horns, obsidian wings, silver-slitted eyes, and tailored crimson blazers stood in neat lines. Sera was the only person in a faded denim jacket and sneakers.

A tall girl with coiled copper horns and a clipboard didn't even look up when Sera reached the front. "Name, lineage, blood-seal."

"Sera Vale. Mortal scholarship. Archive Clerk."

The girl's pen paused. She raised a pair of gold-rimmed glasses. "A mortal clerk? In the same intake hall as the First-Year Pact Draft? You're supposed to be in Sub-Basement Four."

"The stairs only went here," Sera said.

"Great. Just sign the intake ledger so the belltower stops counting you as an unanchored soul, then take the dumbwaiter down." She shoved a thick, ash-grey parchment across the desk. A tiny brass bell sat beside the inkwell, humming softly.

Sera picked up the pen. The nib was dry, so she pressed harder, leaning her cinder-marked palm onto the corner of the page to steady it. The moment her skin touched the parchment, the mark on her hand flared hot as a stove burner. Ink—not black, not red, but a bright, liquid gold—surged from her palm straight into the nib.

Her sloppy signature skidded across Line Seven: the Summons Line.

The tiny brass bell on the desk rang once.

Across the Atrium, a boy in a charcoal blazer dropped his suitcase. A ribbon of golden smoke shot from Sera's pen, braided through the crowd like a lasso, and snapped tight around his wrist.

Silence fell over three hundred demons.

The boy stared at the glowing golden thread on his cuff, then slowly turned his head toward the desk. He had messy black hair, soot-grey wings folded tight against his shoulder blades, and eyes the exact color of cooling embers.

"Tell me," he said, his voice carrying across the dead-quiet hall, "that a scholarship clerk did not just bind me with a pen from the lost-and-found."

"I was trying to write my last name," Sera whispered. "The 'e' got away from me."

CHAPTER THREE: VESPER THORNE

His name was Vesper Thorne, Year Three, formerly of House Thorne—until his father backed the wrong side of an Ash Council vote and lost everything except a pair of scuffed boots and an attitude sharp enough to shave glass.

"Do you have any idea what Line Seven does?" Vesper hissed twenty minutes later as they sat on the stone bench outside Dean Malakor's office. "It's a Mutual Tether! If you fail a class, I lose credit. If I get thrown into the magma pit during Ember Drills, you feel the heat. And worst of all—" He held up his wrist, where a faint golden bracelet now pulsed in time with Sera's heartbeat. "—we can't walk more than three hundred yards apart until the semester ends or the Dean annuls it."

"He'll annul it," Sera said, though her stomach was doing slow, miserable flips. "It was clearly an accident."

Dean Malakor did not annul it.

He sat behind a desk carved from petrified lightning, his tall antlers casting long shadows across the bookshelf. When he smiled, it looked like a match striking a box.

"An unscripted gold-ink tether," Malakor murmured, turning Sera's parchment toward the lamplight. "On a mortal scholarship palm. How delightfully irregular. Tell me, Miss Vale, where did you get that burn on your hand?"

"Boiling kettle," Sera lied automatically. Her aunt had told her never to mention the night the kitchen ceiling caught fire when she was six.

"Of course." Malakor folded his hands. "Because the tether is already anchored to the Sixth Bell, severing it prematurely would crack Mr. Thorne's wing-ligaments and leave Miss Vale with half a shadow. Instead, we shall treat it as a Transfer Placement. Miss Vale, welcome to Year One Combat & Pact Theory. Mr. Thorne, congratulations—you have a partner for the Midnight Gauntlet."

"Sir," Vesper said, standing up so fast his chair skidded. "She doesn't even have a familiar or a spell-focus. She's wearing canvas shoes."

"Then I suggest you lend her a pair of boots before sundown," Malakor said pleasantly. "Class starts at midnight."

CHAPTER FOUR: THE APOTHECARY WING

Sera's roommate wasn't a demon heir. Her name was Mina Park, a second-year mortal exchange student from the Apothecary Wing who kept twelve jars of pickled storm-moths on her windowsill and wore safety goggles pushed up into her hair like a headband.

"You bound Vesper Thorne?" Mina whistled, tossing Sera a spare pair of fireproof leather boots. "Half the third-years are terrified of him, and the other half think he's tragic. Here. Drink this before the Gauntlet."

She handed over a vial that smelled like peppermint and ozone. "What is it?"

"Stabilizer. Keeps your mortal lungs from coughing up sulfur when the magma vents open. Also tastes terrible."

Sera drank it in one gulp. It tasted like licking a nine-volt battery, and immediately made the air in the dorm room feel twice as sharp.

At eleven-thirty, Vesper was waiting outside the dorm archway, leaning against a basalt pillar with his arms crossed. He had changed into dark dueling leathers, and for the first time Sera noticed the ragged notches along the edge of his left wing.

"Rules of the Midnight Gauntlet," he said without preamble, falling into step beside her as they crossed the swaying bridge toward the Training Spire. "First-years and their anchors run the Obsidian Maze to reach the Dawn Beacon. Upperclassmen try to knock your lanterns out. If your lantern dies, you're out—and you clean the ash-flues for a month."

"And what's our plan?"

"Our plan is you stay behind my left shoulder, don't touch any glowing runes, and let me handle the fire."

"You know," Sera said, adjusting her borrowed boots, "for someone who acts like he hates everyone, you're pretty fast with the rescue plan."

Vesper glanced sideways at her. "I'm not rescuing you. I'm protecting my GPA."

CHAPTER FIVE: THE MIDNIGHT GAUNTLET

The Obsidian Maze smelled of wet slate and struck flint. Blue lanterns hung from the rafters, casting long, shifting shadows that made the walls look like they were breathing.

Twenty minutes in, they had already dodged two illusion-traps and a pack of third-year salamander-kin who tried to swamp Vesper in freezing fog. Sera's cinder-mark throbbed steadily, pulling her toward the center of the maze like a compass needle.

"Left," she said suddenly, grabbing Vesper's sleeve as they reached a four-way fork.

"The beacon's straight ahead," Vesper argued, catching his breath.

"There's a wire across the floor straight ahead. Look at the dust."

Vesper squinted. A hair-thin filament of silver-ash glinted an inch above the stones. If he'd stepped forward, the ceiling would have dropped a net of binding salt on both of them.

He looked down at Sera, startled. "How did you see that?"

"My mark gets hot near traps," she admitted. "Actually, it gets hot near everything down here. Come on—left."

They took the left passage at a dead run, emerging onto a narrow glass catwalk high above the lake of magma. Only one obstacle stood between them and the Dawn Beacon: Caleb Cross, the Belltower Prefect, standing calmly at the far end with an iron lantern-staff in both hands.

"Nothing personal, Thorne," Caleb said, his voice steady and polite. "Council kids asked me to make sure the scholarship stays in the basement."

"Move, Cross," Vesper growled, sparks snapping along his knuckles.

Caleb swung the staff, sending a ring of heavy iron pressure rippling across the catwalk. Vesper threw up a soot-shield to block it, his boots skidding backward toward the edge. The golden tether between his wrist and Sera's pulled taut, burning bright white.

Sera didn't think. She slapped her cinder-marked palm flat onto the glass catwalk.

A wave of golden light—not fire, not ash, something older and quieter—rushed straight down the tether into Vesper's wings and out across the bridge. Caleb's iron pressure shattered like dropped crockery. Vesper's wings flared twice their normal span, catching the updraft, and in one clean vault he cleared Caleb's shoulder and slammed his hand onto the Dawn Beacon.

The Sixth Bell overhead tolled once, deep and clear.

Back on the catwalk, Sera stared at her hand. Where the burn mark had been, a tiny golden bell-shaped glyph now glowed faintly beneath her skin.

"Okay," Vesper breathed, landing beside her with his chest heaving and a stunned grin breaking across his face. "Okay. Maybe keep the terrible handwriting."
