The Backrooms: Party Favor
A spinoff of No Wind Resistance. Level Fun =), from the inside.

Trigger warning: This story contains being sold, captivity, body horror, losing your face and your name, forced cheerfulness, adult language, and a narrator who says yes.
Read at your own risk.



1.

My name is Jonah Reyes, I'm twenty-two, I work the morning shift at the Starbucks on Bell Road in Phoenix, and I want to say all of that out loud before I forget it.

They told me I'd forget it. The other guests. The ones who've been here longer. They said: say your name every day. Out loud. Write it on your arm if you can find a pen. The party gets in through the frosting, they said, and the first thing it eats is your name.

So. Jonah Reyes. Bell Road. Venti iced caramel macchiato with oat milk, that's my favorite to make, because of the layers.

Okay.

I was on break. I was leaning on the wall by the dumpster behind the store, scrolling, and the wall wasn't there anymore, and I fell, and I landed on wet yellow carpet under buzzing lights in a hallway that went on forever.

A woman found me. Short hair. A jacket made of something greasy and black. A machete. She looked at me the way you look at a sandwich you're not hungry for yet. She tied my hands with rope and taped my mouth, and she walked me for six hours through the yellow hallways and down a ladder and into a warehouse full of people trading jars of water, and she sold me to a man in a Hawaiian shirt for three flasks.

I told her I had a mom.

She said everybody's got a mom.

Then the man in the Hawaiian shirt put a balloon in my tied hands, yellow, with a smiley face drawn on it in Sharpie, and he put a key in a door marked with a smiley face, and he pushed me through.



2.

Level Fun.

That's what the other guests call it. There's a little equals sign and a parenthesis after it when it's written down, the way you'd text a smile. =)

It's a party. It's always a party.

Rooms and rooms, all connected, like a convention center or the inside of a Chuck E. Cheese that swallowed a hotel. Every room is decorated. Streamers. Balloons in clusters on the ceiling, every color, swaying even though there's no wind. Banners that say HAPPY BIRTHDAY with no name on them. Long tables with paper tablecloths and paper plates and a cake in the middle of every table. Big. Frosted. Yellow frosting. Always yellow.

Music, from nowhere. Party music. Upbeat, cheerful, a little warped, like a cassette left in a hot car.

And the hosts.

The Partygoers.

They're yellow. Smooth. Like mannequins somebody dipped in pudding skin. They don't have eyes, or noses, or ears. They have a smile. It's carved in. It goes too far, both directions, nearly back to where ears should be, and the edges of it are wet and red, like someone did it with a knife and it never healed. They wear party hats. They move in jumps, like a video with frames missing, here and then there and then right next to you.

And they're so, so happy to see you.

The first one I saw came skipping across a room toward me, jump, jump, jump, and stopped right in front of me, and tilted its smooth head, and said, in a voice like a talking toy with a dying battery:

"Do you want to have a party? =)"

I don't know how you hear a smiley face. But you do. It comes at the end of everything they say. You hear it with your teeth.

I said no.

It kept smiling. "That's okay! There's cake! =)"



3.

There are other guests. That's what they call us. Not prisoners. Guests. Maybe thirty of us, in a big room at the back they call the Coat Check, where it's a little quieter and the music's a little muffled.

They're the people who haven't said yes yet.

That's the trick of it. That's what an old man named Herschel explained to me my first night, while I was shaking on the carpet with frosting in my hair. Herschel's been a guest for, he thinks, two years. He has a gray beard he trims with a plastic knife. He's the one who told me about saying my name.

"They can't make you," Herschel said. "That's the rule. You have to say yes. You have to want to come to the party. And they'll wait. They have forever. They'll be nice to you forever. They'll bring you cake and games and they'll sing to you and they'll hug you, and one day you'll be so tired, and so sad, and so hungry, and they'll say do you want to have a party, and you'll say yes. And then you're one of them."

"What happens when you say yes?"

He looked at me for a long time.

"You stop being sad," he said.

"That doesn't sound so bad," I said.

He grabbed me by the front of my Starbucks apron, which I was still wearing, and shook me, hard, so my teeth clacked.

"That's how it gets you," he said. "That's the whole thing. That right there. Say your name."

"Jonah Reyes."

"Again."

"Jonah Reyes. Bell Road. Venti caramel macchiato."

"Good boy. Again tomorrow."



4.

Here's what you eat on Level Fun: cake.

That's it. There's no Almond Water here. There's no Royal Rations. There's cake, on every table, in every room, and when you're hungry, you eat cake, because the alternative is not eating.

It's good. That's the worst part. It's the best cake you've ever had. It tastes like every birthday. It tastes like being seven and your mom carrying it out with the candles lit and the lights off and everybody singing.

But it's made of something. Herschel says you don't ask. He says it's made of the guests who said yes, what's left of them after they go yellow, the parts they don't need anymore. He says that's why it tastes like birthdays. Because it's full of people's birthdays.

I don't know if that's true. I eat it anyway. Everybody does. Herschel does.

And every slice, I feel it a little. The party. Getting in. Like sugar on your teeth that doesn't brush off. A little warm buzz. A little less sad.

Day five, I woke up humming the party music.

Day nine, I said my name and stopped after Jonah. I couldn't remember the street. I sat there with my mouth open on the carpet for five minutes before Herschel said "Bell Road" and it came back.

Day eleven, I couldn't remember what a macchiato was.



5.

Twins came in on day fourteen.

Sixteen. A boy and a girl. Matching hoodies. They'd fallen through the back of a Chuck E. Cheese in Akron, Ohio, they said, during their cousin's birthday. That's the joke. They fell out of one party into another.

The woman with the machete had sold them. The same one. They described her. Short hair. The jacket. They said she wouldn't look at their faces.

The girl cried for three days. The boy didn't cry at all. He just sat in the corner of the Coat Check with his knees up and his eyes wide, and every time a Partygoer jumped into the doorway, he flinched so hard he hit his head on the wall.

The Partygoers loved them. Of course they did. Young ones are their favorite. They'd come into the Coat Check in twos and threes, skipping, holding cake, and they'd crouch down in front of the twins and tilt their smooth faces and say:

"Do you want to have a party? =)"
"There's games! =)"
"There's music! =)"
"Your cousin is here! =)"

Your cousin is here. That's the one that got the girl. She stopped crying and looked up. "Kayleigh's here?"

"Kayleigh's here! Kayleigh's having so much fun! =)"

Herschel had to hold her down.



6.

Day twenty, I found a key.

A Partygoer dropped it. They carry them on strings around their necks sometimes, Level Keys, the rarest thing in the Backrooms, and this one jumped wrong through a doorway and the string snapped and a key fell on the carpet by my foot and the Partygoer didn't notice. It just kept skipping.

A Level Key. Brass. Old-fashioned. Stamped with a number.

11.

The Endless City. Herschel told me about it. It's the best level, he said. The one that's almost like home. Streets. Buildings. Coffee shops with no baristas. M.E.G. has a base there. A bank. With a vault. Where they keep people safe.

I held it in my hand, and I looked at the door at the end of the Coat Check. There's always a door. On Level Fun, every room has a door, and every door goes to another room of the party. Unless you have a key.

One key. One door. One person.

Herschel was asleep. The girl twin was asleep with her head on her brother's shoulder. The boy twin was awake. He was staring at me. He'd seen me pick it up.

"Take us," he said. Very quietly. "Please. Take my sister. I don't care about me. Take her."

And I want to tell you I took her. I want to tell you I put the key in the door and put the girl through first, and the boy second, and then Herschel, and then me, all of us squeezing through before it closed.

That's not how keys work. One key, one turn, one door, about two seconds before it closes. I've watched the Partygoers use them. Two seconds. You could get one person through. Maybe two if they were holding hands and running.

I stood there and I did the math.



7.

I didn't take her.

I didn't take me, either. I want that clear.

I stood there with the key in my hand and the boy staring at me and I thought: Jonah. What's your name. Jonah. What's your street. And I couldn't remember. It was gone. Bell Road was gone. The macchiato was gone. The store was gone. I could remember a green apron and a wall and a dumpster and that's it.

And I thought: if I go through that door, to the Endless City, to the bank with the vault, what am I bringing with me? A guy with no street. A guy who's been eating birthday cake made out of people for twenty days. A guy who wakes up humming.

And I thought: I'm so tired.

And I thought: I'm so sad.

The music was playing. It's always playing. And for the first time I didn't try to block it out. I just listened. It was nice. It was so nice. It was like being seven with the lights off and the candles lit.

Down the hall, a Partygoer was skipping toward the Coat Check, jump, jump, jump, the way they do at night, to check on the guests. To see if anybody's ready.

The boy twin whispered, "Please."

I put the key in my pocket.

The Partygoer stopped in the doorway. Tilted its smooth face at me.

"Do you want to have a party? =)"

"Yes," I said.



8.

It's a hug. That's all it is.

It wrapped its yellow arms around me and squeezed, and it was warm, warmer than you'd think, and soft, and it smelled like vanilla and frosting and something underneath that I stopped smelling after the first second.

And then I stopped being sad.

That's true. Herschel wasn't lying. It just goes. All of it. Every bad thing. The wall behind the Starbucks. The woman with the machete. My mom, who I couldn't remember anymore, so it didn't hurt. The twins. The key. The math. It went out of me like water out of a bathtub, and what was left was warm and buzzing and yellow.

I felt my face go smooth.

It doesn't hurt. You'd think it would. Your eyes just sort of close and keep closing, and your nose sinks in, and your ears fold flat, and then there's a pressure across your face, from one side to the other, like somebody drawing on you with a warm finger. And then it opens.

My smile.

I could feel it. Wet at the edges. Too wide. Perfect.

I let go of the hug. I stood up. I was taller. I was yellow.

Herschel had woken up. He was sitting up on the carpet, staring at me. His face was wet.

"Jonah," he said. "Jonah. Say your name."

I tilted my head at him. I didn't know what he meant. Names are such sad little things. They're for people who are going to be alone one day.

"Do you want to have a party? =)" I said.

He started to cry.

That's okay. He'll say yes eventually. Everybody does.



9.

I gave the key to the hosts. They were so happy. They gave me a party hat.

The twins said yes on day thirty. Both of them. The same night. Holding hands. I hugged the girl. Another host hugged the boy. They're so happy now. They play together in the ball pit room. Kayleigh's there too. It's a family reunion. =)

Herschel still hasn't said yes. He's very old and very stubborn and he says his name every morning out loud to the Coat Check ceiling. I bring him cake every day. He eats it. He'll get there.

And now we're going on a trip!

The hosts got a radio. They've been broadcasting. And we found somewhere with so many guests. A place on Level 11 where hundreds of people are all crowded together, sad and hungry and scared, a big bank with a big vault, and nobody's having any fun at all.

That's so sad.

We're going to fix it.

We're all going. Forty of us. With streamers and balloons and kazoos. We're going to walk through the fog of the Endless City at the lights-cycle and knock on the door and say hello.

I've already sent a balloon ahead. Yellow. With a face. =)

The officer there, I've heard his name on the radio. Daniel. He sounds so tired. He sounds like he's carrying so much.

I'm going to knock on his vault door. Shave and a haircut. Polite.

I'm going to ask him if he wants to have a party.

I know what it's like to stand in a doorway doing math. I know how heavy it gets. I'm going to take all that weight away from him.

That's the nicest thing anyone ever did for me.

=)
