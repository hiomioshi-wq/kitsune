The Backrooms: Base Beta
A spinoff of No Wind Resistance. The field logs of M.E.G. Officer Hays.

Trigger warning: This story contains siege, starvation, people locked out to die, body horror, entity attacks, adult language, and a narrator who follows procedure all the way down.
Read at your own risk.



LOG 001

M.E.G. Field Log. Officer Daniel Hays. Outpost Base Beta, Level 11, the Endless City.

They told me at orientation to keep a log. They said it's good for your sanity. They said the Backrooms gets in your head if you don't write things down, because the days don't match up, the lights don't cycle right, and after a while you forget which memories are yours and which ones the walls gave you.

So. Log one.

Base Beta is a bank. Was a bank. First National something, the sign's half gone. Marble floors, brass teller windows, and a vault in the back with a door three feet thick. That vault's why we're here. Level 11 is the safest level most wanderers ever see, almost like the Frontrooms, streets and skyscrapers and fog, coffee shops with no baristas, but it's still the Backrooms, and when things go bad, they go bad all at once. The vault is where you go when they go bad.

We've got two hundred and twelve wanderers registered at Beta. Sixteen M.E.G. staff. A medic named Okonkwo. A generator running on Firesalt from the Level 2 pipes, very carefully. A water purifier that turns tap water from the Level 11 buildings into something that's almost Almond Water if you don't think about it too hard. A radio that talks to Base Alpha down on Level 1 maybe one day out of three.

It's a good post. I've been on worse. I did eight months on Level 3, the Electrical Station, and I still hear the hum in my sleep. Here, you can sit on the bank steps and drink terrible coffee and watch the fog and almost, almost forget.

Rule one of the Major Explorer Group: Survive. Rule two: Help others survive. Rule three: When one and two conflict, refer to rule one.

I used to think rule three was a joke.



LOG 014

Mae came through again.

Hound-skin jacket. Machete. That look on her face like somebody stapled it on. She drops off Hound hides for the tannery and the routes she's mapped since last time. She's the best cartographer in the Backrooms that isn't on payroll. She knows doors nobody else knows. She found a noclip shortcut from Level 4, the Abandoned Office, straight into Level 9's suburbs, through a supply closet on the fourteenth floor, that saved a whole caravan last month.

She also sells people.

Everybody knows. Fresh noclippers, kids mostly, fall out of a Frontrooms wall into Level 0, and Mae finds them before M.E.G. does, and they don't show up on any registry. They show up at Duke's stall in the B.N.T.G. market on Level 1, and then they don't show up anywhere.

We look the other way. Command says we need her routes. Command says intervening with the B.N.T.G. would compromise the trade agreements that keep our batteries charged. Command says a lot of things.

I told her she's better than that. She told me nobody down here is.

I didn't argue. I wrote it down instead. That's what logs are for.



LOG 027

Bad news from Alpha. Supply line from Level 1 is cut. A Skin-Stealer got into the convoy at the Level 2 transition, wearing the face of a driver named Pruitt. Nobody noticed till it peeled him off at dinner. Twelve dead. The rest scattered into the pipes.

No supply run for at least a month.

I did the math with Okonkwo. Two hundred and twelve wanderers. Sixteen staff. The purifier makes enough not-quite-Almond-Water for a hundred and forty people. Royal Rations for maybe three weeks if we cut to half.

A hundred and forty out of two hundred and twenty-eight.

We cut everyone to half. That buys time. Not enough.

People who go without Almond Water too long go strange. First you get headaches. Then you forget names. Then you start talking to the walls, and the walls start talking back, and eventually the thing that's left of you crawls off into Level 6 where the lights are always out and you become a Wretch. Hairless. Gray. Grinning in the dark. Gnawing on whatever you can find.

I've seen it. I've shot it. They still say your name sometimes.



LOG 033

We've got our first Wretch.

Wanderer named Callum. Registered three years ago. Liked to play chess on the bank steps with anybody who'd sit down. Two days ago he stopped knowing what chess was. Last night we found him in the teller booths, chewing on a stapler, hairless in patches, saying "check, check, check" to the brass bars.

Okonkwo gave him a whole flask of the real stuff from our emergency reserve. Real Almond Water, not purifier sludge. Sometimes that pulls them back if it's early.

It didn't pull him back.

We put him outside the front doors. Into the fog. That's protocol. You can't keep a Wretch inside a base, they don't get better and they bite.

He stood on the steps in the fog all night and said "check, check, check."

In the morning he was gone.



LOG 041

The radio picked up a broadcast today. Not Alpha. Somebody on an open frequency, laughing, playing music. Party music. Then a voice, high, sweet, a little off, like a kid's toy with a dying battery.

"Do you want to have a party? =)"

Everyone in the radio room went quiet.

You don't hear the Partygoers on Level 11. They live on Level Fun. Balloons and streamers and cake that isn't cake. They don't come up here. They're not supposed to come up here.

Okonkwo said it's probably a skip. Radio waves do weird things in the Backrooms. A broadcast from Fun bouncing through some cracked level and landing on our antenna. Doesn't mean anything.

I logged it anyway.

That night, out in the fog, somebody let go of a balloon. Yellow. It drifted past the bank windows, slow, at the height of a person's head.

It had a smiley face drawn on it in Sharpie.

=)



LOG 045

They're here.

They came at the lights-cycle, right when Level 11's streetlights go dim for their twelve-minute flicker. You could hear them before you could see them. Laughing. Party horns. A kazoo. The pop of balloons.

Partygoers. Yellow, faceless, smooth as a mannequin. No eyes. Just a smile carved into them, too wide, the edges wet. They move wrong. They move like a video that's dropping frames. And they're always, always happy.

They came down the street from the north, maybe forty of them, dragging streamers, and they went through the coffee shop on the corner first. Six wanderers had been sleeping in there because the bank was too crowded. We heard them scream. Then we heard them laugh. The same laugh. Then the six of them walked out of the coffee shop, yellow and smooth, smiling, holding balloons.

That's what they do. That's the party. You're the guest, and then you're the host.

I sounded the siren. Everybody to the vault.



LOG 046

Writing this inside the vault. Hands shaking. Sorry for the handwriting.

The vault holds a hundred and sixty people if you pack them in. Standing room. Shoulder to shoulder. I designed the evacuation plan myself. I know the numbers. A hundred and sixty, and the air scrubbers can keep a hundred and sixty breathing for four days.

We had two hundred and twenty-two in the bank when the siren went off.

They came in a crush. Staff first, that's protocol, staff have to run the scrubbers and the door. Then the medical cots. Then kids. Then everybody else, as fast as they could get through.

At a hundred and sixty, I put my hand on the vault door wheel.

There were still sixty people in the lobby. Running. Pushing. Screaming. I could see the front doors of the bank over their heads, and through the glass, yellow shapes, smiling, holding balloons, pressing up against it. The glass was starting to crack. Spider-web cracks, growing out from where their smooth faces touched it.

Okonkwo grabbed my arm. "Hays. Hays, we can fit more. We can fit twenty more if we pack the kids on the shelves."

The scrubbers can't take twenty more. I know the numbers. Twenty more and nobody breathes by day three. A hundred and eighty dead instead of sixty. It's math. It's the simplest math there is.

Rule one. Survive. Rule two. Help others survive. Rule three. When they conflict.

I turned the wheel.



LOG 047

It takes eleven seconds to close the vault door all the way. It's a very heavy door.

I want to write down those eleven seconds, because I'm the one who has to carry them, and maybe if they're on paper I won't have to carry all of them.

Second one: a woman in a Level 10 harvest smock, the kind the wheat-field settlers wear, gets her hand on the edge of the door.

Second three: she's screaming my name. She knows my name. Her name is Delia. She's been at Beta two years. She traded me a Level Key once for a pair of socks.

Second five: she pulls her hand back. I don't know if she chose to or if someone pulled her.

Second seven: through the gap, I see the front doors of the bank give way. Glass everywhere. Party horns.

Second nine: I see Delia turn around to face them.

Second eleven: the door seals. The wheel locks. And through three feet of steel, very faint, like something from another room, I hear sixty people stop screaming and start laughing.

All at once. Same laugh.

Then a kazoo.



LOG 049

Day three in the vault. Air's getting thick. Kids are crying. People are sitting on the floor with their eyes closed, rocking. Someone's been praying to Jerry, the blue bird, very quietly, in the corner. I didn't stop them. I almost asked them to pray louder.

They're still out there. We can hear them. They knock on the vault door sometimes. Polite knocks. Shave and a haircut.

Then a voice, through the steel. Delia's voice. Sweet. Wrong.

"Daniel? Daniel, are you in there? We're having a party. Everyone's here. Everyone except you. =)"

Okonkwo won't look at me. She hasn't looked at me since I turned the wheel. She's done the math too. She knows I'm right. She knows if I'd let twenty more in, we'd all be dead by now. She still won't look at me.

That's fine. I don't look at me either.



LOG 052

Day five. Air ran out on day four. Not all the way. Just enough.

Eleven people didn't wake up this morning. Old ones, mostly. One kid. The scrubbers couldn't keep up with a hundred and sixty scared people breathing hard for four days. My numbers were wrong. They assumed calm breathing. Nobody breathes calm while a party's knocking on the door.

So my numbers were wrong. And I could have fit twenty more, if I'd known eleven would die anyway.

That's the part that's going to get me. Not the sixty. The eleven. The math that didn't work. The fact that I was wrong and turned the wheel anyway, and Delia's hand came off the door, and it didn't even buy what it was supposed to buy.

The knocking stopped this afternoon. Then the music. Then nothing.

Tomorrow we open the door.



LOG 053

They were gone. Mostly.

The lobby was covered in frosting. That's what the Partygoers leave. Yellow frosting, sticky, smelling of vanilla and something rotten underneath, smeared on the marble, on the brass, on the teller windows, in long drag marks toward the front doors. Balloons tied to the chandelier. Confetti in the water fountain. Party hats on the floor, sixty of them, in a neat row, like place settings.

No bodies. There never are, with Partygoers. They don't kill you. They invite you.

There was a note on the vault door, in yellow frosting, in big careful letters like a kid wrote it.

THANK YOU FOR THE PARTY DANIEL =)

Okonkwo looked at it a long time. Then she finally looked at me.

"Write it down," she said. "In your log. Every word. You don't get to skip it."

So I did. You're reading it.



LOG 060

Supply line's back. Base Alpha sent a convoy with an armed escort and forty cases of real Almond Water. Command sent a commendation. Officer Hays, for exemplary adherence to evacuation protocol under extreme duress. Survival rate of seventy percent in a class-five Partygoer incursion. Highest on record.

There's a little ceremony. Somebody pins a little metal M.E.G. badge on my jacket. People clap.

Okonkwo transferred to Level 37, the Poolrooms, the quietest post in the Backrooms, nothing there but white tile and warm water and silence. She didn't say goodbye.

Mae came through the next week. Dropped off hides. Looked at the frosting stains still ground into the marble, the ones that never came out. Looked at the badge on my jacket.

"Seventy percent," she said. "Nice."

"You heard."

"Everybody heard." She lit a cigarette. "Told you. Nobody down here's better than that."

And I wanted to hit her. And I wanted to tell her she was wrong. And I wanted to tell her that what I did was different from what she does, because I did it by the book, because I had a reason, because I had numbers.

But she was right. The numbers were wrong. And I turned the wheel anyway.



LOG 061

New orientation class today. Nine fresh noclippers, the ones M.E.G. got to before Mae did. Wide eyes. Frontrooms clothes. One of them still had a Dunkin' cup in his hand. Empty. He wouldn't put it down.

I gave them the speech.

Drink Almond Water, it keeps you sane. Never trust a smile in the dark. Never follow a balloon. If you hear your name from a voice you know, plug your ears. If a coworker starts peeling at the edges, run. Never touch Liquid Pain. Never go into Level 6 without three light sources. Never answer the door when someone knocks shave-and-a-haircut.

And then I told them the M.E.G. rules.

Rule one: Survive. Rule two: Help others survive.

The kid with the Dunkin' cup raised his hand. "What's rule three?"

I looked at him for a long time.

"There is no rule three," I said. "Forget I said there were rules."

He wrote that down. He's going to keep a log. Good for his sanity, I told him.

It isn't. But it's something to hold onto while you turn the wheel.
