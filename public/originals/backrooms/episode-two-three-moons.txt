The Backrooms: Episode Two · The Three Moons Initiative

The full-length sequel to No Wind Resistance (incorporating Corbenic, Sundays, Spatial Shift, and The Flock)

Trigger warning: death, purgatory, abandonment, a stolen body and a stolen life, drugging a sibling with cult fluid, body horror, bureaucratic mass harvesting, adult language, and two people who did the worst things in the world having to walk back down the stairs together to fix one of them.

COLD OPEN

EXT. THE BOG OF CORBENIC (SCP-2922) - NIGHT THAT NEVER ENDS

A sky the color of a dead channel on an old CRT television. No stars. No clouds. No wind.

Hanging in that grey void, impossibly huge, cold, and absolute: THREE GLOWING MOONS, aligned in a crooked, watching triad.

Below them stretches an infinite wasteland of grey ash-sand, jagged black basalt, and petrified trees without a single leaf.

Parked beside one twisted, black tree—its lowest branch shaped like a human hand held out, open, empty—is a rusted 2008 HONDA CIVIC. One headlight flickers weakly. A crumpled Cool Ranch Doritos bag, empty now, blows an inch across the sand and stops, because there isn't really any wind here.

Around the car stand a HUNDRED STRIDERS. Twenty feet tall. Chitinous black bone, spider-legged, clutching jagged twenty-foot spears. They don't attack. They just stand in a vast, patient ring, mandibles clicking in a slow, dry rhythm.

Click. Click-click.

Sitting on the dented hood of the Civic is KEVIN (19). Torn vintage graphic tee. Bare feet black with Corbenic ash. A rusted tire iron resting across his knees. His eyes are hollow, dry, fixed on the spot in the sky where the neon-blue rift closed and left him behind.

Through the open driver's window, the car radio plays static.

And underneath the static, a single, long, flat, unbroken hospital heart-monitor tone:

BEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEP.

Standing ten feet away, beside the hand-shaped branch of the petrified tree, is THE GRAIL KNIGHT. Ancient, green-rusted silver plate armor, tattered surcoat bearing the faded emblem of three white moons. Its worn sword rests point-down in the sand. Its visor is up.

Inside the helmet there is no face—only grey sand, trickling in a steady, whispering stream down through the gorget like an hourglass that never empties.

THE GRAIL KNIGHT
(A voice like dry wind through a keyhole)
She isn't coming back down the ladder, boy.

Kevin doesn't look at the Knight. His knuckles tighten slightly on the tire iron.

KEVIN
She came once. She blew up a reactor and jumped into hell for me once.

THE GRAIL KNIGHT
Once is what the living have in them. The second time they do the math. Every pilgrim who ever carried a blade into this bog came for someone. Then they saw the moons, and they saw that the hole out only fits one pair of shoulders, and they climbed.

Sand spills from the corner of the Knight's chin-guard as it tilts its helmet toward the Civic's radio.

THE GRAIL KNIGHT
Listen to the radio, Kevin. The tone hasn't changed. Your machine in Ohio is off. Your chart is closed. And yet—listen under the tone.

Kevin frowns. He leans toward the open windshield.

Under the flat BEEEEEEEEEEP of the monitor, another sound is bleeding through the Civic's blown speakers. Not static. A low, rhythmic, mechanical thrum—and the faint, cheerful chime of a Nintendo Switch menu screen.

KEVIN
...What is that?

THE GRAIL KNIGHT
Someone else is using your heartbeat up top. Someone bought the coat you left on the bed in Room 318.

CUT TO:

INT. MAE'S APARTMENT - COLUMBUS, OHIO - SUNDAY AFTERNOON

Real afternoon sunlight slices in thin, dusty stripes through tightly shut vinyl blinds. The apartment smells like old drywall, chamomile, and fear kept in a very small box.

At the kitchen sink stands MAE (25). She wears an oversized grey hoodie pulled high on her throat—and even so, a jagged, raised scar of radioactive neon-blue burn tissue snakes up the side of her neck from where the portable Scranton Reality Anchor was ripped out of her spine.

In her right hand, a glass of tap water. In her left, a tiny plastic bottle of Kroger pure almond extract.

She squeezes a single drop into the water. Her hand is shaking so badly the dropper clinks against the rim of the glass.

In the living room, sitting cross-legged on the sagging floral couch, is LEO (17). The left side of his face, from temple to jaw, is a pale, smooth lattice of old Clump-scar tissue. He's holding a Switch controller, grinning softly at the TV.

Look closer at Leo's eyes. Around the edges of the irises, where the white meets the color, is a faint, luminous ring of swimming-pool blue.

LEO
Mae! Did you hear the stairs? That's his steps!

MAE
(Voice sandpaper-rough)
Lots of people live in the building, Leo.

LEO
No, he skips the fourth step on the second landing because it squeaks. He always skips it!

Mae turns toward the refrigerator. Slowly, silently, she pulls open the freezer door.

Cold white vapor rolls over her scarred throat.

There, on the wire rack, sit three bags of Kroger frozen sweet peas. Mae reaches in and slides the middle bag two inches to the left.

Behind the peas sits a glass mason jar. Half-full of a thick, faintly glowing, bioluminescent blue fluid that smells of summer chlorine and birthday cake frosting. The memory-wiping nectar of Jerry.

Three knocks on the front door. Polite. Measured. Even.

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

Mae shuts the freezer door with her hip.

Before she can cross the linoleum, Leo scrambles off the couch and yanks the deadbolt open.

Standing in the hallway is "KEV" (19).

It is Kevin's body. Kevin's messy dark hair. Kevin's awkward nineteen-year-old shoulders. Kevin's faded vintage graphic tee.

In his left arm he carries a cold two-liter bottle of Mountain Dew. In his right hand, a plastic Kroger grocery bag holding a pint of mint chocolate chip ice cream.

When he smiles, every muscle in Kevin's face moves in the right order—just half a beat too late, and a millimeter too wide.

KEV
Hey, Leo. Sorry I'm late. Bus on High Street had to wait for a train.

LEO
Kevin! You brought Dew! Come on, I already picked Yoshi, your controller's plugged in!

Leo grabs the two-liter and darts back to the couch.

Kev steps over the threshold into the apartment. He doesn't look at the couch. He looks directly across the narrow room into the kitchen doorway, where Mae stands clutching her glass of fake Almond Water.

Their eyes lock.

Mae's gaze drops to Kev's fingers curled around the plastic grocery bag—the slightly wrong tension in the tendons, the way a thing that spent centuries living wet and flat behind the yellow wallpaper of Level 0 wears human joints like borrowed winter gloves.

KEV
(Soft, friendly)
Hi, Mae. It's Kev. Brought mint chip. Thought we were out.

[ACT ONE]

INT. MAE'S APARTMENT - LIVING ROOM / KITCHEN - TWENTY MINUTES LATER

On the television, Mario Kart 8 chimes brightly. Rainbow Road.

On the couch, Leo leans hard to the left as he takes a turn, mouth slightly open, blue-ringed eyes reflecting the screen.

Beside him sits Kev.

Look at how Kev holds the Switch Pro Controller. Not with his thumbs on the sticks and index fingers on the bumpers—he cradles it in both palms the way a child holds a captured frog, pressing the buttons with the flat pads of his middle fingers. His kart drives straight off the edge of Rainbow Road into space.

LEO
Dude! You fell off at the exact same turn last Sunday! You gotta drift!

KEV
(Cheerful, unbothered)
Man, my thumb slipped. You're just too fast for me, Leo. Look at that—first place again.

In the kitchen, Mae opens the freezer. She slides the pint of Kroger mint chocolate chip ice cream next to the frozen peas—two inches in front of the glowing blue jar.

She doesn't close the door right away. She stares at the jar.

KEV (O.S.)
(Quiet, right behind her shoulder)
You shouldn't leave the door open. It sweats if it gets warm.

Mae doesn't flinch. She learned five years ago in Level 0 never to flinch when something gets behind you. Instead, her right hand slides smoothly into the dish-drying rack and closes around the handle of an eight-inch serrated bread knife.

She shuts the freezer door and turns.

Kev is standing three feet away at the kitchen counter, holding two empty plastic cups for the Mountain Dew. On the couch twelve feet behind him, Leo has put on his big over-ear headphones, humming along to the Mario Kart results music, completely oblivious.

Mae keeps the bread knife low against her thigh, blade angled up toward Kev's ribs.

MAE
(Barely above a breath)
You moved the peas three weeks ago.

KEV
I put them back the way I found them.

MAE
You smelled it.

KEV
Everyone from down there knows what the Bird smells like, Mae. Chlorine and cake. They wear it on their robes in Level 11. You gave him a capful in his tea last Sunday, didn't you? That's why he stopped whispering through the bedroom wall at 3:04 in the morning.

Mae's jaw tightens. The neon-blue Anchor scar on her neck pulses once, dimly, under her skin.

MAE
How do you know what time I wake up?

KEV
Because the coat wakes up at 3:04 too.

Kev taps his own chest—Kevin's chest, right over the sternum where the hospital electrodes were peeled off in Room 318 at Riverside Methodist.

KEV
It's still tuned to you. Like an old car radio that only gets one station. Three-oh-four in the morning, I'm lying in his bed at Denise's house looking at the plastic glow-in-the-dark stars on his ceiling, and his chest hears you whisper it across six miles of Columbus. *Spatial shift. Level 3. We got separated.* And then, from way down under the carpet, under the Hub, under everything... he answers you.

Mae brings the point of the bread knife up until it presses directly against the center of Kevin's vintage graphic tee.

MAE
Don't talk about him.

KEV
(Not stepping back; looking down at the knife)
I'm not trying to hurt you, Mae. If I wanted to take your coat, or Leo's, I'd have done it the first Sunday when you opened the door without your machete.

MAE
What are you? A Skin-Stealer doesn't talk like this. A Skin-Stealer digests the flesh in forty-eight hours and wears it until it rots off. You've been wearing him for four months. His skin isn't rotting.

KEV
That's because I didn't kill him to get it. Down in Level 0, when you peel a wanderer who's still inside their body, the body fights you until it sours. Like milk left on a radiator.

Kev tilts his head. His voice drops into something ancient, quiet, and strangely reverent.

KEV
I didn't steal him, Mae. I bought an introduction from Duke at Stall 44. Red-ink page in the frosting ledger. Duke said there was a kid who rode the Metro past the last stop. Said his soul got dumped in the Three Moons bog while his body lay flatlining in Room 318 at Riverside Methodist. Nobody was home. The coat was lying on the bed, still warm, with the monitor going *beeeeeeeeeep* and the nurse out at the desk doing paperwork. I came up through the thin wall behind the mop sink in the third-floor supply closet at four in the morning. I put him on. And I hit the drum in his chest until it started beating again.

Mae's hand on the knife handle is white-knuckled. A single tear cuts through the grime on her cheek, though her face doesn't move.

MAE
His mother thinks you're him.

KEV
Denise. She works customer service at the Graceland Kroger. Thursday nights we watch *Jeopardy!* and eat rotisserie chicken. When she hugs me, the coat's arms go around her all by themselves. It remembers how much pressure to use so her bad shoulder doesn't hurt. I take very good care of it, Mae. I brush its teeth twice a day. I don't even let it eat Doritos because the salt makes my throat dry.

MAE
I saw you in aisle four. You put the Cool Ranch back on the shelf.

KEV
The fingers wanted them. I didn't.

MAE
And last Sunday? After I came back from Site-19 with my neck burned open? You looked right at me and asked if I hated Denny's. And when I said yeah, you said *Me too*.

KEV
(A small, almost gentle smile)
The coat has a pocket full of that booth. You, and Leo, and a waitress offering Dasani, and the walls flaking into grey ash. And underneath that, it has sixty hours of you and him walking through the pipes in Level 2 talking about how all he wanted in the whole world was a Moons Over My Hammy at the Morse Road Denny's.

MAE
You failed the question on purpose.

KEV
Of course I did. You came back from Corbenic smelling like grey sand and Strider ash, Mae. And you came back alone. You climbed the ladder without him. Just like you climbed the ladder in Level 3 without Leo. I thought it would be impolite to pretend I was the boy you left in hell. Somebody in this apartment ought to tell the truth with you, now that you poured the blue jar down your brother's throat so he'd stop knowing what you did.

Mae shoves the bread knife forward a quarter-inch, piercing the cotton of the shirt, drawing a single bead of bright red—human red—blood from Kevin's sternum.

MAE
Give me one reason I don't open you from the collarbones down and dump you back behind the Morse Road dumpster.

KEV
(Pointing one slightly too-long finger toward the couch)
Because in ten seconds Leo's going to take off his headphones and ask why you haven't poured the Mountain Dew yet. And because if you kill me, Denise has to bury her son on Tuesday, and Leo loses his only friend, and thereal Kevin is still sitting on a rusted Honda Civic under three moons either way.

On the couch, Leo pulls one ear-cup off his scarred head and looks back over the sofa cushions.

LEO
Hey, Kev? Did you pour the Dew? The Special Cup's starting!

For three eternal seconds, Mae and the thing wearing her best friend stare into each other's eyes.

Slowly, Mae lowers the bread knife. She sets it silently on the laminate counter beside the sink.

She picks up the two-liter bottle, twists the cap—*PSSSSHT*—and pours two plastic cups of neon-green soda.

MAE
(Hollow)
Go play the Special Cup.

Kev picks up both cups. He pauses by her shoulder.

KEV
Wait.

Kev's smile vanishes. His head snaps toward the kitchen window with the sudden, unnatural speed of an apex predator in the dark.

The vinyl blinds are still shut.

Outside, in the Columbus afternoon... the birds have stopped singing. The traffic on Summit Street has gone dead silent.

And from the glass of fake Almond Water sitting on the counter, concentric ripples begin to vibrate across the surface.

HUMMMMMMMMMMMMMMMMMM.


The neon-blue scar running up Mae's throat flares like a welding arc under her skin. Mae gasps, dropping to one knee as her nervous system is hit by a massive sub-etheric drop in barometric pressure.

MAE
(Clutching her neck)
...Hume drop.

KEV
(Setting the plastic cups down very carefully)
Mae. Your wall.

Mae looks up at the drywall above the kitchen stove.

The off-white apartment paint is bubbling. Peeling backward in curling, dry flakes—and underneath it, bleeding through the plaster like a bruise rising to the surface of skin, is a familiar, damp, repeating pattern of mono-yellow arrows and fleur-de-lis.

LEVEL 0 WALLPAPER.

The kitchen ceiling light—an ordinary sixty-watt bulb—stretches horizontally with a sickening *CRACK* of warping geometry, flattening into a four-foot fluorescent office tube that begins to buzz at sixty cycles per second.

On the TV, Rainbow Road freezes. The Switch screen fractures into red halftone dots and VHS tracking lines.

LEO
(Taking off his headphones, blinking his blue-ringed eyes)
Mae? Why does the carpet smell wet?

Mae scrambles to the hall closet, wrenches the door open, and hauls out a heavy canvas duffel bag. She unzips it in one violent motion: inside lies her greased black Hound-skin jacket, a thirty-inch carbon-steel machete, and the stolen Site-19 AA-12 automatic shotgun loaded with glowing blue Liquid Pain shells.

KEV
What did you do at Site-19 when you went down to Corbenic?

MAE
(Shrugging on the Hound-skin jacket, strapping the machete to her thigh)
I blew up their primary Scranton Reality Anchor to tear the rift open!

KEV
You blew up a macro-scale Anchor on the Eastern Seaboard grid?! Do you have any idea how much reality that unit was holding up?! That's a fifty-mile Hume sinkhole—Columbus isn't just thinning, Mae, the whole zip code is de-rendering!

Outside on Summit Street, the heavy, diesel roar of four unmarked black armored vans slamming to a halt at the curb shakes the third-floor windowpane.

Car doors slam in synchronized unison. Heavy tactical boots pound up the brick stairwell below.

And rising above the boots—a soft, eerie, melodic chorus of voices humming a single flat chord in unison, accompanied by the smell of ozone, hospital chlorine, and vanilla frosting.

Mae parts two slats of the vinyl blinds with the barrel of the AA-12.

Down on Summit Street, the pavement itself is flickering like a corrupted JPEG—squares of grey Ohio asphalt blinking out to reveal endless miles of wet yellow Level 0 carpet underneath. A mailman on the corner takes a step off the curb and simply *noclips* waist-deep through the sidewalk, screaming before vanishing entirely.

Around the perimeter of Mae's brick apartment building stand two dozen operatives.

Half of them are SCP FOUNDATION MOBILE TASK FORCE soldiers in full tactical plate, wearing miniaturized backpack Scranton Anchors wired to green-glowing Kant counters on their rifles.

The other half wear pale, floor-length, sky-blue bathrobes and smooth white porcelain masks with painted blue beaks, carrying glass lanterns filled with swirling blue Jerry-nectar.

And in the center of the street, rolling down the hydraulic ramp of an armored command van in a motorized titanium wheelchair, is DIRECTOR STERLING (60s).

Both of his legs from the mid-thigh down are gone—replaced by brutal, whistling pneumatic steel prosthetics wired into a portable Hume stabilizer bolted to the back of his wheelchair. Over his Foundation trench coat, pushed up on his forehead like a visor, is a cracked blue Partypooper mask: `=(`

Sterling raises a heavy silver bullhorn to his mouth. His voice booms up through the de-rendering brick wall of the third floor, amplified by an anomalous sub-bass frequency:

STERLING (O.S.)
(Through bullhorn)
Attention, Apartment 3B. Subject 404—Maeve Vane. This is Director Sterling, Site-19 Containment & Salvage. Your apartment is currently sitting at 0.14 Humes and falling. Within four minutes, 43201 Summit Street will cease to exist in baseline consensus reality.

In the living room, Leo stands up from the couch. His scarred face goes slack. The swimming-pool blue ring around his irises dilates until it almost swallows the pupils.

LEO
(In a flat, serene monotone)
...The flock is downstairs.

MAE
Leo, get away from the window! Get behind the counter!

Mae grabs Leo by his hoodie sleeve and yanks him toward the kitchen.

In the hallway outside Apartment 3B, heavy boots reach the third-floor landing. A magnetic breaching charge clamps onto the deadbolt with a metallic *CLACK*.

STERLING (O.S.)
(Through bullhorn, calmly lighting a cigarette)
When you detonated the Sub-Level 9 Anchor to throw your little tantrum in Corbenic, Vane, you cost the Foundation twelve years of B.N.T.G. trade surplus. More importantly, you fused our only prototype Soul-Transference Anchor into your cervical spine. We want our hardware back. The Flock wants their apostate brother and their stolen jar back. Come out with your hands up, or we turn off the perimeter stabilizers and let the apartment fall into Level Fun.

KEV
(Looking at the front door as the breaching charge whines to a high pitch)
Mae. How many shells do you have for that gun?

MAE
One drum. Twenty rounds.

KEV
That's not enough for twenty-four guys and a wheelchair.

MAE
Then get out of my way or pick up a knife!

Kev looks down at Kevin's hands—the soft, nineteen-year-old community-college hands he's brushed and washed and kept unscarred for Denise on Thursdays.

KEV
(Quietly, to himself)
Sorry, Denise. I'll sew the sleeves back together after.

KABOOOOOM!

The apartment door blows off its hinges in a sheet of white smoke and splintered oak.

Three MTF breachers in black armor storm into the living room, carbines raised, laser sights cutting red lines through the dust—followed immediately by two blue-robed FOLLOWERS OF JERRY in porcelain bird-masks swinging censers of vaporized blue memory-mist.

MTF BREACHER 1
Target 404 in the kitchen! Anchor signature confirmed—take her spine intact!

Mae steps out from behind the refrigerator, raises the AA-12 to her shoulder, and pulls the trigger.

BANG-BANG-BANG-BANG!

Deafening auto-shotgun thunder fills the tiny apartment. The glowing blue Liquid Pain buckshot punches straight through the lead breacher's ceramic chest plate, crystallizing his torso into brittle blue glass that shatters against the doorframe.

The second MTF soldier dives behind the floral couch, firing a burst of 5.56 rounds that chews up the kitchen cabinets, shattering Mae's glass of fake Almond Water and peppering the refrigerator door with bullet holes.

Meanwhile, the two porcelain-masked Followers of Jerry rush straight toward Leo, uncapping a heavy glass jar of raw blue nectar.

FOLLOWER RUTH
(Behind the painted beak, soothing, maternal)
Little brother! Come back to the rafters! The Bird kept your mask clean!

LEO
(Dropping to his knees, hands rising toward his face, entranced by the blue vapor)
Ruth...? My face hurts, Ruth—

MAE
LEO, NO!

Before Mae can swing the shotgun barrel around, the third MTF breacher tackles her across the kitchen linoleum, slamming her neck against the stove where the wall has completely decayed into damp Level 0 wallpaper.

And then—Kev moves.

He doesn't fight like a teenager. He doesn't fight like a human being.

With a wet, sickening *SCHLUCK* of separating cartilage, Kevin's jaw unhinges four inches lower than a human skull allows. From beneath the sleeves of the vintage graphic tee, the pale skin splits neatly along the forearms like an unbuttoned shirt cuff—and two whip-thin, multi-jointed, translucent grey tendons of pure Level 0 Skin-Stealer muscle erupt outward.

Kev vaults across the coffee table on all fours in a single blur.

His right tendril lashes around Follower Ruth's wrist, shattering the porcelain bird-mask with a backhand strike that sends her slamming into the Mario Kart television screen in a shower of sparks. His left arm catches the second MTF soldier by the throat, lifting a two-hundred-pound armored man clean off the carpet and hurling him through the de-rendering drywall into the hallway.

Mae drives the butt of her AA-12 up into the visor of the soldier pinning her, then fires a single point-blank round into his backpack stabilizer.

CRACK-ZZZZZZT!

The soldier's portable Anchor implodes in a flash of purple Hume lightning, instantly dropping the local reality threshold in the kitchen to zero.

The refrigerator *glitches*. Half of the appliance sinks six inches into the floor.

Behind the bullet-riddled freezer door, cold blue light begins to pour through the steel seams.

Kev retracts his grey tendrils back inside Kevin's forearms, zipping the human skin closed like wet Velcro, and hauls Leo up by the back of his collar.

KEV
Mae! The floor's going! Get the jar!

Mae wrenches the mangled freezer door open. One of the rifle bullets grazed the mason jar behind the frozen peas—a hairline crack runs down the glass, leaking thick, glowing blue Jerry-fluid over the pint of mint chocolate chip ice cream.

Mae snatches the cracked jar, wraps it in a dish towel, and shoves it deep into her canvas duffel bag.

From the hallway landing comes the heavy, mechanical whine of a hydraulic lift and pneumatic steel legs locking into place.

DIRECTOR STERLING rolls into the blown-out doorway of Apartment 3B.

Attached to the right armrest of his motorized wheelchair is a heavy, belt-fed Foundation Thaumaturgic Nailgun. Four MTF troopers and six blue-robed Followers fill the stairwell behind him.

STERLING
(Smiling coldly beneath the cracked blue `=(` mask on his brow)
Look at that. A Level 0 parasite playing house in the Denny's kid's corpse, a brainwashed cult choirboy, and the girl who sold Britney in the Wendy's visor to Stall 44 just to drug her own brother's chamomile tea.

Leo freezes in Kev's grip.

Slowly, Leo turns his scarred face toward Mae. The blue rings in his eyes flicker as the smell of the cracked jar in her duffel hits him.

LEO
...Mae? What did he say about my tea?

MAE
(Heart stopping; then raising the shotgun at Sterling)
Shut up, Sterling.

STERLING
You didn't tell him? Oh, Maeve. And here I thought the Three Moons Initiative only knighted paladins. You want to know why your portable Anchor is burning through your neck? Because Corbenic armor runs on righteous conviction, sweetheart—and you left the real boy on the car roof and poisoned your brother to cover your tracks. Your Hume battery's at two percent.

Sterling squeezes the trigger on his armrest mount.

THWUMP-THWUMP-THWUMP-THWUMP!

Four six-inch cold-iron Scranton spikes fire down the narrow hallway, pinning the wallpaper back into solid brick where they strike—and grazing Mae's shoulder, spinning her into the kitchen counter.

KEV
(Baring teeth that are shifting from white enamel to translucent bone)
Window! Fire escape! NOW!

Kev kicks out the vinyl blinds and the third-floor window frame in a single blow.

Mae grabs Leo's hand—holding on tight, both palms locked around his wrist so she cannot let go—and dives through the shattered window onto the rusted iron fire escape alongside Kev.

EXT. SUMMIT STREET / ALLEY BEHIND MORSE ROAD DENNY'S - CONTINUOUS

There is no Columbus skyline anymore.

Above the rooftops of Summit Street, the blue Ohio sky is tearing apart in massive, jagged strips like wet poster paper glued to a billboard—and behind the sky isn't outer space.

Behind the sky is an infinite, upside-down ceiling of humming fluorescent office tiles and damp acoustic panels stretching across the horizon.

The alley below the fire escape is warping. Space itself is folding inward like an accordion as the fifty-mile Hume sinkhole collapses toward the nearest permanent thin spot in the city grid:

Two blocks away—folded directly against the brick wall of Mae's building by a non-Euclidean spatial crush—sits the rear dumpster and yellow neon sign of the MORSE ROAD DENNY'S.

Mae, Kev, and Leo sprint down the clanging iron steps of the fire escape as MTF bullets spark off the railings around them.

STERLING (O.S.)
(From the third-floor window above, into his radio)
Cut the quadrant anchors! Flush the whole block down the drain and meet them at the Level 1 freight intake!

At the bottom of the fire escape ladder, the alley asphalt drops out from under their boots like a trapdoor made of wet cardboard.

LEO
(Screaming as the gravity shifts ninety degrees sideways)
MAE!

MAE
I'VE GOT YOU! I'M NOT LETTING GO!

Mae wraps both arms around Leo's chest. Kev grabs the back of Mae's Hound-skin jacket with one hand—even as the seams along his neck split open for a fraction of a second beneath the Hume shear—and all three of them plummet headfirst through the dissolving brick wall behind the Morse Road Denny's—

Falling down, down, down out of the Frontrooms and straight into the cold, oil-stained concrete abyss of LEVEL 1.

ACT TWO: STALL 44 & THE FROSTING PAGES

[SCENE START]

INT. LEVEL 1 (THE HABITABLE ZONE) - B.N.T.G. TRADER'S KEEP - CONTINUOUS

CRAAAAASH!

A four-foot slab of poured, oil-stained warehouse ceiling gives way in a roar of rebar dust and blue-white Scranton sparks.

Mae, Leo, and "Kev" plummet twenty feet out of the upper geometry and slam directly through a sagging blue tarpaulin suspended over a crowded trading aisle.

Pallets of dented metal crates collapse. Strings of naked, cage-wrapped industrial work lights swing wildly, throwing jagged shadows across a cavernous concrete parking structure that stretches into infinite, damp gray fog in every direction.

LEVEL 1. THE HABITABLE ZONE.

Specifically: THE TRADER'S KEEP. A sprawling, subterranean night-market run by the B.N.T.G. (Backrooms Non-Aligned Trade Group). Hundreds of makeshift stalls built from corrugated iron, office cubicles, and welded shopping carts line the oil-slicked concrete pillars. The air reeks of ozone, wet cardboard, boiled synthetic grease, and sweet, stale vanilla.

Leo hits a stack of damp drywall sheets, rolling off onto the concrete floor, clutching his cracked porcelain Jerry mask to his chest.

Kev lands next to him—not rolling, not bracing, just absorbing the twenty-foot drop into his knees with a wet, rubbery *thwack* that no nineteen-year-old human skeleton should survive. He immediately drops onto his side and lets out a performative, theatrical groan.

KEV
(Rubbing his shin, slightly off-beat)
Ow. My... meniscus. That was a major OSHA violation, bro.

Mae doesn't groan. She lands on one combat boot and one knee, the dead Scranton burn-scar along her collarbone smoking faintly through the collar of her hound-skin jacket. Before the ceiling rubble even stops bouncing, she is on her feet, her rusted machete already out of its thigh sheath, her left hand checking Leo's ribs.

MAE
Leo. Fingers and toes. Move 'em.

LEO
(Wheezing, eyes wide with pale blue edges)
I'm—I'm good. I landed on the gypsum. Mae, the ceiling—there isn't a hole up there.

Venti caramel macchiato. Oat milk. Bell Road.

JONAH
(Smiling—a regular, human smile that doesn't hurt his cheeks)
Every morning, Mae.

`00:03 ... 00:02 ...`

Mae grabs Real Kevin by one wrist, Leo and Kev by the other, and steps backward through the sliding glass doors into Room 318.

*SHHHK-CLACK.*

The hospital glass seals shut—and turns Backrooms-side into a normal, reflectionless mirror behind the Snackrooms bar.

POST-CREDITS SCENE

INT. DENNY'S - MORSE ROAD, COLUMBUS - 3:04 A.M.

Fluorescent lights. Not the screaming sixty-hertz yellow buzz of Level 0—just cheap Ohio diner bulbs, one of them flickering slightly over the pie case.

Rain streaks the big front plate-glass window. Outside, a semi-truck splashes past on Morse Road, its taillights reflecting red across wet real-world asphalt.

In the corner vinyl booth sit four people.

MAE sits on the inside bench in an oversized hoodie, the faint blue scar peeking above her collar, both hands wrapped around a white ceramic mug.

Next to her sits LEO. The pale blue ring around his irises is completely gone. The scar on his left cheek is uncovered in the diner light, and he isn't hiding it against his shoulder anymore. He's building a sugar-packet house on the Formica table.

Across from them sit REAL KEVIN and KEV, squished side by side on the same vinyl bench.

In front of Real Kevin is a massive, steaming plate of a **Moons Over My Hammy** sandwich with hash browns, and an empty Cool Ranch Doritos bag smoothed flat beside his fork like a napkin. He is eating like a wolf that hasn't seen food since the Pleistocene.

Beside him, KEV—wearing his olive B.N.T.G. courier coat over his clean graphic tee, his Columbus State student ID clipped crookedly to the lapel—has a white mug of chamomile tea with honey in front of him. He isn't drinking it yet. He is holding the laminated Denny's late-night menu in both hands, very gently, the exact way you'd hold a small animal you're afraid of dropping.

A tired WAITRESS (50s, name tag `BRENDA`, pencil in her hair) walks up to the booth with four heavy glass tumblers on a tray.

WAITRESS
Alright, hon. One Moons Over My Hammy, extra hash, two chamomiles, one short stack. And four waters.

REAL KEVIN
(Swallowing a mouthful of egg and ham, pointing his fork at the tray)
Wait. Excuse me. Brenda. Is that Dasani?

WAITRESS
(Looking at him like he's high)
It's tap water from the fountain with a lemon wedge in it, sweetheart. It's three in the morning.

Real Kevin looks at Mae.

Mae looks at the lemon wedge floating in the ice water. She picks up the glass and takes a sip. It tastes like cold Columbus municipal pipes and real citrus.

MAE
Tap water's good. Tap water's great, Brenda. Thank you.

The Waitress sets the check down and walks off toward the kitchen.

KEV
(Looking carefully at the picture of the Sizzlin' Skillet on the menu)
Mae?

MAE
Yeah, Kev.

KEV
On Thursdays, when we go to Denise's house after her shift at the Kroger... how do we explain why there are two of us?

REAL KEVIN
(Wiping egg yolk off his chin with a paper napkin)
Easy. I already thought about this in the hospital elevator. We tell her you're Uncle Gary's kid from Toledo whose Honda Civic got repossessed, so you moved down for the computer program at Columbus State. Mom hasn't talked to Uncle Gary since 2014 and she loves feeding people. She'll buy two roast chickens every Thursday.

KEV
(Eyes widening slightly, touching his student ID)
Two chickens?

REAL KEVIN
And you can still have Sundays on the Switch if you want. You're actually way better at pretending to lose to Leo than I am.

LEO
(Flicking a Pink Sweet'N Low packet at Real Kevin's nose)
Hey. I beat both of you on Wario Stadium and you know it.

MAE
(Leaning her head back against the vinyl booth, closing her eyes for just a second)
I hate Denny's.

REAL KEVIN
Yeah, Mae. We know.

LEO
You say it three times a week.

KEV
(Very softly, smiling at his chamomile tea)
I like the carpet. It's almost yellow, but it's dry.

Under the table, between four pairs of sneakers, the shadow beneath the booth is slightly too dark.

Not diner-shadow dark. *Vantablack* dark. Fuzzier around the edges than a shadow has any architectural right to be.

A tiny, half-inch crumble of hash brown and cheddar cheese drops off Real Kevin's plate and lands on the linoleum under the table.

From the fuzzy black mass beneath the vinyl bench, two dinner-plate-sized, glowing white eyes blink open. Once. Twice.

A single, kitten-sized tendril of pure void-wool reaches out, pats the piece of hash brown—*FOOMP*—and absorbs it without a crumb left behind.

The silverware on the table rattles gently as a deep, twenty-hertz subwoofer purr vibrates up through the floorboards:

*VRRRRRRRRRRUMMMMMM...*

And then, very softly, from under the booth comes a tiny, high-pitched *"mew"*—that sounds unmistakably like a corrupted `.WAV` file of a very happy kitten.

Real Kevin nudges the rest of his hash browns to the edge of the table with his elbow and lets another piece drop.

Mae opens her eyes, looks at the three boys across the table, and drinks her tap water as the rain comes down on Columbus.

FADE TO BLACK.
