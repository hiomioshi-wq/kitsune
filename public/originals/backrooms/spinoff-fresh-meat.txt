The Backrooms: Fresh Meat

A prequel to No Wind Resistance

Trigger warning: abandonment, a sibling left behind, people traded as goods, body horror, cults, drug use, adult language, and a lead who lies about the worst thing she ever did for five years straight.

COLD OPEN

INT. LEVEL 3 - THE ELECTRICAL STATION - 2018

Brick tunnels. Copper pipes sweating. Sparks raining from junction boxes like sick fireworks. A roar of machinery so loud it's almost a silence.

MAE, 19, runs. No hound-skin jacket. No machete. A cheap denim jacket, a dollar-store flashlight, a backpack with a cartoon frog keychain. She looks like a college freshman on a dare, because she is one.

She's holding a hand. LEO, 15, vintage graphic tee, skateboard under his arm, sprinting behind her, gasping.

LEO
Mae, slow down, I can't—

MAE
Don't stop! Don't fucking stop!

Behind them, down the tunnel, the darkness MOVES. A wet, clicking, accordion sound. Something made of too many arms and too many faces, rolling forward like a tumbleweed made of people.

A CLUMP.

It fills the tunnel wall to wall. It's gaining.

Ahead: a maintenance ladder. Rusted. Bolted to the wall. Leading up to a hatch with daylight-yellow light leaking around the edges. Level 0 light. Familiar light.

MAE
Ladder! Go! Go!

They hit the ladder. Mae first, because she's holding his hand and pulling. She climbs. Leo climbs under her. The rungs are slick.

Leo's skateboard catches on a pipe. Yanks him sideways. His hand slips out of hers.

LEO
MAE!

He's dangling by one hand from the bottom rung. The Clump is twenty feet away. Fifteen.

Mae looks down at him.

She reaches down. Her fingers brush his.

Then she looks past him. At the Clump. At the faces in it. One of them is screaming. One of them is laughing. One of them looks like a kid Leo's age.

Ten feet.

She looks up. At the hatch. At the yellow light.

She climbs.

LEO (O.S.)
MAE! MAE, PLEASE!

She doesn't look back. She shoves the hatch open with her shoulder, hauls herself through, slams it shut, and sits on it.

Under her, through the metal: a scream. A crunch. Then the accordion sound, slowly rolling away.

Then nothing.

Mae sits on the hatch in the endless yellow hallway of Level 0, under the humming lights, with her hand still held out in front of her, open, the shape of his hand still in it.

She stares at it.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

INT. LEVEL 1 - B.N.T.G. TRADING POST - 2020

Two years later. A concrete warehouse with fluorescent lights that flicker like they're nervous. Crates. Barrels. Stalls made of old office partitions. Wanderers in patchwork clothes haggling over Almond Water, batteries, and Level Keys.

MAE, 21, walks in. Different. Harder. Hair hacked short with a knife. A jacket made from the shredded hide of a Hound, still a little greasy. A machete on her thigh. She smells like a sewer and doesn't care.

She's dragging someone behind her on a rope.

A YOUNG MAN, early twenties, in a Starbucks apron, wrists tied, mouth taped, eyes huge. Fresh. You can always tell the fresh ones. They still look surprised.

At a stall built out of filing cabinets sits DUKE, B.N.T.G. trader, fifties, a gold tooth, a Hawaiian shirt that's seen things. He's eating Royal Rations out of a can with his fingers.

DUKE
Mae! My favorite supplier! Whatcha got for me today?

MAE
Noclipper. Fell out of a ceiling in Level 0 about six hours ago. Still has all his teeth.

She shoves the Starbucks kid forward. He stumbles. Duke looks him over like a used car.

DUKE
Mm. Soft. Good skin. Clean hands. The Partygoers on Fun love 'em soft. Tastes like frosting, apparently.

The Starbucks kid starts screaming through the tape.

MAE
Usual rate.

DUKE
Two flasks of Almond Water, one battery pack, one Level Key to 11.

MAE
Three flasks.

DUKE
Mae.

MAE
He's got all his teeth, Duke.

Duke sighs. Counts out three flasks. Slides them across. Mae takes them. Hands over the rope. Doesn't look at the kid.

STARBUCKS KID
(through tape, muffled, sobbing)
Please. Please, I have a mom.

MAE
Everybody's got a mom.

She turns to walk away.

DUKE
Hey. Mae.

She stops.

DUKE (CONT'D)
That brother of yours. Still looking?

MAE
(not turning around)
Spatial shift. Level 3. We got separated. He's out there somewhere.

DUKE
That's what you always say.

MAE
'Cause it's what happened.

Duke looks at her a long beat. Then shrugs. Goes back to his Royal Rations.

DUKE
Sure, kid. Sure.

Mae walks out. Behind her, the Starbucks kid is still screaming for his mom. The fluorescent lights hum louder, like they're trying to drown him out.

ACT TWO

INT. LEVEL 8 - CAVE SYSTEM - DAY

Stalactites. Damp. The skittering of Deathmoths on the ceiling. Mae crawls through a narrow passage on her belly, flashlight in her teeth.

She comes out into a cavern. In the center: a nest. Not a bird nest. A pile of things. Shoes. Glasses. Wallets. Bones, some. And grinning out of the dark all around it, dozens of pairs of floating white teeth.

SMILERS.

Mae doesn't move. She clicks off her flashlight. In the dark, the teeth drift closer. Then, slowly, lose interest. Drift off.

She waits. Then clicks it back on, low, cupped in her hand.

She crawls to the nest. Digs through it.

A backpack. Small. Torn. A cartoon frog keychain hanging off the zipper.

Not his. Hers. She gave it to him that morning in Ohio, in 2018, because his was too heavy.

She holds it. She opens it. Inside: a Walkman. A crushed bag of Doritos. A photo. Her and Leo. A park. Sunshine. He's holding the skateboard. She's smiling.

Mae makes a sound. One sound. Then she bites down on it, hard, until it stops.

She puts the photo in her jacket. She leaves the backpack in the nest.

MAE
(whispering, to no one)
Spatial shift. Level 3.

She says it again. Firmer.

MAE (CONT'D)
Spatial shift. Level 3. We got separated.

She crawls back out the way she came.

INT. LEVEL 11 - THE ENDLESS CITY - ALLEY - NIGHT

Six months before Kevin. Fog. Infinite skyscrapers. Neon signs in languages that don't exist.

Mae, 24, in the full kit now: hound jacket, gas mask, bandolier of Almond Water flasks, machete, a sawed-off shotgun. She's tracking someone. A trail of blue feathers on the wet pavement.

She comes around a corner and stops.

A procession. Figures in blue robes, walking slow, chanting. THE FOLLOWERS OF JERRY. White porcelain masks. Blue feathers in their hands.

At the back of the line, a young man. Vintage tee under the robe. Skateboard slung over his back on a rope.

Mae's whole body goes still.

MAE
(barely a breath)
Leo.

The figure stops. Turns. The mask tilts.

LEO
(calm, soothing)
Jerry loves you.

MAE
Leo. It's me. It's Mae.

She takes a step forward. He takes a step back.

LEO
I know who you are.

MAE
Then take that fucking thing off and come with me. Right now. I've been looking for you for six years. I've been looking everywhere.

LEO
(very gently)
No you haven't.

Mae stops.

LEO (CONT'D)
The Bird showed me. The Bird shows you everything you lost. I remember the ladder. I remember your hand. I remember you looking up.

MAE
That's not... It was a spatial shift. We got separated, Leo, the geometry shifted, I couldn't—

LEO
You looked at me and you climbed.

Silence. Just the hum of the city. Somewhere far off, a car alarm that's been going for a hundred years.

LEO (CONT'D)
It's okay. The Bird forgives you. The Bird forgives everybody. That's why I stay. Out there, nobody forgave anybody. Down here, I don't have to remember your face.

He turns and walks back into the procession. Mae lunges after him. Two robed figures step between them, knives out.

FOLLOWER
Jerry loves you.

MAE
FUCK JERRY!

She shoots one. The other one runs. When the smoke clears, the procession is gone into the fog.

Just a trail of blue feathers. And then not even that.

Mae stands in the alley, shotgun smoking, breathing hard.

And then, very quietly, she starts to say it again.

MAE
Spatial shift. Level 3.

She can't get to the end. Her voice breaks on "separated."

ACT THREE

INT. M.E.G. OUTPOST "BASE BETA" - LEVEL 11 - THE NEXT DAY

A bank vault converted into a field hospital. Cots. IV bags of Almond Water. HAYS, M.E.G. officer, forties, beard, tired eyes, cleaning a rifle.

Mae sits on an ammo crate across from him, sharpening her machete. Her knuckles are still split from the alley.

HAYS
You look like shit.

MAE
I found him.

Hays stops cleaning the rifle.

HAYS
Leo? Mae, that's... Where is he? We can send a team—

MAE
He's with the Jerry freaks. Mask and everything.

HAYS
Fuck. Okay. Okay. We can still... Those masks, the conditioning, it's reversible sometimes. If you get him somewhere quiet. Somewhere he's got a reason to come back. If he sees something that reminds him who he was—

MAE
He remembers who he was. That's the problem.

Hays doesn't understand. She doesn't explain.

HAYS
Mae. What happened on Level 3?

MAE
(flat, practiced)
Spatial shift. We got separated.

HAYS
That's what you always say.

MAE
'Cause it's what happened.

Long beat. Hays goes back to cleaning his rifle.

HAYS
Duke from the trading post's been asking around about you. Says you haven't brought him anything in a while.

MAE
I've been busy.

HAYS
Good. Stay busy.
(beat)
I know what you sell him, Mae. Everybody does. M.E.G. looks the other way because you bring us Hound hides and you know the routes. But you're better than that.

MAE
(standing, sheathing the machete)
No I'm not. Nobody down here is. That's the whole point of down here.

She walks out.

ACT FOUR

INT. LEVEL 0 - THE LOBBY - SIX MONTHS LATER

The hum. The yellow. The damp carpet. Infinite.

Mae walks. Cigarette. Bandolier. Gas mask around her neck. She's on her way to the Level 11 hub, on her way back to the city, on her way to try one more time, maybe, or maybe not.

Her radio crackles on her belt.

DUKE (V.O.)
(through static)
Mae. You out there? Got a buyer. Partygoers on Fun want someone fresh. Someone new to the level. They're paying in Level Keys. Five of them.

Mae doesn't answer. Keeps walking.

DUKE (V.O.) (CONT'D)
Five keys, Mae. That's a door to anywhere. That's a door to the Frontrooms, maybe. Think about it.

Mae stops walking. Stares at the radio.

Five keys. A door home. Real sky. A real Denny's. A place where nobody knows about the ladder.

And then a loud, wet THUD.

Ahead, in the corridor: a teenager drops out of thin air from the ceiling and lands on the damp carpet. Vintage graphic tee. Messy hair. A bag of Doritos. He groans.

KEVIN, 19.

He sits up. Rubs his head.

KEVIN
Ow! What the fuck?

Mae stares at him.

Not at a fresh one. At a face.

Same hair. Same awkward shoulders. Same graphic tee, practically. He looks like Leo would have looked if Leo had gotten to grow up somewhere with sunlight.

Her hand drifts to the radio.

Five keys.

She could. She's done it a hundred times. Rope, tape, a walk to Level 1, Duke's gold tooth, three flasks. It takes an afternoon. She's never looked at their faces. She doesn't have to look at this one.

She looks at it anyway.

INSERT: The photo in her jacket. Leo. The park. The skateboard.

And she thinks something. We see it cross her face. Not mercy. Something colder. Something with an angle.

The Jerry freaks want the young ones too. Fresh ones from the Frontrooms. They recruit them. They'd come for a kid like this. Leo would come for a kid like this. Leo would see a kid who looks like Leo and he would come.

A kid like this isn't five keys.

A kid like this is bait.

She takes her hand off the radio.

KEVIN
Where the hell am I? Is this a prank? Chad, you recording this?!

Mae takes a long drag on her cigarette. She steps out of the shadows. Combat boots splashing on the wet carpet.

She blows the smoke out slow.

MAE
Oh, for fuck's sake. Another fresh piece of meat.

The radio on her belt crackles.

DUKE (V.O.)
Mae? You there? Five keys.

She reaches down and switches it off without looking at it.

KEVIN
Hey! Excuse me! Do you know where the exit to this escape room is?

MAE
Listen to me very carefully...

We hold on her face as she says it. We've seen this scene before. Now we know what's behind it. Not a guardian. Not a rescuer. A girl who let go of a hand on a ladder and has been trying, ever since, to buy it back with other people.

She'll get Leo back. She'll get on the train. She'll sit in a Denny's and say she hates Denny's and smile like it's over.

It won't be over. Kevin will never know what she almost did on that first day in the yellow hallway. Leo won't ever say what the Bird showed him. And every night for the rest of her life, in a real bed, under a real roof, in the real world, Mae will wake up at 3 a.m. with her hand held out in front of her, open, in the shape of a hand that isn't there.

And she'll whisper it to the ceiling.

MAE
Spatial shift. Level 3. We got separated.

CUT TO BLACK.

END OF EPISODE
